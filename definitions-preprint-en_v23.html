The Meta-Tagging Project

Evaluating Definitions in a Verbatim-Grounded,
Field-Neutral Tag Layer
From provenance-preserving annotations to measurable concept membership

Shir Sivroni
Department of Mathematics and Computer Science, The Open University of Israel
ORCID: 0009-0000-2597-2824

Definition-evaluation corpus: 372 case–definition observations (289 literature-positive · 83 literature-negative) from 34 papers.
Tag-layer corpus: 538 records spanning 86 disciplines.
Scope: This paper presents a field-neutral framework for representing concepts as provenance-grounded records and evaluating competing definitions against an explicitly specified corpus. Art serves as a worked example rather than as the object of the study.
Data and code: doi:10.5281/zenodo.21432188.
Objective. Cross-disciplinary scholarship lacks a shared terminology. Researchers therefore cannot determine from terminology alone whether a phenomenon has already been studied elsewhere under a different name, or whether identical labels denote equivalent constructs or merely homonyms. Both failures are documented beyond the present corpus. The preferential association of similar entities is studied as homophily in sociology and as assortative mixing in network physics (McPherson et al., 2001; Newman, 2003). Conversely, criticality denotes a phase-transition regime in statistical physics, whereas neuroscience often operationalises “brain criticality” through signatures such as power-law neuronal avalanches, which are not sufficient on their own to establish critical dynamics (Mariani et al., 2022). This paper introduces an evidence-bearing concept layer to make such questions empirically tractable. Each concept becomes a record linking attested uses and senses to verbatim source passages, enabling convergent senses to be joined, divergent senses retained, and competing definitions evaluated against explicit corpus judgements. Art provides the worked example. The framework thereby turns canonicalisation from an implicit editorial choice into an auditable research procedure.

Status and intended scale. What is reported here is a proof of concept, and its corpus is sized accordingly: 538 tagged records, of which 34 papers and 372 adjudicated observations carry the definition evaluation. The programme it belongs to is larger — an interactive tagging platform holding tens of thousands of papers, in which a reader assembles a corpus, submits a definition of their own, and has it judged against that corpus in a single run. Nothing in the method reported here depends on the corpus being small; what the small corpus establishes is that the procedure is auditable, and what it cannot yet establish is how the measurements behave at scale.

Seeing the idea. A prototype exists and can be looked at without an account: the definition scorer shows what the layer is meant to feel like — choose a concept, choose the corpus, and each candidate definition appears with its confusion matrix, its case table, and the licensing quotation under every score. It is a demonstration rather than a working tool: two concepts are loaded, the scored corpus is the 29 papers judged in one run, and a reader cannot yet submit a definition of their own for a fresh judging run. It is shown here so the shape of the thing is visible, not because it is ready to be used. The platform is at its home page and the tag layer, the TEI editions and the code are at the showcase.

Study overview

A concise orientation to the argument. Each panel opens to show the supporting rationale.

1

Research problem

Competing definitions are commonly assessed through argument and counterexample. This study asks whether the classifications they entail can also be evaluated systematically and reproducibly against source-grounded cases.

Rationale

Cross-disciplinary synthesis is hindered by terminological heterogeneity: related phenomena are described differently across fields, while shared labels may denote distinct constructs. Ontologies, citation graphs, and keyword indexes expose part of this structure, but often retain discipline-specific vocabularies or require intensive curation. They therefore do not reliably supply evidence-grounded cross-field join keys. The problem also extends to interpretive and social research: framing has been described as a “fractured paradigm” whose theoretical and operational uses span psychology, sociology, political science, and communication (Entman, 1993). Lexical overlap must therefore be treated as a question to be tested, not as evidence of conceptual identity.

The study is situated within digital-humanities research on representing textual evidence computationally without erasing its provenance or interpretive context.

Common ground and methodological distinction

The cited projects annotate phenomena expressed in text, including figurative instances, place names, delegitimisation, and idioms. In those studies, annotation is the primary analytic object. Here, annotation supplies the evidence against which alternative definitions are evaluated. The central question is therefore not only what a text contains, but which stated definition best reproduces a corpus's explicit case-level judgements.

Two methodological gaps are visible across several of the reviewed projects.

1 · Category semantics are not always operationalised. When category definitions and scope notes are absent, agreement may depend on tacit coder judgement. 2 · Reliability is reported unevenly. In the publications summarised here, only the Rivlin-Angert study reports a formal agreement estimate (three coders, 170 sentences, κ = 0.82); other projects either do not estimate reliability or adopt a different methodological objective.

The present contribution is to couple each non-trivial tag with source evidence and to treat category membership as an explicit, measurable decision rule.

Structural analogy and remaining problem

Rusinek's Kima has a structure analogous to the record used here — place → name variant → dated attestation against concept → wording → quotation. The analogy is useful but incomplete: a place can often be resolved to an external referent, whereas an abstract concept lacks an equivalent direct test. Establishing when two textual uses instantiate the same sense therefore remains an interpretive and empirical problem.

Interoperability requirements

Cross-project interoperability does not require identical annotation schemes, but it does require compatible identifiers, anchors, and authority links:

  • An opaque identifier instead of a label. Where the tag is the word, any change of wording silently breaks every external link. A stable identifier, with the words hanging off it as skos:prefLabel and skos:altLabel.
  • A textual anchor that is not an offset. A TextQuoteSelector (quotation plus preceding and following context) survives re-editions; character offsets do not. Offsets for tools that need them are derived from it at export time.
  • Resolution to an external authority — Wikidata, GeoNames. Once two layers point at the same identifier they join without any bilateral arrangement.

Two export formats reflect common practice in the reviewed work: two-tier TEI (metadata in teiHeader, span annotations in standOff), and a pair of Gephi CSV files (nodes and edges).

A reciprocal test is possible: applying the present protocol to an external category system would provide an out-of-domain evaluation of the method while also making that system's category semantics and reliability empirically inspectable.
3

Methodological contribution

Each candidate definition is operationalised as a binary decision rule and compared with cases that the source literature explicitly includes in or excludes from the target concept.

Method and implementation status

Earlier analyses found that free-text labels rarely joined across fields, whereas a controlled statistical vocabulary did. The methodological implication is that canonicalisation requires not merely a preferred label, but an explicit and testable membership rule.

Canonicalisation is a definitional operation. Assigning different surface forms to one canonical term entails a judgement about category membership. In many tagging workflows this judgement remains implicit; the framework proposed here makes it explicit and subjects its application to reliability analysis.

7.1 Concepts as records

A concept represented only as a string attached to a paper cannot directly encode distinct senses, contrast cases, relations among interpretations, or evidence provenance. The proposed representation therefore treats the concept as a record rather than as a label.

The concept_records.json build dated 9 August 2026, with material appended on 10 August, contains 2,103 ConceptNode records grouped into 2,464 paper-level mentions and comprising 2,599 Attestation objects. Of these attestations, 1,880 carry a quotation and 719 still require grounding. The schema also records role (defines / exemplifies / refutes / mentions) and assertion status (affirmed / denied / unresolved). These figures specify both the implemented data model and the remaining corpus-wide grounding work.

The reference model is a historical gazetteer: place → name variant → dated attestation, where the entity is the record and the document is the evidence. Here it is concept → wording → quotation. The structures are analogous; the substantive difference is that an abstract concept does not generally have a directly observable referent. Sense resolution must therefore remain evidence-bearing and open to reliability assessment.

7.2 A concept may hold more than one sense

The same lexical form may express distinct theoretical constructs. Collapsing those constructs into a single aggregate can therefore obscure rather than summarise the corpus. The schema can represent senses and evidence-bearing relations among them (contrasts, extends, narrows), although corpus-wide population of these fields awaits completion of the attestation-grounding stage.

A worked example concerns priming. In one literature, the prime and target are different stimuli linked by category, and the outcome is the interpretation assigned to an ambiguous description; in another, they are the same stimulus and the outcome is a latency reduction. The latter literature explicitly rejects facilitation of the stage implicated by the former. A canonical merge without sense disambiguation would therefore create a relation that the source literature does not support.

7.3 Two gates, not one

The verbatim rule proves that a sentence exists in a paper. That is a string comparison, and a string comparison establishes provenance and nothing about meaning. A second gate asks whether the sentence licenses the concept at all.

In an initial audit, 82 attestations passed the substring gate and four failed the meaning gate — graph theory tagged on a sentence that never names it, machine learning tagged on a clause arguing the opposite, deep learning tagged on a paper that trains no deep network, bias tagged inside a three-item list that did not establish its role. All four were exact quotations. This result shows that exact-string verification establishes provenance but is insufficient to establish that the cited sentence licenses the assigned concept.

7.4 Measuring the membership rule

The rest of this paper (§§8–13) sets out the measurement: a definition treated as a binary classifier over cases the literature itself adjudicated, ranked against rivals in a single run, after three logical gates that no statistical measure detects — circularity, recursion without a base case, and a free parameter the author never fixed. Inter-coder reliability is reported on the act of application itself (κ = 0.547, n = 140).

The contribution of this paper is methodological rather than a claim of new cross-disciplinary discovery. It specifies a concept-record schema, distinguishes provenance from semantic licensing, and evaluates definition application on a documented corpus. The public implementation remains incomplete where concept-level quotations and sense relations have not yet been populated.
4

Evaluation design

A case is positive only when a source explicitly includes it in the target concept and negative only when a source explicitly excludes it. Silence is treated as unresolved, not negative.

Validity gates and scoring

Candidate definitions are evaluated only on explicitly adjudicated cases. Each positive or negative label is linked to a verified quotation from the source paper. The labels therefore represent documented corpus judgements rather than claims of context-independent truth.

What the gates disqualify, and why before scoring rather than after

Some candidate definitions achieve high classification scores despite structural defects that the confusion matrix cannot identify. The framework therefore applies logical validity checks before comparative ranking and reports gated definitions as unranked.

Gate 1 — circularity

The definiens contains the definiendum. Our circular control ("whatever people call art") achieves an almost perfect MCC because it reproduces the corpus label. Predictive scores alone cannot identify this form of target leakage.

Gate 2 — recursion without a base case

Levinson's historical definition: "a thing intended for regard as prior artworks were correctly regarded." It defines art by reference to prior art and never states what the first art was. The mathematical defence (0 is a number; n+1 is a number) works only when the base is given. Here there is n+1 and no 0.

Gate 3 — failure of point identification

Gaut's cluster account: an object is art if it meets enough of ten criteria, none of which is necessary — and he never says how many "enough" is. Dutton, who defends the same approach, concedes the point explicitly: "A recognition-criteria approach ... does not tell us in advance how many of the criteria need be present."
We decomposed the ten criteria and coded 120 cases against each. Moving the threshold from k = 1 to k = 10 moves the score across a range of 0.6 MCC, and if k may be chosen per case the account attains MCC 1.000. Without a fixed threshold, the definition is not point-identified for quantitative evaluation: its reported score is contingent on an analyst-selected parameter.

Why the gates precede scoring

All three defects are external to the confusion matrix. A circular definition may classify well because it reproduces the target label, whereas a definition with a free threshold receives the score associated with the selected value. Applying these gates after model selection would permit structurally invalid candidates to influence the ranking. The gates are therefore evaluated before scoring and before automated definition induction.

Measurement versus refinement — a distinction that must be recorded

Iterative revision requires versioned definitions and a clear distinction between prospective evaluation and post hoc refinement:

Measurement — the wording was fixed before the cases were seen. The score is a test.
Refinement — the wording was fixed after it became clear which cases failed. The score is not a test, because the cases are already known.

Both activities are legitimate, but their scores have different inferential meanings. The data model therefore retains each version and records whether its wording preceded or followed inspection of the evaluation cases.

Sub-concepts must themselves be defined

A definition that depends on an undefined subsidiary term transfers part of the decision to tacit judgement. In one analysis, operationalising such a subsidiary term without changing the outer wording increased MCC from +0.380 to +0.664. The framework therefore decomposes subsidiary terms until further operationalisation no longer changes classification outcomes.

5

Interpretive scope

The framework does not determine what art is. It quantifies how closely a stated definition reproduces explicit judgements in a specified corpus.

Scope and intended use

The layer is intended as a hypothesis-generating instrument for far reading. It identifies candidate regions in which close reading may be informative; it does not replace interpretive analysis or establish context-independent conceptual truth.

6.1 Why a grounded layer, rather than reading on demand

A language model can generate an immediate cross-disciplinary answer, but the basis of that answer may be difficult to audit. A grounded layer imposes a slower evidential workflow in which each supported claim resolves to a source passage. Its intended advantage is traceability rather than speed.

What the matrix covers, and what it does not

Three of the six criteria are computed from these four cells: coverage (recall = TP/(TP+FN)), discrimination (precision = TP/(TP+FP)), and prediction — the same four cells on a held-out sample not used in constructing the definition. The remaining three lie outside it. Typicality ranking requires an order rather than labels, and is measured by rank correlation between the ranking a definition predicts and frequency of attestation in the corpus. Applicability is measured by κ. Minimality is a property of the definition rather than of the cases, and is measured by ablation: remove one condition, re-score; a condition whose removal changes nothing is ornament.

Three states, not two

A case is positive when a paper explicitly includes it in the concept and negative when a paper explicitly excludes it. The third state is unresolved: absence of an assertion is not evidence of negation. Treating silence as negative would systematically convert non-mention into exclusion and bias the evaluation.

The unit of analysis is accordingly neither the paper nor the passage but the (case, paper) pair. The same case may be positive in one paper and negative in another, and that disagreement is the most valuable datum in the corpus rather than noise: collapsing to the paper loses it, collapsing to the case loses who said it. Variance is decomposed with the paper as a random effect (ICC = between / (between + within)), so that twenty-five attestations from a single paper inflate nothing.

Abstract
Background, methods, results, and conclusions.

Background. Cross-disciplinary synthesis is constrained by terminological heterogeneity: related phenomena may be named differently, whereas identical labels may denote distinct constructs. Earlier work introduced a field-neutral tag layer in which each non-trivial assignment is linked to an exact licensing passage. The structure proved auditable, but free-text phenomenon labels rarely produced cross-field joins.

Methods. We distinguish two datasets. The tag-layer corpus contains 538 records across 86 disciplines; its provenance audit re-checked 18,416 evidence strings. The definition-evaluation corpus contains 372 explicitly adjudicated case observations from 34 papers (289 positive; 83 negative). Candidate definitions are treated as binary decision rules, screened for circularity, unsupported recursion, and unidentified parameters, and then compared using Matthews correlation coefficient (MCC). Application reliability was estimated on a blinded sample of 140 case–definition pairs independently labelled by a second language-model system with the first labels withheld.

Results. The provenance audit identified three failed strings. Among 511 papers, 500 distinct free-text phenomenon labels yielded only two cross-disciplinary matches. By contrast, a controlled statistical vocabulary produced join keys spanning as many as 31 disciplines; re-audit of 519 paper–term assignments retained 420 and rejected 99 (19.1%). In the definition-evaluation corpus, the highest-ranked eligible definition achieved MCC = 0.700. Reliability of application was κ = 0.547 with 78.6% observed agreement (n = 140). The prespecified expectation of distinctly lower agreement for the cluster account was not supported in this sample.

Conclusions. Canonicalisation is not merely a normalisation step: it entails an explicit rule for concept membership. A defensible tag layer must therefore preserve provenance, represent alternative senses, and measure the reproducibility of its membership decisions. The concept_records.json build dated 9 August 2026, with material appended on 10 August, contains 2,103 concept records, 2,464 paper-level mentions, and 2,599 attestation objects; 1,880 attestations carry a quotation and 719 still require grounding. Sense relations also remain incompletely populated. The work should thus be read as a validated measurement framework with a quantified implementation gap, not as a completed corpus-wide semantic layer.

Scope. This study evaluates a field-neutral method rather than a substantive definition of art. In principle, any concept can be represented as an evidence-bearing record and competing definitions assessed against an explicitly delimited corpus. Art is used because its literature states definitional disagreements unusually clearly; game provides a secondary test case. The present demonstration remains bounded in two respects. First, the scored corpus includes 29 of 60 papers so that every definition is evaluated within a single, comparable run. Second, newly submitted definitions can currently be assessed only against previously adjudicated cases. User-defined corpora and fresh adjudication runs are implementation targets, not results reported here.

0. Scope and contribution
What the paper establishes, and what it does not.

A shared, verbatim-grounded annotation format can be audited at scale, but free-text phenomenon labels rarely support cross-disciplinary joining. This paper addresses the methodological consequence of that null finding.

The annotation layer

Each paper is annotated by the shape of its contribution rather than by topic alone: claim type, phenomenon, claim relations, replication status, and limitations. The shared tag registry (SCHEMA/tags.json, version 0.5) contains 45 tags in seven layers.

Verbatim grounding is a provenance constraint. Each non-trivial tag must be accompanied by an exact licensing passage. In the standing audit, 18,416 evidence strings were re-checked against source text and three failed. This procedure verifies that the cited text exists; it does not by itself verify that the passage semantically licenses the assigned tag.

Why canonicalisation forces a definition

Free-text phenomenon labels produced almost no cross-field joins, whereas the controlled statistical vocabulary produced many. This contrast identifies canonical vocabulary as a necessary joining layer. Canonicalisation, however, requires a rule for deciding which surface forms and cases belong under the same term. That rule is a definition in operational form.

Contribution

The paper links canonicalisation to explicit definition evaluation: concepts are represented as records capable of holding senses, evidence, and contrast cases, while candidate membership rules are evaluated on a documented corpus.

The conceptual and operational components are interdependent. A concept record provides a place to represent competing senses; a measured membership rule makes those senses empirically inspectable rather than merely asserted.

Implementation status

  • Implemented: the concept_records.json build dated 9 August 2026, with material appended on 10 August, contains 2,103 concept records grouped into 2,464 paper-level mentions and comprising 2,599 attestations; the schema also provides sense relations, explicit positive/negative/unresolved case states, definition scoring, and the three pre-evaluation validity gates.
  • Measured: definition performance on 372 adjudicated observations and blinded application reliability on 140 case–definition pairs.
  • Not yet complete: according to the same concept_records.json build, 1,880 of the 2,599 attestations carry a quotation and 719 still require grounding; sense and sense-relation fields are not yet populated corpus-wide.
The claim is deliberately limited. This paper contributes a traceable data model and an evaluated membership procedure. It does not overturn the earlier null finding, and it does not claim that the public corpus has already become a complete semantic knowledge layer.
1. Background and motivation
Terminological heterogeneity, auditable annotation, and the definition problem.

Scholarly findings are primarily communicated in natural-language prose, while cross-disciplinary retrieval depends on identifying relations among differently worded claims. Keyword search, citation networks, and discipline-specific ontologies each expose part of this structure, but none guarantees that semantically related observations will share a searchable label.

The Meta-Tagging Project addresses this problem by describing the form of a contribution and grounding each non-trivial assignment in a source passage. The earlier empirical result was negative but informative: source-faithful phenomenon strings seldom matched across disciplines. A controlled statistical vocabulary joined more effectively, suggesting that cross-field retrieval requires canonical categories.

Research question. If canonicalisation requires a membership rule, how can competing rules be represented, applied, and compared without discarding provenance or converting interpretive disagreement into unrecorded coder discretion?

The study answers this question in three steps. It distinguishes provenance verification from semantic licensing, represents concepts as evidence-bearing records capable of holding multiple senses, and evaluates candidate definitions as decision rules over explicitly adjudicated cases. The worked example concerns definitions of art, but the measurement problem is intended to generalise to other contested scholarly categories.

2. Conceptual framework
A small, field-neutral facet set with explicit provenance.

Each paper is annotated with a deliberately compact set of facets intended to describe the form of a contribution rather than its topic: claim-type (existence / mechanism / method / prediction / critique / null-result), phenomenon, claim-relations (builds-on / extends / contradicts / replicates, each linked to a named target), replication-status, and limitations.

The defining constraint is verbatim grounding. Because a tag is an interpretation of natural language, each non-trivial assignment must include a licensing excerpt and a resolvable provenance state. This design makes the annotation inspectable in a way that an ungrounded assertion or dense embedding is not. Human-readable HTML views and machine-readable JSON records are generated from the same canonical data.

2.1 Data structure

Each paper is represented by one canonical record. Tag assignments point to their licensing passages. The shared registry (SCHEMA/tags.json, version 0.5) currently contains 45 tags in seven layers. The registry centralises labels and descriptors, but formal scope notes, inclusion rules, and grounded examples are not yet complete for every tag; this is treated as an implementation requirement rather than as a finished property.

3. Corpus, annotation, and validation
Two corpora, provenance auditing, and deterministic derived outputs.

3.1 Tag-layer corpus

The tag-layer corpus is curated for disciplinary breadth and serves as a proof of format and procedure; it is not a probability sample and does not support claims of disciplinary representativeness. The current build contains 538 records across 86 disciplines. The build pipeline validates annotation invariants, recalculates reported quantities, derives postings and record shards for search, exports an adjacency structure for implemented relations, and regenerates audit and reading views.

Of the 126 papers in the proof-of-format corpus, 10 had unusable text layers. Among the remaining 116 papers with usable text, faceting was completed for 116/116 papers (100%). These status values can be checked against DATA/corpus.json; the ten unusable records are retained with an explicit untaggable status rather than silently excluded.

A standing provenance audit re-checked 18,416 evidence strings against their associated source text and identified three failures. A further 623 strings matched only an older stored copy of the corresponding paper and are reported separately as a weaker provenance state.

3.2 Definition-evaluation corpus

The definition analysis uses a distinct corpus of 372 adjudicated case observations drawn from 34 papers: 289 observations in which a source explicitly includes the case in the concept and 83 in which a source explicitly excludes it. Unresolved or silent cases are not treated as negative. All candidate definitions are evaluated within the same run and under the same coding instruction.

4. Empirical results and the null finding
Free-text labels rarely join across disciplines.

In the audited proof-of-format, 116 papers across 38 disciplines were fully faceted. The resulting layer contained 127 claim-relation assignments and 87 limitation assignments, each verified by exact-string matching to the available source text.

Free-text phenomenon labels did not provide an effective cross-field join layer. In the audited corpus, only one pair of disciplines shared a phenomenon string verbatim. In the expanded corpus, 511 papers yielded 500 distinct phenomenon strings, only two of which crossed a disciplinary boundary. A separate novelty scan did not identify a candidate cross-disciplinary relation that had not already been described in the literature.

The result distinguishes conceptual overlap from string identity. Motifs such as priming or power-law structure may recur across fields, yet source-faithful free-text descriptions remain too specific to match reliably. These observations motivate a canonical vocabulary layer above, rather than in place of, the grounded source wording.

5. Controlled statistical vocabulary as a cross-field join layer
A controlled methodological vocabulary joins where free text does not.

A controlled vocabulary of statistical terms provides a direct test of the proposed canonical layer. The broadest audited term spans 31 disciplines. Re-audit of all 519 legacy paper–term assignments rejected 99 (19.1%) and retained 420 as semantically valid. Because source availability and exact local-body grounding are incomplete, both the primary counts and a stricter exact-local-basis sensitivity analysis are reported.

This result supports a bounded conclusion: controlled terms can function as cross-field join keys, but only because their labels and intended statistical meanings have already been normalised. The statistical layer therefore demonstrates the value of canonicalisation; it does not eliminate the need to specify and test membership rules for less standardised concepts.

5.2 Query implementation

The public interface implements static, client-side search using an inverted index of 1,943 keys mapped to document postings. Queries are served without a backend; their cost depends on postings-list lengths and output size rather than being independent of corpus size.

5.3 Unlabelled regions as hypotheses

Regions that contain multiple grounded observations but lack a shared label may indicate a terminological gap. This pattern is hypothesis-generating rather than decisive: absence of a label in the sampled literature does not establish that no term exists. In the memory taxonomy example, one such region corresponds approximately to what might be termed short-term implicit memory and warrants targeted literature review.

6. Interpretive scope
Corpus-relative measurement rather than an ontological verdict.

The framework estimates how well a stated definition reproduces explicit judgements in a specified corpus. It does not determine what art—or any other contested concept—ultimately is. Positive and negative labels record what a cited source explicitly includes or excludes; silence, ambiguity, and unexamined cases remain unresolved.

This distinction is central to the intended use. The tag layer supports far-reading and hypothesis generation by identifying patterns that warrant close reading. It does not replace historical context, interpretive argument, or expert adjudication. A high score therefore indicates fit to documented corpus judgements under a particular instruction, not metaphysical truth or universal disciplinary consensus.

7. Concept records and measurable membership
From labels to evidence-bearing records with explicit decision rules.

A concept is represented as a record rather than as a string attached to a paper. The normative model can store labels, external identifiers, source-grouped attestations, distinct senses, relations among senses, criteria, and contrast cases. This structure allows disagreement to remain visible instead of being collapsed into one preferred wording.

Canonicalisation is treated as a definitional operation: assigning several expressions to one key entails a rule about membership. That rule is made inspectable by linking decisions to quotations and measurable by applying candidate definitions to explicitly adjudicated cases. Current implementation boundary. 8. Pre-evaluation validity gates
Circularity, unsupported recursion, and unidentified parameters.

Classification performance is interpreted only after three logical checks. First, a definition is circular when its definiens reproduces the definiendum; a high score in that case reflects target leakage. Second, a recursive definition requires an explicit base case. Third, a quantitative application must fix any threshold or parameter needed to determine membership.

The gates are applied before ranking because none can be diagnosed from a confusion matrix alone. A circular definition may classify nearly perfectly, while a free threshold can be chosen to maximise performance after the outcomes are known. Gated candidates remain visible for diagnostic comparison but are not assigned a rank.

The cluster-account sensitivity analysis illustrates point identification: changing the required number of satisfied criteria from one to ten spans 0.6 MCC, and allowing the threshold to vary by case produces perfect apparent fit. This is evidence of flexibility in the operationalisation, not evidence of perfect definition performance.

Nine criteria: three matrix measures, three external measures, and three prior gates.

10. Classification framework and results
Corpus-relative confusion matrices and MCC-based comparison.

Each eligible definition is applied as a binary decision rule, D(x), to cases for which the source literature supplies an explicit inclusion or exclusion. This produces the four conventional cells: true positives and false negatives among literature-positive observations, and false positives and true negatives among literature-negative observations. The interactive explanation and complete result table are provided in Interactive Figure 2.

The primary summary measure is the Matthews correlation coefficient because 289 of the 372 observations (77.7%) are positive. MCC incorporates all four cells and assigns a value of zero to an all-positive classifier in this dataset, whereas F1 can remain high when true negatives are ignored.

Primary result. Among candidates that passed the logical gates, the condition-based definition achieved the highest observed MCC (0.700; TP = 260, FP = 13, FN = 29, TN = 70). The aesthetic definition ranked second descriptively (MCC = 0.477). These are within-corpus estimates; close comparisons require uncertainty intervals and held-out evaluation.

Gated candidates are displayed but unranked. In particular, the circular control produced near-perfect classification because it reproduced the target label. This is a diagnostic control demonstrating that predictive fit alone is insufficient evidence of a valid definition.

The reference variable has three states: positive, negative, and unresolved. Only the first two enter the confusion matrix. The observational unit is the case–paper pair so that different sources may explicitly classify the same case differently without that disagreement being erased.

11. Concept-record data model
Provenance, source grouping, senses, criteria, and contrast cases.

The proposed model centres on ConceptNode, with paper-level provenance stored through PaperSource and mention-level evidence represented by Attestation. Optional structures represent Sense, SenseRelation, Criterion, ContrastCase, and Theorist. Grouping attestations by source prevents many mentions from one paper from being treated as independent positions.

Each evidential relation is designed to resolve to a quotation and locator. Positive, negative, and unresolved assertion states are retained explicitly, and the object_level field distinguishes a case of the concept from a proposition about the concept. The linked, field-level glossary is available through Interactive Figure 1 and Appendix B.

The model is partly prospective. Record types and identifiers are implemented, but public attestation quotations, senses, and sense relations remain unpopulated. Claims in this article therefore distinguish schema capability from populated empirical content.

12. Reliability of definition application
Agreement between two independently prompted language-model coding systems.

Applicability is evaluated separately from predictive performance. If independent coders apply the same wording differently to the same case, the confusion matrix combines properties of the definition, the instruction, and the coding system.

A blinded sample of 140 case–definition pairs (20 per definition) was coded by a second language-model system that had not produced the first set of judgements. Both systems received the same operational question: “Does this definition, read literally, apply to this case?” The task was therefore definition application rather than an unconstrained classification of whether a case is art.

Common ground and methodological distinction

These projects annotate phenomena in texts, including figurative expressions, place names, delegitimisation, and idioms. The present study uses annotation as an evidential substrate for a different question: which operational definition best fits a corpus of explicitly adjudicated cases? This shift makes category specification, provenance, and application reliability direct objects of evaluation.

The contribution addresses two recurring, but not universal, methodological gaps. Some category systems provide labels without operational scope conditions, and several projects do not report intercoder reliability. Rivlin-Angert and Mor-Lan are an important counterexample, reporting κ = 0.82 for three coders on a 170-sentence sample. The present framework combines verbatim evidence for each non-trivial assignment with explicit membership rules whose application can be tested.

Structural analogy and its limit

The Kima data model provides a useful structural analogy: place → name variant → dated attestation can be compared with concept → wording → quotation. The models are not identical, however. Geographic variants can often be checked against an external referent or phonetic normalisation, whereas abstract concept membership generally lacks an equivalent independent test. The latter therefore requires explicit adjudication and uncertainty states.

Interoperability — what must be emitted

Technical connection need not require a shared interpretive theory, but it does require compatible representation choices:

  • An opaque identifier instead of a label. Where the tag is the word, any change of wording silently breaks every external link. A stable identifier, with the words hanging off it as skos:prefLabel and skos:altLabel.
  • A textual anchor that is not an offset. A TextQuoteSelector (quotation plus preceding and following context) survives re-editions; character offsets do not. Offsets for tools that need them are derived from it at export time.
  • Resolution to an external authority — Wikidata, GeoNames. Once two layers point at the same identifier they join without any bilateral arrangement.

Two export formats are especially compatible with the practices reviewed here: two-tier TEI (metadata in teiHeader, span annotations in standOff), and a pair of Gephi CSV files (nodes and edges).

External validation opportunity. Applying this framework to an independently developed category system would test whether explicit scope rules improve reliability outside the corpus used to develop the method.
14. Limitations and threats to validity
Sampling, model dependence, implementation status, and reproducibility constraints.

The results should be interpreted within the following constraints.

  • Reliability is model-based and imprecisely estimated. κ = 0.547 over 140 blinded pairs reflects agreement between two language-model coding systems, not human coders. The per-definition sample is 20 and confidence intervals are not reported.
  • Scores are instruction-sensitive. The same definition scored +0.714 and +0.591 on identical cases under differently worded instructions. Cross-run comparisons therefore require an identical protocol; MCC here is a property of the definition–instruction–coder combination, not of a definition in isolation.
  • The negatives are not a representative sample. They are contrast cases someone troubled to write down, not a random sample of what is not the concept. Accuracy and specificity should therefore not be interpreted as population estimates. Researcher-labelled hard negatives are reported separately from literature-grounded cases.
  • There is no access to authorial intent. What was not written is not measured.
  • Recall has a ceiling. What was missed cannot be known without knowing the whole set; coverage is therefore coverage relative to a documented pool, and must be named as such.
  • The corpus is still small. 34 papers. The bootstrap unit is the paper, so the effective n is 34 rather than 372 — which is why the lower places in a ranking are not separable.
  • The public concept layer is not yet evidentially complete. The concept_records.json build dated 9 August 2026, with material appended on 10 August, contains 2,103 concept records grouped into 2,464 paper-level mentions and comprising 2,599 attestation objects. Of these, 1,880 carry a quotation and 719 still require grounding; sense and relation fields are likewise incomplete. The diagrams describe both the implemented data model and work not yet completed corpus-wide.
  • The registry remains underspecified for evaluation. Its 45 tags have labels and brief descriptors, but comprehensive scope notes, inclusion rules, and grounded examples are not yet available for every tag.
  • Independent recomputation of the κ table is currently incomplete. The public repository provides the coding manual, task instructions, and blank coding sheet, but not the completed response file required by the scoring script.
  • External validity has not been established. Cross-corpus evaluation and the proposed scoring workflow for independently developed category systems remain future work.
  • Open-textured positions require a different target. A view that rejects necessary and sufficient conditions cannot be evaluated as though it supplied such a classifier; its operational consequences would require a separately specified test.

Appendix A — formulae
The formulae in full.

All measures are computed from the four cells. TP and FN are counted over cases the corpus adjudicated positive, FP and TN over those it adjudicated negative.

precision = TP / (TP + FP) — discrimination
recall = TP / (TP + FN) — coverage
F1 = 2 · precision · recall / (precision + recall)
MCC = (TP·TN − FP·FN) / sqrt((TP+FP)(TP+FN)(TN+FP)(TN+FN))

Why MCC and not F1. F1 ignores TN, so on a positively skewed corpus a classifier that says yes to everything obtains a high F1. MCC gives that same classifier exactly 0.000 (Matthews, 1975; Chicco & Jurman, 2020). F1 is reported beside it, never alone.

Reliability

κ = (p_o − p_e) / (1 − p_e)
p_o = observed agreement · p_e = agreement expected by chance from the two coders' marginals (Cohen, 1960). Interpretation of values is reported with the commonly used Landis and Koch (1977) descriptors, which are treated as heuristic rather than as evidential thresholds. For designs with more than two coders or missing observations, Krippendorff's alpha may be preferable (Hayes & Krippendorff, 2007).

Variance between papers and within them

The same case may be judged in several papers. Variance is decomposed by analysis of variance:

ICC = σ²_between / (σ²_between + σ²_within) (Shrout & Fleiss, 1979)
Higher ICC is consistent with observations clustering by paper and may motivate investigation of disciplinary or author-level differences.
Lower ICC indicates greater within-paper variation and may signal locally contested or context-dependent usage.

A hierarchical model with paper as a random effect can represent both levels and reduces, but does not automatically eliminate, the influence of many correlated attestations from a single source.

Confidence intervals and comparison

Definitions are ranked with a paired paper-level bootstrap of 2,000 resamples: two definitions share a place unless one achieves the higher score in at least 95% of resamples, so that ties are ties on the evidence rather than artefacts of overlapping intervals. The paired bootstrap resamples the same papers for both definitions and asks how often one actually beats the other, which is the question a ranking answers; papers rather than cases are resampled, because cases drawn from the same paper are not independent. Each definition's score is additionally reported with a 95% confidence interval from 3,000 paper-level bootstrap resamples, and paired case-level differences are assessed with McNemar's test (McNemar, 1947). Scoring is performed by TOOLS/art_score_v3.py with results in ART_CASE/scores_v3.json; the ranking and its tie rule are TOOLS/final_ranking.py, with output in ART_CASE/final_ranking.json.

Appendix C — automated construction of candidate definitions
Generation from atomic conditions and the need for prior validity gates.

The exploratory procedure can assemble a candidate from atomic conditions extracted from definitions in the corpus and evaluate it under the same scoring framework.

Validity gates must precede optimisation. In an initial run, the candidate inventory included the circular condition “produced with the intention of making a work of art.” The search selected this condition because it maximised the observed score. Circular conditions are therefore marked in the data and excluded before candidate search; post hoc rejection would otherwise permit target leakage to determine the selected model.
Discrimination is constrained by corpus coverage. A later candidate used the property “exhibits an individual point of view” and scored highly until seven researcher-labelled negative controls were added. It admitted all seven. This sensitivity analysis shows that a corpus cannot test exclusions it does not contain. The controls are therefore stored separately from literature-grounded evidence, identified as researcher judgements, and reported both with and without inclusion. They probe failure modes but do not substitute for source-grounded negative cases.
