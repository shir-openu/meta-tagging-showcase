phenomenonlocalization of single-neuron recordings in human hippocampal CA1 and CA3
claim-typemethod
mechanismMonosynaptic Schaffer collateral transmission from CA3 to CA1 produces the 2-5 msec lagged firing correlation, while shared perforant path input to both subfields produces the 0-msec synchronous correlation. — “Synchronous activity (0-msec differential) indicates common input, consistent with perforant path projections to both CA3 and CA1. Feed-forward short-lag (2–5 msec) correlation is consistent with correctly categorized CA1 and CA3 neurons connected monosynaptically by Schaffer collateral projections.”
relation — “Electrophysiological recordings were limited due to anesthesia, and this approach required additional time and risk associated with placing depth electrodes during planned temporal lobe resection.14 In this study we demonstrate a combined imaging and electrophysiological recording methodology that successfully confirms electrode placement within the CA1 and CA3 subfields of the human hippocampus without requiring postoperative histological confirmation.”
holds whenfeed-forward correlations → synaptic connectivity — only for correlations in the 2- to 5-msec window — “only the 2- to 5-msec feed-forward correlations”
replicationuntested
limitationOnly the 2-5 msec feed-forward correlations can be taken as definitive evidence of CA3-to-CA1 synaptic connectivity; the synchronous and long-lag pairs are inferred to be correctly assigned rather than proven so. — “While only the 2- to 5-msec feed-forward correlations (20.3%) can be definitively identified as CA3-to- CA1 synaptic connectivity, the predominance of positive correlations (77.8%) and infrequent occurrence of inverse correlations suggest that the synchronous and multisynaptic correlate cell pairs are correctly identified with respect to CA3 and CA1 localization, and are further consistent with known common projections from entorhinal cortex to hippocampus, as well as indirect projections within the CA3 and CA1 cell layers.”
limitationThe localization procedure used here is not a standardized stereotactic system, which the authors say would be needed for cross-centre standardization. — “Localization using standardized temporal stereotactic systems, such as that described by Miller et al.,25 would be useful for future iterations or for standardization across surgical centers.”
limitationA cell type is inferred from a firing-rate threshold and those cells are excluded from the sample. — “Neurons with steady-state resting firing rates > 20 Hz (i.e., clearly interneurons) were not recorded”
limitationThe cell-type labels are confirmed by the connectivity pattern they are used to demonstrate. — “Cells were selected as candidate CA3 and CA1 principal neurons on the basis of waveform and firing rate characteristics and confirmed within the CA3-to-CA1 neural projection pathways via measures of functional connectivity”
limitationThe human proportion is validated by its resemblance to published animal numbers. — “feed-forward correlation between CA3-CA1 pairs was identified in 1071 (26%) of 4070 total pairs, which favorably compares to reports of 20%–25% feedforward CA3-CA1 correlation noted in published animal studies”

findingNearly 80% of putative CA3-to-CA1 cell pairs showed positive cross-correlations compatible with a feed-forward connection, whereas only 2.6% showed inverse (feedback) correlation. — “Cross-correlation analysis confirmed that nearly 80% of putative CA3-to-CA1 cell pairs exhibited positive correlations compatible with feed-forward connection between the cells, while only 2.6% exhibited feedback (inverse) connectivity.”
findingFeed-forward correlation was found in 1,071 of 4,070 CA3-CA1 pairs (26%), a rate close to the 20%-25% reported in animal studies, even after synchronous and long-latency correlations were excluded. — “Even though synchronous and long-latency correlations were excluded, feed-forward correlation between CA3-CA1 pairs was identified in 1071 (26%) of 4070 total pairs, which favorably compares to reports of 20%–25% feedforward CA3-CA1 correlation noted in published animal studies.”
findingIn non-epileptic patients the CA1 region lay at an average depth of 36.06 mm from the outer table of the skull on the left and 34.84 mm on the right. — “The hippocampal morphometric study revealed that across normal patients, the CA1 region was encountered at an average depth of 36.06 ± 2.88 mm from the outer table of the skull on the left hemisphere, and 34.84 ± 2.78 mm on the right hemisphere.”
findingOnly the 2-5 msec feed-forward correlations (20.3%) are definitive CA3-to-CA1 synaptic connectivity, but positive correlations dominated (77.8%) and inverse correlations were rare, which the authors read as evidence that the cells were correctly assigned to CA3 and CA1. — “While only the 2- to 5-msec feed-forward correlations (20.3%) can be definitively identified as CA3-to- CA1 synaptic connectivity, the predominance of positive correlations (77.8%) and infrequent occurrence of inverse correlations suggest that the synchronous and multisynaptic correlate cell pairs are correctly identified with respect to CA3 and CA1 localization”
findingAnterior hippocampal neurons fired mainly at the sample response, whereas posterior hippocampal neurons fired at elevated rates in the interval between the sample and the match responses. — “These examples demonstrate that putative anterior hippocampal neurons fire primarily in response to sample response, with some firing to the match response. Conversely, putative posterior hippocampal neurons appear to fire at elevated rates between the sample response and match response peaks.”
findingAbout five times as many recorded hippocampal neurons fired below 5.0 Hz as fired above 5.0 Hz. — “Table 3 shows that approximately 5 times as many neurons exhibited firing rates below 5.0 Hz than above 5.0 Hz”
