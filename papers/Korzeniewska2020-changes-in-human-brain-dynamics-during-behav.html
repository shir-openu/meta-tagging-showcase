phenomenonbehavioural priming and repetition suppression— “Behavioral responses to a perceptual stimulus are typically faster with repeated exposure to the stimulus (behavioral priming).”
claim-typemechanism— “The latter results indicate that repetition suppression and behavioral priming are associated with strengthening of top-down network influences on perceptual processing, consistent with predictive coding models of repetition suppression, and they support a central role for changes in large-scale cortical dynamics in achieving more efficient…
method — evidence— “Here, we used the unparalleled precision of electrocorticography (ECoG) to analyze the timing and magnitude of task-related changes in neural activation and propagation while patients named novel vs repeated visual objects.”
transferfrom econometrics / time-series causality analysis: The Granger-causality framework for time series is taken over, as event related causality built on multivariate autoregressive models, to estimate the direction and timing of neural propagation between cortical recording sites.— “we evaluated changes in the propagation of high gamma activity using a recently developed technique – event related causality (ERC), based on the concept of Granger causality, to measure the timing, magnitude and directionality of task-related changes in neural propagation across cortical networks activated during visual object naming”
transferfrom Bayesian statistics: The predictive coding account the paper adopts treats the cortex itself as a hierarchical generative Bayesian statistical model, with top-down predictions and bottom-up prediction error.— “In this model, the cortex is considered a form of hierarchical generative Bayesian statistical model.”
mechanismWith repetition the cortex's generative model predicts the stimulus better, so top-down predictive input grows stronger and the prediction-error cells it suppresses fire less, which is what is measured as repetition suppression— “As the model improves its predictions with stimulus repetition and experience, the top-down input grows stronger and activity in cells that encode prediction error decreases, yielding repetition suppression.”
mechanismStrengthened top-down propagation from left prefrontal cortex drives down activation in ventral occipito-temporal cortex and elsewhere, producing repetition suppression— “These findings are suggestive of a role for repetition-related top-down propagation from LPFC in driving effects on repetition suppression that are observed in VOTC and elsewhere.”
mechanismAt sensorimotor and superior-temporal sites the apparent temporal shift is not a change in perception but a consequence of the shorter speaking latency: the activity is locked to the response, so aligning to response onset abolishes the shift— “This shift, however, was no longer apparent when cortical signals were aligned to response onset, suggesting that activation at these sites was linked to response processing and that the temporal shift was due to shorter latencies to respond to repeated stimuli.”
builds-on Ghuman et al. (2008) MEG study of LPFC-temporal phase locking during priming— “Our finding of early increased top-down propagation with repetition complements a previous study by Ghuman et al. (2008) using MEG, which reported enhanced phase-locking between LPFC and temporal cortex in low-beta frequencies (12–14 Hz) for repeated objects during object identification, beginning ~200 ms after stimulus onset.”
contradicts the “facilitation” model of repetition suppression (James et al., 2000; Henson, 2003)— “Importantly, we did not observe a consistent temporal shift in the temporal envelope of VOTC activation to earlier latencies, as predicted by the “facilitation” model.”

senseTHE SAME PARADOX Gotts2012 REVIEWS, AND THIS IS THE EXPERIMENT IT ASKED FOR. Gotts said the four candidate models were `in need of testing`; this paper says why they had not been tested - `noninvasive techniques for human studies have had INSUFFICIENT SPATIAL-TEMPORAL PRECISION for testing their predictions` - and then supplies the precision with electrocorticography. A review naming a gap and a study filling it, two records in one corpus— “Several neurophysiological models have been proposed to resolve this paradox, but noninvasive techniques for human studies have had insufficient spatial-temporal precision for testing their predictions. Here, we used the unparalleled precision of electrocorticography”
senseTHREE EFFECTS, NOT TWO. Beside suppression the study finds repetition ENHANCEMENT at some sites and a TEMPORAL SHIFT to earlier responses at others - `repetition suppression (RS) at some sites, and repetition enhancement (RE) in others, the latter often followed by repetition suppression (~600 ms)`. The paradox as usually stated has two terms; measured at this resolution the cortex does three different things, sometimes in sequence at one site— “we observed a more complex pattern of changes, reflecting repetition suppression (RS) at some sites, and repetition enhancement (RE) in others, the latter often followed by repetition suppression”
senseAND THE NOVEL EFFECT DOES NOT SURVIVE THE GROUP ANALYSIS, IN THE PAPER'S OWN WORDS: `Group analyses aggregating recording sites with statistically significant repetition enhancement (RE) DID NOT REACH STATISTICAL SIGNIFICANCE for VOTC, LPFC, or OAS.` So RE is a per-site observation, reported honestly as one, with the closing verdict that further investigation `is warranted` rather than that the effect is established. The result the abstract calls `interesting` is the one the results section declines to claim at group level— “Group analyses aggregating recording sites with statistically significant repetition enhancement (RE) did not reach statistical significance for VOTC, LPFC, or OAS”
senseWHAT THE ELECTRODES COULD SEE IS A LIMIT ON WHAT WAS FOUND, and the paper says so: `the numbers of sites revealing repetition suppression, repetition enhancement, or temporal shift were LIMITED BY THE ELECTRODE LOCATIONS UNIQUE TO EACH PATIENT`. Coverage is clinical, not experimental - electrodes go where the epilepsy surgery needs them - so absence of an effect at a site is not evidence of its absence in that region. A sampling constraint that no amount of statistics repairs— “Therefore, the numbers of sites revealing repetition suppression, repetition enhancement, or temporal shift were limited by the electrode locations unique to each patient”
controlobject names matched for word frequency, and the lists of repeated versus novel objects counterbalanced across patients — held — rules out that a difference between novel and repeated objects is a difference in word frequency, or a property of which particular objects happened to land in which list— “Object names were matched for word frequency, and lists of repeated versus non-repeated (novel) objects were counterbalanced across patients.”
differs inbehavioral priming —contrasted-with→ repetition suppression — THE DIRECTION EACH MOVES ON THE SAME REPETITION - responses get FASTER while neural activity gets WEAKER. The paper calls it a paradox in the sentence that states it, and the whole study exists to resolve it— “Many studies of the neural bases for behavioral priming have encountered an interesting paradox: in spite of faster behavioral responses, repeated stimuli usually elicit weaker neural responses (repetition suppression)”— “in spite of faster behavioral responses”
