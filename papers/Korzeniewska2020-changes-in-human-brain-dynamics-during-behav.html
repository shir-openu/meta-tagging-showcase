phenomenonbehavioural priming and repetition suppression
claim-typemechanism
transferfrom econometrics / time-series causality analysis: The Granger-causality framework for time series is taken over, as event related causality built on multivariate autoregressive models, to estimate the direction and timing of neural propagation between cortical recording sites. — “we evaluated changes in the propagation of high gamma activity using a recently developed technique – event related causality (ERC), based on the concept of Granger causality, to measure the timing, magnitude and directionality of task-related changes in neural propagation across cortical networks activated during visual object naming”
transferfrom Bayesian statistics: The predictive coding account the paper adopts treats the cortex itself as a hierarchical generative Bayesian statistical model, with top-down predictions and bottom-up prediction error. — “In this model, the cortex is considered a form of hierarchical generative Bayesian statistical model.”
mechanismWith repetition the cortex's generative model predicts the stimulus better, so top-down predictive input grows stronger and the prediction-error cells it suppresses fire less, which is what is measured as repetition suppression — “As the model improves its predictions with stimulus repetition and experience, the top-down input grows stronger and activity in cells that encode prediction error decreases, yielding repetition suppression.”
mechanismStrengthened top-down propagation from left prefrontal cortex drives down activation in ventral occipito-temporal cortex and elsewhere, producing repetition suppression — “These findings are suggestive of a role for repetition-related top-down propagation from LPFC in driving effects on repetition suppression that are observed in VOTC and elsewhere.”
mechanismAt sensorimotor and superior-temporal sites the apparent temporal shift is not a change in perception but a consequence of the shorter speaking latency: the activity is locked to the response, so aligning to response onset abolishes the shift — “This shift, however, was no longer apparent when cortical signals were aligned to response onset, suggesting that activation at these sites was linked to response processing and that the temporal shift was due to shorter latencies to respond to repeated stimuli.”
relation — “Our finding of early increased top-down propagation with repetition complements a previous study by Ghuman et al. (2008) using MEG, which reported enhanced phase-locking between LPFC and temporal cortex in low-beta frequencies (12–14 Hz) for repeated objects during object identification, beginning ~200 ms after stimulus onset.”
relation — “Importantly, we did not observe a consistent temporal shift in the temporal envelope of VOTC activation to earlier latencies, as predicted by the “facilitation” model.”

replicationuntested
limitationRepetition enhancement was significant only at individual sites; group analyses failed to reach significance in any region of interest, possibly through timing variability and uneven sampling. — “Although we observed statistically significant RE at 30 recording sites across 10 patients, our group analyses did not reach statistical significance for any of the regions of interest, potentially due to variations in the timing and magnitude of RE across patients, as well as inter-individual variations in spatial sampling of our regions of interest.”
limitationOnly very short (few-second) repetition delays were examined, while the models at stake concern much longer delays. — “It will be important to examine repetition effects on neural dynamics over much longer repetition delays than examined here (a few seconds), as both behavioral priming and repetition suppression have been observed over delays of hours and days, and these longer delays are of paramount importance to models positing long-lasting changes to cortical representations (e.g. perceptual sharpening and predictive coding models).”
limitationHigh gamma responses cannot adjudicate models that posit changes in single-neuron tuning, such as the sharpening model. — “Although these responses cannot be used to test models that posit changes in the tuning preferences of individual neurons (e.g. sharpening), they can be used to measure neural dynamics at time scales relevant to the evaluation of other models.”
limitationElectrode placement was clinically, not experimentally, determined, constraining which sites could show each repetition effect. — “It is important to note that implantation of electrodes was performed solely according to clinical needs.”

findingBehavioral priming was accompanied by suppressed high gamma activity in ventral occipito-temporal cortex, left prefrontal cortex and other activated sites, plus a shift of activity to earlier time points in sensorimotor regions that mediate speaking. — “In the current study, we have shown that behavioral priming following short-term repetitions in humans is accompanied by suppression of neural activity in VOTC, LPFC, and other activated sites, as well as temporal shifts of activity to earlier time points in sensorimotor regions mediating verbal responses.”
findingRepetition also increased activation at 30 sites in lateral frontal, parietal and temporal cortex, but never in ventral occipito-temporal cortex, so repetition enhancement and suppression occupy different territory. — “Repetition enhancement (RE) was observed in 30 sites located in lateral frontal, parietal, and temporal cortices, but not in VOTC.”
findingRepeated stimuli produced a statistically significant increase in high gamma propagation from left prefrontal cortex into ventral occipito-temporal cortex beginning about 100 ms after stimulus onset. — “This analysis revealed a statistically significant ( p < 0.05, FDR-corrected to q <.05) increase in propagation from LPFC to VOTC for repeated stimuli relative to novel stimuli, which started around 100 ms after stimulus onset”
findingThe repetition-related rise in top-down propagation became significant before repetition suppression appeared in VOTC, putting the top-down change first in the causal order. — “Note that the repetition-related increase in top-down propagation from LPFC to VOTC became significant numerically earlier than repetition suppression in VOTC did.”
findingWithin individual patients the dominant direction of flow reversed with repetition: novel stimuli drove propagation bottom-up from VOTC to LPFC, whereas repeated stimuli drove it top-down from LPFC to VOTC. — “In ERC analyses within individual patients we observed that for trials with novel stimuli, the most prominent neural propagation (ERC flow) was from VOTC to LPFC, with far less propagation in the opposite direction.”
findingThe facilitation model's central prediction failed in ventral occipito-temporal cortex: repeated trials showed no consistent shift of the activation envelope to earlier latencies, only a reduced peak and an earlier return to baseline. — “Importantly, we did not observe a consistent temporal shift in the temporal envelope of VOTC activation to earlier latencies, as predicted by the “facilitation” model.”
findingRepetition suppression emerged in ventral occipito-temporal cortex at about 140 ms but only reached significance in left prefrontal cortex at about 280 ms. — “Interestingly, in group analyses of repetition suppression (RS), it became statistically significant in LPFC later (~ 280 ms, Fig. 3 center) than in VOTC (~ 140 ms, Fig. 3 left), with a slight time-shift in the peak activation in LPFC to earlier time points in the repeat condition”
findingRepetition enhancement, although present at individual electrodes, did not survive group-level statistics in any region of interest. — “Group analyses aggregating recording sites with statistically significant repetition enhancement (RE) did not reach statistical significance for VOTC, LPFC, or OAS.”

definitionrepetition suppression — “Repetition suppression, a decrease in cortical activation upon repeated stimulus exposure, has been widely observed under a variety of experimental conditions”

definitionevent related causality (ERC) — “Event-related causality (ERC, implemented in custom analysis interface (ANIN) software ( Franaszczuk and Jouny, 2004 ), finds statistically significant event-related effective connectivity between ECoG recording sites using multivariate autoregressive (MVAR) models of ECoG activity in a subset of recorded signals.”
definitionpredictive coding model (of repetition suppression) — “Given that perception relies on the integration of sensory information and prior expectations, the “predictive coding” model claims that repetition leads to optimized neural representations, with top-down activity serving as model-based predictions about the current stimulus and bottom-up activity representing the prediction error related to various stimuli and their constituent elements.”
definitionfacilitation model — “The “facilitation” model ( James et al. , 2000 ; Henson, 2003 ) proposes that neural activity is advanced in time with repetition and terminates more rapidly.”
