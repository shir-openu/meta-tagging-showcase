The six precordial leads were placed at the left and right parasternal positions in the second (V1 II ICS, or V1; V2 II ICS, or V2), third (V1 III ICS, or V3; V2 III ICS, or V4), and fourth intercostal spaces (V1 IV ICS, or V5; V2 IV ICS, or V6).

Independent validation of DNNsterm·mth Two distinct tests confirm the validity with which the DNNterm·mth models developed in this work classify newly encountered ECGsterm·mth for BrSterm·dom. Discussion The present study demonstrates a neural network model that learns to recognize the signature of Brugada Syndrometerm·dom hidden in a conventional ECGterm·mth in the absence of any proarrhythmic drug challenge. These results establish that an appropriately trained DNNterm·mth model responds to subtle ECGterm·mth abnormalities that are undetectable to the human eye. The algorithm correctly classifies a majority of ECGsterm·mth by predicting the response of a patient to an ajmalineterm·key challenge without the need for other clinical information. Despite the relatively limited size of this database, the neural network assesses apparently normal ECGsterm·mth with an AUCterm·key of 0.912 ± 0.017 (accuracy of 84.0%) and 0.934 ± 0.027 (accuracy of 88.4%) in the training and validation datasets respectively.

To date, when evaluating a patient at an arrhythmia clinic, the clinician must decide whether to administer a sodium channel blockerterm·mth in an effort to unmask the BrSterm·dom phenotype ( 5 , 13 , 14 , 39 , 40 ). This drug challenge exposes the patient to the possibility of a life-threatening arrhythmia; but, a decision not to challenge risks overlooking a patient with hidden BrSterm·dom. At present, owing to the possibility of life-threatening side effects, only patients with a family history of SCDterm·dom or a suspicious ECGterm·mth undergo a drug challenge.

Thus, many people who show no visible sign of BrSterm·dom may well remain undiagnosed ( 9 ). The identification of BrSterm·dom is particularly limited in countries, including the United States of America and Japan, where ajmalineterm·key is generally unavailable, and the SCBterm·mth challenge is limited to less effective agents such as procainamide and flecainide. Moreover, several conditions expressly contraindicate this diagnostic approach. For example, a fatal ventricular arrhythmia calls for the assessment of family members for BrSterm·dom; but, clinicians may hesitate to administer ajmalineterm·key to children. This limitation may leave many young carriers of BrSterm·dom undiagnosed. These drugs are also contraindicated in cases of pregnancy, liver diseases, and advanced cardiac conduction disturbances.

Sodium channel blockersterm·mth have negative inotropic properties that preclude use for patients suffering from cardiomyopathies, particularly those associated with mutations in the SCN5A gene and other conditions overlapping with BrSterm·dom, such as hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) and arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy (ARVC) ( 41–44 ). Dynamic effects of these drugs vary a great deal from individual to individual, and this clouds the significance of any particular test result. The direct hepatotoxic effect of these agents further excludes their diagnostic applicability as a screening tool.

Thus, significant considerations limit the diagnostic application of SCBterm·mth drugs compared with an instrumental measure of heart function, such as an electrocardiogramterm·key, which does not rely upon the action of an agonist. While work still remains to validate machine-learning classification algorithms for patients with accompanying conditions not considered in the present study, the accuracy demonstrated thus far, approaching that normally achieved in SCBterm·mth tests, offers a potential of great benefit as a safe first step in screening a wider population.

To this purpose, several studies have reported the usefulness of some ECGterm·mth parameters in the discrimination of BrSterm·dom patterns ( 45–49 ). However, intra- and inter-observer variability in specific measurements limit the power of univariate pattern-recognition methods. More generally, the ability to identify ECGterm·mth predictors of BrSterm·dom depends on a clinician’s level of clinical experience. Very recently, a convolutional neural network approach showed success in recognizing the characteristic ST elevation of a type 1 BrS patternterm·dom ( 33 ). However, only a small fraction of BrSterm·dom( + ) individuals spontaneously present this plainly visible ECGterm·mth feature.

Indeed, in the present cohort, roughly 90% of patients showed a near-normal or merely suspicious ECGterm·mth waveform, challenging to evaluate even by experienced cardiologists. Thus, the development of this advanced DNN-based ECGterm·mth interpretation algorithm promises to aid in the diagnosis of BrSterm·dom by providing a discerning and objective tool to assist the clinician prior to the possible use of potentially harmful SCBterm·mth challenges. This tool will add considerable insight when clinicians, in keeping with current guidelines, combine the results of an ECGterm·mth with a full scope of other clinical data to establish a final diagnosis of BrSterm·dom ( 5 ). The Supplementary material offers additional details concerning classification success upon manual versus DNNterm·mth analysis with reference to patient demographics and clinical characteristics.

The sensitivity and specificity of our DNNterm·mth model compare favorably with other diagnostic tests used in cardiology, such as exercise electrocardiography to diagnose coronary artery disease ( 50 ), and exceed other medical screening tests, such as the BNP immunoassay for heart failure (AUCterm·key 0.60–0.70) ( 51 ), the Papanicolaou smear for cervical cancer (AUCterm·key 0.70) ( 52 ), and the CHA2DS2-VASc Score for stroke risk (AUCterm·key 0.57–0.72) ( 53 ). Moreover, the relatively high negative predictive valueterm·mth (NPVterm·mth) of the DNNterm·mth classification will lend reassurance in decisions to forego the invasive and risky step of an ajmalineterm·key challenge. The current findings will translate to a clinical application that informs clinicians who are considering ordering an SCBterm·mth challenge for suspected Brugada Syndrometerm·dom.

Of 1,455 patients in the present cohort, 7% presented an ECGterm·mth with a spontaneous type 1 patternterm·key. Using standard clinical protocols, a conventional BrSterm·dom screening program would have found only these patients. Among asymptomatic individuals in this study, we diagnosed more than 49% as having BrSterm·dom. Most of these patients had no idea that they carried this condition. They were referred for screening only because of the sudden and unexplained death of a relative, reinforcing the idea that the actual prevalence of BrSterm·dom is likely underestimated.

Remarkably, the DNNterm·mth model correctly classifies every patient who presented a spontaneous type 1 ECGterm·mth. This marker signals a heightened danger of cardiac arrest ( 5 , 54 , 55 ). But, nearly half of BrSterm·dom patients who survive cardiac arrest do not show a spontaneous type 1 patternterm·key, underlining the critical need to clinically identify patients that harbor a hidden disease signature and face a substantial probability of SCDterm·dom ( 12 ). In young adults, the first clinical manifestation of BrSterm·dom is often atrial fibrillation ( 56 ). This suggests that BrSterm·dom may represent a complex and diffuse phenotype, sharing genetic mutations with various supraventricular arrhythmias ( 43 , 57 , 58 ), as well as with other cardiomyopathies, such as HCM ( 44 ), ARVC ( 10 ) and early repolarization syndrome ( 59 ).

This broader relevance points to an additional reach of noninvasive BrSterm·dom screening as a means to reduce the risk of life-threatening arrhythmias and sudden death. This is particularly relevant for patients who present a nondiagnostic ECGterm·mth pattern that may mask a clinically aggressive phenotype requiring further cardiac investigation. The classification success of the DNNterm·mth suggests that machine learning will enable clinicians to identify these patients even from an apparently normal ECGterm·mth.

Furthermore, as plainly evident in Fig. 4 , a completely independent cross-platform dataset validates the DNNterm·mth with virtually the same accuracy (AUCterm·key 0.93 vs 0.9) as LOOCVterm·mth applied to the training dataset. This not only proves again that the algorithm generalizes well to classify previously unseen data, but also to successfully classify ECGterm·mth data acquired on different machines. A larger ECGterm·mth dataset and multiparametric diagnostic assessment, performed by combining ECGterm·mth machine learning and genetic information ( 60 ), could provide better accuracy, and advance substantially in diminishing the devastating impact of a family of lethal diseases by screening the general population. Additionally, next-generation electrocardiographs and new implantable or wearable devices could support operating systems that incorporate artificial intelligence built upon this DNNterm·mth model for output processing ( 61 ).

A DNNterm·mth algorithm for assisting the diagnosis of BrSterm·dom offers several advantages compared with current practices: (i) reduction of test-related adverse events, such as life-threatening arrhythmias and ajmaline-induced liver injury, (ii) reduction of the cost compared with an SCBterm·mth test, which must be performed in a hospital setting, and (iii) identification of family members at risk of BrSterm·dom in centers with limited access to drug testing or no access to ajmalineterm·key.

Interpreting DNNterm·mth feature importance Understanding where a DNNterm·mth places the greatest importance in the input data is crucial for interpreting the decisions a model makes (see Fig. 5 ). By identifying which ECGterm·mth features the DNNterm·mth weights most heavily in its classification model, we can understand which aspects of the ECGterm·mth signal play the greatest role in identifying the BrSterm·dom phenotype. This information can help guide the selection of new data for training, improve the interpretability of the model, and explain why the model might fail to correctly identify the BrSterm·dom phenotype in some cases.

We observe that the DNNterm·mth’s interpretation largely aligns with the cardiologists’ approach, as it considers not only ST elevation, but also other aspects of the ECGterm·mth, including QRS interval, QT interval, J point depression, and the presence of an incomplete right bundle branch block. This convergence is significant, as it illustrates how the DNNterm·mth learns to recognize important ECGterm·mth features in a completely unbiased data-driven approach, supervised solely on a patient’s response to the ajmalineterm·key challenge.

DNNterm·mth multivariate classification evaluates ECGterm·mth data by means of thousands of nonlinear decision-making pathways, allowing it to identify patterns and features that escape notice in conventional approaches. This enables the DNNterm·mth model to recognize subtle covariance of diagnostic significance. We believe that feature importance patterns established by our DNNterm·mth approach could help refine and expand the understanding of Brugada Syndrometerm·dom, potentially leading to the development of more robust diagnostic criteria.

The substantial data preprocessing method described here reduces a raw, noisy ECGterm·mth trace to yield a representative heartbeatterm·key as a single, averaged complete cardiac cycle (750 ms in duration) centered near the R peak. The high signal-to-noise representative heartbeatterm·key yields a persistent, reproducible waveform for classification by the DNNterm·mth. Our segmentation strategy overcomes uncorrelated beat-to-beat variance in the raw traces. This transformation aids the DNNterm·mth in recognizing persistent ECGterm·mth morphology that consistently encodes for BrSterm·dom while ignoring transient abnormalities.

Conclusion The present work demonstrates an ECGterm·mth screening algorithm for BrSterm·dom that competes with the sodium-channel-blocker challenge while presenting the clear advantage of no life-threatening side effects. This mode of computer-assisted analysis can be applied to digital ECGterm·mth traces from all current electrocardiographs. The broad application of this methodology will enable clinicians to test widely for this deadly disease. We foresee that this machine learning approach to ECGterm·mth analysis will extend beyond the diagnosis of BrSterm·dom to broader risk stratification and mitigation of SCDterm·dom.
