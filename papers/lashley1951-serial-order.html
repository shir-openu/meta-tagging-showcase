Not in the idea - so it is not simply read off the meaning either. **Both of the obvious places to put it are refused in one sentence**, which leaves ordering as a separate mechanism and is the move that made this paper foundational for everything that came after it— “From such considerations it seems to follow that syntax is not inherent in the words employed or in the idea to be expressed.”
sense**WHAT THIS RECORD DOES AND DOES NOT SAY ABOUT THE REST OF THE CORPUS.** Lashley's target is the associative chain - `Watson (26) sought to identify thought with inaudible movements of the vocal organs, LINKED TOGETHER IN ASSOCIATIVE CHAINS` - and this corpus is largely built on measuring association between elements: meyer1971's free-association norms, collins1975's spreading activation, hutchison2003's association strength. **They are not the same claim.** Lashley is about the ORDER of action and speech; spreading activation is about retrieval, and retrieval has no ordering problem to solve. The honest statement is narrower and still worth holding: the corpus contains, from 1951, a sustained argument that association cannot by itself produce order - and from 1971 onward, a literature that measures association and rarely asks what orders anything— “Watson (26) sought to identify thought with inaudible movements of the vocal organs, linked together in associative chains.”

conceptserial order— “This is the essential problem of serial order; the existence of generalized schemata of action which determine the sequence of specific acts, acts which in themselves or in their associations seem to have no temporal valence.”
conceptschemata of action — Lashley's answer: `GENERALIZED schemata of action which determine the sequence of specific acts`. **Generalized is the load-bearing word** - a schema is not a stored sequence but a form that can order many different sequences, which is why the same plan produces the movement with either hand and the sentence with different words— “This is the essential problem of serial order; the existence of generalized schemata of action which determine the sequence of specific acts, acts which in themselves or in their associations seem to have no temporal valence.”
conceptassociative chain — each element evoking the next, and nothing above them. **The position being refuted**, attributed here to Watson: thought as `inaudible movements of the vocal organs, linked together in associative chains`. Lashley's objection is not that chains are wrong about association - it is that association carries no information about ORDER— “Watson (26) sought to identify thought with inaudible movements of the vocal organs, linked together in associative chains.”
conceptsyntax — the ordering structure of an utterance, and the claim about it is unusually strong: `syntax is NOT INHERENT in the words employed OR in the idea to be expressed.` **Not in the words and not in the meaning** - so it is a third thing, which is precisely the conclusion an associative account cannot reach— “From such considerations it seems to follow that syntax is not inherent in the words employed or in the idea to be expressed.”
