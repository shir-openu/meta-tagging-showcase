https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.38506.015 Optogeneticterm·mth suppression of neuronal activity and synaptic outputs is an essential approach for dissecting the roles of specific neuronsterm in brain functions. Light-gated chlorideterm channelsterm, particularly GtACR1 and GtACR2term, are increasingly used due to their large photocurrentsterm and high sensitivity to light ( Forli et al., 2018 ; Mardinly et al., 2018 ; Mauss et al., 2017 ; Mohamed et al., 2017 ; Mohammad et al., 2017 ). To use these tools to their full potentials, it is necessary that we understand their function and, importantly, their limitations. Our experiments reveal that wide-field activation of these channelsterm in cortical neuronsterm suppresses action potentials at the somaterm but also triggers neurotransmitterterm release at the presynapticterm terminals, thereby voiding inhibitionterm of neuronal activity. As demonstrated, the excitatoryterm action of chlorideterm channelsterm in the axonterm and presynapticterm terminals is due to the high intracellular chlorideterm concentrations that create a depolarizing chlorideterm electrochemical gradientterm. The depolarizing effect of presynapticterm chlorideterm channelsterm, GABA A or glycine receptors, has been documented at a few synapses in the hippocampus, cerebellum, and brainstem through application of agonists or antagonists ( Jang et al., 2006 ; Pugh and Jahr, 2011 ; Ruiz et al., 2010 ; Stell et al., 2007 ; Turecek and Trussell, 2001 ; Zorrilla de San Martin et al., 2017 ). Our results in cortical excitatoryterm and inhibitory neuronsterm expand previous findings and indicate that a presynapticterm depolarizing chlorideterm electrochemical gradientterm is likely a general property across brain regions and neuronal types. More importantly, our experiments directly demonstrate that activation of presynapticterm chlorideterm channelsterm is in fact excitatoryterm, as it can elicit action potentials with short latency, which was difficult to explicitly demonstrate by agonist application ( Pugh and Jahr, 2011 ). Thus, light-gated chlorideterm channelsterm can be targeted to different brain regions, cell types, and subcellular compartmentsterm to study chlorideterm electrochemical gradientsterm with unprecedented temporal precision.

Although the excitatoryterm effect of light-gated chlorideterm channelsterm is undesired for neuronal silencing, a potential utilization of their dual actions at the presynapticterm terminals and somaterm is to activate specific projections of neuronsterm while minimizing the effect of antidromic spikes. For example, long-range projection neuronsterm often target multiple brain areas, and sometimes it is desired to selectively excite the axonal terminals projecting to one particular area. If ChR2 is used, local activation of ChR2-expressing axonal terminals may generate antidromic spikes, which will affect other projections. However, if GtACR2term is used, one can simultaneously activate GtACR2term in the somaterm and axonal terminals. Axonal depolarization will result in neurotransmitterterm release, but the antidromic spikes will be reduced or suppressed by the hyperpolarization originating from the somatodendritic domain, which reduces the likelihood of activating other projections.

To create a better inhibitory optogeneticterm·mth tool, we tested a number of somatodendritic targeting motifsterm to confine GtACR2term in the somaterm and dendrites. We generated a hybrid motif, Kv2.1C-linker-TlcnC, that was more effective than the widely used Kv2.1C ( Baker et al., 2016 ; Mardinly et al., 2018 ; Wu et al., 2013 ). The Kv2.1C motif was recently used in a bioRxiv preprint ( Mahn et al., 2017 ) to target GtACR2term to the somatodendritic domain. This GtACR2term variant achieved a greater reduction of neurotransmitterterm release from the contralateral callosal projections of medial prefrontal cortical neuronsterm than what we observed with our GtACR2-EYFP-Kv2.1C and GtACR2-EYFP-Kv2.1C-linker-TlcnC in visual cortical neuronsterm. This quantitative difference is likely due to different expression levels, light stimulation strengths, or neuronal types (see discussion below), which reiterates the importance of precisely characterizing the effect of light-gated chlorideterm channelsterm, specific to each experimental design, when using them to manipulate neuronal activity.

While GtACR2-EYFP-Kv2.1C-linker-TlcnC can still traffic to the axonterm to cause neurotransmitterterm release, it is thus far the most improved light-gated chlorideterm channelterm for optogeneticterm·mth inhibitionterm. Since it can generate much larger photocurrentsterm in the somatodendritic domain than what is necessary to suppress action potentials, one approach to use this tool is to reduce the overall GtACR2term expression level to further decrease its presence in the axonterm while still generating sufficient inhibitory photocurrentsterm at the somaterm. Supporting this idea, post hoc analysis of our experiments indicates that, for a given experiment, it is possible to identify a light stimulation strength that is low enough not to cause neurotransmitterterm release but still produce large inhibitory photocurrentsterm in GtACR2term + neuronsterm ( Figure 5—figure supplement 1 ). However, it is difficult to apply this photoactivation approach to freely moving animals or deep brain areas. Therefore, it is imperative that we further engineer light-gated chlorideterm channelsterm to eliminate their excitatoryterm action in the axonal terminals. Future strategies include generating more effective somatodendritic targeting motifsterm, creating outwardly rectifying channelsterm, and the combination of both strategies. The GtACR2-EYFP-Kv2.1C-linker-TlcnC reported here enhances the available toolkit for optogeneticterm·mth inhibitionterm and will serve as the foundation for future improvement.
