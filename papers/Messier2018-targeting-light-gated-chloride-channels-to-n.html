Targeted manipulation of neural activity is a powerful approach in neuroscience that has provided fundamental insights into the roles of specific neurons in nervous system functions. Genetically encoded actuators such as light-gated ion channels or pumps enable control of neural activity with unprecedented spatiotemporal specificity and are transforming neuroscience research ( Boyden et al., 2005 ; Han and Boyden, 2007 ; Li et al., 2005 ; Nagel et al., 2003 ; Zhang et al., 2007 ). Actuators that enable neuronal activation are frequently used, but inhibitory optogeneticterm·mth tools are increasingly crucial because reversible and temporally precise suppression of neuronal activity is key to revealing the causal roles of specific neurons in network dynamics and behavior. The widely used light-driven inward chlorideterm pumps and outward proton pumps, such as Natronomonas pharaonis halorhodopsin (NpHR) and Halorubrum sodomense archaerhodopsin (Arch), can hyperpolarize membrane potentials, independent of the electrochemical gradients, to inhibit action potentials with millisecond precision ( Chow et al., 2010 ; Chuong et al., 2014 ; Han and Boyden, 2007 ; Han et al., 2011 ; Zhang et al., 2007 ). However, their efficacies are limited because only one ion is transported per absorbed photon, and their activation does not decrease membrane resistance. Light-gated chlorideterm channels, such as Guillardia theta anion channelrhodopsin 1 and 2 (GtACR1 and GtACR2term), iC++, and iChloC, overcome these limitations ( Berndt et al., 2014 ; Govorunova et al., 2015 , 2017b ; Wietek et al., 2014 ). They are highly sensitive to light, allow multiple ions to cross the membrane per photocycle, and reduce membrane resistance, thereby potently inhibiting action potentials. Thus, light-gated chlorideterm channels are emerging as promising optogeneticterm·mth tools for suppressing neuronal activity ( Govorunova et al., 2017a ).

Determining the precise effects of light-gated ion channels or pumps under defined conditions is a prerequisite to use them for interrogating the functions of specific neurons and circuits. This is due to the possibility that these channels or pumps not only modulate membrane potentials but also may affect other processes such as ion homeostasis or neurotransmitterterm release. For example, activation of a light-driven inward chlorideterm pumpterm, eNpHR3.0, can transiently change the reversal potentialterm of GABA A receptors and alter the inhibitory synaptic inputs ( Raimondo et al., 2012 ). Prolonged activation of a light-driven outward proton pumpterm, eArch3.0, can increase presynapticterm calcium concentrations and spontaneous neurotransmitterterm release ( Mahn et al., 2016 ). Despite these potential confounds, these inhibitory optogeneticterm·mth molecules become increasingly important tools for targeted silencing of neuronal populations, as long as these confounds are understood and controlled ( Allen et al., 2015 ). Therefore, it is also crucial to thoroughly characterize light-gated chlorideterm channels, as their effect depends on the difference between the membrane potentialterm and the reversal potentialterm for chlorideterm, both of which can vary in different neuronal types and subcellular compartments ( Marty and Llano, 2005 ; Trigo et al., 2008 ).

To this end, we investigated the effects of activating GtACR2term in mouse cortical excitatoryterm and inhibitory neurons. Much to our surprise, wide-field light activation of GtACR2term not only inhibited the somaterm, but also caused neurotransmitterterm release onto neighboring neurons. A similar phenomenon was observed with GtACR1, iC++, and iChloC. We further showed that GtACR2term activation in the axonterm and presynapticterm terminals directly depolarized the membrane to induce neurotransmitterterm release due to high chlorideterm concentrations in these compartments. These data explain the recent observations that photostimulation of neurons expressing GtACR1 or GtACR2term can paradoxically release neurotransmitters or generate action potentials ( Mahn et al., 2016 ; Malyshev et al., 2017 ). To reduce the excitatoryterm effect of GtACR2term, we screened a panel of somatodendritic targeting motifs to reduce the trafficking of GtACR2term to the axonterm and presynapticterm terminals. We created a hybrid motif (Kv2.1C-linker-TlcnC) that is most effective in concentrating GtACR2term in the somatodendritic domain. Activation of somatodendritically targeted GtACR2term resulted in larger photocurrents at the somaterm and less neurotransmitterterm release than wild type GtACR2term. Thus, restricting the localization of light-gated chlorideterm channels to the somatodendritic domain improves the inhibitory efficacy of these optogeneticterm·mth tools. Our results also suggest that light-gated chlorideterm channels can be a useful tool for studying the physiological functions of chlorideterm electrochemical gradients in specific brain regions, cell types, or subcellular domains that are otherwise difficult to access, such as small axons and presynapticterm terminals.

To determine if the phenomenon of GtACR2term-induced neurotransmitterterm release also occurs in GABAergic inhibitory neurons, we expressed GtACR2term in parvalbumin-expressing (Pv) neurons by injecting a Flpo recombinase-dependent adeno-associated virus (AAV) into the visual cortex of Pvalb-2A-Flpo mice ( Pvalb Flpo/+ ) ( Madisen et al., 2015 ) at postnatal day 1 ( Figure 1H ). Using acute brain slices from 3 to 6 week-old mice, we found that activation of GtACR2term in Pv neurons generated IPSCs in all recorded GtACR2term – layer 2/3 pyramidal neurons, and the IPSCs were abolished by Gabazine, a GABA A receptor antagonist, or TTX ( Figure 1I–K ). The onsets of the IPSCs followed the onset of the blue light by 2.47 ± 0.15 ms (mean ± s.e.m., n = 17), indicating that they were monosynaptic IPSCs caused by the GABA transmitter released from GtACR2term + Pv neurons.

We next determined how repetitive or prolonged activation of GtACR2term would affect neurotransmitterterm release. We activated GtACR2term in layer 2/3 pyramidal neurons or Pv neurons with a high-frequency train of light pulses and found that each light pulse produced reliable EPSCs or IPSCs, respectively ( Figure 1—figure supplement 2 ). Interestingly, continuous activation of GtACR2term in layer 2/3 pyramidal neurons or Pv neurons with a long pulse of light (e.g., 2 s) transiently generated large EPSCs or IPSCs, respectively, followed by various amounts of smaller synaptic responses ( Figure 1—figure supplement 3A,B,E,F,G ). Typically, more than 50% of the neurotransmitterterm release occurred within the first 100–200 ms of the light pulse ( Figure 1—figure supplement 3C,D,H,I ).

The finding of GtACR2term-induced neurotransmitterterm release was unexpected, because the Nernst equilibrium potential of chlorideterm becomes lower than the action potential threshold in rodent cortical neurons after the second postnatal week, and activation of chlorideterm channels should inhibit, not promote, neurotransmitterterm release onto neighboring cells ( Ben-Ari, 2002 ; Owens et al., 1996 ). We thus sought to identify the cause of this paradoxical neurotransmitterterm release and considered three possibilities. First, GtACR2term may conduct cations to depolarize neurons. Second, an increase in the intracellular chlorideterm or strong hyperpolarization induced by GtACR2term activation may lead to rebound spikes. Third, GtACR2term-mediated chlorideterm currents may be excitatoryterm.

First, it was reported that GtACR2term did not conduct physiological cations ( Govorunova et al., 2015 ), but some other light-gated chlorideterm channels retained certain cation conductance ( Berndt et al., 2014 ; Wietek et al., 2014 ). Thus, we sought to verify that in cortical neurons, GtACR2term has a similar reversal potentialterm as a known chlorideterm channelterm. To accomplish this, we determined the reversal potentialterm of GtACR2term-mediated photocurrents in comparison with that of IPSCs mediated by the endogenous GABA A receptors. We used in utero electroporation to express GtACR2term in layer 2/3 pyramidal neurons and a Cre recombinase-dependent AAV to express a red light-gated cation channelrhodopsin, ReaChR, in Pv neurons of Pvalb-2A-Cre mice ( Pvalb Cre/+ ) ( Madisen et al., 2010 ). We performed whole-cell voltage clamp recordings at the somaterm of a GtACR2term + layer 2/3 pyramidal neuronterm and a nearby GtACR2term – pyramidal neuronterm simultaneously ( Figure 2—figure supplement 1A ). In the GtACR2term + neuronterm, we sequentially recorded the IPSCs induced by activating ReaChR in Pv neurons via 617 nm light and the GtACR2term-mediated photocurrents activated by 455 nm light. Both IPSCs and photocurrents were recorded at different membrane potentials to determine their reversal potentials in the same neuronterm ( Figure 2—figure supplement 1B,C ). 617 nm light does not activate GtACR2term ( Govorunova et al., 2015 ), whereas 455 nm light partially activates ReaChR ( Lin et al., 2013 ). Thus, to avoid Pv neuronterm-mediated IPSCs contaminating GtACR2term-mediated photocurrents, we monitored the IPSCs in the GtACR2term – neuronterm at the membrane potentialterm of +10 mV to ensure that the intensity of the 455 nm light was not sufficient to activate Pv neurons and generate IPSCs ( Figure 2—figure supplement 1B ). We found that the reversal potentials of GtACR2term-mediated photocurrents and GABAergic IPSCs were similar for each neuronterm and well below the action potential threshold ( Figure 2—figure supplement 1D ), indicating that GtACR2term does not conduct cations to cause neurotransmitterterm release.

Second, we tested if GtACR2term-induced neurotransmitterterm release could be due to rebound depolarization. An increase in the intracellular chlorideterm caused by GtACR2term activation may trigger rapid efflux of chlorideterm after the blue light illumination terminates. However, this possibility is unlikely because when GtACR2term was activated by a long pulse of blue light, neurotransmitterterm release occurred before the light illumination ended (see examples in Figure 1F,G and Figure 1—figure supplement 3 ). Another possibility is that the strong hyperpolarization induced by GtACR2term activates hyperpolarization-activated I h currents, which may depolarize the membrane potentialterm above the action potential threshold. However, pharmacological inhibitionterm of I h currents slightly increased the amplitudes of GtACR2term-induced EPSCs ( Figure 2—figure supplement 2A,B ), most likely because inhibiting I h currents increases neuronal membrane resistances ( Robinson and Siegelbaum, 2003 ). Thus, GtACR2term-induced neurotransmitterterm release is not caused by rebound depolarization.

Third, although GtACR2term-mediated photocurrents are inhibitory at the somaterm, it is possible that the chlorideterm concentrations are higher in some other cellular compartments, such that the electrochemical gradientterm causes chlorideterm to exit the cell upon GtACR2term channelterm opening, resulting in depolarization of the membrane potentialterm. To test this hypothesis, we pharmacologically inhibited the activity of Na + -K + -2Cl – cotransporter 1 (NKCC1) with bumetanide (50 or 100 µM) to decease the intracellular chlorideterm concentrations, as NKCC1 is responsible for transporting chlorideterm into neurons ( Ben-Ari, 2017 ). When we activated GtACR2term in layer 2/3 pyramidal neurons, the resulting EPSCs in GtACR2term – pyramidal neurons were diminished by bath application of bumetanide ( Figure 2A,B ), indicating that GtACR2term-induced neurotransmitterterm release requires high concentrations of intracellular chlorideterm. An alternative interpretation of this result would be that bumetanide blocks GtACR2term itself. To test this possibility, we simultaneously recorded the photocurrents and EPSCs in GtACR2term + and GtACR2term – pyramidal neurons, respectively. While bumetanide diminished the EPSCs in GtACR2term – neurons, it had no effect on the photocurrents in GtACR2term + neurons ( Figure 2—figure supplement 3A,B ), thereby ruling out the possibility that bumetanide affects GtACR2term itself. Furthermore, when a cation channelterm, channelrhodopsin-2 (ChR2) ( Boyden et al., 2005 ; Li et al., 2005 ; Nagel et al., 2003 ), was expressed and activated in layer 2/3 pyramidal neurons, the resulting EPSCs in ChR2 – neurons were not affected by bumetanide ( Figure 2C,D ), indicating that reducing the intracellular chlorideterm concentration only affects chlorideterm-mediated, and not cation-mediated, EPSCs. Finally, bumetanide also diminished the IPSCs resulting from GtACR2term activation in Pv neurons ( Figure 2E,F ). Together, these results demonstrate that light activation of GtACR2term generates an excitatoryterm chlorideterm conductance in certain neuronal compartments to trigger neurotransmitterterm release.

To selectively express GtACR2term in adult neurons, we in utero electroporated a Flpo-dependent plasmid into layer 2/3 pyramidal neurons that will express GtACR2term only if Flpo is present ( Figure 2—figure supplement 2C,D ). A Flpo-expressing AAV was then injected into the electroporated mice at postnatal week 4 or 9 to turn on the GtACR2term expression. We obtained acute coronal brain slices 1–3 weeks after injecting Flpo-expressing AAV and found that light activation of GtACR2term, again, produced bumetanide-sensitive EPSCs in GtACR2term – neurons ( Figure 2—figure supplement 2E ). Thus, it is unlikely that GtACR2term expression during neuronal development alters the chlorideterm homeostasis to render GtACR2term excitatoryterm, as acute expression of GtACR2term in mature neurons had the same effect.

To determine if activation of other light-gated chlorideterm channels can trigger neurotransmitterterm release, we examined iC++ and iChloC, two engineered blue light-gated chlorideterm channels that were converted from cation channelrhodopsins ( Berndt et al., 2016 ; Wietek et al., 2015 ), and GtACR1, another natural anion channelrhodopsin from Guillardia theta ( Govorunova et al., 2015 ). We in utero electroporated plasmids to express iC++ in layer 2/3 pyramidal neurons of the visual cortex and obtained acute coronal brain slices from 3 to 9 week-old mice. Similar to GtACR2term, light activation of iC++ generated EPSCs in iC++ – layer 2/3 pyramidal neurons that were abolished by NBQX and CPP (amplitude reduced by 97.3 ± 0.9%, mean ± s.e.m., n = 4). Bumetanide diminished the iC++-induced EPSCs ( Figure 2G,H ) without affecting the iC++-mediated photocurrents ( Figure 2—figure supplement 3C,D ). We also expressed iC++ in Pv neurons by injecting a Cre-dependent AAV into Pvalb Cre/+ mice and found that light activation of iC++ caused bumetanide-sensitive IPSCs in iC++ – layer 2/3 pyramidal neurons ( Figure 2I,J ). Similarly, when we expressed iChloC in Pv neurons, light activation of iChloC resulted in IPSCs in 10 out of 17 recorded iChloC – layer 2/3 pyramidal neurons (183 ± 42 pA, mean ± s.e.m., n = 10), presumably because iChloC generated smaller photocurrents than iC++ (iChloC, 292 ± 62 pA, n = 7; iC++, 2182 ± 291 pA, n = 15; recorded at the membrane potentialterm of +10 mV; mean ± s.e.m., p<0.0001, t test with Welch’s correctionterm·stat). Finally, activation of GtACR1 in layer 2/3 pyramidal neurons produced EPSCs onto all recorded GtACR1 – pyramidal neurons (149 ± 47 pA, mean ± s.e.m., n = 11). Altogether, these results demonstrate that activation of different light-gated chlorideterm channels in neurons can trigger neurotransmitterterm release.

We hypothesized that the most likely neuronal compartments rendering GtACR2term excitatoryterm were the distal axonterm and presynapticterm terminals because of the following previous findings. First, activation of presynapticterm GABA A or glycine receptors enhanced neurotransmitterterm release at several synapses of the hippocampus, cerebellum, and brainstem ( Jang et al., 2006 ; Pugh and Jahr, 2011 ; Ruiz et al., 2010 ; Stell et al., 2007 ; Turecek and Trussell, 2001 ; Zorrilla de San Martin et al., 2017 ). Second, the chlorideterm concentrations were 4–5 times higher in the presynapticterm terminals of the Calyx of Held than the parent somaterm ( Price and Trussell, 2006 ). Third, there appeared to be an axo-somato-dendritic gradientterm in which the reversal potentials of GABA from the axonterm to the somaterm and dendrites of cortical neurons become progressively more negative ( Khirug et al., 2008 ). To test our hypothesis, we expressed GtACR2term in layer 2/3 pyramidal neurons of the visual cortex in one hemisphere as described above and obtained acute coronal slices from the contralateral hemisphere ( Figure 3A ). GtACR2term was present in the long-range callosal projections in the contralateral hemisphere ( Figure 3—figure supplement 1A ), which enabled us to activate GtACR2term in the axonterm and presynapticterm terminals that were severed from their parent somas. Light activation of GtACR2term in the callosal projections generated EPSCs in layer 2/3 pyramidal neurons of the contralateral cortex, which were diminished by TTX ( Figure 3—figure supplement 1B,C ) or bumetanide ( Figure 3B ). These results demonstrate that activation of GtACR2term in the axonterm and presynapticterm terminals is sufficient to trigger neurotransmitterterm release.

If GtACR2term-mediated chlorideterm currents are excitatoryterm in the presynapticterm terminals, then GtACR2term should be similar to ChR2, whose activation can directly depolarize the presynapticterm membrane in the absence of action potentials to trigger neurotransmitterterm release ( Petreanu et al., 2009 ). To test this prediction, we recorded EPSCs or IPSCs in GtACR2term – neurons while activating GtACR2term in layer 2/3 pyramidal neurons or Pv neurons, respectively ( Figure 3C,E ). As described above, bath application of TTX abolished the EPSCs and IPSCs. However, when we further blocked voltage-gated potassium channels by 4-aminopyridine (4-AP) and tetraethylammonium (TEA) to prolong membrane depolarization ( Petreanu et al., 2009 ), the EPSCs and IPSCs were partially recovered ( Figure 3D,F ). These results indicate that in the absence of action potentials, light activation of GtACR2term is sufficient to depolarize the presynapticterm membrane to open voltage-gated calcium channels and trigger neurotransmitterterm release.

We further tested if GtACR2term-induced axonal depolarization could evoke antidromic action potentials by performing extracellular loose-patch or whole-cell current clamp recordings at the somas of GtACR2term + pyramidal neurons. We observed antidromic spikes in 9 out of 88 neurons recorded in loose-patch configuration and 21 out of 56 neurons recorded in whole-cell configuration in response to blue light stimulation ( Figure 3G,H ). In the whole-cell current clamp recordings, although the chlorideterm concentration in the patch pipette solution sets the Nernst equilibrium potential of chlorideterm around −85 mV (see Materials and methods), blue light induced a depolarization following the initial hyperpolarization. This observation is consistent with the notion that the depolarization antidromically propagated from the distal axonterm to the somaterm ( Figure 3H ). The antidromic spikes were not affected by NBQX and CPP, but were abolished by TTX ( Figure 3H ), indicating that the spikes were generated within the GtACR2term + neurons, rather than by excitatoryterm inputs from other neurons. Antidromic spikes were only observed in a subset of neurons, likely because the GtACR2term expression levels are heterogeneous in different neurons, and the hyperpolarization initiated at the somaterm can orthodromically propagate to counteract the antidromic spikes. Similarly, TTX-sensitive antidromic spikes were observed in a subset of GtACR2term + Pv neurons (3 out of 11 neurons in loose-patch configuration and 7 out of 17 neurons in whole-cell configuration, Figure 3I,J ). These results show that GtACR2term-induced axonal depolarization can be sufficient to elicit antidromic action potentials.

Activating GtACR2term and other light-gated chlorideterm channels inhibits the somaterm but depolarizes the presynapticterm terminals to release neurotransmitters. This dichotomic effect can confound the utilization of these channels as inhibitory optogeneticterm·mth tools for suppressing neuronal activity. We reasoned that reducing the trafficking of light-gated chlorideterm channels into the axonterm and presynapticterm terminals should reduce or eliminate their depolarizing action. Thus, we sought to restrict GtACR2term within the somatodendritic domain of neurons by fusing GtACR2term with a number of reported somatodendritic targeting motifs including a 26-amino acid Myosin Va-binding domain of Melanophilin (MBD) ( Lewis et al., 2009 ), a 32-amino acid cytoplasmic C-terminal motif of Neuroligin 1 (Nlgn1C) ( Rosales et al., 2005 ), a 16-amino acid dileucine-containing motif of potassium channelterm Kv4.2 (Kv4.2LL) ( Rivera et al., 2003 ), the N-terminal 150 residues of kainate receptor subunit 2 (KA2N) ( Shemesh et al., 2017 ), the C-terminal 17 residues of Telencephalin (TlcnC) ( Mitsui et al., 2005 ), and a 65-amino acid cytoplasmic C-terminal motif of potassium channelterm Kv2.1 (Kv2.1C) ( Lim et al., 2000 ; Wu et al., 2013 ). Each of these GtACR2term variants ( Table 1 ), along with tdTomato, were expressed in layer 2/3 pyramidal neurons of the visual cortex by in utero electroporation ( Figure 4A ). Since GtACR2term was tagged with EYFP or EGFP, we compared the EYFP or EGFP fluorescence in layer 5, which only contains the axons of layer 2/3 pyramidal neurons, with the EYFP or EGFP fluorescence in layer 2/3 to estimate the distribution of GtACR2term between the axonterm and somatodendritic domain. We normalized the EYFP or EGFP fluorescence ratio between layer 5 and layer 2/3 by the tdTomato fluorescence ratio between layer 5 and layer 2/3 to control for variations in the collateral axons. Among tested motifs, TlcnC and Kv2.1C were most effective in targeting GtACR2term to the somaterm and dendrites ( Figure 4D ). As these two motifs may engage different trafficking mechanisms, we combined them to create two hybrid motifs, Kv2.1C-TlcnC and Kv2.1C-linker-TlcnC. Kv2.1C-linker-TlcnC turned out to be the best in restricting GtACR2term within the somatodendritic domain ( Figure 4A,D ). Interestingly, GtACR2term-EYFP-Kv2.1C and GtACR2term-EYFP-Kv2.1C-linker-TlcnC showed less intracellular aggregation than wild type GtACR2term ( Figure 4B ), suggesting that the somatodendritic targeting motifs also enhance the surface expression of GtACR2term. Finally, the EYFP fluorescence of the callosal projections in the contralateral hemisphere was also reduced for GtACR2term-EYFP-Kv2.1C and GtACR2term-EYFP-Kv2.1C-linker-TlcnC as compared to GtACR2term-EYFP ( Figure 4C,E ), demonstrating that both somatodendritic targeting motifs decreased the trafficking of GtACR2term into the distal axonterm.

Optogeneticterm·mth suppression of neuronal activity and synaptic outputs is an essential approach for dissecting the roles of specific neurons in brain functions. Light-gated chlorideterm channels, particularly GtACR1 and GtACR2term, are increasingly used due to their large photocurrents and high sensitivity to light ( Forli et al., 2018 ; Mardinly et al., 2018 ; Mauss et al., 2017 ; Mohamed et al., 2017 ; Mohammad et al., 2017 ). To use these tools to their full potentials, it is necessary that we understand their function and, importantly, their limitations. Our experiments reveal that wide-field activation of these channels in cortical neurons suppresses action potentials at the somaterm but also triggers neurotransmitterterm release at the presynapticterm terminals, thereby voiding inhibitionterm of neuronal activity. As demonstrated, the excitatoryterm action of chlorideterm channels in the axonterm and presynapticterm terminals is due to the high intracellular chlorideterm concentrations that create a depolarizing chlorideterm electrochemical gradientterm. The depolarizing effect of presynapticterm chlorideterm channels, GABA A or glycine receptors, has been documented at a few synapses in the hippocampus, cerebellum, and brainstem through application of agonists or antagonists ( Jang et al., 2006 ; Pugh and Jahr, 2011 ; Ruiz et al., 2010 ; Stell et al., 2007 ; Turecek and Trussell, 2001 ; Zorrilla de San Martin et al., 2017 ). Our results in cortical excitatoryterm and inhibitory neurons expand previous findings and indicate that a presynapticterm depolarizing chlorideterm electrochemical gradientterm is likely a general property across brain regions and neuronal types. More importantly, our experiments directly demonstrate that activation of presynapticterm chlorideterm channels is in fact excitatoryterm, as it can elicit action potentials with short latency, which was difficult to explicitly demonstrate by agonist application ( Pugh and Jahr, 2011 ). Thus, light-gated chlorideterm channels can be targeted to different brain regions, cell types, and subcellular compartments to study chlorideterm electrochemical gradients with unprecedented temporal precision.

Although the excitatoryterm effect of light-gated chlorideterm channels is undesired for neuronal silencing, a potential utilization of their dual actions at the presynapticterm terminals and somaterm is to activate specific projections of neurons while minimizing the effect of antidromic spikes. For example, long-range projection neurons often target multiple brain areas, and sometimes it is desired to selectively excite the axonal terminals projecting to one particular area. If ChR2 is used, local activation of ChR2-expressing axonal terminals may generate antidromic spikes, which will affect other projections. However, if GtACR2term is used, one can simultaneously activate GtACR2term in the somaterm and axonal terminals. Axonal depolarization will result in neurotransmitterterm release, but the antidromic spikes will be reduced or suppressed by the hyperpolarization originating from the somatodendritic domain, which reduces the likelihood of activating other projections.

To create a better inhibitory optogeneticterm·mth tool, we tested a number of somatodendritic targeting motifs to confine GtACR2term in the somaterm and dendrites. We generated a hybrid motif, Kv2.1C-linker-TlcnC, that was more effective than the widely used Kv2.1C ( Baker et al., 2016 ; Mardinly et al., 2018 ; Wu et al., 2013 ). The Kv2.1C motif was recently used in a bioRxiv preprint ( Mahn et al., 2017 ) to target GtACR2term to the somatodendritic domain. This GtACR2term variant achieved a greater reduction of neurotransmitterterm release from the contralateral callosal projections of medial prefrontal cortical neurons than what we observed with our GtACR2term-EYFP-Kv2.1C and GtACR2term-EYFP-Kv2.1C-linker-TlcnC in visual cortical neurons. This quantitative difference is likely due to different expression levels, light stimulation strengths, or neuronal types (see discussion below), which reiterates the importance of precisely characterizing the effect of light-gated chlorideterm channels, specific to each experimental design, when using them to manipulate neuronal activity.

While GtACR2term-EYFP-Kv2.1C-linker-TlcnC can still traffic to the axonterm to cause neurotransmitterterm release, it is thus far the most improved light-gated chlorideterm channelterm for optogeneticterm·mth inhibitionterm. Since it can generate much larger photocurrents in the somatodendritic domain than what is necessary to suppress action potentials, one approach to use this tool is to reduce the overall GtACR2term expression level to further decrease its presence in the axonterm while still generating sufficient inhibitory photocurrents at the somaterm. Supporting this idea, post hoc analysis of our experiments indicates that, for a given experiment, it is possible to identify a light stimulation strength that is low enough not to cause neurotransmitterterm release but still produce large inhibitory photocurrents in GtACR2term + neurons ( Figure 5—figure supplement 1 ). However, this stimulation strength must be empirically determined for every experiment. Furthermore, since during continuous activation of GtACR2term, large and synchronous neurotransmitterterm release occurs at the early phase of the light stimulation ( Figure 1—figure supplement 3 ), one may also take advantage of the late phase when neurotransmitterterm release is reduced but photocurrentterm is still robust for optogeneticterm·mth inhibitionterm experiments. Another approach is to selectively photostimulate GtACR2term at the somaterm by two-photon excitation ( Forli et al., 2018 ; Mardinly et al., 2018 ). However, it is difficult to apply this photoactivation approach to freely moving animals or deep brain areas. Therefore, it is imperative that we further engineer light-gated chlorideterm channels to eliminate their excitatoryterm action in the axonal terminals. Future strategies include generating more effective somatodendritic targeting motifs, creating outwardly rectifying channels, and the combination of both strategies. The GtACR2term-EYFP-Kv2.1C-linker-TlcnC reported here enhances the available toolkit for optogeneticterm·mth inhibitionterm and will serve as the foundation for future improvement.
