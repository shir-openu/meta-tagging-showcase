On the neurophysiological level, models of WMterm·dom capacityterm·dom suggest that interference between memory representations (‘ items ’) within the neuronal network is a source of informationterm loss and capacityterm·dom limitation ( Bouchacourt and Buschman, 2019 ; Lundqvist et al., 2016 ; Lundqvist et al., 2011 ; Schneegans et al., 2020 ). Divisive normalizationterm·def is a computational principle that acts upon neurons when presenting multiple stimuli simultaneously, it normalizes neuronal responses by creating ‘a ratio between the response of an individual neuron and the summed activity of a pool of neurons’ ( Carandini and Heeger, 2011 , p. 51). An effect related to divisive normalizationterm·def can be observed when two stimuli are presented either individually or simultaneously within the receptive field of a visual sensory neuron. The neuron’s firing rate when the stimuli are presented simultaneously becomes normalized by the populations’ responses to each individual stimulus ( Carandini et al., 1997 ; Heeger, 1992 ). This effect also occurs in relation to attentive processes and has been formalized into the ‘normalizationterm·dom modelterm of attentionterm·dom’ ( Reynolds et al., 1999 ; Reynolds and Heeger, 2009 ). Normalizationterm·dom of neuronal responses is commonly observed in many species throughout the animal kingdom, not just in sensory, but also in cognitive domains ( Carandini and Heeger, 2011 ). Investigations into WMterm·dom capacityterm·dom and modelterm predictions focus mostly on humans and monkeys. By extending this work to include birds, one can gain a unique comparative perspective. To resolve this, it is crucial to investigate the avian brain to understand how its different organization can produce such similar behavioral and neurophysiological results. While the neuronal correlates of WMterm·dom maintenance in birds have been investigated in some detail ( Diekamp et al., 2002a ; Hartmann et al., 2018 ; Rinnert et al., 2019 ; RosepersName and ColombopersName, 2005 ; Veit et al., 2014 ), a neurophysiological investigation of WMterm·dom capacityterm·dom limitation is still lacking. Higher loads produce divisive normalization-like neuronal responses

We next wanted to understand the neuronal mechanisms behind the informationterm loss at higher WMterm·dom loads. For that, we analyzed how the responses of individual sampleterm and delayterm neurons changed when the loadterm increased from one colorterm to two colors. For the ‘sampleterm populations’ and the ‘delayterm populations’, an increasing number of items reduced the amount of encoded informationterm about the colorterm identity ( Figure 4 ). This effect was due to neurons that had a large difference of firing rates between the colorterm A and colorterm B at loadterm 1 (high PEV, i.e., informationterm about colorterm), and reduced differentiation at loadterm 2 (small PEV, no or little informationterm about colorterm, e.g., Figure 2A ). ‘Divisive-normalization-like regularization’ (DNRterm·mth; Carandini and Heeger, 2011 ) can explain this effect. DNRterm·mth describes the computation that takes place when two stimuli are presented simultaneously. In a simplified case a neuronal response becomes normalized, analogous to vector normalizationterm·dom, with a normalizationterm·dom factor consisting of the simultaneous stimuli ( Carandini and Heeger, 2011 ). Applied to our context, a consequence of DNRterm·mth would be a reduced differentiation between two colorterm identities at loadterm 2 because differences in firing rate (for each stimulus by itself) at loadterm 1 would be normalized at loadterm 2 (resulting in informationterm loss, e.g., Figure 2A ). We, therefore, hypothesized that DNRterm·mth was observable for neurons with significantterm informationterm at loadterm 1. We tested for DNRterm·mth in the NCLterm by calculating a selectivity index (SE)term·def and a sensory interaction index (SI)term·def for each neuron for the sampleterm phase and the memory delayterm phase ( Reynolds et al., 1999 , see Materials and methods for details). SE indicates how strongly the neuronal response is driven by a colorterm at the favorite location of the neuron (reference) in relation to a selected probe colorterm (when either is presented alone). SI indicates how the probe colorterm interacts with the reference colorterm when both are presented simultaneously. Values of both indices, SE and SI, lie between –1 and +1. The addition of a probe colorterm influences the response to the reference colorterm by either suppressing the firing rate of the reference colorterm (if the reference elicits a higher firing rate than the probe, i.e., SE <0), or increasing the firing rate for the reference colorterm (if the probe elicits a higher firing rate than the reference, i.e., SE >0). If DNRterm·mth was present, this influence to suppress or enhance neuronal responses should be an even mixture at the population level, resulting in a significantterm regression between SE and SI with a slopeterm of around 0.5 ( Bouchacourt and Buschman, 2019 ). We compared regressions for the sampleterm and delayterm phase (each as one bin, see Materials and methods for details) for two groups of neurons: information-carrying neurons (significantterm informationterm at loadterm 1), and non-informative neurons (no informationterm at loadterm 1 or at loadterm 2; Figure 5—figure supplement 1 ). We found that DNRterm·mth was present in both sampleterm and delayterm phases ( Figure 5A ). Information-carrying sampleterm neurons had a fitted slopeterm of 0.47 ( R 2 adj 0.39, F (1,838) = 547.69, p < 0.001, CI = [0.43 0.51]) and delayterm neurons had a slopeterm of 0.50 ( R 2 adj 0.34, F (1,342) = 175.60, p < 0.001, CI = [0.43 0.58]). As the slopes were not significantly different from 0.5, this indicates that reference and probe colorterm had an equal influence on neuronal responses. We thus show that DNRterm·mth was observable in the neuronal population, and as a consequence of this computation, neurons had generally less informationterm about the colorterm identity at loadterm 2.

Discussion

Neuronal resources of WMterm·dom capacityterm·dom are hemifieldterm independent and gradually allocated

Our results confirm behavioral findings that have been discussed in detail in an earlier study ( BalakhonovpersName and RosepersName, 2017 ). In brief, we found that the WMterm·dom capacityterm·dom of crows is limited to about four items, and that the two visual hemifields are largely independent (i.e., the number of items on one side does not affect change detection performance on the other side). Within each hemifieldterm, performance dropped gradually with the addition of a second and third item but remained above chance. Fittingly, on the neuronal level, we found a markedly reduced amount of colorterm informationterm when the number of colored squares was increased from one to two (roughly 50% reduction in correct trials). This suggests that WMterm·dom could be conceptualized as a continuous resourceterm·dom that has to be divided between the two items ( Bays and Husain, 2008 ; van den Berg et al., 2012 ; Wilken and Ma, 2004 ), rather than two ‘simple’ slots that would each have the same amount of informationterm irrespective of the memory loadterm. This is also consistent with results of human neuroimaging that report decreased signal amplitude and precision with increasing memory loadterm ( Emrich et al., 2013 ; Sprague et al., 2014 ). In contrast, the hemispheric independence we observed would fit a slot-like modelterm, in which the hemispheres as a whole act like discrete slots. A more nuanced version of the slotterm·dom modelterm (‘slots and averaging’; Zhang and Luck, 2008 ) could also account for graded amounts of informationterm within a limited number of slots ( Fukuda et al., 2010 ; Zhang and Luck, 2008 ), as we found here. The mix of discrete and independent hemispheres with a graded allocation of informationterm between items that we found is comparable to results by Buschman et al., 2011 , observed in monkeyterm PFCterm. On the neuronal level, recurrent connections between neurons within a hemisphere may reduce item differentiation when multiple items are present simultaneously, creating capacityterm·dom limitations within the hemisphere ( Matsushima and Tanaka, 2014 ). A lack of interhemispheric recurrent connections would make processing in the other hemisphere independent. Like in monkeys, WMterm·dom capacityterm·dom in crows may therefore result from neuronal activity patterns governed by multiple individual items. We probed the WMterm·dom capacityterm·dom of crows using colored squares, based on the taskterm design of Buschman et al., 2011 . Using the identical taskterm allowed us to directly compare our neuronal results of WMterm·dom capacityterm·dom from NCLterm to results from PFCterm of monkeys. Taskterm similarity is very important for such cross species comparisons as even small changes in taskterm parameters may introduce substantial differences in neuronal responses, leading to potentially different conclusions. In a taskterm similar to the one used here, Lara and Wallis, 2014 , have found that neurons in the PFCterm of monkeys encoded nearly no informationterm about colorterm, but instead about location. In their taskterm monkeys had to memorize the colorterm of squares at two locations on a screen, and were again confronted with a colored square at one of the two locations after a delayterm. Lara and Wallis, 2014 , discuss the absence of colorterm informationterm in the neurons they recorded in relation to the taskterm of Buschman et al., 2011 , who like us, did find colorterm informationterm. In brief, the exact taskterm design may determine the neuronal encoding of taskterm relevant informationterm ( Lara and Wallis, 2014 ). Similar to the complex contribution of PFCterm neurons to WMterm·dom, neurons of NCLterm can also encode a wide range of very different taskterm relevant aspects, like colorterm (this study), spatial locations ( Rinnert et al., 2019 ; Veit et al., 2017 ), and more abstract items like rules ( Veit and NiederpersName, 2013 ) and numerosities ( Ditz and NiederpersName, 2015 ).

Neuronal correlates of WMterm·dom and attentionterm·dom overlap in PFCterm neurons, for example, Lebedev et al., 2004 , found that a substantial amount of PFCterm neurons encode either an attentional signal, or a memory signal, and some (hybrid) neurons do both. The WMterm·dom modelterm of Bouchacourt and Buschman, 2019 , is based solely on data from monkeyterm electrophysiologyterm·mth, and thus implicitly tied to the layered columns of the neocortexterm. The results we report here show that the modelterm also fits the neurophysiology of WMterm·dom in crows. The transient activity of neurons that we report ( Figures 2 and 3A ) might fit into such models. However, our results can only be compared very cautiously to this (since even small changes in taskterm design significantly alter neuronal responses, e.g., Lara and Wallis, 2014 ). Therefore, while we cannot, yet, fully equate crowterm and monkeyterm WMterm·dom, our results raise two important questions about how WMterm·dom is implemented on the level of neuronal networks that have implications for our comparative view of crowterm WMterm·dom. The first regards the neuronal computations underlying WMterm·dom. We do, however, show that mammalianterm models of WMterm·dom are in line with WMterm·dom in birds, which implies that fundamental aspects of WMterm·dom are shared between these animal groups.
