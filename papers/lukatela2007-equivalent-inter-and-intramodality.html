phenomenoncross-modal long-term word priming
claim-typenull-result
mechanismMixing study modalities within subjects pulls attention toward modality-specific processing and away from word phonology, so at test the stems whose modality matches the prime are the ones completed - manufacturing the apparent intramodal advantage. — “If the mixing of visual and auditory presentations draws attention to aspects of modality processing per se and away from word processing, then at test, those critical word stems sharing modality processing with the primes are more likely to be completed.”
mechanismStudy-to-test carryover runs simultaneously along two dimensions - modality-independent lexical access, which yields the priming, and modality-dependent sensory analysis, which yields the overall performance difference between seeing and hearing. — “It is tempting to assume that this dichotomy reflects two different but co-occurring processes in implicit memory tasks: modality-independent lexical access and modality-dependent sensory analysis (see the General Discussion section).”
mechanismPriming transfers through activation of a word's phonology, a code common to the word seen and the word heard, so its magnitude is unaffected by which sense delivered the prime. — “Its invariance over AV and VV rules out the distinct sensory qualities of seeing words and hearing words and points to the activation of phonology as the dimension of transfer.”
relation — “Importantly, for the primary goal of the present research, the three conclusions for Experiment 2 run counter to the experimental observations that had led Morton (1979) to the hypothesis of separate lexicons for words seen and words heard.”
relation — “In an experiment with auditory stem completion, Pilotti, Gallo, and Roediger (2000, Experiment 3) manipulated visual and auditory study between subjects and found better performance for auditory study, contrary to the present Experiments 1 and 2.”
relation — “This apparent dependence of relative performance in the two conditions on experimental design is in agreement with the observations of Brown et al.”
replicationcontested
limitationThe paper says it cannot say precisely why its results diverge from the conventional finding. — “At the current juncture, the precise reason is unclear.”
limitationThe between-subjects design alone does not explain the contradiction with prior work, since other between-subjects studies did not find equivalence. — “As central as it may be, the between-subjects design that marks the present series of experiments cannot provide a complete account of the contradictory findings.”
limitationThe account offered for the within-subjects asymmetry found in Experiment 3 is described as tentative. — “The identified asymmetry and the account given of it are obviously tentative, but not unreasonable.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingAcross five experiments using stem completion, fragment completion and perceptual identification at test, priming from a different study modality was as large as priming from the same modality. — “Equal magnitudes of inter- and intramodality priming were found in experiments with visual and auditory stem completion at test, with visual fragment completion at test, and with visual and auditory perceptual identification at test.”
findingThe authors conclude that one amodal lexicon serves words seen and words heard, and that implicit word memory rests on modality-independent phonological processing. — “The results point to a single amodal lexicon and to modality-independent phonological processing as the basis of implicit word memory.”
findingIn Experiment 1, auditory-study/visual-test stem completion primed as much as visual-study/visual-test stem completion, contrary to the standard intramodal advantage. — “In sum, contrary to the standard observation, Experiment 1 showed an equivalence of inter- and intramodal priming.”
findingExperiment 2 found auditory-to-visual and visual-to-auditory transfer equal to each other, and auditory-to-auditory transfer equal to visual-to-visual transfer. — “First, cross-modal transfer occurs to the same degree in AV and VA. Second, AA transfer and VV transfer are of the same magnitude.”
findingExperiment 3, which mixed study modalities within subjects and delivered all instructions visually, restored the conventional superiority of visual-to-visual over auditory-to-visual transfer. — “The within-subjects design of Experiment 3 benefited transfer in the same-modality condition, relative to the cross-modality condition.”
findingIn fragment completion (Experiment 4) the size of the priming effect was the same after visual and auditory study, but the overall level of fragment completion, primed and unprimed alike, was higher after visual study. — “The magnitude of priming was independent of stimulus modality, whereas the overall magnitude of fragment completion was modality dependent.”
findingDeep study (imagery) and shallow study (counting ascenders/descenders, repeating the spoken word) produced equal priming, which the authors read as equal lexical involvement in the two tasks. — “The failure to find an influence on long-term priming of level of processing at study suggests that the processing tasks were alike in lexical involvement”
