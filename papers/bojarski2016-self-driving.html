phenomenonend-to-end learning of lane and road following
claim-typemethod
mechanismthe steering command is stored as 1/r rather than r, which removes the singularity at straight-ahead and makes the label continuous through zero across left and right turns — “In order to make our system independent of the car geometry, we represent the steering command as 1/r where r is the turning radius in meters. We use 1/r instead of r to prevent a singularity when driving straight (the turning radius for driving straight is infinity). 1/r smoothly transitions through zero from left turns (negative values) to right turns (positive values).” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
mechanismrecovery is taught rather than encountered: the training set is augmented with images of the car already displaced from the lane centre, because human driving alone never shows the network a mistake — “Training with data from only the human driver is not sufficient. The network must learn how to recover from mistakes. Otherwise the car will slowly drift off the road. The training data is therefore augmented with additional images that show the car in different shifts from the center of the lane and rotations from the direction of the road.”
mechanismoff-centre viewpoints are synthesised under an approximation the paper states it knows to be wrong - everything below the horizon treated as flat ground, everything above it as infinitely far - accepted because the resulting distortions do not hurt training — “Precise viewpoint transformation requires 3D scene knowledge which we don’t have. We therefore approximate the transformation by assuming all points below the horizon are on flat ground and all points above the horizon are infinitely far away. This works fine for flat terrain but it introduces distortions for objects that stick above the ground, such as cars, poles, trees, and buildings. Fortunately these distortions don’t pose a big problem for network training.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
mechanismthe label for a synthesised viewpoint is not the driver's command but the command that would return the car to the correct place within a fixed two-second horizon — “The steering label for transformed images is adjusted to one that would steer the vehicle back to the desired location and orientation in two seconds.”
mechanismthe training frames are filtered by the driver's activity label and then downsampled, because consecutive frames at full rate are near-duplicates — “To train a CNN to do lane following we only select data where the driver was staying in a lane and discard the rest. We then sample that video at 10 FPS. A higher sampling rate would result in including images that are highly similar and thus not provide much useful information.”
mechanismthe perturbations are drawn from a normal distribution deliberately twice as wide as the variation measured in human drivers — “The magnitude of these perturbations is chosen randomly from a normal distribution. The distribution has zero mean, and the standard deviation is twice the standard deviation that we measured with human drivers.”
mechanismautonomy is computed from a COUNTED event and an ASSUMED constant: each simulated intervention is charged a fixed six seconds of human takeover time — “We assume that in real life an actual intervention would require a total of six seconds: this is the time required for a human to retake control of the vehicle, re-center it, and then restart the self-steering mode.”
mechanismthe normalisation layer is inside the network but hard-coded, so it is not learned and can be changed with the architecture and run on the GPU — “The first layer of the network performs image normalization. The normalizer is hard-coded and is not adjusted in the learning process. Performing normalization in the network allows the normalization scheme to be altered with the network architecture and to be accelerated via GPU processing.”

findingAn end-to-end CNN mapping raw front-camera pixels directly to steering commands learned to drive on local roads and highways with ~98% autonomy, without hand-coded lane detection or path planning. — “We trained a convolutional neural network (CNN) to map raw pixels from a single front-facing camera directly to steering commands. This end-to-end approach proved surprisingly powerful.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
findingThe network learned useful road features using only the human steering angle as the training signal, never explicitly supervised to detect them. — “detecting useful road features with only the human steering angle as the training signal. We never explicitly trained it to detect, for example, the outline of roads.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
