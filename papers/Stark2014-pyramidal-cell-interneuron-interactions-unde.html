findingOptogenetically depolarizing a small local group of CA1 pyramidal cells was enough to generate ripple-frequency oscillations, while silencing pyramidal cells directly or indirectly (by driving PV or somatostatin interneurons) terminated ripples that were already underway. — “Using multisite optogenetic manipulations in freely behaving rodents, we found that depolarization of a small group of nearby pyramidal cells was sufficient to induce high-frequency oscillations, whereas closed-loop silencing of pyramidal cells or activation of parvalbumin-(PV) or somatostatin-immunoreactive interneurons aborted spontaneously occurring ripples.”
findingFocal blockade of GABA-A receptors completely abolished locally induced high-frequency oscillations, showing that pyramidal-pyramidal interactions by themselves cannot produce ripples and that fast inhibition is required. — “The full suppression of local iHFOs following GABA A receptor blockade indicates that PYR-PYR interactions alone ( Figure 1A ) are not sufficient for ripple generation and highlights the critical contribution of fast inhibition.”
findingDriving PV interneurons alone, even at very high light intensities, never produced a field-potential ripple, which rules out the simple interneuron-interneuron model as the source of the LFP oscillation. — “Thus, in contrast to PYR activation, which readily generates iHFOs ( Figure 2 ), non-rhythmic activation of PV interneurons cannot induce LFP oscillations, indicating that a simple version of the INT-INT model ( Figure 1B ) cannot account for the generation of LFP ripples.”
findingAlthough PV activation produced no field ripple, it did impose ripple-frequency coherence on ensemble spiking, so interneuron-interneuron interactions can set the timing even when they cannot create the oscillation. — “Thus, although tonic light activation of PV interneurons cannot induce LFP ripples, it can organize neuronal ensemble spiking into coherent ripple-frequency oscillations, consistent with a modified version of an INT-INT-based timing mechanism ( Figure 1D ).”
findingBlocking fast inhibition reduced the phase coherence between simultaneously induced oscillations at separate sites, showing that interneurons are what keeps spatially separate ripple oscillators locked together. — “Furthermore, the high iHFO coherence between simultaneously activated loci is reduced by blocking fast inhibition, indicating that interneurons are critical for maintaining spatial coherence of ripples ( Traub et al., 1996 ).”
findingPyramidal cells fired roughly a quarter-cycle (about 90 degrees) ahead of interneurons on every ripple cycle, with mean phases of 157 degrees for pyramidal cells and 242 degrees for interneurons. — “Spikes of CA1 units were phase-locked to the ripple cycles (PYR: 1105/1864, 59%; INT: 254/369, 69%), and PYR spiked about 90° earlier than INT (mean ± SEM phases: PYR, 157° ± 1°; INT, 242° ± 4°; 0° corresponds to an LFP peak) ( Figure S3Aa ) on every ripple cycle ( Figure 3A ).”
findingSuperficial CA1 pyramidal cells were entrained to ripples more often (64% versus 57%) and with higher peak firing gain (12.4 versus 9.2) than deep pyramidal cells. — “While roughly equivalent numbers of neurons were sampled in deep (toward str. oriens; 994 units) and superficial (toward str. radiatum; 1,131 units) sublayers ( Figure 3B ), we found that entrainment to spontaneous ripples was more ubiquitous in superficial PYR than deep PYR (609/954, 64%, versus 478/842, 57%; p < 0.001, Fisher’s exact test) and that superficial PYR had higher peak gain (mean ± SEM. PYR gain, 12.4 ± 0.5 versus 9.2 ± 0.5; p < 0.001, U test) ( Figure S3Ab ).”
findingPhysiological ripples and pathological "fast ripples" are produced by different mechanisms, because removing fast inhibition destroys the first while creating the second. — “Our experiments demonstrate that physiological ripples and “fast ripples” arise from different mechanisms, since loss of fast inhibition disrupts the first and induces the latter.”
findingLocally induced oscillations fell to 2% of local power just 400 micrometres away and to baseline beyond that, whereas spontaneous ripples retained 89% of their power at 400 micrometres, so ripple coherence across sites is not volume conduction. — “Two shanks away (400 μm), oscillation power was 2% ± 0.3% of the local power (mean ±SEM; 14 experiments in nine mice; p < 0.001, Wilcoxon’s signed-rank test with a zero-power null) ( Figure 7B ), while >400 μm away, induced power was indistinguishable from baseline (0.3% ± 0.1%; p > 0.05).”
findingdepolarising a small local group of pyramidal cells is sufficient to induce a ripple-like oscillation — “Our principal findings are as follows: (1) activation of a small group of pyramidal cells is sufficient to generate iHFOs. (2) Fast GABA A -mediated inhibition is critical for the generation of iHFOs. (3) Pyramidal cell activity is critical for the maintenance of ripples. (4) PV interneurons can pace spiking in local populations. (5) Multisite activation induces temporally coherent ripples mediated by phase-locked interneuron spiking. These findings are consistent with a model of ripple generation based on PYR-INT-INT interactions”
