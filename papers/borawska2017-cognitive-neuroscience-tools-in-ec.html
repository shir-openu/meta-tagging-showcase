Object of study —

Research question — How can cognitive neuroscience techniques contribute to economic experiments on decision making, and which stages of the decision process can each technique address?

Contribution — A literature reviewterm·mth that maps cognitive-neuroscience measurement techniques (fMRI, EEG, fNIRS, GSR, heart rate, eye-tracking) onto Simon's three phases of the decision-making process and identifies which pairings have and have not yet been exploited in economic experiments.

Abstract

Abstract

Experimental economics utilises a lot of different techniques to support its research. Applying computers and IT has already become common. As a novel approach the use of cognitive neuroscience tools is now being considered. Investigating the neurophysiological signals of experiment participants can give researchers a deeper insight into a decision making processterm·dom. The aim of the article is to show how neuroscience techniques can contribute to economic experiments, especially those concerning decision making. The overview and presentation of the possibilities of such tools is shown regarding different stages of the decision making processterm·dom and related experimental studies. The proposed analysis could allow for the better design of economic experiments conducted with the use of the most up-to date technology available.
