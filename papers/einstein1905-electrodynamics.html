mechanismA CONJECTURE IS RAISED TO THE STATUS OF A POSTULATE BY DECLARATION, AND NAMED IN THE SAME SENTENCE: the epistemic status of the claim is changed by an act of writing rather than by evidence, and the name it will carry hereafter is fixed in the same breath. A second postulate is introduced alongside it and described as ONLY APPARENTLY irreconcilable with the first — “We will raise this conjecture (the purport of which will hereafter be called the “Principle of Relativity”) to the status of a postulate, and also introduce another postulate, which is only apparently irreconcilable with the former, namely, that light is always propagated in empty space with a definite velocity c which is independent of the state of motion of the emitting body.”
mechanismAN ENTITY IS DECLARED SUPERFLUOUS RATHER THAN REFUTED: the luminiferous ether is not argued to be absent but shown to be unnecessary, because the view to be developed will not require an absolutely stationary space provided with special properties, nor assign a velocity-vector to a point of empty space — “The introduction of a “luminiferous ether” will prove to be superfluous inasmuch as the view here to be developed will not require an “absolutely stationary space” provided with special properties, nor assign a velocity-vector to a point of the empty space in which electromagnetic processes take place.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
mechanismA TERM IS COINED EXPRESSLY TO MAKE A VERBAL DISTINCTION, AND THE STATED PURPOSE IS PRESENTATIONAL: the coordinate system is called the stationary system in order to render the presentation more precise and to distinguish it VERBALLY from others that will be introduced later — “In order to render our presentation more precise and to distinguish this system of co-ordinates verbally from others which will be introduced hereafter, we call it the “stationary system.””
mechanismA COMMON TERM CANNOT BE DEFINED AT ALL WITHOUT A STIPULATION, AND THE PAPER NAMES WHICH PART IS STIPULATED: an A-time and a B-time have been defined, but a common time for A and B CANNOT BE DEFINED AT ALL unless it is established BY DEFINITION that light takes the same time from A to B as from B to A. The equality is established, not measured — “We have so far defined only an “A time” and a “B time.” We have not defined a common “time” for A and B, for the latter cannot be defined at all unless we establish by definition that the “time” required by light to travel from A to B equals the “time” it requires to travel from B to A.”
mechanismTHE PROPERTIES THE DEFINITION NEEDS ARE ASSUMED, NOT DERIVED: that the definition of synchronism is free from contradictions and possible for any number of points is ASSUMED, and so are the two relations quoted - the symmetry of synchrony and its transitivity - which are stated as universally valid rather than shown — “We assume that this definition of synchronism is free from contradictions, and possible for any number of points; and that the following relations are universally valid:— If the clock at B synchronizes with the clock at A, the clock at A synchronizes with the clock at B. If the clock at A synchronizes with the clock at B and also with the clock at C, the clocks at B and C also synchronize with each other.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
mechanismA DEFINITION IS DECLARED SATISFACTORY AT ONE SCOPE AND UNSATISFACTORY AT ANOTHER, WITH BOTH SCOPES NAMED: substituting the position of a watch's hand for time works when defining a time exclusively for the place where the watch is, and stops working as soon as events at different places must be put in one series. The word does not fail - it fails OUTSIDE A STATED RANGE — “It might appear possible to overcome all the difficulties attending the definition of “time” by substituting “the position of the small hand of my watch” for “time.” And in fact such a definition is satisfactory when we are concerned with defining a time exclusively for the place where the watch is located; but it is no longer satisfactory when we have to connect in time series of events occurring at different places, or—what comes to the same thing—to evaluate the times of events occurring at places remote from the watch.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
mechanismONE WORD IS GIVEN TO TWO DIFFERENT OPERATIONS AND THE PAPER'S RESULT IS THAT THEY DISAGREE: two measuring procedures are set out, the outcome of each is called THE LENGTH OF THE ROD, and they are told apart only by a trailing qualifier - in the moving system, in the stationary system - after which the text states plainly that the second differs from the first — “In accordance with the principle of relativity the length to be discovered by the operation ( a )—we will call it “the length of the rod in the moving system”—must be equal to the length l of the stationary rod. The length to be discovered by the operation ( b ) we will call “the length of the (moving) rod in the stationary system.” This we shall determine on the basis of our two principles, and we shall find that it differs from l .” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
mechanismTHE PREVIOUS THEORY'S ASSUMPTION IS MADE VISIBLE BY BEING NAMED AS TACIT: current kinematics is said to assume TACITLY that the two operations give precisely equal lengths, and the assumption is then restated in other words - that a moving rigid body may be perfectly represented by the same body at rest — “Current kinematics tacitly assumes that the lengths determined by these two operations are precisely equal, or in other words, that a moving rigid body at the epoch t may in geometrical respects be perfectly represented by the same body at rest in a definite position.”
mechanismTWO SETS OF OBSERVERS APPLY THE SAME STATED CRITERION AND REACH OPPOSITE VERDICTS: one finds the clocks not synchronous while the other DECLARES them synchronous, from which the paper concludes that no ABSOLUTE SIGNIFICATION can be attached to the concept of simultaneity. The concept survives and loses its claim to hold independently of a frame — “Observers moving with the moving rod would thus find that the two clocks were not synchronous, while observers in the stationary system would declare the clocks to be synchronous. So we see that we cannot attach any absolute signification to the concept of simultaneity, but that two events which, viewed from a system of co-ordinates, are simultaneous, can no longer be looked upon as simultaneous events when envisaged from a system which is in motion relatively to that system.”
mechanismTHE SAME EQUATIONS ARE CLOTHED IN WORDS TWICE AND THE TWO READINGS ARE LABELLED BY THEIR ERA: identical mathematics is given an OLD MANNER OF EXPRESSION, in which a moving charge feels an extra electromotive force, and a NEW MANNER OF EXPRESSION, in which it feels the electric force found by transforming to its own rest frame. THE CONTENT IS ONE AND THE VOCABULARY IS TWO, and the paper marks the difference as historical rather than factual — “Consequently the first three equations above allow themselves to be clothed in words in the two following ways:— If a unit electric point charge is in motion in an electromagnetic field, there acts upon it, in addition to the electric force, an “electromotive force” which, if we neglect the terms multiplied by the second and higher powers of v / c , is equal to the vector-product of the velocity of the charge and the magnetic force, divided by the velocity of light. (Old manner of expression.) If a unit electric point charge is in motion in an electromagnetic field, the force acting upon it is equal to the electric force which is present at the locality of the charge, and which we ascertain by transformation of the field to a system of co-ordinates at rest relatively to the electrical charge. (New manner of expression.) The analogy holds with “magnetomotive forces.”” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
mechanismA CONCEPT IS DEMOTED TO AN AUXILIARY THAT OWES ITS EXISTENCE TO THE DESCRIPTION FRAME: electromotive force is said to play MERELY the part of an auxiliary concept, which OWES ITS INTRODUCTION to the circumstance that electric and magnetic forces do not exist independently of the state of motion of the coordinate system. The term is explained by the bookkeeping, not by the world — “We see that electromotive force plays in the developed theory merely the part of an auxiliary concept, which owes its introduction to the circumstance that electric and magnetic forces do not exist independently of the state of motion of the system of co-ordinates.”
mechanismA CLASS OF QUESTIONS IS DISSOLVED RATHER THAN ANSWERED: the asymmetry the paper opened with is said to DISAPPEAR, and questions as to the SEAT of electrodynamic electromotive forces in unipolar machines are said to NOW HAVE NO POINT - retired without being settled either way — “Furthermore it is clear that the asymmetry mentioned in the introduction as arising when we consider the currents produced by the relative motion of a magnet and a conductor, now disappears. Moreover, questions as to the “seat” of electrodynamic electromotive forces (unipolar machines) now have no point.”
mechanismA DEFINITION IS CRITICISED AS NOT ADVANTAGEOUS RATHER THAN WRONG, AND THE CRITERION IS SIMPLICITY OF THE RESULTING LAWS: a footnote records that the definition of force given here is not advantageous, as Planck first showed, and that it is more to the point to define force so that the laws of momentum and energy take their simplest form. THE GROUND FOR PREFERRING ONE DEFINITION IS WHAT IT DOES TO THE REST OF THE THEORY — “The definition of force here given is not advantageous, as was first shown by M. Planck. It is more to the point to define force in such a way that the laws of momentum and energy assume the simplest form.”
