mechanismrepeated cannula and injector insertion destroys tissue in Layers I-III of the mPFC on both sides of the midline, removing projection neurons that normally regulate the nucleus accumbens and basolateral amygdala, and the loss of that top-down regulation is what blocks extinction learning — “Therefore, it is conceivable that the damage caused by the microinjection procedure resulted in altered mPFC regulation of the NAc and BLC, thereby impairing extinction of EtOH-CPP.”
mechanismcumulative mechanical damage from implantation plus repeated infusions amounts to a partial lesion of a region already shown to be necessary for extinction, so the injection itself reproduces the lesion effect regardless of what is infused — “The most parsimonious explanation for these findings is that the microinjection procedure, regardless of the compound being injected, caused tissue damage to an area of the brain shown to be necessary for extinction of EtOH-CPP [ 1 ].”

limitationThe planned within-subject comparisons were run regardless of the interaction and are acknowledged as not statistically justified. — “This method of analysis, although not statistically justified, was implemented because of the narrow detection window and increased variability that are inherent to our extinction procedure (e.g., [ 1 , 8 ]).”
limitationThe proposed explanation via damage to mPFC projection neurons is untested and needs confirmation. — “Of course, further examination of the effects of the microinjection procedure on these specific projections within the mPFC, as well as their projection targets, is required to confirm this suggestion.”
limitationAs performed, the microinjection procedure cannot be used to test intra-mPFC pharmacological manipulations of EtOH-CPP extinction. — “It is important to note that irrespective of how each component of the microinjection procedure contributes to the observed effect, as currently performed, this procedure is not adequately suited to examine the effects of intra-mPFC pharmacological manipulations on extinction of EtOH-CPP in mice.”
limitationThe authors state that their own analysis is not statistically justified, and say why they used it anyway — “This method of analysis, although not statistically justified, was implemented because of the narrow detection window and increased variability that are inherent to our extinction procedure (e.g., [ 1 , 8 ]).”
limitationAnimals were removed for failing to acquire a preference and for histology, about a third of each experiment — “In Experiment 1, a total of 18 animals were removed from analysis (9 showed less than 50% preference on Test 1 and an additional 9 were removed for not meeting histological criteria).”
limitationNon-learners were excluded by an a priori rule, which is disclosed and defended by reference to prior practice — “Because the goal of these experiments was to specifically manipulate extinction of an acquired EtOH-CPP, we decided, a priori , to remove animals that failed to express a place preference of greater than 50% on Test 1.”
limitationThe strongest extra evidence - doubling extinction to six days - is reported as data not shown — “In fact, in one of the replicates of Experiment 1 we extended extinction by an additional 3 days (for a total of 6 days). Despite doubling the number of extinction trials, we found that both mPFC-injected groups continued to exhibit impaired extinction—that is, both groups showed persistent CPP (data not shown).”
limitationThe damage-to-projection account is offered as conceivable and explicitly not established — “Of course, further examination of the effects of the microinjection procedure on these specific projections within the mPFC, as well as their projection targets, is required to confirm this suggestion.”
limitationNo group-by-test interaction reached significance in either experiment, so the key contrast rests on the planned within-group comparisons — “Specifically, the initial 2-way ANOVA revealed a main effect of Test [ F (1,40) = 9.3, p < .005] but not Group, and no significant Group × Test interaction ( p's > .05).”

findingNeither the bupivacaine group nor its vehicle-injected control extinguished ethanol place preference, so the intended pharmacological comparison was uninterpretable. — “Thus, neither the control (Vehicle) nor the experimental group (Bupivacaine) showed significant extinction of EtOH-CPP.”
findingIn the second experiment only the group that never underwent surgery showed a reliable drop in place preference, isolating the surgery/injection procedure rather than the drug as the cause. — “The results of the separate paired t-tests showed a significant decrease in preference for only the No Sx group [ t (11) = 2.5 p < .05].”
findingBecause a no-surgery control that received the same handling did extinguish, the disruption is attributable to the microinjection procedure itself and not to the stress of being handled. — “Together, these experiments suggest some component of the microinjection procedure (but not merely the handling required to make the injections) interfered with extinction of EtOH-CPP.”
findingExtending extinction training to six days did not rescue extinction in either injected group, showing the impairment is persistent rather than a matter of insufficient training. — “Despite doubling the number of extinction trials, we found that both mPFC-injected groups continued to exhibit impaired extinction—that is, both groups showed persistent CPP (data not shown).”
findingThe authors conclude the repeated intra-mPFC microinjection technique, as performed, is unsuitable for studying extinction of ethanol place preference in mice. — “It is important to note that irrespective of how each component of the microinjection procedure contributes to the observed effect, as currently performed, this procedure is not adequately suited to examine the effects of intra-mPFC pharmacological manipulations on extinction of EtOH-CPP in mice.”
findingSurgery and anaesthesia alone are ruled out as the cause, since a single electrode insertion at the same coordinates in earlier work left extinction intact. — “It is unlikely that the observed effect was a direct result of the surgery/anesthesia per se as we have previously shown that a one-time insertion of an electrode into the same area of the mPFC while under general anesthesia did not impair subsequent extinction”
findingvehicle and bupivacaine groups both failed to extinguish, leaving the drug effect untestable — “Thus, neither the control (Vehicle) nor the experimental group (Bupivacaine) showed significant extinction of EtOH-CPP.”
findingin the second experiment only the unoperated group extinguished — “These data suggest that the No Sx, but not Vehicle or Bupivacaine, group significantly extinguished.”
