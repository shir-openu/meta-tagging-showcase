method — evidence— “The study is a cross-sectional, hospital-based survey conducted via a region-stratified, 2-stage cluster sampling from January 29, 2020, to February 3, 2020. During this period, the total confirmed cases of COVID-19 exceeded 10 000 in China.”
object of study — evidence— “This cross-sectional, survey-based, region-stratified study collected demographic data and mental health measurements from 1257 health care workers in 34 hospitals from January 29, 2020, to February 3, 2020, in China.”
research question — evidence— “To assess the magnitude of mental health outcomes and associated factors among health care workers treating patients exposed to COVID-19 in China.”
contribution — evidence— “However, evidence-based evaluations and mental health interventions targeting front-line health care workers are relatively scarce.”
mechanismexposure intensity the authors' account of why frontline nurses fare worst - close, frequent contact and longer hours than usual, which is exposure to the hazard and exposure to the workload at once— “Frontline nurses treating patients with COVID-19 are likely exposed to the highest risk of infection because of their close, frequent contact with patients and working longer hours than usual.”
mechanismperceived personal danger human-to-human transmissibility and potential fatality are offered as intensifying the perception of personal danger - a belief about the disease rather than a dose of it— “The fact that COVID-19 is human-to-human transmissible,1, 3 associated with high morbidity, and potentially fatal16 may intensify the perception of personal danger.”
mechanismresource shortage predictable shortages of supplies together with rising case influx are named as contributors to pressure, separately from contact with patients— “Additionally, predictable shortages of supplies and an increasing influx of suspected and actual cases of COVID-19 contribute to the pressures and concerns of health care workers.17”
mechanisminexperience 71.5% of nurses held junior titles, which the authors read as fewer years of work experience - offered as why the most exposed group is also the least practised— “Moreover, 71.5% of all nurses had junior titles, indicating that most had fewer years of work experience.”
mechanismloss of control a list of distress sources taken from the SARS literature: vulnerability or loss of control, concern for self and family, changed work, isolation— “Sources of distress may include feelings of vulnerability or loss of control and concerns about health of self, spread of virus, health of family and others, changes in work, and being isolated.15”
builds-on the SARS-outbreak literature on health care workers (Maunder 2003, Bai 2004, Lee 2007, Chua 2004), which reported 89% with psychological symptoms in high-risk situations— “In a previous study during the acute SARS outbreak, 89% of health care workers who were in high-risk situations reported psychological symptoms.8”
confirms the SARS emergency-department finding that nurses developed more distress and more behavioural disengagement than physicians— “During the SARS outbreak, a study conducted among health care workers in emergency departments also showed that nurses were more likely to develop distress and use behavioral disengagement than physicians.15”

sensesevere symptoms - A SECOND AND HIGHER THRESHOLD IN THE SAME PAPER: a score greater than 10, 7, 15 or 26.
