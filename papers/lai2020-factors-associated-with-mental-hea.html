## Discussion

This cross-sectional survey enrolled 1257 respondents and revealed a high prevalence of mental health symptoms among health care workers treating patients with COVID-19term·dom in China. Overall, 50.4%, 44.6%, 34.0%, and 71.5% of all participants reported symptoms of depressionterm, anxietyterm, insomniaterm, and distressterm, respectively. Participants were divided in 3 groups (Wuhanterm, other regions in Hubei provinceterm, and regions outside Wuhanterm province) to compare interregional differences. Most participants were female, were nursesterm, were aged 26 to 40 years, were married, and worked in tertiary hospitals with a junior technical title. Nursesterm, women, those working in Wuhanterm, and frontline workersterm reported more severe symptoms on all measurements. Our study further indicated that being a woman and having an intermediate technical title were associated with experiencing severe depressionterm, anxietyterm, and distressterm. Working in the front line was an independent risk factor for worse mental health outcomes in all dimensions of interest. Together, our findings present concerns about the psychological well-being of physiciansterm and nursesterm involved in the acute COVID-19term·dom outbreak.

In this study, a significant proportion of participants experienced anxietyterm, depressionterm, and insomniaterm symptoms, and more than 70% reported psychological distressterm. In a previous study during the acute SARSterm·dom outbreak, 89% of health care workers who were in high-risk situations reported psychological symptoms.8 The psychological response of health care workers to an epidemic of infectious diseases is complicated. Sources of distressterm may include feelings of vulnerability or loss of control and concerns about health of self, spread of virus, health of family and others, changes in work, and being isolated.15 The fact that COVID-19term·dom is human-to-human transmissible,1, 3 associated with high morbidity, and potentially fatal16 may intensify the perception of personal danger. Additionally, predictable shortages of supplies and an increasing influx of suspected and actual cases of COVID-19term·dom contribute to the pressures and concerns of health care workers.17

Of note, 76.7% of all participants were women, and 60.8% were nursesterm (90.8% of whom were female). This study has several limitations. First, it was limited in scope. Most participants (81.2%) were from Hubei provinceterm, limiting the generalization of our findings to less affected regions. Second, the study was carried out during 6 days and lacks longitudinal follow-upterm·mth. Because of the increasingly arduous situation, the mental health symptoms of health care workers could become more severe. Third, this study was unable to distinguish the association of symptoms with being a clinician in this region vs simply living in this region (because there was no comparator groupterm·mth) and was also unable to distinguish preexisting mental health symptoms vs new symptoms.
