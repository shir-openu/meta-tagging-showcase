phenomenonperception without awareness
claim-typemethod
mechanismInstructing an observer to respond on a given stimulus dimension makes them exploit whatever conscious information about that dimension exists, which is why a direct task can never be beaten by an indirect one on conscious information. — “This assumption is based on the reasonable supposition that instructions directing an observer to respond on the basis of a particular stimulus dimension should optimize the observer's use of conscious information that may be available concerning this dimension.”
mechanismApparent direct/indirect dissociations can be manufactured by measuring the two indexes on different stimulus states and displays, so the gap reflects unequal discrimination difficulty rather than unconscious perception. — “These procedures do not satisfy either Criterion 2 or Criterion 3, because the direct and indirect indexes measured discriminative responding be tween different stimulus states under different display con ditions.”
relation — “One very straightforward implication of the present results is that they provide absolutely no support for Avant and Thieman's (1985) claim that judgment of apparent visual duration constitutes a sensitive measure of unconscious perception.”
relation — “On the other hand, the present results are completely inconsistent with the negative relation between stimulus familiarity and perceived duration reported by Avant and his colleagues (Avant & Lyman, 1975; Avant et al., 1975; Avant & Thieman, 1985).”
relation — “This finding represents a successful replication of the filled duration illusion.”
differs indirect measures → measure of awareness — WHETHER THE EXHAUSTIVENESS ASSUMPTION IS MADE. Reading a null on a direct measure as an absence of awareness 'can be true ONLY IF it is also assumed that direct measures provide AN EXHAUSTIVE MEASURE of conscious perceptual experience. IF THIS ADDITIONAL EXHAUSTIVENESS ASSUMPTION IS NOT MADE, THEN THERE IS NO REASON TO EXPECT that a direct measure of perception will always indicate greater than zero perceptual sensitivity, given conscious perceptual experience' — “If this additional exhaustiveness assumption is not made, then there is no reason to expect that a direct measure of perception will always indicate greater than zero perceptual sensitivity”
differs inindirect measure → direct measures — WHETHER THE TWO ARE MEASURED UNDER COMPARABLE CONDITIONS. Avant and Thieman (1985) claimed an indirect measure was MORE sensitive than a direct one; 'CONTRARY TO THIS CLAIM, WHEN DIRECT AND INDIRECT INDEXES ARE MEASURED UNDER COMPARABLE CONDITIONS', the indirect measure is LESS sensitive. The ordering of the two instruments reverses on the comparability of the conditions, not on the instruments — “when direct and indirect in dexes are measured under comparable conditions”
replicationcontested
limitationThe empirical conclusion may not generalise beyond the specific display and stimulus parameters used. — “Although this conclusion may be limited to the display and stimulus parameters used in the present studies”
limitationBecause the indirect measure was the less sensitive one, the experiments themselves settle nothing about unconscious perception. — “Given that the sensitivity of the indirect measure in both studies was less than the sensitivity of the comparable direct measures, the present results are inconclusive with regard to unconscious perception.”
limitationThe paper cannot explain why its familiarity/duration correlation runs opposite to Avant's, and leaves that to future work. — “Clearly, additional studies are required to isolate the critical methodological factors responsible for these different patterns of results.”
limitationThe field's results are stated to be inconclusive because it has no agreed measure of its central term, not because the experiments are poor. — “Although the logic underlying this paradigm is relatively straightforward, definitive results have been elusive in the absence of any general consensus as to what consti tutes an adequate measure of awareness.”
limitationWhether the available measures are even comparable to one another is stated to be unknowable without a framework that does not exist. — “However, without a general theoretical framework and sufficient empirical data to guide the selection of measures, it is impossible to know if these different measures of conscious aware ness are either adequate or comparable”
limitationThe opposing position is rejected on the ground that the thing it depends on cannot be observed. — “The problem with Holender's po sition is that the intentions of an observer cannot be directly observed or measured.”
limitationThe paper's own diagnosis is that decades of experimental work could not settle the question because the field had no agreed measure of the thing being denied. An empirical programme is stalled by a definitional gap, and the remedy offered is not a better definition but a weaker assumption - an ordering between two sensitivities rather than a claim that either instrument is complete. — “Although the logic underlying this paradigm is relatively straightforward, definitive results have been elusive in the absence of any general consensus as to what consti tutes an adequate measure of awareness. We propose an alternative approach that involves com parisons of the relative sensitivity of comparable direct and indirect indexes of perception. The only assumption required by the proposed approach is that the sensitivity of direct discrimina tions to relevant conscious information is greater than or equal to the sensitivity of comparable indirect discriminations.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingThe paper's central deduction is that unconscious perception is established as soon as an indirect measure is absolutely more sensitive to a stimulus dimension than a comparable direct measure. — “The important implication of the above assumption is that unconscious perception is demonstrated whenever an indirect measure shows greater absolute sensitivity than does a comparable direct measure to a particular stimu lus dimension.”
findingBecause only relative sensitivity matters, the direct measure no longer has to show zero sensitivity, which removes the null-awareness requirement that had blocked the dissociation paradigm. — “To demonstrate uncon scious perception, it is sufficient to establish that the sen sitivity of the indirect measure is greater than the sensi tivity of a comparable direct measure, even if the sensitivity of the directmeasure is greater than zero.”
findingExperiment 1 replicated the filled-duration illusion: displays containing a word or a nonword were judged longer than blank displays of identical physical duration. — “As expected, filled intervals were judged as longer than blank intervals of the same stimulus duration for both word-blank pairs [t(19) = 12.0, p < .0001] and nonword-blank pairs [t(19) = 9.32, p < .00(1).”
findingAt 50 ms, words were judged longer than nonwords, i.e. familiarity and perceived duration were positively related, the opposite of the negative relation Avant and colleagues report. — “More specifically, words, in general, were judged as longer than nonwords [t(19) = 2.44, p < .05].”
findingThe duration-judgment measure is blind to lexical status at 33 ms exposure, showing no word/nonword difference at all. — “However, as suggested by an inspection of Table I, when the exposure duration was 33 msec, no significant difference was found between the proportion of "longer" responses for words and nonwords [1(19) < 1].”
findingLengthening exposure only strengthens the positive familiarity effect rather than reversing its direction. — “Considered together, the results of Experiments 1 and 2 demonstrate that increases in exposure duration simply increase the magnitude of the positive relation between stimulus familiarity and perceived duration.”
findingWith display conditions equated, the direct lexical decision was more sensitive to lexical status than the indirect duration judgment at 33 ms. — “Only the main effect of task was significant [F(l,38) = 5.10, P < .05], in dicating that performance on the lexical decision task was significantly better than performance on the duration judg ment task.”
findingThe same ordering held for stimulus detection in Experiment 4, so on both dimensions the direct measure beat the comparable indirect duration measure and Avant and Thieman's claim was not supported. — “Given the greater sensitivity of the detection measure, the present results support the same conclusion as the results of Experiment 3; namely, a direct measure provides a more sensitive index of perception than does a comparable indirect measure based on judgments of per ceived temporal duration.”
