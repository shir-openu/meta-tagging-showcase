for which uselimitation — any claim that a direct discrimination reflects intentional responding: the paper states an observer's intentions cannot be directly observed or measured— “The problem with Holender's po sition is that the intentions of an observer cannot be directly observed or measured.”

limitationThe empirical conclusion may not generalise beyond the specific display and stimulus parameters used.— “Although this conclusion may be limited to the display and stimulus parameters used in the present studies”
limitationBecause the indirect measure was the less sensitive one, the experiments themselves settle nothing about unconscious perception.— “Given that the sensitivity of the indirect measure in both studies was less than the sensitivity of the comparable direct measures, the present results are inconclusive with regard to unconscious perception.”
limitationThe paper cannot explain why its familiarity/duration correlation runs opposite to Avant's, and leaves that to future work.— “Clearly, additional studies are required to isolate the critical methodological factors responsible for these different patterns of results.”
limitationThe field's results are stated to be inconclusive because it has no agreed measure of its central term, not because the experiments are poor.— “Although the logic underlying this paradigm is relatively straightforward, definitive results have been elusive in the absence of any general consensus as to what consti tutes an…
limitationWhether the available measures are even comparable to one another is stated to be unknowable without a framework that does not exist.
limitationThe opposing position is rejected on the ground that the thing it depends on cannot be observed.
limitationThe paper's own diagnosis is that decades of experimental work could not settle the question because the field had no agreed measure of the thing being denied. An empirical programme is stalled by a definitional gap, and the remedy offered is not a better definition but a weaker assumption - an ordering between two sensitivities rather than a claim that either instrument is complete.
