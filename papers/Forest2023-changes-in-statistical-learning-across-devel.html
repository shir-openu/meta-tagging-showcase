phenomenondevelopment of statistical learning— “Discovering whether and how statistical learning changes with development is likely to inform long-standing questions about why children excel relative to adults in some areas, such as learning language16 and solving problems that require unconventional solutions17,18.”
claim-typeprediction— “Reviewing related behavioural and neural evidence, we posited that infants and young children might have broader and less-filtered access to the statistical structure present in their learning environments and that what they represent after statistical learning might shift with age.”
method — evidence— “In this Review, we first synthesize empirical work on the development of statistical learning, which indicates that it improves with development only for certain forms of input.”
mechanismfaster forgetting in infancy strips away the idiosyncratic details of individual experiences, so what accumulates is a general, fuzzy representation of the commonalities— “Thus, faster forgetting — and greater forgetting between experiences — can produce fuzzy, general representations (Fig. 5).”
mechanismimmature selective attention leaves children unable to filter their input, which widens the pool of structure available for statistical learning— “Because children cannot use attention to filter their experience as well as adults, they can potentially learn more about the parts of experience that adults exclude for being outside their narrower focus of attention.”
mechanismattention gates statistical learning in adults, so a narrower adult attentional focus means less input is eligible to be learned at any moment— “Attention can gate10,81 statistical learning in adults such that unattended aspects of an environment are not always learned.”
mechanismchildren's distributed attention makes them sample many structures at once, trading speed of learning any single structure for knowledge of relationships between structures— “In a complex environment with multiple structures, infants and children might therefore spend more time learning from multiple sources at once, slowing their learning of any given structure, but also potentially bolstering their knowledge of possible relationships among different sources of structure.”
mechanismthe early-maturing monosynaptic hippocampal pathway extracts regularities across many similar experiences and therefore yields fuzzy rather than specific representations— “The early-developing monosynaptic pathway is thought to learn regularities from many similar experiences, yielding fuzzy representations in humans and monkeys alike78.”
mechanismprior knowledge blocks learning of conflicting structure, so accumulated experience narrows what a learner can pick up— “In adults, knowledge seems to impact statistical learning: adults fail to learn structures that conflict with their previous knowledge.”
builds-on previous work arguing that children's advantage in language learning stems from their ongoing cognitive development (refs 16, 171, 172)— “Regarding language, we join previous work that suggests that children’s prowess in language learning has to do with their ongoing cognitive development16,171,172.”
contradicts definitions treating statistical learning as a singular, inherently implicit learning system (ref 185)— “Although some definitions conceptualize statistical learning as a singular and inherently implicit learning system185, we favour the conceptualization of statistical learning as supported by a set of learning processes, some of which can result in conscious access to learning20,21,34.”

findingIndirect measures such as saccade latency and neural entrainment show essentially no developmental change in statistical learning.— “Indirect measures suggest that statistical learning changes little or not at all with development.”
findingDirect measures such as two-alternative forced-choice tests do show age-related improvement, so the answer to whether statistical learning develops depends on how it is measured.— “By contrast, direct measures suggest developmental change in statistical learning…
findingThe improvement with age is domain-specific: visual and non-linguistic auditory statistical learning improve across childhood while learning of aurally presented syllables does not.
findingThe review's central synthesis is that the format of what is learned changes with age, from fuzzy in infancy to specific in early childhood to broad-plus-specific later.
findingDevelopmental studies implicate hippocampus and prefrontal regions but conspicuously fail to implicate the sensory cortices and basal ganglia that dominate adult statistical learning findings.
findingThe developmental neuroimaging evidence base is extremely thin, resting on a single infant functional imaging study.
