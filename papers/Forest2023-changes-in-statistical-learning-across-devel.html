phenomenondevelopment of statistical learning
claim-typeprediction
mechanismfaster forgetting in infancy strips away the idiosyncratic details of individual experiences, so what accumulates is a general, fuzzy representation of the commonalities — “Thus, faster forgetting — and greater forgetting between experiences — can produce fuzzy, general representations (Fig. 5).”
mechanismimmature selective attention leaves children unable to filter their input, which widens the pool of structure available for statistical learning — “Because children cannot use attention to filter their experience as well as adults, they can potentially learn more about the parts of experience that adults exclude for being outside their narrower focus of attention.”
mechanismattention gates statistical learning in adults, so a narrower adult attentional focus means less input is eligible to be learned at any moment — “Attention can gate10,81 statistical learning in adults such that unattended aspects of an environment are not always learned.”
mechanismchildren's distributed attention makes them sample many structures at once, trading speed of learning any single structure for knowledge of relationships between structures — “In a complex environment with multiple structures, infants and children might therefore spend more time learning from multiple sources at once, slowing their learning of any given structure, but also potentially bolstering their knowledge of possible relationships among different sources of structure.”
mechanismthe early-maturing monosynaptic hippocampal pathway extracts regularities across many similar experiences and therefore yields fuzzy rather than specific representations — “The early-developing monosynaptic pathway is thought to learn regularities from many similar experiences, yielding fuzzy representations in humans and monkeys alike78.”
mechanismprior knowledge blocks learning of conflicting structure, so accumulated experience narrows what a learner can pick up — “In adults, knowledge seems to impact statistical learning: adults fail to learn structures that conflict with their previous knowledge.”
relation — “Regarding language, we join previous work that suggests that children’s prowess in language learning has to do with their ongoing cognitive development16,171,172.”
relation — “Although some definitions conceptualize statistical learning as a singular and inherently implicit learning system185, we favour the conceptualization of statistical learning as supported by a set of learning processes, some of which can result in conscious access to learning20,21,34.”
replicationuntested
limitationThe paper's proposed developmental account is speculative and not yet tested. — “Although this discussion builds on previous observations about developmental changes in statistical learning, it is largely speculative and remains to be tested.”
limitationThe developmental neuroimaging base is too thin to support conclusions about shifting neural substrates. — “However, a confident appraisal of potential developmental shifts in the neural structures that are involved in statistical learning cannot be made from just two studies that have measured the function of specific brain regions.”
limitationThe developmental timelines invoked come from animal histology and general memory research, not from human statistical-learning data. — “Much of the data on these exact timelines come from histological studies in animals and behavioural human memory development research, so these possibilities for statistical learning require confirmation in human infants.”
limitationThe review adopts one of two competing conceptualizations of its subject and says which it rejects. — “Although some definitions conceptualize statistical learning as a singular and inherently implicit learning system185, we favour the conceptualization of statistical learning as supported by a set of learning processes”
limitationA category boundary is drawn by how the excluded thing is typically assessed. — “We exclude motor-based skill learning (such as serial reaction time tasks) from statistical learning, because it is typically assessed in tasks that provide external feedback”
limitationThe quantity being compared across ages may itself differ by age. — “However, this availability does not necessarily mean that statistical learning is age-invariant15. Indeed, statistical learning likely changes in quality with age, operating over different sources of data and producing different representations”
Content tags
content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingIndirect measures such as saccade latency and neural entrainment show essentially no developmental change in statistical learning. — “Indirect measures suggest that statistical learning changes little or not at all with development.”
findingDirect measures such as two-alternative forced-choice tests do show age-related improvement, so the answer to whether statistical learning develops depends on how it is measured. — “By contrast, direct measures suggest developmental change in statistical learning ability.”
findingThe improvement with age is domain-specific: visual and non-linguistic auditory statistical learning improve across childhood while learning of aurally presented syllables does not. — “A comparison of the developmental trajectories of visual and aural-syllable statistical learning in separate groups of children aged from 5 to 12 years found age-related improvement in visual but not aural-syllable statistical learning performance23.”
findingThe review's central synthesis is that the format of what is learned changes with age, from fuzzy in infancy to specific in early childhood to broad-plus-specific later. — “Taken together, these data provide preliminary support for the idea that representations that are formed after statistical learning shift with development: being fuzzy in infancy, highly specific in early childhood, and then increasingly broad in later childhood and adulthood.”
findingDevelopmental studies implicate hippocampus and prefrontal regions but conspicuously fail to implicate the sensory cortices and basal ganglia that dominate adult statistical learning findings. — “Conspicuously missing from the list of regions underpinning statistical learning during infancy and childhood are sensory cortices and, often, the basal ganglia.”
findingThe developmental neuroimaging evidence base is extremely thin, resting on a single infant functional imaging study. — “There is just one functional neuroimaging study in infants, which implicates the hippocampus and medial prefrontal cortex in visual statistical learning52.”
findingOverall the review concludes that the two domains follow different trajectories and that this divergence appears only on direct measures. — “Our review of the development of statistical learning revealed different developmental trajectories in linguistic and non-linguistic domains, with non-linguistic auditory and visual statistical learning improving across childhood whereas linguistic (specifically aurally presented syllables) learning does not change.”
quantity0–24 months / 2–7 years / 7–10 years / 18–35 years — the four developmental brackets the review uses to organize the literature — “In this Review, we consider statistical learning across a broad range of development, including infants (0–24 months old), young children (2–7 years old), older children (7–10 years old), and young adults (typically 18–35 years old).”
quantityone of four — how many developmental statistical learning studies found basal ganglia involvement — “has been observed in only one50 of four statistical learning studies with infants or children31,45,52”
quantity18–24 months — estimated age at which the trisynaptic hippocampal pathway supporting specific memories emerges in humans — “The trisynaptic pathway of the hippocampus — which yields rapid encoding of specific memories — is not thought to emerge in humans until around 18–24 months of age, in the shift from infancy to young childhood143,145.”
quantity5–7 vs 8–9 years — the age split at which children move from remembering only specific transitions to remembering both broad groups and specific transitions — “Children 5–7 years old showed memory for the specific transitions but not the broad groups, whereas 8–9 year olds and adults remembered both broad and specific information165.”
quantity8-month-old — age at which infants were shown to represent statistical input as chunks rather than as specific transitional probabilities — “For instance, 8-month-old infants represent their statistical experiences in ‘chunks’ (general groupings), instead of as specific transitional probabilities between items133 as adults have been shown to do163.”
definitionstatistical learning — “We define statistical learning as the learning of structure — for example, frequency, probabilities, or mutual information — from repeated exposure to environmental statistics19–21 that occurs with no explicit instruction or external feedback (Box 1).”
definitionfuzzy representations — “The first type of general representation might emerge in infancy and could be due to neural maturation or forgetting130; we refer to the resulting representations as fuzzy.”
definitionbroad representations — “The second type of general representation might emerge later in childhood123,131,132, reflect commonalties across events and experiences, and exist alongside specific information; we refer to the resulting representations as broad.”
definitionneural entrainment — “In another indirect measure known as ‘neural entrainment’, largescale electrical oscillations in the brain are measured to determine their synchrony to the rhythm of structured stimuli.”
definitionindirect measures of statistical learning — “Learning on indirect measures is inferred without overt report from the participants33 (Fig. 2b) and can be used to measure learning during the learning process itself.”
