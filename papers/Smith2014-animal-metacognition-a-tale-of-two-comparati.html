mechanismWhen deferred feedback camouflages the task's reinforcement structure, the animal builds its own subjective decisional framework and responds uncertain to whatever is near its own subjective breakpoint, not the task's objective one — “Absent access to the task’s associative structure, the animal constructed his own subjective decisional framework based on subjective difficulty, or uncertainty, or even metacognition.”
mechanismUncertainty responses draw on executive attention and working memory, which is why occupying those resources with a concurrent task selectively degrades them while leaving stimulus-driven perceptual classification intact — “This dissociation suggests that the uncertainty response is a higher-level, decisional response that is particularly dependent on working memory and attentional resources.”

limitationThe authors concede that not all of the models they criticise have had a fair opportunity to address the newest phenomenon. — “There has not been time to address it yet.”
limitationThey hedge their own positive interpretation: information-seeking need not imply full conscious metacognition. — “Of course one need not attribute full, conscious, self-imbued metacognition to macaques even if one adopts this information-seeking hypothesis.”
limitationThey acknowledge they cannot presently settle whether one criticised model succeeds in practice. — “It is presently difficult to judge the model’s pragmatic success.”
limitationThe empirical results themselves are not in dispute - the whole disagreement is over interpretation — “These empirical results are not disputed.”
limitationThe animals report nothing, so the human evidence for conscious uncertainty has no counterpart in the animal case — “Macaques have also shown this pattern repeatedly, though they report nothing.”
limitationThe paper's own positive claim is hedged to possibly-metacognitive rather than metacognitive — “The research clearly suggests that what animals do in some tasks is higher-level and cognitive, deliberate and decisional, and possibly metacognitive and conscious.”
limitationThe critique is of a specific class of models, which the authors accept would be a legitimate goal if it could be made to work — “As part of this consideration, some associative modelers have attempted to describe animals’ “metacognitive” performances in low-level, associative terms—an important goal if achievable.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingRe-running Le Pelley's associative-learning model shows it can only shift the uncertainty-response curve leftward by making the uncertainty response dominate the whole stimulus range, so it never reproduces the macaque's actual pattern. — “Consequently, Le Pelley’s model never produces anything like the UR data pattern that Smith et al. observed ( Figure 1C ).”
findingAt its best fit the associative model mispredicts each of 120 data points by .10 on average. — “The associative model at best (i.e., at the minimum AAD) averaged a misprediction of .10 for each of 120 data points.”
findingA non-associative uncertainty model fits the same data more than three times more closely, with an AAD of .03. — “The AAD for that model fit to the same data is only .03, a far closer fit than achieved by the associative-learning model.”
findingThe better-fitting cognitive model is also the more parsimonious one, using 3 free parameters against the associative model's 5. — “Not only that, but the cognitive model needs only 3 parameters to fit the data, not the 5 that Le Pelley uses.”
findingThe associative model achieves its best fit precisely by predicting a symmetric uncertainty curve centred on the task's true breakpoint, i.e. by abandoning the displaced Level-14 centring that is the result needing explanation. — “Thus, the model fits best when it simply gives up predicting the crucial result that the macaque showed: an asymmetrical centering of the UR at Level 14, in sharp disalignment with the task’s associative structure.”
findingCapuchin monkeys almost never used the uncertainty response despite receiving immediate trial-by-trial feedback, which is the condition most favourable to associative learning. — “Strikingly, capuchin monkeys essentially did not use the UR ( Figure 3A ), even though in this experiment the capuchin monkeys received immediate feedback following every trial.”
findingThe same capuchins learned the Middle response to the same intermediate stimuli easily, so their failure was specific to the uncertainty response and cannot be an associative-learning deficit. — “The Sparse-Uncertain-Dense task, on the associative hypothesis, should have let capuchin monkeys associate URs to difficult discriminative stimuli.”
findingA concurrent memory load selectively suppressed macaques' uncertainty responses while leaving their Sparse, Middle and Dense perceptual responses largely intact. — “Concurrent tasks disrupted macaques’ URs far more than their Sparse, Middle, or Dense perceptual responses.”
findingPigeons, which are excellent associative learners, show no memory-monitoring capacity in the paradigms where macaques do. — “They confirmed that pigeons do not express any memory-monitoring capacity.”
findingPigeons' uncertainty-response rate stays flat across retention intervals and they gain nothing from choosing which memory tests to take. — “Their UR curve stays flat over the range of retention intervals—they do not make more URs when given longer to forget (though they should because they remember less after longer delays).”
findingTo reproduce the serial-position results the associative model would need nine free parameters, a level of flexibility the authors say they have never seen accepted as plausible. — “Notice also that on adding these parameters the associative model in Le Pelley’s case would have nine free parameters (i.e., response variation, memory variation for foils, memory variation for samples, the primacy parameter, the recency parameter, reward value for errors, reward values for corrects, reward values for bailouts, and a scaling parameter).”
findingLe Pelley's simulation of Hampton's task set the uncertainty response's reward value to zero although Hampton always rewarded it with a food pellet, so the published simulations misdescribe the method they claim to model. — “Nonetheless, the published simulations must be discounted because they misstate a crucial aspect of Hampton’s methodology.”
findingHampton's blank-trial control shows macaques were reading memory-trace strength rather than the length of the retention interval, since with delay held constant they declined blank trials far more often. — “Now, with delay interval controlled, animals made far more URs on blank trials.”
findingA single memory-monitoring model fitted to two separate studies recovers the same criterion of memory strength in both, a convergence the associative models cannot produce. — “They found that monkeys defended the same criterion of memory strength in both studies, a striking convergence across laboratories, animals, and methodologies.”
findingThe authors argue that a strict associative reading of comparative data exaggerates the distance between human and animal minds and strips animal models of value for studying human metacognition. — “Those models reflect an ultimately strict adherence to Morgan’s (1906) Canon, which even Morgan did not intend and which many comparative-cognition researchers do not accept now.”
findinganimals show uncertainty responses where no cuing stimulus is present, on first trials, and without feedback — “They can respond uncertain absent any cuing stimuli ( Hampton, 2001 ), on the first trial of novel tasks ( Washburn, Smith, & Shields, 2006 ), and even when there are no immediate feedback or reinforcement signals so that the processes of conditioning and association are ruled out ( Smith, Beran, Redford, & Washburn, 2006 ).”
findingchimpanzees checked the container before naming only on trials where they had not seen its contents — “Chimpanzees were significantly more likely to visit the food container first—before choosing a lexigram—on trials in which they did not know the container’s contents.”
findingthe empirical pattern is agreed by both sides; only its interpretation is disputed — “These empirical results are not disputed.”
findinga broad enough associative label can no longer separate performances that differ psychologically — “Unfortunately, then the associative label becomes meaningless, because one can no longer make distinctions among performances that may be highly distinctive theoretically and psychologically.”
findingthe same formal machinery can model fully conscious metacognitive states, so the formalism decides nothing — “Moreover, this claim implies that any kind of information processing can be lumped under the construct associative, which makes that term theoretically useless.”
