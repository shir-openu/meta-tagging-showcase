phenomenonanimal metacognition (uncertainty monitoring)— “A growing literature considers whether animals have capacities that are akin to human metacognition (i. e., humans’ capacity to monitor their states of uncertainty and knowing).”
claim-typecritique— “The associative descriptions in the animal-metacognition literature fail to encompass important phenomena.”
method — evidence— “The present article summarizes the empirical and theoretical situation regarding these associative models and descriptions of animal-metacognition phenomena.”

mechanismWhen deferred feedback camouflages the task's reinforcement structure, the animal builds its own subjective decisional framework and responds uncertain to whatever is near its own subjective breakpoint, not the task's objective one— “Absent access to the task’s associative structure, the animal constructed his own subjective decisional framework based on subjective difficulty, or uncertainty, or even metacognition.”
mechanismUncertainty responses draw on executive attention and working memory, which is why occupying those resources with a concurrent task selectively degrades them while leaving stimulus-driven perceptual classification intact— “This dissociation suggests that the uncertainty response is a higher-level, decisional response that is particularly dependent on working memory and attentional resources.”
mechanismIn Hampton's task no memory-relevant stimulus is present at choice time, so the animal must itself initiate and restrict a search of memory for a strong trace — an operation with no associative cue to react to— “What the animal must do is use those response icons to cue a memory search for a hot or recent trace.”
mechanismCasting every performance as a register of activation strengths updated by reinforcement artificially equates psychologically different performances, which is what empties the label 'associative' of content— “Indeed, forcing all performances onto this structure artificially equates them—really, this is what you want to do and what you have to do if you want to label them all associative.”
mechanismThe uncertainty response is designed to carry no reward of its own, so choosing it cannot be explained by direct reinforcement of that response— “It would never bring any direct reward or other consequence.”
mechanismIn the memory task the response icons carry no trace-strength information, so there is nothing on the screen for an association to attach to - the animal must initiate its own search— “In this case, there are no memory-relevant stimuli on the scene. There is nothing to cue the animal’s response to some stimulus’s trace hotness.”
mechanismThe search must also be restricted to the right memory locations, and nothing in the display can tell the animal how to do that— “And nothing on the response screen can cue the macaque associatively to conduct this search of memory or to restrict that search to the required set of memory locations, or on how to judge when a remembered item has been retrieved or has not been retrieved.”
mechanismThe associative models work by mapping every performance onto one abstract register of activations updated by reinforcement, which is precisely what makes them unable to tell performances apart— “Indeed, forcing all performances onto this structure artificially equates them—really, this is what you want to do and what you have to do if you want to label them all associative.”
mechanismThe reductio: the same formalism works with a register of fully conscious metacognitive states updated by reinforcement, which shows the formalism settles nothing— “In this way, we could claim mathematically and formally that animals learn associatively through reinforcement to use fully conscious metacognitive states. This claim is useless.”
mechanismThe all-or-none framing is itself the error: uncertainty monitoring may exist in graded stages without the whole human package— “It can acknowledge that a capacity like metacognition may not be all or none, and that animals might have a basic uncertainty-monitoring system without having bought all the apps of human metacognition, like consciousness, self-awareness, and even self-loathing from cognitive failure and indecision.”
contradicts Le Pelley (2012), associative-learning model of animal metacognition— “This poor fit by itself sharply undermines the descriptive value of Le Pelley’s associative-learning model in this case.”
contradicts Jozefowiez et al. (2009a)— “For the same reason, Jozefowiez et al. (2009a) are incorrect when they claim that the underlying processes are exactly the same whether a triggering stimulus is on the screen or the animal must initiate its own targeted search for active memory traces.”
