content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingRight rostrolateral prefrontal cortex met all three of the paper's criteria for a role in metacognition: greater activity during self-report, correlation with confidence, and prediction of metacognitive ability across people. — “Right rlPFC showed greater activity during self-report compared to a matched control condition, activity in rlPFC correlated with reported confidence, and the strength of the relationship between activity and confidence predicted metacognitive ability across individuals.”
findingConfidence-related activity was exclusively negative: dACC, right posterior parietal cortex and bilateral rlPFC scaled inversely with reported confidence and no region scaled positively with it. — “Negative correlations with confidence were seen in dACC, right posterior parietal cortex (R-PPC), and bilateral rlPFC (all clusters p < 0.05, corrected for multiple comparisons within mask volume; Fig. 4 A and Table 2 ). No activity was found to correlate positively with reported confidence.”
findingMetacognitive ability was statistically independent of perceptual task performance, replicating an earlier dissociation. — “A roc was independent of performance as measured by perceptual threshold ( r = −0.063; p = 0.78) and Report trial type 1 d ′ ( r = 0.19, p = 0.38), replicating previous findings of a separation between task performance and metacognitive ability”
findingSimply moving the confidence cursor in the control (Follow) condition did not modulate activity in the confidence-sensitive regions, showing the effect is specific to genuine self-report. — “This result indicates that simply using the confidence scale (in the Follow condition) is not sufficient to modulate activity in R-PPC, dACC, and rlPFC.”
findingFunctional connectivity of right rlPFC with contralateral prefrontal cortex and occipital cortex rose during metacognitive report relative to the control condition. — “We found significant increases in connectivity with contralateral PFC and occipital cortex ( p < 0.05, corrected for multiple comparisons; Table 3 ).”
findingThe confidence effect in right rlPFC was not an artefact of reaction time, since it survived orthogonalizing confidence with respect to RT. — “The effect of reported confidence remained after controlling for the variance in activity accounted for by RT ( Fig. 6 ; t (22) = 2.35, p = 0.028).”
findingThe individual-differences effect was lateralized: left rlPFC showed no relationship between confidence-related activity and metacognitive ability. — “No significant correlation was found in left rlPFC ( r = 0.024, p = 0.91).”
findingright rlPFC activity was greater during confidence report than in the yoked control — “We show that activity in right rostrolateral prefrontal cortex (rlPFC) satisfies three constraints for a role in metacognitive aspects of decision-making. Right rlPFC showed greater activity during self-report compared to a matched control condition, activity in this region correlated with reported confidence, and the strength of the relationship between activity and confidence predicted metacognitive ability across individuals.”
findingthe strength of the confidence-activity relation in right rlPFC predicted metacognitive ability across people, r = -0.47 — “Indeed, in right rostrolateral PFC we found a significant negative correlation between the confidence beta and A roc ( r = −0.47, p = 0.025; Fig. 5 ), which remained after controlling for Report trial type 1 d”
findingno such correlation in left rlPFC, r = 0.024 — “No significant correlation was found in left rlPFC ( r = 0.024, p = 0.91).”
findingconfidence-related activity was absent in the Follow condition, so using the scale is not sufficient to produce it — “Follow confidence betas did not significantly differ from zero in these regions (one-sampled t tests, t (22) < 1.6; p > 0.1). This result indicates that simply using the confidence scale (in the Follow condition) is not sufficient to modulate activity in R-PPC, dACC, and rlPFC. Instead, this pattern of activity is consistent with a role for these regions in representing confidence during the Report condition.”
findingactivity did not differentiate correct from incorrect trials, so it is not an error signal — “Activity did not differentiate between error and correct trials per se ( Fig. 6 ; t (22) = 1.45; p = 0.16). Furthermore, there was no significant difference between confidence betas on correct compared to incorrect trials ( t (22) = 0.18; p = 0.86) and confidence was significantly correlated with activity on both trial types (one-tailed t test against zero; both p < 0.05).”
findingthe confidence effect survived orthogonalising against reaction time — “Model 3 orthogonalized confidence with respect to reaction time to assess whether reaction time could account for the effect we observe in rlPFC. The effect of reported confidence remained after controlling for the variance in activity accounted for by RT ( Fig. 6 ; t (22) = 2.35, p = 0.028).”
findingconnectivity between right rlPFC and contralateral PFC and occipital cortex rose during report — “This analysis isolated brain regions that selectively increased in connectivity with right rlPFC during Report compared to Follow trials ( Fig. 5 ). We found significant increases in connectivity with contralateral PFC and occipital cortex ( p < 0.05, corrected for multiple comparisons; Table 3 ).”
findingmetacognitive ability did not correlate with fluid intelligence — “We note that in the present study, metacognitive ability did not significantly correlate with fluid intelligence ( g ).”
