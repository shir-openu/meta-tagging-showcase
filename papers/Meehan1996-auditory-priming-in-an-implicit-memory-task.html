phenomenonauditory repetition priming— “Auditory priming occurs when a response to a repeated spoken word is facilitated without intentional recollection (Church & Schacter, 1994).”
claim-typemechanism— “In conclusion, the results of this study demonstrate that the signifi cance ofhuman voice in implicit memory is dependent on both the level of processing required by the task and the acoustic environment.”
method — evidence— “To emphasize the subword level, the present study employed a phoneme-monitoring task at both encoding (study) and retrieval (test).”
mechanismProcessing an increasingly varied and less familiar set of voices consumes additional perceptual operations, and those extra operations break the ability of voice-plus-word repetition to speed the response.— “The additional operations required to process an increas ingly varied and progressively less familiar acoustic en vironment (Mul1enix & Pisoni, 1990) disrupted the abil ity of voice and word repetition to sustain priming.”
mechanismWhen encoding must handle different and progressively less familiar voices, repetition of voice and word stops being sufficient to support priming.— “when the encoding of acoustic information required the processing of different and progressively less familiar voices, voice and word repetition were not sufficient to support priming.”
extends Schacter & Church (1992)— “The absence of an effect ofword frequency on priming strengthened the idea that implicit memory for spoken words, at the level of processing entailed by pho neme monitoring, is based on surface processing rather than word-level processing (see Schacter & Church, 1992, for additional qualifications).”
builds-on Mullenix & Pisoni (1990)— “The additional operations required to process an increas ingly varied and progressively less familiar acoustic en vironment (Mul1enix & Pisoni, 1990) disrupted the abil ity of voice and word repetition to sustain priming.”
senseTHE CONCLUSION IS ABOUT THE INSTRUMENT, NOT ABOUT MEMORY: `the significance of voice in implicit memory is DEPENDENT ON THE LEVEL OF PROCESSING REQUIRED BY THE TASK and the acoustic environment.` Whether voice matters to implicit memory has no answer on its own - it has an answer per task. **So two studies of `auditory priming` that used different tasks are not measuring the same thing**, and a corpus that pools their effect sizes is pooling answers to different questions. The paper was designed to demonstrate exactly this: it swapped the task and the role of voice changed— “This study demonstrates that the significance of voice in implicit memory is dependent on the level of processing required by the task and the acoustic environment”
senseTHE PRIOR LITERATURE ALREADY DISAGREED WITH ITSELF, BY TASK, AND THIS PAPER NAMES IT: `a voice change between a spoken word and its repetition has been shown to have a DETRIMENTAL EFFECT on auditory priming in STEM COMPLETION and WORD IDENTIFICATION ... BUT NOT IN LEXICAL DECISION.` Three tasks, all called auditory priming, two showing an effect of voice and one not. That is the observation the whole study is built on - not a puzzle to be resolved but a clue about what the tasks measure— “In fact, a voice change between a spoken word and its repetition has been shown to have a detrimental effect on auditory priming in stem completion and word identification (Church & Schacter, 1994; Goldinger, 1992; Schacter & Church, 1992), but not in lexical decision”
senseVOICE REPETITION TURNS OUT TO BE NECESSARY, NOT MERELY HELPFUL: `Voice changes between study and test, which eliminated acoustic repetition, ALSO ABOLISHED PRIMING.` Word repetition was intact in that condition - the same words came back - and priming did not. At the level this task works on, repeating the word is not enough; the voice has to repeat too, which is a much stronger claim than voice being one contributor among several— “Voice changes between study and test, which eliminated acoustic repetition, also abolished priming”
senseTHE DOSE-RESPONSE HAS A CROSSING VARIABLE, WHICH IS WHY THE DESIGN NEEDED A SECOND ROUND. Priming appeared `only in the one- and two-speaker conditions` and vanished at three and four - but voice FAMILIARITY falls as the number of speakers rises, so the two are confounded by construction. When familiarity was equated by pre-training, priming came back at three voices. **`Number of speakers` is therefore not the variable it appears to be**, and the paper takes it apart rather than reporting the monotone result it could have
senseAND THE TASK WAS CHOSEN TO DEFEAT A RESPONSE BIAS, WHICH IS AN ARGUMENT ABOUT MEASUREMENT RATHER THAN ABOUT MEMORY. The introduction's charge against the standard tasks is that requiring participants to report whole words `renders priming dependent on the activation of whole words while focusing performance away from the acoustic component` - so the instrument was producing the word-level answer it kept finding. Phoneme monitoring forces attention below the word, and the result changes. A methodological objection of the same shape as Holcomb2005's against Kiefer, in a different literature
