phenomenondreaming as conscious experience during sleep
claim-typeexistence
mechanismConscious experience during sleep requires local activation (loss of low-frequency, bistable mode) of the posterior hot zone, which is why dreaming can occur in NREM and REM alike regardless of the EEG in the rest of cortex — “The present results resolve these paradoxes by showing that in both REM and NREM sleep, dreaming, and more generally, conscious experience during sleep, requires a localized activation of a posterior hot zone, irrespective of the EEG in the rest of the cortex.”
mechanismLow-frequency (1-4 Hz) activity indexes neuronal down-states and bistability, which block stable causal interaction between cortical areas and so abolish experience — “EEG slow frequencies between 1 and 4 Hz during sleep are associated with neuronal down-states and bistability 12, which prevent the emergence of stable causal interactions among cortical areas 13, and have been linked to the loss of consciousness 14,15.”
mechanismOnce the hot zone is out of its low-frequency mode, which specific neuronal groups fire strongly determines which contents are experienced, as in waking perception — “A plausible interpretation of this finding is that, as long as the posterior hot zone is not in a low-frequency mode that prevents experiences, the specific contents of experiences when dreaming are dictated by which specific groups of neurons fire strongly and which do not, just as in wake.”
relation — “Our results with high-density EEG extend these findings beyond falling asleep to periods of consolidated sleep, providing further evidence that dream reports reflect conscious experience during sleep rather than confabulations upon awakening 44,45.”
relation — “By exploiting a within state, no stimulus, no task paradigm, our findings indicate that the neural correlate of consciousness, rather than involving the broad lateral fronto-parietal network, is restricted to a posterior hot zone.”
differs inrelation — THE AXIS IS THE PARADIGM, AND IT IS NAMED IN THE SAME SENTENCE AS THE CONCLUSION. 'BY EXPLOITING A WITHIN STATE, NO STIMULUS, NO TASK PARADIGM, our findings indicate that the neural correlate of consciousness, RATHER THAN INVOLVING THE BROAD LATERAL FRONTO-PARIETAL NETWORK, IS RESTRICTED TO A POSTERIOR HOT ZONE.' The fronto-parietal result is not called wrong - it is attributed to paradigms that require a task and a report, which this one removes. A bare `contradicts` records a dispute about ANATOMY; the parameter shows it is a dispute about METHOD. Q-448. — “By exploiting a within state, no stimulus, no task paradigm”
replicationuntested
limitationThe hot zone was identified by averaging across many awakenings and subjects, so individual episodes of experience may involve additional regions. — “Of course, since the posterior hot zone was highlighted by averaging EEG recordings preceding many awakenings from many subjects, the full neural correlates of experiences ins sleep could occasionally include additional brain regions.”
limitationParts of the posterior hot zone may support unconscious dream-generating processes rather than experience itself. — “Conversely, we cannot rule out that the posterior hot zone may include, besides brain regions supporting experiences, areas that might support unconscious aspects of dream generation.”
limitationSource modelling does not fully remove volume conduction, which inflates absolute low-frequency power values. — “Indeed, source modeling strongly reduces but does not completely eliminate the effects of volume conduction.”
limitationThe behavioural state does not settle whether there was an experience, and the paper says so of both the traditional EEG features and the neuroimaging correlates. — “Thus, whether one experiences something or not during sleep cannot be determined simply by assessing one’s behavioral state based on traditional EEG features or neuroimaging correlates”
limitationThe identification the field worked with is reported as failing in both directions: dreams are absent from some REM awakenings and present in NREM sleep. — “Conversely, in a small but consistent minority of cases, subjects deny having had any experience when awakened from REM sleep.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingIn both NREM and REM sleep, reporting a dream on awakening was preceded by lower low-frequency (1-4 Hz) EEG power in a localised posterior parieto-occipital region, while reporting no experience was preceded by higher low-frequency power there. — “In both NREM and REM sleep, the presence of dreaming was associated with a local decrease in low-frequency activity in posterior cortical regions.”
findingReports of having experienced something without recalling its content also showed the posterior low-frequency decrease relative to no-experience reports, so the posterior marker tracks having an experience rather than remembering it. — “Thus, decreased low-frequency power in this posterior cortical zone was correlated with the occurrence of dreaming experiences, irrespective of the ability to remember the contents of the experience.”
findingAwakenings triggered in real time by a bispectral low/high-frequency threshold over the posterior hot zone predicted the presence or absence of dreaming in NREM sleep with 87% overall accuracy (91.6% for dreaming predictions, 80.7% for no-experience predictions). — “After removal of trials containing artifact (see Online Methods), there remained 36 awakenings initiated by the algorithm with a DE prediction, out of which 33 were accurate (DE mean accuracy = 91.6%, p=0.00001), and 26 awakenings with a NE prediction, out of which 21 were accurate (NE mean accuracy = 80.7%, p=0.0003); total prediction accuracy across all states was 87% (Fig. 5B).”
findingHigh-frequency activity during REM dreams was higher in frontal regions when the dream was rated thought-like and higher in parietal, occipital and temporal regions when it was rated perceptual, an anterior-posterior gradient mirroring wakefulness. — “We found that high-frequency activity correlated with the thinking dimension in frontal brain regions and with the perceiving dimension in parietal, occipital and temporal areas (r>0.14, Fig. 4A).”
findingDreams containing faces were accompanied by increased high-frequency activity in a temporo-occipital region matching the fusiform face area, confirmed by a right-FFA region-of-interest test. — “As shown in Fig. 4B, DEs containing faces, compared to DEs without faces, were associated with increased high-frequency activity in a circumscribed temporo-occipital region that closely matched the fusiform face area (FFA).”
findingDreams with a definite spatial setting showed increased high-frequency activity in right posterior parietal cortex, an area supporting spatial perception and visuo-spatial attention in wakefulness. — “We found that DEs with a definite spatial setting were associated with increased high-frequency activity in the right posterior parietal cortex (rPPC) (Fig. 4C), an area involved in spatial perception and visuo-spatial attention 20, whose lesion can cause spatial neglect.”
findingDreams containing speech showed increased high-frequency activity in a left posterior temporal region corresponding to Wernicke's area. — “Finally, as shown in Fig. 4C, DEs containing speech were associated with increased high frequency activity over a left posterior temporal region corresponding to Wernicke’s area, as confirmed by a ROI analysis 24 (p=0.048, paired one-tailed t-test).”
findingThe results showed no reliable hemispheric asymmetry, apart from a borderline right-sided effect for the high-frequency dreaming-versus-no-experience contrast in REM sleep. — “We did not find significant interactions between the type of report and hemisphere, except for a barely significant right-sided lateralization (p= 0.046) for the high-frequency power contrast between DE and NE in REM sleep.”
findingThe low- and high-frequency effects on dreaming survived control for participant age, with a significant main effect of report type and no age effect. — “A significant main effect of report type (DE, NE) was confirmed for both low-frequency (p=0.003) and high-frequency activity (p=0.005), while no significant effects of age emerged (p > 0.05).”
findingBecause the correlate of experience was confined to a posterior region rather than a lateral fronto-parietal network, the authors conclude the neural correlate of consciousness is posterior rather than fronto-parietal. — “By exploiting a within state, no stimulus, no task paradigm, our findings indicate that the neural correlate of consciousness, rather than involving the broad lateral fronto-parietal network, is restricted to a posterior hot zone.”
quantity87% — Overall accuracy of real-time prediction of the presence or absence of dreaming in NREM sleep from posterior hot-zone EEG — “After removal of trials containing artifact (see Online Methods), there remained 36 awakenings initiated by the algorithm with a DE prediction, out of which 33 were accurate (DE mean accuracy = 91.6%, p=0.00001), and 26 awakenings with a NE prediction, out of which 21 were accurate (NE mean accuracy = 80.7%, p=0.0003); total prediction accuracy across all states was 87% (Fig. 5B).”
quantityN=32, 233 awakenings; N=7, 815 awakenings — Sample sizes for experiment 1 (many naive subjects, few awakenings each) and experiment 2 (few trained subjects, many awakenings each) — “In experiment 1 we studied a large group of subjects who underwent few awakenings (N=32, 233 awakenings), while in experiment 2, we investigated a smaller group of trained subjects who underwent many awakenings (N=7, 815 awakenings) (see Supplementary table S2).”
quantity84 — Total number of algorithm-triggered awakenings in the real-time prediction experiment — “In total, participants were awakened 84 times (mean: 12.0 ± 3.4; Supplementary table S3).”
quantityp=0.023 — Region-of-interest test showing higher high-frequency activity in the right fusiform face area for dreams containing faces — “A region of interest (ROI) analysis19 confirmed that high-frequency activity in the FFA on the right side was significantly higher for DEs containing faces (p=0.023, paired one-tailed t-test) compared to DEs without faces, consistent with the specialization of this area in the perception of faces during wakefulness 19.”
quantityr>0.14 — Correlation threshold at which high-frequency power tracked the thinking-versus-perceiving rating of REM dreams — “We found that high-frequency activity correlated with the thinking dimension in frontal brain regions and with the perceiving dimension in parietal, occipital and temporal areas (r>0.14, Fig. 4A).”
quantityp=0.003 / p=0.005 — Main effect of report type on low-frequency and high-frequency activity after controlling for age — “A significant main effect of report type (DE, NE) was confirmed for both low-frequency (p=0.003) and high-frequency activity (p=0.005), while no significant effects of age emerged (p > 0.05).”
quantityp= 0.046 — The single borderline hemispheric lateralization effect, for high-frequency DE-vs-NE in REM sleep — “We did not find significant interactions between the type of report and hemisphere, except for a barely significant right-sided lateralization (p= 0.046) for the high-frequency power contrast between DE and NE in REM sleep.”
variabletype of report (DE, DEWR, NE) (independent) — “we computed a repeated-measure-ANOVA with hemisphere and type of report (DE, DEWR, NDE) as within-subject factors for all the contrasts”
variablelow-frequency (1-4 Hz) source-level power (dependent) — “By computing power spectral density at the source level within the 1-4 Hz frequency band we found that reports of DE, compared to reports of NE, were preceded by decreased low-frequency power.”
variablehigh-frequency (20-50 Hz) band power (dependent) — “We then investigated differences in high-frequency band power (20-50 Hz), since high EEG frequencies often reflect high rates of neuronal firing 17,18 and may thus indicate brain regions that show increased activity.”
variablehemisphere (left vs right) (moderator) — “To test whether our results were characterized by a significant hemispheric lateralization, we computed a repeated-measure-ANOVA with hemisphere and type of report (DE, DEWR, NDE) as within-subject factors for all the contrasts (See ‘Online Methods’ and Supplementary table 5).”
variableparticipant age (covariate) — “Given the age variability in our sample, an additional ROI-based repeated measures ANCOVA was performed in the significant clusters using this parameter as nuisance covariate.”
variabletiming of the experimenter-triggered awakening (bispectral threshold over the posterior hot zone) (independent) — “On the experimental (second and third) nights, participants were awoken from NREM (sleep stage 2 or 3) when their neural activity surpassed a bispectral threshold in lowand highfrequency band power from an electrode cluster centered over the posterior hot zone.”
variablesleep stage held to N2 for the main low-frequency contrast (control) — “and on N2 sleep, since the number of DE and NE reports in this stage were well balanced.”
definitiondreaming experience (DE), dreaming experience without recall of content (DEWR), no experience (NE) — “participants were awakened throughout the night and were asked to report whether, just before the awakening, they had been experiencing anything (dreaming experience, DE), experiencing something but could not remember the content (dreaming experience without recall of content, DEWR), or not experiencing anything (no experience, NE, see Online Methods).”
definitionposterior cortical 'hot zone' — “The results highlight a posterior cortical ‘hot zone’ where a local decrease in low-frequency EEG activity during both NREM and REM sleep is associated with reports of experiences upon awakening, whereas a local increase in low-frequency activity is associated with the absence of experience.”
definitionbispectral threshold (real-time dreaming/no-dreaming trigger rule) — “The bispectral threshold required that for an DE prediction to be initiated low-frequency power had to be less than or equal to the lower 15% threshold while high-frequency power had to simultaneously meet the top 15% threshold for that subject.”
definitionEEG slow waves / bistability of up- and down-states — “During NREM sleep, EEG slow waves in the delta range reflect alternations between periods of depolarization (up-states), during which neurons can fire, and periods of hyperpolarization (down-states) during which neurons cease firing 12.”
