phenomenonspatial cognition in birds— “Spatial cognition in birds is an exciting, forming research field which will continue to provide important insights on the evolution and mechanisms of spatial cognition.”
claim-typeexistence— “Taken together, these and other results are compelling evidence that the hippocampus serves as a center for spatially related behaviors in birds, as in mammals.”
method — evidence— “The aim of this primer is to highlight the insights and questions in the field of spatial cognition in birds, with an emphasis on recent research attempting to close the knowledge gap between birds and mammals by shedding light on the neural mechanisms of spatial cognition in birds.”
mechanismSpecies differ in place-cell abundance because the ecological demand for spatial memory differs: caching and homing species that must remember many locations develop robust place coding, species that do not need it show little— “A hypothesis explaining this diversity is that the occurrence of place cells across species is correlated with strong reliance on spatial memory capacities.”
mechanismBecause caching birds must retrieve many hidden items, selection for that behaviour is what produces the enlarged hippocampus and abundant place cells, so place cells are proposed to serve spatial memory rather than merely accompany it— “finding robust place cells in a bird that specializes in its food-caching behaviors, and not in a species that does not exhibit such behavior, provides strong support for the hypothesis that place cells contribute to spatial memory.”
mechanismHead direction tuning in a migratory seabird is anchored by the earth's magnetic field, the cells encoding head angle relative to magnetic north— “The plausible explanation is that the cells are sensitive to the angle of the head relative to the magnetic north.”
mechanismKnowing one's own place and heading requires converting head- and body-centred sensory input into an environment-fixed frame, a transformation performed in and upstream of the hippocampus— “To know one’s own position and direction requires a transformation from egocentric sensory inputs that move with the animal’s head and body to allocentric representations that are fixed in the environment.”
builds-on Edward Tolman, 'Cognitive maps in rats and men'— “This metaphor, dated to the early days of spatial cognition research, stresses the importance of studying cognitive maps in birds.”
sensea place cell - A RODENT-DERIVED CRITERION APPLIED ACROSS A PHYLUM: about 2% of more than 2,000 quail hippocampal units passed the criterion, and most of those lacked the clear single field of activity the classical rodent place cells show, so the category is being tested in birds by a standard defined on the animal it was discovered in— “Extensive analysis of more than 2,000 single units in the hippocampus of quails found little evidence of place cells in quails: only ~2% of the recorded units passed the criterion for place cells, and even in this small population of cells, most units did not show a clear single field of activity as expected from the classical place cells…
sensethe avian hippocampus - A HOMOLOGUE THAT DIFFERS FUNDAMENTALLY: identified as the avian homologue of the mammalian hippocampus by development, connectivity and cellular markers, and stated in the next sentence to have an architecture fundamentally different after three hundred million years of independent evolution - the correspondence and the difference are asserted together— “Research on the development, connectivity and cellular markers of avian brains has identified the avian homologue of the mammalian hippocampus. Three hundred million years of independent evolution have given rise to an hippocampal architecture in birds which differs fundamentally from that of the mammalian hippocampus”
sensea cognitive map - A DIFFERENT ORDER OF MAGNITUDE IN BIRDS: the well-studied rodent maps cover small areas while avian maps must represent ranges of hundreds and thousands of kilometres, so the same term names representations whose spatial scale differs by several orders— “Unlike the wellstudied cognitive maps in rodents traversing small areas, however, these cognitive maps need to represent, in some species of birds, ranges of hundreds and thousands of kilometers”
