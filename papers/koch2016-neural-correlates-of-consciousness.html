content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingBoth the full and the content-specific NCC sit in a restricted temporo-parieto-occipital posterior hot zone rather than in the wide fronto-parietal network that earlier work emphasised.— “The anatomical basis of the full NCC and contentspecific NCC do not comprise the wide fronto-parietal network emphasized in past studies, but are primarily localized to a more restricted temporo- parietal-occipital hot zone with additional contributions from some anterior regions (FIG. 2).”
findingParadigms in which participants make no overt report yield a narrower content-specific NCC that contains posterior cortical areas but excludes prefrontal cortex.— “Such no-report paradigms identify a more restricted content-specific NCC, which typically includes posterior cortical areas but not the prefrontal cortex.”
findingGamma synchrony survives or grows during early NREM sleep, anaesthesia and seizures and accompanies unconscious emotional stimuli, so it occurs without consciousness.— “Gamma synchrony can persist or even increase during early NREM sleep, during anaesthesia171,172 or during seizures173, and can be present during exposure to stimuli that induce unconscious emotional responses174, suggesting that gamma synchrony can occur in the absence of consciousness.”
findingThe P3b is missing in most clearly conscious brain-damaged patients and detects minimally conscious patients very poorly, so it fails as a signature of consciousness.— “In fact, the P3b is absent in the majority of patients with brain damage who remain clearly conscious182–184 and has minimal sensitivity (0.14) in identifying minimally conscious patients185.”
findingPrimary visual cortex V1 is not part of the content-specific NCC for any visual attribute tested so far.— “Taken together, the evidence discussed here supports the hypothesis that V1 is not part of the content-specific NCC for the attributes that have been tested so far.”
findingConsciousness survives removal of prefrontal cortex: complete bilateral frontal lobectomy and large bilateral prefrontal resections leave it intact.— “For example, complete bilateral frontal lobectomy123 and large bilateral prefrontal resections do not impair consciousness124.”
findingCerebellar lesions barely affect consciousness or its contents despite the cerebellum's huge neuron count and dense connectivity.— “Lesions of the cerebellum have little effect on consciousness and its contents, however47.”
findingWhat matters for consciousness is local EEG activation over the posterior hot zone rather than globally activated EEG, since conscious patients can show persistent global slow waves and unconscious ones can show alpha rhythm.— “Thus, it may be that local, rather than global, EEG activation is important for consciousness, especially over the posterior cortical hot zone.”
findingThe perturbational complexity index is the first measure that tracks the level of consciousness across sleep stages, anaesthetics and disorders of consciousness and does so for individual participants.— “PCI is the first metric that successfully assesses the level of consciousness under different conditions (for example, during different sleep stages, during exposure to anaesthetics and in disorders of consciousness) and at the level of single participants (FIG. 4).”
findingNo single brain area turns out to be necessary for being conscious, though areas of the posterior cortical hot zone are the best candidates for both full and content-specific NCC.— “In short, no single brain area seems to be necessary for being conscious, but a few areas, especially in the posterior cortical hot zone, are good candidates for both full and content-specific NCC.”
