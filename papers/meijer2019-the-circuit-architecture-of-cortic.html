mechanismsensory noise is independent across sense organs but shared within one modality, so pooling across modalities cancels more noise and yields a bigger behavioral gain than pooling within a modality — “A possible explanation is that sensory noise is largely non-overlapping between different sensory organs whereas it is mostly shared within a specific sensory modality.”
mechanismsound enhances vision by disinhibition: A1 layer-5 axons drive layer-1 interneurons in V1, which inhibit the layer 2-3 interneurons that normally suppress pyramidal cells — “Excitatory influences were mediated by projections from layer 5 in A1 to layer 1 interneurons in V1; these interneurons would then further inhibit layer 2–3 interneurons impinging on pyramidal cells, which would thus be disinhibited, resulting in enhanced visual responses”
mechanisma cross-modal stimulus resets ongoing oscillations, imposing a cycle of high and low excitability so that later same-modality inputs are amplified or depressed according to oscillation phase — “In this way, the stimulus instantiated a rhythmic fluctuation of the excitability of the auditory cortical circuit (mediated via neurons in supragranular layers), by which responses to auditory stimuli occurring at the peaks of the oscillation were enhanced and responses occurring at the troughs were depressed.”
mechanismprefrontal input to the thalamic reticular formation sets thalamic sensory gain, gating how much of each modality reaches primary cortex and thereby implementing sensory selection — “The reticular thalamic formation acts as a relay station which – based on the inputs received from the medial prefrontal cortex – is able to selectively control thalamic sensory gain and therefore gate the level of stimulation that reaches primary cortices (Wimmer et al., 2015)”
mechanismselecting neurons at the low tail of a normal response distribution guarantees they drift upward on remeasurement, manufacturing an apparent inverse-effectiveness effect out of nothing — “Therefore, even without any quantitative difference between condition A and B, neurons that have been selected to be on the low end of the normal distribution in condition A will ‘regress to the mean’ in condition B resulting in a seemingly higher response and vice versa.”
mechanisma loud auditory distractor overshadows a concurrent visual cue, capturing processing resources and cutting the behavioral response to the visual stimulus — “This indicates that the loud auditory noise overshadowed the visual stimulus, resulting in a reduced behavioral output in response to the visual cue.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingThe review concludes that different multisensory processes reuse the same overlapping anatomical network but are carried out by several distinct micro-circuits running in parallel, each recruited according to stimulus features and behavioral constraints. — “We conclude that distinct multisensory processes share overlapping anatomical substrates, are implemented by diverse neuronal micro-circuitries that operate in parallel, and are flexibly recruited based on factors such as stimulus features and behavioral constraints.”
findingMultisensory processing is not one operation but a family of functions that includes segregating modalities as well as integrating them. — “In this review, we expand the framework on the circuit-level mechanisms of cortical multisensory processing by highlighting that multisensory processing is a family of functions – rather than a single operation – which involves not only the integration but also the segregation of modalities.”
findingThe outcome of multisensory processing is set jointly by physical stimulus properties (bottom-up) and by cognitive resources and situational demands (top-down). — “We pursue our main aim by considering a scheme in which the outcome of multisensory processing is dependent on both physical stimulus properties (low level factors), cognitive resources and situational demands (high level factors)”
findingOne and the same anatomical connection between primary visual and auditory cortex can produce opposite effects (suppression or enhancement) depending on stimulus volume, frequency, temporal pattern and cross-modal synchrony, so circuit anatomy alone does not fix the function. — “Thus, the same anatomical circuit in the primary sensory cortex (e.g. direct connections between V1 and A1) can harbor distinct connection patterns to exert different effects based for instance on the type (volume, frequency, temporal pattern, cross-modal synchrony) of sensory stimuli, and thus implement distinct forms of context-dependent crossmodal behaviors.”
findingThe two population-level mechanisms previously proposed (divisive normalization and phase resetting) cannot account for the whole of multisensory processing; a larger set of computational rules is needed. — “However, while some forms of MP (e.g. cue integration) might be fully explained by the proposed two mechanisms, we believe that a complex, diverse phenomenon such as MP can only be explained by considering a larger set of computational rules.”
findingBecause unisensory integration typically weakens neuronal responses while multisensory integration enhances them, the authors argue that separate circuits evolved for the two and that multisensory integration is not merely extra converging input. — “This indicates that different circuits evolved for multi- versus unisensory processing, and that multisensory integration cannot just be seen as a simple consequence of additional inputs converging onto multisensory neurons.”
findingCorrelating unisensory responsiveness with multisensory modulation can produce a spurious inverse-effectiveness result because the two variables are not independent and are subject to regression to the mean. — “First, computing the correlation between two variables which are not independent from one another might suffer from a ‘regression to the mean’ effect.”
findingThe authors prescribe that any observed inverse-effectiveness correlation be tested against a null distribution built by shuffling before it is claimed as real. — “First, to control for the possibility of regression to the mean, the observed correlation between unisensory responsiveness and multisensory response modulation should be tested against a null-distribution which is obtained by a shuffling procedure.”
findingUsing only simple cross-modal cues biases the field toward primary sensory cortices and hides the contribution of association cortices. — “Thus, using very simple cues in cross-modal paradigms may lead to a biased view on the involved brain areas and neuronal mechanisms, because involvement of the primary sensory cortices may be over-represented whereas potential involvement of association cortices may remain unnoticed.”
findingPrior multisensory experience shifts cross-modal object recognition from a feedforward route that ships information from posterior parietal to perirhinal cortex to a feedback route in which perirhinal multisensory representations act back on earlier sensory cortices. — “This indicates that experience may alter the neural substrates and mechanisms associated with multisensory behaviors, i.e. switching from a feedforward-mediated approach in which sensory information is being transferred from PPC to perirhinal cortex to a feedback-mediated strategy in which multisensory representations stored in perirhinal cortex affect sensory representations in earlier cortices.”
findingConscious experience is proposed to be a higher-order form of multisensory integration that sits above cue integration, object recognition and sensory selection, none of which alone suffices for consciousness. — “This proposal thus amounts to characterizing conscious experience as a higher-order form of multisensory integration which supervenes on lower-level forms such as multisensory cue integration, object recognition and sensory selection (which are by themselves insufficient to generate conscious experience).”
findingIn the authors' own mouse detection data, models in which visual and auditory inputs are combined fit behavior significantly better than a model treating the two modalities as processed independently, so the multisensory gain reflects genuine cue integration rather than redundancy. — “We showed that models -involving cue integration according to – (non-) linear combinations of visual and auditory inputs fitted significantly better to the average behavioral performance of all mice as well as to the performance of the majority of individual mice than the model assuming independent processing of stimuli.”
findingRodents can combine cross-modal stimuli supra-linearly, exceeding Bayes-optimal performance, implying that cross-modal sampling is an active synergistic process rather than pure statistical fusion. — “However, recent studies showed that rodents are also able to integrate sensory stimuli in a supra-linear, better than Bayes-optimal fashion (Nikbakht et al., 2018).”
quantity80 ms — temporal separation beyond which two cross-modal signals are likely to be treated as coming from different sources and segregated — “Moreover, the timing of the two sensory signals can determine the likelihood that they should be fused: for example, stimuli that are separated in time by more than 80 ms are likely to be segregated (Meredith et al., 1987).”
quantity0–100 ms — the lead time by which a somatosensory stimulus must precede a visual one for layer-4 responses in rat parietal cortex to be enhanced rather than suppressed — “current source density responses in layer 4 were enhanced when somatosensory stimuli preceded visual stimuli closely in time (0–100 ms), but suppressed when the stimuli were presented in opposite order (Lippert et al., 2013)”
quantity100% — the misleadingly large percentage increase produced by one extra spike when the baseline response is a single spike, used to argue against percentage metrics of multisensory modulation — “Therefore, a neuron that increases its spiking from one to two spikes might show a 100% increase in activity but this does not necessarily translate to a proportionate increase in driving downstream neurons to fire.”
quantityp < 0.01 — significance of the drop in novel-object exploration ratio after perirhinal-parietal disconnection lesions in cross-modal object recognition — “Excitotoxic disconnection lesions of the perirhinal cortex and posterior parietal cortex (PRC & PPC lesion) disrupted crossmodal -, but not tactile-tactile and visual-visual object recognition as indicated by a significant decrease in the ratio of time the rat spent exploring the novel object in these conditions (discrimination ratio; ** p < 0.01).”
variablestimulus modality combination and stimulus intensity (visual alone, auditory alone, or both together, at varying contrast and amplitude) (independent) — “Left Stimulus detection paradigm in which head-fixed mice were required to make a licking response in relation to visual moving gratings (V), auditory stimuli (A) of different contrasts and amplitudes, respectively, presented alone or in concert (AV).”
variablepercentage of correct responses (dependent) — “Correct responses: the number of correct responses as percentage of the total number of trials performed.”
definitionmultisensory cue integration — “This is a mechanism by which the integration of external cross-modal cues can provide a more reliable estimate of an object or event as compared to modality-specific (stand-alone) cues, leading to behavioral benefits such as responding faster and more accurately in a given situation (Gielen et al., 1983; Gingras et al., 2009).”
definitionsensory selection — “For instance, MP includes also sensory selection, a form of attention in which processing of one sensory modality is prioritized at the expense of others (Lakatos et al., 2008; Schroeder and Lakatos, 2009; Wimmer et al., 2015; Ahrens et al., 2015), but also processes such as object recognition impinging on multiple sensory modalities.”
definitionmultisensory (cross-modal) integration, broad definition — “This view is implicit to the definition of multisensory or cross-modal integration as any process in which information across sensory modalities is combined to make a perceptual inference (Bizley et al., 2016).”
definitionprinciple of inverse effectiveness — “The magnitude of multisensory enhancement was shown to be inversely proportional to the ability of the modality-specific stimulus component to elicit a neuronal response, which is known as the principle of inverse effectiveness (Meredith and Stein, 1986)”
definitiontemporal principle of multisensory integration — “Lastly, for multisensory enhancement to occur, stimulus constituents must be presented closely related in time – the temporal principle of multisensory integration (Meredith et al., 1987).”
definitionunisensory integration — “Notably, multisensory integration is different from unisensory integration, which is defined as the integration of multiple stimuli from a single modality – e.g. two cooccurring visual stimuli (Alvarado et al., 2007; Stein and Stanford, 2008).”
definitionMaximum-Likelihood Estimate (Bayesian optimal cue integration) — “The theory of Bayesian optimal cue integration states that the statistically optimal combination of estimates from individual sensory systems, i.e. the Maximum-Likelihood Estimate (MLE), is equal to the sum of these estimates, each weighted according to the reliability of the corresponding sensory system.”
definitiondivisive normalization — “Divisive normalization operates by having a pool of surround neurons performing a normalization of the integrated outputs of a given neuron (Carandini et al., 1997; Carandini and Heeger, 2012; Fetsch et al., 2013; Ohshiro et al., 2017).”
definitionassociation cortices (as used in this review) — “Historically speaking, much attention has been granted to the association cortices, which in the context of this review refers to cortical areas that are neither primary sensory nor motor, and receive converging inputs from sensory areas.”
