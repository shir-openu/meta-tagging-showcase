phenomenonconceptual and procedural knowledge in mathematics teaching— “The terms conceptual knowledge and procedural knowledge are often used by teachers and never more so than when discussing how teachers teach, and children learn mathematics.”
claim-typecritique— “For example, the false positioning of procedural and conceptual knowledge as being two extremes of a continuum, rather than them having the capacity to occupy different places on that continuum depending on the situation, does little to acknowledge their complexity.”
method — evidence— “A thorough synthesis of extant and seminal literature will provide advice to teachers and teacher educators on how a deeper insight into conceptual and procedural knowledge could improve the quality of mathematics teaching.”
mechanismonce procedures have been memorised and practised without understanding, students lose the motivation to look for their meaning, which is why a procedure-first sequence blocks the conceptual half of the iteration— “who asserted that once students have memorised and practised procedures (including written algorithms, that they do not necessarily understand) they have less motivation to comprehend their meaning or the reasoning behind them.”
mechanismacquiring procedural knowledge reduces the load on working memory, and the freed capacity can then be spent on building conceptual knowledge— “There is also evidence that gaining procedural knowledge can lessen the cognitive demands on working memory. This lessening of demand can then free the working memory to focus on conceptual knowledge development”
mechanisma powerful earlier understanding (e.g. reading the equals sign as 'give me an answer') obtrudes into later learning and obfuscates the teaching that follows— “Cognitive interference, considered by Pesek and Kirshner as being the more important interference, is when previous understandings of something are so poweful they obtrude into subsequent learning.”
contradicts the belief, held by the experienced teachers the author surveyed, that conceptual and procedural knowledge are mutually exclusive and that conceptual is always the more necessary— “the teachers indicated that they carried the belief that conceptual and procedural knowledge were mutually exclusive, and that conceptual knowledge was, without exception, more appropriate or necessary than procedural knowledge”
senseconcept - a mental representation embodying the essential features of an object, situation or idea, whose function is to let phenomena be classified as belonging together or not— “According to Westwood (2008) a concept can be defined as “a mental representation that embodies all the essential features of an object, a situation, or an idea. Concepts enable us to classify phenomena as belonging, or not belonging, together in certain categories”
senseprocedural knowledge - characterised by some researchers as the capacity to follow steps in sequence to reach a mathematical goal— “Procedural knowledge is characterised by some researchers (Canobi, 2009; Miller & Hudson, 2007; Rittle-Johnson & Schneider, 2015) as the capacity to follow steps in sequence to solve mathematical problems or reach a mathematical goal.”
sensethe conceptual/procedural relation - bi-directional but NOT always symmetrical, with conceptual knowledge at times the stronger support— “although the relationship between conceptual and procedural knowledge is bi-directional, it is not always symmetrical, and that, at times, conceptual knowledge is stronger and more consistent in supporting procedural knowledge, than the reverse.”
senseconceptual/procedural - a single distinction carrying eight different name pairs across eight traditions (Skemp's instrumental/relational, Tulving's episodic/semantic, Anderson's procedural/declarative...), which the paper asserts are the same division— “In the past, the terms have been couched differently (Table 1), but regardless of the labels, the divisions regarding the types of knowledge are consistent”
sensedeep - in Star's matrix a property that BOTH knowledge types can have, not a property of conceptual knowledge alone— “The matrix indicates that for each knowledge type (conceptual and procedural) there is the possibility of developing a superficial knowledge or a deep knowledge”
senseadaptive expertise - both knowledge types deep AND applicable to unfamiliar situations; routine expertise is the same knowledge without the transfer— “Routine expertise is where there is a superficial conceptual and/or procedural knowledge, which is able to be applied to familiar situations, but not unfamiliar ones, or to new tasks. Adaptive expertise is where both conceptual and procedural knowledge is deep, and where that knowledge can be aplied creatively, flexibly and appropriately to…
