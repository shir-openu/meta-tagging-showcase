phenomenonconceptual and procedural knowledge in mathematics teaching
claim-typecritique
mechanismonce procedures have been memorised and practised without understanding, students lose the motivation to look for their meaning, which is why a procedure-first sequence blocks the conceptual half of the iteration — “who asserted that once students have memorised and practised procedures (including written algorithms, that they do not necessarily understand) they have less motivation to comprehend their meaning or the reasoning behind them.”
mechanismacquiring procedural knowledge reduces the load on working memory, and the freed capacity can then be spent on building conceptual knowledge — “There is also evidence that gaining procedural knowledge can lessen the cognitive demands on working memory. This lessening of demand can then free the working memory to focus on conceptual knowledge development”
mechanisma powerful earlier understanding (e.g. reading the equals sign as 'give me an answer') obtrudes into later learning and obfuscates the teaching that follows — “Cognitive interference, considered by Pesek and Kirshner as being the more important interference, is when previous understandings of something are so poweful they obtrude into subsequent learning.”
relation — “the teachers indicated that they carried the belief that conceptual and procedural knowledge were mutually exclusive, and that conceptual knowledge was, without exception, more appropriate or necessary than procedural knowledge”
differs inconceptual knowledge → procedural knowledge — NOT depth, which is the assumption the paper is attacking: Star's matrix gives each knowledge type its own superficial and deep form, so `deep` is a second dimension rather than a synonym for `conceptual` — “The matrix indicates that for each knowledge type (conceptual and procedural) there is the possibility of developing a superficial knowledge or a deep knowledge”
differs inadaptive expertise → routine expertise — whether the knowledge transfers to unfamiliar situations - routine expertise applies to familiar ones only — “Routine expertise is where there is a superficial conceptual and/or procedural knowledge, which is able to be applied to familiar situations, but not unfamiliar ones, or to new tasks. Adaptive expertise is where both conceptual and procedural knowledge is deep, and where that knowledge can be aplied creatively, flexibly and appropriately to all situations, familiar or new”
replicationuntested
limitationThe paper's claim that misunderstanding of the two knowledge forms is widespread among teachers rests on anecdote rather than on collected data. — “Anecdotally there is a good deal of misinformation regarding the efficacy of both knowledge forms amongst teachers, and this is unhelpful.”
limitationThe paper concedes that the concept-first iterative approach it endorses has little empirical evidence behind it. — “With little empirical evidence to support a concept- first iterative approach, why then has it enjoyed such a pervasive adoption?”
limitationThe dichotomy the paper's title turns on is reported as having been declared fundamentally unsound, on the ground that procedures are conceptual in nature. — “Kieran (2013) declared the dichotomy between them to be fundamentally unsound. Kieran (2013) writes that “…during any period of elaboration, procedures are conceptual in nature”
limitationTreating the two knowledge forms as two ends of a continuum is called false; the paper holds that either can occupy different positions depending on the situation. — “the false positioning of procedural and conceptual knowledge as being two extremes of a continuum, rather than them having the capacity to occupy different places on that continuum depending on the situation”
limitationTwo cells of Star's own matrix have no worked exemplars - the paper reports that deep-procedural and shallow-conceptual knowledge are hard even to illustrate. — “it is challenging to determine an illustration or articulation of deep-procedural, or shallow-conceptual knowledge”
Content tags
content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingExperienced primary and lower-secondary teachers believed that conceptual and procedural knowledge are mutually exclusive and that conceptual knowledge is always the more appropriate thing to teach. — “In essence, the teachers indicated that they carried the belief that conceptual and procedural knowledge were mutually exclusive, and that conceptual knowledge was, without exception, more appropriate or necessary than procedural knowledge.”
findingBoth procedural and conceptual knowledge matter and each reinforces the other, so teaching should aim at both rather than choosing between them. — “Procedural knowledge and conceptual knowledge are both important and help to strengthen each other.”
findingEither knowledge type can be shallow or deep, so treating conceptual as inherently deep and procedural as inherently shallow misrepresents both. — “Both procedural and conceptual knowledge are more nuanced structures than many teachers realise. Both can be ‘shallow’ (superficial) or ‘deep’.”
findingThe synthesis endorses teaching conceptual knowledge before procedural knowledge in most cases. — “Conceptual knowledge in most cases should precede procedural knowledge.”
findingStarting with conceptual knowledge and moving to procedural knowledge makes conceptual understanding more likely, whereas the procedural-to-conceptual direction risks students never reaching conceptual knowledge. — “There is more chance of students developing a conceptual knowledge if they start with conceptual knowledge and then move to procedural knowledge.”
findingThe empirical support for concept-first is that students taught conceptually and then procedurally outperform students taught procedurally and then conceptually. — “students who are taught for a conceptual understanding followed by a procedural understanding, outperform students who are instructed for procedural then coneptual knowledge (Pesek & Kirshner, 2000).”
findingProcedural practice only feeds conceptual growth when the problems are sequenced to expose conceptual relationships; randomly ordered problems produced no such effect. — “Research (Canobi, 2009; McNeil et al., 2012) showed how sequencing arithmetic problems to encourage the identification of conceptual relationships proved to be efficacious, whereas when the problems were in random order this effect was not noted.”
findingThe conceptual-procedural relationship is bi-directional but asymmetric: conceptual knowledge supports procedural knowledge more strongly and more consistently than procedural supports conceptual. — “the relationship between conceptual and procedural knowledge is bi-directional, it is not always symmetrical, and that, at times, conceptual knowledge is stronger and more consistent in supporting procedural knowledge, than the reverse.”
findingThe paper's answer to its own title is that teachers need conceptual AND procedural knowledge, with the timing and placement left to the teacher's professional judgement. — “In answer to the title of this article it should be Conceptual knowledge and Procedural knowledge, the when and where, that is determined by the professional call of the teacher.”
findingBecause both knowledge types can occupy different points on a quality continuum, the dichotomy between conceptual and procedural knowledge is judged fundamentally unsound. — “Kieran (2013) declared the dichotomy between them to be fundamentally unsound.”
