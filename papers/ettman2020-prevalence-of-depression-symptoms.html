senseCOVID-19 stressor exposure - A COUNT, 0 to 13, OF REALISED EVENTS (job loss, a death, financial problems), then CUT INTO THIRDS. AND THE THIRDS ARE NOT THIRDS OF THE SCALE: equal parts of 0-13 would break at about 4 and 9, and the paper breaks at 2 and 4. The cut points are therefore fitted to the RESPONSES, not to the instrument, so `high exposure` means `in the upper part of THIS survey` and does not transfer

limitationTHE TWO ARMS ARE TWO DIFFERENT SURVEYS OF DIFFERENT PEOPLE, and the paper says so first: `we are assessing different individuals ... the NHANES data can provide only a PROXY for the true baseline`. The instrument is held constant and the sampling frame is not
limitationthe during-COVID arm is conditioned on things the baseline arm is not: respondents had to SPEAK ENGLISH and to have COMPLETED AN AMERISPEAK SURVEY IN THE PREVIOUS SIX MONTHS, and 94.2% answered over the internet. NHANES imposes none of these
limitationand the panel was PAID - a cash equivalent of $3 - which NHANES participants are not. The paper reports the payment in the methods and does not carry it into the limitations
limitationthe panel also came in with MORE PRIOR DEPRESSION than the national baseline, 18.8% against 16.5%. The paper reports the gap, calls it small, and then states plainly that `IT IS IMPOSSIBLE TO ANTICIPATE HOW THE DIFFERENCE ... COULD, IF AT ALL, AFFECT THE MAGNITUDE` - an unresolved confound, declared as unresolved
limitationthe comparability argument is an ARGUMENT, not a demonstration: both frames are probability-based and address-based, therefore `mitigating this concern`. No empirical check that the two frames yield the same PHQ-9 distribution on a common population is offered, and none could be from these data
limitationthe instrument is a SCREENER and the paper is the one in this group that says so: `a diagnosis of depression is ultimately a diagnosis made by a clinician`. 27.8% is 27.8% scoring at or above 10 on the PHQ-9
