phenomenoncontext-specific improvements in interference resolution (priming of control)
claim-typemechanism
mechanismTop-down control settings are bound into episodic event-file representations, so that later presentation of another feature of that representation (the contextual cue) implicitly retrieves and reinstates the control setting. — “we propose that top-down control settings associated with specific contextual information can be bound into episodic “event file” representations, and that, subsequently, their retrieval can be implicitly primed by other features of that representation”
mechanismExperienced conflict triggers an arousal/noradrenergic response which, via Hebbian associative learning, binds the current bottom-up context representation to the top-down task/biasing representation. — “According to the model, the experience of conflict elicits an arousal response (accompanied by noradrenalin release) that in turn promotes the binding of current bottom-up stimulus/context representations and top-down task representations/biasing processes via Hebbian (associative) learning”
relation — “These findings extend the established role of the mSPL in mediating explicitly cued shifts of attention ( Vandenberghe et al., 2001 ; Yantis et al., 2002 ; Serences et al., 2004 ; Shulman et al., 2009 ) to include the retrieval and execution of implicitly primed control settings.”
relation — “Furthermore, contrary to the suspicion that that this phenomenon merely reflects aggregate congruency sequence effects, context-specific improvements in interference resolution were dissociable from such trial-by-trial “conflict adaptation” effects in the current study, verifying previous reports ( Crump et al., 2006 ).”
effect directioncontextual cues → top-down control — increases — “associative triggering of specific top-down control states”
effect directioncontextual cues → interference resolution — increases — “implicit contextual cues can modulate performance”
replicationreplicated
limitationThe functional profile of mSPL was only sampled at two conflict-frequency levels and would need parametric variation of cues or load to be characterised properly. — “Future studies could potentially delineate the functional characteristics of this region more systematically by varying attentional cues or load in a more continuous, parametric fashion.”
limitationHow the reduced FFA lateralisation in the high-conflict context actually produces the behavioural benefit is not established. — “Although this finding echoes recent reports of spatially global gain modulation under conditions of increased cognitive control ( Ester et al., 2009 ; King et al., 2010 ), the precise mechanisms by which this bilateral recruitment of the FFA for stimuli presented in the high-conflict hemifield translated into context-specific improvements in interference resolution remain unclear.”
Content tags
content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingInterference (flanker congruency) effects in reaction time were smaller for stimuli appearing in the location associated with frequent conflict than in the low-conflict location. — “interference effects were reduced for stimuli presented in the high-conflict location (94 ms) relative to those in the low-conflict context (123 ms; F (1,24) = 7.6; p = 0.01”
findingTwo regions, left dorsal premotor cortex and right medial superior parietal lobule, showed the context-by-congruency interaction in hemodynamic activity. — “Two regions emerged displaying differential patterns of conflict processing for stimuli presented in the high- versus low-conflict context: the left dorsal premotor cortex and the right medial superior parietal lobule”
findingThe mSPL showed context-specific functional coupling with visual cortex and fusiform face area regions, indicating top-down biasing of sensory processing. — “In line with a role of the mSPL in contextual conflict-control, BOLD activity in this region associated with differential conflict processing in the high- versus low-conflict context significantly covaried with that in both visual cortex ROIs”
findingContextual control was implemented online rather than as a sustained attentional set: the behavioural benefit was absent on the first trial after a switch into the high-conflict context and present only on repetitions. — “The three-way interaction was significant ( F (1,24) = 10.9; p < 0.005), such that reduced interference effects were not evident following a transition from the low- to high-conflict context”
findingParticipants were essentially unaware of the location-based conflict-frequency manipulation, establishing that the effect is implicit. — “only one of 25 participants in the current experiment reported noticing the location-based manipulation of conflict frequency in our post-test survey”
findingStimulus-driven lateralization in the fusiform face area was abolished in the high-conflict context, consistent with spatially global gain modulation under elevated control. — “while lateralization was present in the FFA for stimuli presented in the low-conflict context ( t (24) = 3.0; p < 0.01), it was absent for stimuli presented in the high-conflict context”
findingIndividual differences in the FFA lateralization reduction tracked individual differences in the behavioural interference reduction. — “the magnitude of contextual lateralization reduction in the FFA (lateralization low-conflict context − lateralization high-conflict context ) correlated with the degree of contextual interference reduction in behavior”
quantity94 ms vs 123 ms — size of the flanker interference effect in the high-conflict versus low-conflict location — “interference effects were reduced for stimuli presented in the high-conflict location (94 ms) relative to those in the low-conflict context (123 ms; F (1,24) = 7.6; p = 0.01”
quantity25 — number of participants retained for analysis after exclusions — “The remaining 25 participants were 14 females and 11 males (mean age = 23.8 years; range = 18–33 years).”
quantity93% — overall accuracy on the flanker task — “Overall performance of the flanker task was high (93% correct) and characterized by typical interference effects.”
quantity783 ms vs 674 ms — mean reaction time on incongruent versus congruent trials — “RTs were slower for incongruent stimuli (783 ms) than for congruent arrays (674 ms; F (1,24) = 133.8; p < 0.0001).”
quantityp = 0.054 — binomial test on the 17-vs-8 split of participants correctly forced-choosing the high-conflict side, taken as evidence of implicit knowledge — “17 individuals correctly reported which side of the display had a greater proportion of incongruent trials in their version of the experiment, while only 8 answered incorrectly (binomial test; p = 0.054)”
quantity25% congruent/75% incongruent — the conflict-frequency ratio defining the high-conflict context (reversed for the low-conflict context) — “Conflict frequency was manipulated according to stimulus location by defining one side of fixation as a high-conflict context (25% congruent/75% incongruent trials) and the other as a low-conflict context”
variableconflict-frequency context (high- vs low-conflict stimulus location) (independent) — “Conflict frequency was manipulated according to stimulus location by defining one side of fixation as a high-conflict context (25% congruent/75% incongruent trials) and the other as a low-conflict context”
variableflanker congruency (congruent vs incongruent) (independent) — “Target and flanker face viewpoint direction was congruent in half of all trials and incongruent in the other.”
variablecontext transition (repetition vs switch) (independent) — “we reanalyzed the RT data with 2 (context transition: repetition vs switch) × 2 (context) × 2 (congruency) ANOVAs”
variablereaction time and error rate (interference effect) (dependent) — “Our primary analyses tested for contextual variation in interference effects (incongruent − congruent) in mean reaction times (RT) on correct trials (excluding posterror trials) and error rates with 2 (context: high-conflict vs low-conflict) × 2 (congruency: congruent vs incongruent) repeated-measures ANOVAs.”
variableindividual mean reaction time (overall response speed) (covariate) — “and, to control for between-subject variability in overall response speed, individual mean RT as covariates”
definitionpriming of control — “This has raised the intriguing prospect of “priming of control,” the possibility that top-down control may be subject to associative bottom-up retrieval, triggered by stimulus context”
definitionpsychophysiological interaction (PPI) analysis — “Psychophysiological interaction (PPI) analysis provides a measure of functional connectivity by quantifying covariation of activation time courses between different brain regions”
