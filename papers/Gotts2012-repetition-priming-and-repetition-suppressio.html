phenomenonrepetition priming and repetition suppression
claim-typemechanism
mechanismRepetition makes task-engaged cells fire more synchronously even as their average rates fall, so processing becomes more efficient and behaviour improves despite reduced activity. — “A very different proposal that may help to resolve this puzzle is that as cells are firing at lower overall rates, they are firing more synchronously with one another, leading to more efficient neural processing ( Gilbert, Gotts, Carver, & Martin, 2010 ; Gotts, 2003 ) ( Figure 1C ).”
mechanismCoincident input spikes sum on the receiving cell's membrane before the depolarization decays, so synchronous inputs cross spike threshold more reliably than the same number of asynchronous inputs. — “Small depolarizations that occur simultaneously (i.e. synchronously) in a receiving cell will be much more likely to sum above the voltage threshold needed to evoke an action potential.”
mechanismIn the Bayesian alternative, learned top-down predictions inhibit the bottom-up sensory evidence, so the residual prediction error - and hence the measured activity - shrinks with repetition. — “This process is commonly referred to in the literature on Bayesian networks as "explaining away" (e.g. Pearl, 1988 ), since as the appropriate causes of the sensory evidence are learned, the incorrect causes (i.e. prediction error) are reduced and explained away.”
relation — “Having reviewed four basic proposals as to how repetition suppression might afford repetition priming, the only view that we consider definitively ruled out by current data is the Facilitation Model, at least in its existing form.”
relation — “We found that stimulus repetition led to repetition priming in both patients and increases in low-frequency evoked power (1–15 Hz) for virtually all task-engaged electrodes (i.e. those that exhibited significant evoked responses), replicating the basic pattern of Gilbert et al. (2010) .”
relation — “In a simplified neocortical circuit model that incorporated biologically proportionate numbers of excitatory and inhibitory cells and short-term plasticity mechanisms, Gotts (2003) showed that it was possible to simultaneously address short-lag repetition suppression and priming effects through enhanced synchronization.”
differs inrepetition priming → repetition suppression — THE DIRECTION EACH MOVES ON THE SAME EVENT - repetition IMPROVES the behavioural measure and ATTENUATES the neural one. Not two phenomena but two readings of one, pointing opposite ways, which is the puzzle the paper's four mechanisms exist to dissolve — “improved behavioral performance”
replicationuntested
limitationThe favoured synchrony account has been evaluated empirically in very few studies. — “Despite the promise that the Synchrony model holds for resolving the puzzle of repetition priming and repetition suppression, there are relatively few studies that have evaluated it empirically.”
limitationNeither of the two surviving models has been subjected to a full set of experimental tests. — “However, the Bayesian Explaining Away model has received experimental support, as well, and neither the Synchrony nor Explaining Away models have been run through a full gauntlet of experimental tests.”
limitationHow long-term plasticity would link activity decreases to changes in synchrony is unclear. — “It is further possible that LTD dominates the changes such that activities will be reduced overall, producing repetition suppression, but how this would relate to changes in synchrony is quite unclear.”

findingOf the four candidate accounts reviewed, only the Facilitation model is judged definitively ruled out by existing data. — “Having reviewed four basic proposals as to how repetition suppression might afford repetition priming, the only view that we consider definitively ruled out by current data is the Facilitation Model, at least in its existing form.”
findingThe authors judge the enhanced-synchronization account the most promising of the surviving proposals. — “At present, we view the Synchrony model as the most promising.”
findingTheir earlier neocortical circuit model reproduced repetition suppression and priming together, with spike synchronization rising as firing rates fell. — “Importantly, as the model's firing rates decreased with repetition, the synchronization of the spike times simultaneously increased.”
findingTheir preliminary intracranial EEG study in two surgical patients found behavioural priming together with increased low-frequency evoked power at essentially every task-engaged electrode. — “We found that stimulus repetition led to repetition priming in both patients and increases in low-frequency evoked power (1–15 Hz) for virtually all task-engaged electrodes (i.e. those that exhibited significant evoked responses), replicating the basic pattern of Gilbert et al. (2010) .”
findingThe same intracranial recordings showed increased fronto-temporal phase-locking with repetition, in alpha and low beta bands. — “Like Ghuman et al. (2008) , we also observed increases in phase-locking (LFP-LFP coherence) between task-engaged frontal and temporal electrodes in the alpha (8–12 Hz) and low beta (12–18 Hz) frequency ranges.”
findingThey propose that the processes producing synchronization and those producing activity decreases may be partly separate, which would explain reported dissociations between repetition suppression and priming. — “We would tentatively suggest that the mechanisms producing changes in synchronization and those resulting in overall activity decreases may be at least partially independent, possibly explaining the lack of relationship between repetition suppression and repetition priming that has occasionally been observed”

definitionrepetition priming — “When we repeatedly encounter an object in the environment, we become faster and more accurate at identifying it, a phenomenon referred to as "repetition priming" (see Tulving and Schacter, 1990 ; Schacter & Buckner, 1998 , for review).”
definitionrepetition suppression — “While behavioral performance improves with stimulus repetition, neural activity in humans (BOLD fMRI) and monkeys (single-cell firing rates) tends to decrease, a phenomenon often referred to as "repetition suppression" (see Desimone, 1996 ; Henson, 2003 ; Grill-Spector, Henson, & Martin, 2006 , for reviews).”
definitionFacilitation model — “The "Facilitation" model ( Henson, 2003 ; James, Humphrey, Gati, Menon, & Goodale, 2000 ; James & Gauthier, 2006 ) is perhaps the most straightforward resolution, positing that with repetition, neural activity is advanced in time with a more rapid overall time course (see Figure 1A ).”
definitionSharpening — “A second idea, often referred to as "Sharpening" ( Desimone, 1996 ; Wiggs & Martin, 1998 ), holds that while neural activity is decreasing on the average, the decreases are carried mainly by the cells that are poorly tuned and/or weakly responsive to the repeated stimuli with the "best" cells (i.e. most selective/responsive) instead retaining their activity levels ( Figure 1B ).”
