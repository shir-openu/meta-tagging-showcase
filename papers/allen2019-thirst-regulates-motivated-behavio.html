findingMore than half of the ~24,000 neurons recorded across 34 brain regions changed their firing with the thirst task, responding rapidly to the water-predicting odour cue before sustained water-acquisition activity. — “We found that more than half of recorded neurons were modulated by this task, with a rapid and widespread response to a water-predicting olfactory cue preceding sustained activity related to water acquisition.”
findingThe rewarded odour cue triggered a wave of activity that started in olfactory regions about 150 ms after odour onset and reached every recorded brain region within about 300 ms. — “The Go cue produced a wave of activity in thirsty animals that began in olfactory regions within ~150 ms of odor onset and rapidly (within ~300 ms) spread to neurons in every region we recorded from”
findingSatiety gated the wave: once mice were sated the identical odour cue produced only a brief transient change in firing and no licking. — “The animal’s satiety state gated this wave of activity: In satiated mice, the same sensory cue produced only a transient change in activity and no behavioral response.”
findingSpontaneous pre-stimulus baseline firing, not just cue-evoked firing, shifted with satiety state in neurons across many brain regions. — “Surprisingly, the spontaneous baseline activity of neurons across many brain regions was also modulated by the animal’s satiety state.”
findingOdour identity could be decoded from populations throughout the brain in thirsty mice, but decoding fell to near chance across much of the brain once the mice were sated. — “In sated mice, the ability to decode odors dropped to near chance levels throughout much of the brain”
findingSingle neurons were specialised: each mostly encoded one task aspect (state, cue, or behaviour), while every brain region held a mixture of all these neuron types. — “Each individual neuron primarily encoded one aspect of the task, and every brain region contained a diverse mixture of neurons encoding different aspects of the task.”
findingOptogenetic stimulation of hypothalamic thirst neurons in sated mice pushed behaviour, within-trial brainwide dynamics, and single-neuron satiety correlates back to their thirsty-state values. — “We found that this focal stimulation returned the behavior, brainwide single-neuron within-trial dynamics, and brainwide single-neuron correlates of satiety state back to the state corresponding to thirst.”
findingRe-creating the thirst representation in only a subset of brain regions was enough to restore thirst-motivated sensory processing, behaviour and the associated brainwide dynamics. — “These results suggest that recapitulation of the thirst state in a subset of regions is sufficient to restore thirst-motivated sensory processing and behavior, along with the associated brainwide activity dynamics.”

findingthe initial onset and the brainwide propagation of the activity wave both preceded the onset of licking; first licks were measured at 0.86 s after odor onset — “Both the initial onset and the brainwide propagation of this activity wave substantially preceded the onset of licking (first licks were measured at 0.86 ± 0.04 s after odor onset, mean ± SEM across sessions).”
