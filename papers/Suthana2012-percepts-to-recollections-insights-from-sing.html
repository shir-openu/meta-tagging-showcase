transferfrom rodent hippocampal physiology: the place cell, transferred twice - literally to human virtual navigation, and metaphorically to concept coding in a cognitive multidimensional space — “The invariant responses to individual persons and landmarks observed in the human MTL may be compared to the striking place fields in rodents, which appear to encode particular locations in the animal”
transferfrom rodent optogenetics: reactivation of a fear memory by stimulating specific hippocampal neurons, held up as the model for a causal human approach — “Since the pioneering observations of Wilder Penfield, focal electrical stimulation of MTL circuits has been shown to evoke old memories or the feeling of déjà”
mechanismMTL neurons transform novel percepts into sparse, abstracted-yet-detailed representations, and it is this abstraction that later allows the representation to be summoned by either internal or external cues, producing conscious episodic recall — “Given the critical role of these regions in episodic memory, we posit that these responses are central in the transformation of novel stimuli to representations that can be later consciously retrieved as episodic memories.”
mechanismRepresentations must carry both detail and abstraction (loss of detail) so that a partial internal or external cue is sufficient to reactivate them — “As such, these representations need to possess detail but also abstraction (i.e. the loss of detail), so that they can be later summoned by internal as well as external cues.”
mechanismEntorhinal stimulation enhances memory by resetting the phase of the hippocampal theta rhythm, aligning encoding with a favourable oscillatory phase — “Interestingly stimulation of the entorhinal area reset the phase of the theta rhythm in the hippocampus, providing a possible mechanism for memory enhancement.”

relation — “Gelbard-Sagiv and colleagues [ 58 ] designed a study where participants were able to freely report spontaneous recollections of a previously experienced episode while MTL neuron activity was recorded [ 58 ]; participants viewed various video clips representing episodes (e.g. Simpson’s TV show clip) and later explicitly declared clips that “came to mind” (i.e. freely recalled). Interestingly, similar to the highly specific and invariant responses to individual persons and landmarks reported before, sustained responses specific to distinct episodes were observed. Strikingly, hippocampal and entorhinal neurons that were selectively active during the initial viewing of distinct episodes were similarly reactivated just prior to the verbal report of recall of those very same episodes.”
relation — “To summarize then, the neural code which characterizes the representation of external stimuli in the MTL is expressed in the responses of individual neurons, which appear to be highly specific yet invariant, suggesting that neurons may respond in an explicit fashion to concepts such as a particular person or landmark. These responses appear to be preserved (i.e. invariant) through multiple representations of the same stimulus independent of physical features, independent of modality of sensory presentation, and in fact present even in the absence of the stimulus altogether such as in imagery responses. Obviously, a particular concept is not represented by a single cell alone, although the coding is ultra sparse”
relation — “The correlation of single neuron responses in the MTL with specific conscious percepts does not imply that these regions are necessary for conscious percepts, yet these responses may reflect the link between conscious percepts and episodic memories that can be later consciously accessed”
relation — “These results are consistent with the view that the hippocampus does not differentiate between recollected versus familiar items in memory, rather it relates to the strength of the memory”
relation — “It has been suggested that the response pattern seen in humans are indeed “place cells” in a cognitive multidimensional space”

limitationThe human single-neuron dataset is rare and constrained by what clinical circumstances permit. — “Although the data presented are rare and limited by clinical opportunities, it offers exciting future avenues for further exploration.”
limitationRecordings are only possible where electrodes are implanted for clinical reasons, restricting the tasks and settings that can be studied. — “Due to the obvious invasive nature of single neuron recordings, studies are done only in special circumstances where electrodes are placed in the brain for specific clinical reasons.”
limitationSampling is biased to anterior and middle hippocampus, so anterior-posterior comparisons cannot yet be made. — “The majority of human single neuron studies to date report recordings from the anterior and middle portion of the hippocampus and systematic anterior-posterior analysis in the hippocampal axis will have to await future studies with larger neuronal samples.”
limitationNo human perirhinal neurons have been recorded, so the proposed hippocampus/perirhinal recollection-familiarity dissociation cannot be tested. — “Since human single neuron studies to date have not recorded from perirhinal neurons, future studies are needed to investigate this dissociation between human hippocampal and perirhinal neurons and their involvement in recollection vs. familiarity.”
limitationcorrelation of single-neuron responses with conscious percepts does not establish that the region is necessary for them — “The correlation of single neuron responses in the MTL with specific conscious percepts does not imply that these regions are necessary for conscious percepts, yet these responses may reflect the link between conscious percepts and episodic memories that can be later consciously accessed”
limitationmost stimuli used were highly familiar to the participant, so the responses may partly reflect familiarity — “The human single neuron studies we have discussed thus far [ 17 , 33 – 36 , 38 ] often used stimuli that were highly familiar to the participant (e.g. a family member or famous person/landmark) and thus may to some extent reflect familiarity.”
limitationthe recognition-related responses were not stimulus-specific and so need not represent single memories — “It is important to note, however, that the responses in the previously discussed studies were not specific to individual stimuli and thus do no necessarily represent single memories. Rather, the hippocampal and amygdala neurons may reflect the judgment of recognition independent of recollection or familiarity of individual items.”
limitationno human single-neuron study has recorded from perirhinal cortex, leaving the key dissociation untested — “Since human single neuron studies to date have not recorded from perirhinal neurons, future studies are needed to investigate this dissociation between human hippocampal and perirhinal neurons and their involvement in recollection vs. familiarity.”
limitationrecordings come only from patients confined to a hospital room who cannot navigate a real environment — “Due to the obvious invasive nature of single neuron recordings, studies are done only in special circumstances where electrodes are placed in the brain for specific clinical reasons. These often involve closely monitored patients limited to their hospital room who cannot navigate a real environment during recordings.”
limitationfewer than 100 neurons are recorded at once, scattered across regions chosen on clinical grounds — “In a given human microelectrode recording set up, such as in chronic epilepsy patient monitored with intracranial depth electrodes, the activity of less than 100 neurons are simultaneously recorded and these neurons may be distributed across several brain areas, which need to be clinically sampled.”
limitationspikes on one microelectrode probably come from several neighbouring neurons, especially in densely packed hippocampus — “However, spikes detected on a single microelectrode most likely originate from several neighboring neurons, especially within brain areas that have densely packed cellular structures, such as the hippocampus.”
limitationrecordings are almost all anterior and middle hippocampus, so the anterior-posterior axis cannot yet be analysed — “The majority of human single neuron studies to date report recordings from the anterior and middle portion of the hippocampus and systematic anterior-posterior analysis in the hippocampal axis will have to await future studies with larger neuronal samples.”
limitationelectrode bundles can be localised to about 1 mm but individual electrodes within a bundle cannot be separated — “When registered to the electrode image, bundles of microelectrodes can be localized to areas as small as 1mm [ 97 ], although separation of a single microelectrode from its bundled neighboring electrodes is still a challenge.”

findingThe selective responses of human MTL neurons are invariant not only across different pictures of the same concept but across sensory modality, firing to the spoken name, written name or image of the same entity. — “Recently, it was shown that this invariance crosses modalities, meaning that MTL neurons may exhibit a selective and “invariant” response to a particular stimulus out of 100 images and do so independent of the sensory modality (visual image, audio, or written iconic representations) through which the stimulus was presented”
findingSelective and invariant MTL responses are slow, typically occurring 300-500 ms after stimulus onset, far later than sensory-cortical latencies. — “The selective and invariant responses described above are of relative long latencies often in the 300 msec - 500 msec range.”
findingMTL neurons increase firing to their preferred image only when the subject consciously recognises it, remaining largely silent when the same image is presented but not recognised. — “When varying the display time of an image between 33 to 256 msec, MTL neurons selective for an image significantly increase in firing rate only if the image is recognized by the subject, even if the image is presented for as briefly as 33 msec.”
findingSubjects can volitionally drive the firing of individual MTL neurons to determine which of two competing images is displayed, overriding the actual visual input on the screen. — “Subjects were able to successfully control which image was projected by altering the firing rate of two independently selective neurons independent of the visual input of the stimulus on the screen”
findingHippocampal and entorhinal neurons that fired during the initial viewing of a specific film episode become reactivated seconds before the subject spontaneously reports recalling that same episode. — “Strikingly, hippocampal and entorhinal neurons that were selectively active during the initial viewing of distinct episodes were similarly reactivated just prior to the verbal report of recall of those very same episodes.”
findingTighter phase coordination between hippocampal spiking and the theta LFP during encoding predicts whether that particular image will later be remembered. — “A tighter coordination between hippocampal single neuron spiking and the simultaneously recorded theta LFP oscillation during initial viewing of the image predicted the success of the formed memory for that image”
findingGrowing correlation of a hippocampal neuron's firing across successive time points within an episode, built up over repetitions, predicts later recall of that episode. — “Furthermore, the degree of correlation of hippocampal neuronal firing rate across successive time points within an episode increased over subsequent repetitions of the same episode, and predicted subjects’ future recall performance”
findingHippocampal neurons discriminate old from new stimuli after a single trial regardless of whether the item's spatial position is recollected, suggesting they track memory strength rather than distinguishing recollection from familiarity. — “Results showed that single hippocampal neurons recorded during recognition could distinguish between novel (new) versus recognized (old) stimuli independent of whether spatial position was recalled and even after a single trial”
findingDeep-brain stimulation of the entorhinal white matter during learning enhances later recall, whereas stimulation of the hippocampus itself does not. — “Suthana and colleagues [ 63 ] have recently shown that stimulation of the entorhinal white matter area, but not the hippocampus itself, enhances memory.”
findingMTL neurons respond to a concept across radically different images and even its written name — “Thus a neuron may respond to a picture of the Sydney Opera House and exhibit no response to 50 other landmarks, yet also respond to many permutations and physically different representations of the Sydney Opera House, seen in color, in black and white, or from different angles. In fact, the neuron may also respond to the iconic representation, namely the words “Sydney Opera”, which is obviously quite different in its visual properties than the image of this landmark.”
findingthe invariance holds across sensory modality - image, audio, written word — “Recently, it was shown that this invariance crosses modalities, meaning that MTL neurons may exhibit a selective and “invariant” response to a particular stimulus out of 100 images and do so independent of the sensory modality (visual image, audio, or written iconic representations) through which the stimulus was presented”
findingselective responses are late, 300-500 ms, and longer than those seen in animal studies — “Responses are late. The selective and invariant responses described above are of relative long latencies often in the 300 msec - 500 msec range. Interestingly and consistent with the anatomical heirarchy, responses in the parahippocampal cortex are significantly shorter than those in the hippocampus, amygdala and entorhinal cortex [ 35 ], but still with longer latencies than those observed in animal studies.”
findinga neuron fires for its concept only when the subject reports recognising it, even at 33 ms exposure — “Using flash suppression and backward masking paradigms it has been shown that selective MTL responses are mainly triggered when accompanied by the participants’ conscious perception or recognition of the stimulus [ 37 – 38 ]. When varying the display time of an image between 33 to 256 msec, MTL neurons selective for an image significantly increase in firing rate only if the image is recognized by the subject, even if the image is presented for as briefly as 33 msec. Conversely, if the subject reported no recognition of the image, the MTL neuron selective for that image is mostly silent”
findingimagining a stimulus recruits the same neuron as seeing it — “Generating an internal percept of a stimulus through imagery in the absence of an external visual stimulus, recruits the same MTL neuron activated during viewing of the stimulus itself”
findingsubjects steered a display by modulating two independently selective neurons, overriding the visual input — “Subjects were able to successfully control which image was projected by altering the firing rate of two independently selective neurons independent of the visual input of the stimulus on the screen ( Figure 3 ). These results highlight the power of internal representation to override sensory input.”
