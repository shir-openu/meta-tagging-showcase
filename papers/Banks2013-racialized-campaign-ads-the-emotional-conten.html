phenomenonracial priming by implicit racial appeals— “Our theory suggests that anger and fear, in the context of an implicit racial appeal, influence whites’ views about race differently.”
claim-typemechanism— “We contend that racial priming is most effective when an IRA arouses anger.”
method — evidence— “Using an experiment over two waves, we induce anger and fear from an implicit racialized campaign ad and find that anger uniquely boosts opposition to racial policies among white racial conservatives.”
transferfrom psychology (emotion / memory research): the paper takes Bower and Forgas's theory of emotional priming — a psychological account of mood-congruent recall — and uses it as the explanatory engine for a political-communication effect— “We rely on Bower and Forgas’s (2001) theory of emotional priming to argue that specific emotions from IRAs play an important role in activating racial attitudes.”
mechanismanger and symbolic racism are stored as tightly linked associations in memory, so arousing anger activates the racial belief system and makes it available for the subsequent policy judgement— “We argue that anger and symbolic racism (SR) are tightly linked in memory, and evoking this emotion in the context of an IRA should then activate this belief system and cause a shift in opposition to racial policies.”
mechanismemotional (mood-congruent) priming: being returned to the emotional state in which an attitude was learned makes that attitude easier to recall— “We rely on Bower and Forgas’s (2001) theory of emotional priming to argue that specific emotions from IRAs play an important role in activating racial attitudes.”
mechanismbecause contemporary racial discourse is dominated by blame attribution, and blame is the appraisal that produces anger, anger rather than fear is the emotion bonded to whites' racial predispositions— “If contemporary discussions about race are dominated by blame rhetoric, we suspect that anger should be strongly linked to whites’ negative racial predispositions.”
builds-on Bower and Forgas (2001)— “We rely on Bower and Forgas’s (2001) theory of emotional priming to argue that specific emotions from IRAs play an important role in activating racial attitudes.”
builds-on Banks and Valentino (2012)— “In fact, Banks and Valentino (2012) observe such an effect; they find that anger (independent of racial or political content), and not disgust or fear, is highly correlated with, and can in fact activate, SR.”
replicates Banks and Valentino (2012)— “However, Banks and Valentino (2012) find similar results on an adult national sample.”
sensenegative emotion, as this paper refuses to use it - the charge is CONFLATION: most research on racial priming treats negative affect as one thing, and the paper's contribution is to show that two negative emotions from the same advertisement do different work. A category being used where a distinction was needed— “Most of the research on racial priming seems to conflate the effect of negative emotions”
senseanger, in this account - not a stronger version of negative feeling but a SPECIFIC ROUTE: it is argued to be tightly linked in memory to whites' negative racial attitudes, so it reaches them where another negative emotion may not. The claim is about association, not intensity— “We suspect this effect occurs because anger and whites’ negative racial attitudes are tightly linked in memory”
senseracial conservative and racial liberal, as defined here by WHERE THEY LOCATE THE CAUSE: conservatives find fault in the moral character of blacks, liberals blame the racism and discrimination endured. The two are separated by an attribution rather than by a policy position, which is what lets one emotion move them in opposite directions— “While racial conservatives find fault in the moral character of blacks, racial liberals blame the racism and discrimination that blacks have endured over the years”
controla manipulation check on the campaign advertisements, coded from an open-ended `how did the advertisement make you feel` by two trained research assistants, double-coded — partial— “However, they also expressed some fear (β = .19; p ≥ .05) relative to the control condition.” — rules out that the anger and fear advertisements produced the emotions they were built to produce, rather than undifferentiated negative affect— “Results We conducted a manipulation check to test whether our ads produced the intended emotion.”
differs inanger —distinct-from→ fear — WHICH ONE IS BOUND TO RACIAL ATTITUDES IN MEMORY - the paper's claim is that they are not interchangeable negatives, and that the difference is associative rather than a matter of degree— “Our theory suggests that anger and fear, in the context of an implicit racial appeal, influence whites’ views about race differently”— “anger and fear, in the context of an implicit racial appeal”
noteanger —distinct-from→ fear — THE DISTINCTION THE PAPER EXISTS TO DRAW, and it is drawn against its own field: the charge is that research on racial priming conflates the two under `negative emotion`.— “Our theory suggests that anger and fear, in the context of an implicit racial appeal, influence whites’ views about race differently”
