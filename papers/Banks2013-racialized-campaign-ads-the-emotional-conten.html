phenomenonracial priming by implicit racial appeals
claim-typemechanism
transferfrom psychology (emotion / memory research): the paper takes Bower and Forgas's theory of emotional priming — a psychological account of mood-congruent recall — and uses it as the explanatory engine for a political-communication effect — “We rely on Bower and Forgas’s (2001) theory of emotional priming to argue that specific emotions from IRAs play an important role in activating racial attitudes.”
mechanismanger and symbolic racism are stored as tightly linked associations in memory, so arousing anger activates the racial belief system and makes it available for the subsequent policy judgement — “We argue that anger and symbolic racism (SR) are tightly linked in memory, and evoking this emotion in the context of an IRA should then activate this belief system and cause a shift in opposition to racial policies.”
mechanismemotional (mood-congruent) priming: being returned to the emotional state in which an attitude was learned makes that attitude easier to recall — “We rely on Bower and Forgas’s (2001) theory of emotional priming to argue that specific emotions from IRAs play an important role in activating racial attitudes.”
mechanismbecause contemporary racial discourse is dominated by blame attribution, and blame is the appraisal that produces anger, anger rather than fear is the emotion bonded to whites' racial predispositions — “If contemporary discussions about race are dominated by blame rhetoric, we suspect that anger should be strongly linked to whites’ negative racial predispositions.”
relation — “We rely on Bower and Forgas’s (2001) theory of emotional priming to argue that specific emotions from IRAs play an important role in activating racial attitudes.”
relation — “In fact, Banks and Valentino (2012) observe such an effect; they find that anger (independent of racial or political content), and not disgust or fear, is highly correlated with, and can in fact activate, SR.”
relation — “However, Banks and Valentino (2012) find similar results on an adult national sample.”
differs inanger → fear — WHICH ONE IS BOUND TO RACIAL ATTITUDES IN MEMORY - the paper's claim is that they are not interchangeable negatives, and that the difference is associative rather than a matter of degree — “anger and fear, in the context of an implicit racial appeal”

Content tags
content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingAn implicit racial appeal that arouses anger significantly amplifies the effect of symbolic racism on opposition to racial policies among whites. — “The interaction between the anger condition and SR is positive, substantively large, and statistically significant.”
findingAn implicit racial appeal that arouses fear does not amplify the effect of symbolic racism, so the priming effect is specific to anger rather than to negative emotion in general. — “Conversely, the fear condition does not boost the effect of SR on racial policy opinions.”
findingAmong whites low in symbolic racism, anger moved policy opinion in the opposite direction, making racial liberals more supportive of racial policies. — “Looking at whites low in SR, we find that the effect of anger is negative and significant. This result suggests that anger also motivates racial liberals to be more supportive of racial policies.”
findingThe marginal effect of anger on racial policy opinion grows monotonically with symbolic racism while the marginal effect of fear stays flat across the same range. — “As we suspect, the marginal effect of anger on racial policy opinions increases as SR increases. The marginal effect of fear is relatively flat across levels of SR.”
findingThe emotion manipulation worked: open-ended responses in the anger ad condition showed significantly more intense anger than both the control and the fear conditions. — “Participants in the anger condition expressed significantly more intense anger relative to subjects in the control condition (β = .44; p ≥ .001) and respondents in the fear condition (β = .34; p ≥ .001).”
findingOverall, the experiment shows anger is the emotional channel through which an implicit racial appeal primes symbolic racism. — “In summary, our experimental results show that anger from an IRA primes SR, while fear has no effect on SR.”
quantity113 — total number of experimental subjects in wave 2, of whom 85 were white — “Our total sample size was 113 and included 85 whites.”
quantity65 percent — share of the student sample who were liberals (a stated limitation of the convenience sample) — “We obtained good variation on gender (47-percent male), but liberals were overrepresented in the sample (65 percent).”
quantity59 percent — retention/response rate from wave 1 (190 subjects) to wave 2 — “Wave 1 had 190 subjects, while 113 participated in wave 2. Our response rate was 59 percent.”
quantityβ = .44; p ≥ .001 — intensity of expressed anger in the anger-ad condition relative to control, in the manipulation check — “Participants in the anger condition expressed significantly more intense anger relative to subjects in the control condition (β = .44; p ≥ .001) and respondents in the fear condition (β = .34; p ≥ .001).”
quantityβ = .57; p ≥ .001 — intensity of expressed fear in the fear-ad condition relative to control — “In the fear condition, respondents expressed an intense amount of fear (β = .57; p ≥ .001) relative to the control group.”
variableemotional condition of the ad (anger vs fear vs control) (independent) — “Anger and Fear are dummy variables, where 1 = if they were in the treatment condition and 0 = if they were in the control condition.”
variablesymbolic racism (pretest racial predisposition) (moderator) — “In our model, we regressed Racial Policy Opinions on emotion dummies (Anger and Fear), SR, and the interaction between the two, controlling for Gender and Age.”
variableracial policy opinions (opposition to racial policies) (dependent) — “Racial Policy Opinions ranges from 0 to 1, where the higher value corresponds with the more conservative response.”
variableage (control) — “Age is a continuous variable and ranges from 18 to 38 years old.”
definitionimplicit racial appeal (IRA) — “An IRA appears in a nonracial manner; it omits racial nouns or adjectives such as “black” or “racial” and instead consists of negative images of blacks (Mendelberg 2001).”
definitionsymbolic racism (SR) — “Sears and Kinder define this belief system as an amalgam of anti-black affect and a violation of core American values like individual initiative.”
definitionracial priming — “Mendelberg (2001) characterizes racial priming as a political appeal wherein a message’s racial content is unrecognized by the audience, thereby allowing the cue to effectively activate whites’ racial attitudes.”
definitionappraisal theory of emotion — “The appraisal theory of emotion allows scholars to differentiate between discrete emotions and understand their likely antecedents and consequences.”
definitionanger (as distinguished from fear by its appraisals) — “People experience anger when they feel threatened and are certain who is blameworthy for the threatening action (Lazarus 1991).”
