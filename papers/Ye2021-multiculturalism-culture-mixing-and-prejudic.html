phenomenonpriming of cultural diversity models
claim-typemechanism
mechanismReading a societal diversity model first elicits an emotional reaction, and that emotional reaction is what carries the prime through to intergroup attitudes and identity — “Thus, we propose that priming societal models that relate to outgroups could elicit emotional responses; specifically, especially among a young university student population, multicultural primes might lead to more positive emotions than assimilation primes, which could serve as the underlying processes that explain their effects on attitudes toward outgroups.”
mechanismA multicultural frame lowers the perceived threat to the local culture, which raises the perceived compatibility of the two cultures and so reduces disgust at their mixing — “It is likely that to the degree that multiculturalism reduced Hongkongers’ perceived sense of threat, it enhanced the perceived compatibility of these cultures, as shown by reduced disgust toward mixed cultural symbols and increased integration of Chinese and Hong Kong identities; while assimilation had the opposite effects.”
relation — “This is dissimilar from previous research among Turkish minorities in the Netherlands, where priming multiculturalism tended to decrease outgroup evaluations relative to ingroup evaluations (Verkuyten, 2005).”
relation — “The instructions and Multiculturalism priming material were adapted from previous research in the United States (Todd and Galinsky, 2012) with content modified through group discussions to fit the Hong Kong cultural context.”
relation — “The priming material was adapted from a previous study about assimilating into superordinate identities (Stone and Crisp, 2007) and modified to emphasize the assimilation process.”
relation — “The results are an interesting complement to findings of Morrison et al. (2010), who found that White Americans’ negative reaction to multiculturalism primes (vs. color-blindness) was mediated by their sense of symbolic threat; here, we find that Hong Kong participants’ negative reaction to an assimilation prime (vs. multiculturalism) was mediated by more negative emotional reactions.”
differs inmulticulturalism → assimilation — WHETHER CULTURAL DIFFERENCE IS APPRECIATED OR IGNORED - the paper states the axis rather than leaving it to be inferred, and puts colorblindness on the ignoring side with assimilation — “a preference toward ignoring cultural differences”
effect directionmulticulturalism → social distancing — reduces — “indirectly reduced social distancing from Mainland Chinese”
effect directionassimilation → social distancing — increases — “the assimilation prime had the opposite indirect effects”
replicationcontested
limitationBoth studies were underpowered for the effect sizes assumed. — “The two studies were slightly underpowered due to limited sample size.”
limitationConvenience sample of Hong Kong university students limits generalisation. — “In addition, the two studies also employed convenience sampling methods among Hong Kong university students, which may not generalize to all sectors of the Hong Kong population.”
limitationIdentity measures were placed after the manipulation, so they could not be used as moderators. — “In these studies, we conceptualized identity measures as dependent variables and included them immediately after the experimental manipulations, precluding their use as individualdifference moderators of reactions.”
limitationBecause the two identity styles were outcomes rather than predictors, their distinct functions could not be separated. — “Moreover, as the two multicultural identity styles were examined as outcomes rather than predictors, the distinctions between hybrid identity style and alternating identity style that can be found through controlling for one another (Ward et al., 2018) and thus more complex features of these cultural identities could not be thoroughly examined.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingIn Study 1 the multiculturalism prime lowered desire for social distance from Mainland Chinese relative to no prime, but left disgust toward culture mixing and national identity strength unchanged. — “In summary, compared to a noprime control, the multiculturalism prime significantly decreased social distancing but had no statistically significant effects on disgust toward Hong Kong-Mainland culture mixing or national identity strength.”
findingIn Study 2 the three primes produced direct effects only on emotion and on ethnic Chinese identity strength; social distancing, disgust and both identity styles showed no direct priming effect. — “Summarizing the above, we found a significant effect of priming conditions only on emotions and ethnic identity strength, but not on HIS, AIS, social distancing, or disgust toward culture mixing.”
findingRelative to control, the multiculturalism prime raised positive emotion and thereby indirectly cut disgust toward culture mixing and raised ethnic identity strength, hybrid identity style and alternating identity style, with a marginal indirect reduction in social distancing. — “Compared to control, the multiculturalism prime, through increasing positive emotion, marginally reduced social distancing and significantly reduced disgust; it also significantly increased ethnic identity strength, HIS, and AIS.”
findingThe assimilation prime produced the mirror-image indirect pattern, lowering positive emotion and thereby increasing social distancing and disgust while decreasing ethnic identity strength and both multicultural identity styles. — “On the contrary, through reducing positive emotion, the assimilation prime, significantly increased social distancing and disgust; it also significantly decreased ethnic identity strength, HIS, and AIS.”
findingReading that Hong Kong is being assimilated into China directly weakened participants' ethnic Chinese identity compared with the control condition, a defensive response. — “Post hoc tests with Tukey correction showed that ethnic identity strength decreased under the assimilation prime (M = 3.38, SD = 1.76) compared to both the control (M = 3.93, SD = 1.25)”
findingBreaking the affect composite apart, the beneficial multiculturalism effects ran specifically through pride, happiness and interest. — “Here, the exploratory analyses suggested that the positive effects of multiculturalism priming went through increasing pride, happiness and feeling interested”
quantityn = 118 — final analysed sample of Study 1, split 57 primed and 61 no-prime — “The final sample for data analysis consisted of 118 participants (87 females, 30 males and 1 unreported, Mage = 21.69, SD = 4.69) with 57 participants in the priming condition and 61 participants in the no prime condition.”
quantity168 — sample size of Study 2, which the authors call slightly underpowered — “Given the sample size of 168 participants, this study is therefore slightly underpowered.”
quantityt(116) = −2.19, p = 0.03, d = 0.40 — Study 1 effect of the multiculturalism prime on social distancing — “The results showed that the multiculturalism (MC) priming significantly reduced social distancing compared to the control condition, t(116) = −2.19, p = 0.03, Cohen’s d = 0.40, 95% CI = [0.04,0.77]”
quantityF(2,165) = 15.19, p < 0.001, η2 = 0.16 — overall Study 2 effect of priming condition on positive emotion — “An overall ANOVA predicting positive emotion from the three priming conditions showed that the primes had statistically significant different impact, F(2,165) = 15.19, p < 0.001, η2 = 0.16.”
quantityCohen’s d = 0.93 — size of the positive-emotion gap between the multiculturalism and assimilation prime groups — “Post hoc analyses with Tukey correction showed that participants in the multiculturalism (MC) prime group (M = 1.14, SD = 1.38) had higher positive emotion compared to the assimilation (A) prime group (M = −0.16, SD = 1.42), t = 5.45, p < 0.001, Cohen’s d = 0.93”
quantityω = 0.90 and α = 0.90 — internal reliability of the Study 1 social distancing scale — “The scale yielded excellent reliability, McDonald’s ω = 0.90 and Cronbach’s α = 0.90.”

definitionPositive Emotion (the study's composite affect index) — “To simplify results description, a combination of positive minus negative affect, termed “Positive Emotion,” was calculated by subtracting the mean score of negative affect from positive affect.”
