phenomenonmaximum-likelihood effect sizes that blow up when counts are low, producing large estimates that are not differences— “When the read counts are low or highly variable, the maximum likelihood estimates for the LFCs has high variance, leading to large estimates not representative of true differences, and poor ranking of genes by effect size.”
claim-typemethod— “The proposed method, Approximate Posterior Estimation for generalized linear model, apeglm , has lower bias than previously proposed shrinkage estimators, while still reducing variance for those genes with little information for statistical inference.”
method — evidence— “Here, we propose the use of a heavy-tailed Cauchy prior distribution for effect sizes, which avoids the use of filter thresholds or pseudocounts.”
object of study — evidence— “The difference is usually defined in terms of the logarithmic fold change (LFC) between average expression levels of different conditions.”
research question — evidence— “However, in our analysis, we found that filtering or application of Normal priors each can have drawbacks, either leading to loss of genes with sufficient signal, or overly aggressive shrinkage of true, large LFCs.”
contribution — evidence— “Here, we propose the use of a heavy-tailed Cauchy prior distribution for effect sizes, which avoids the use of filter thresholds or pseudocounts.”
mechanismthe estimate is unreliable exactly where the counts are small, so the maximum likelihood LFC has high variance and produces `LARGE ESTIMATES NOT REPRESENTATIVE OF TRUE DIFFERENCES` - the noise appears as a big effect, not as a small one— “When the read counts are low or highly variable, the maximum likelihood estimates for the LFCs has high variance, leading to large estimates not representative of true differences, and poor ranking of genes by effect size.”
mechanisma heavy tail on the prior is what lets shrinkage be aggressive near zero and gentle far from it: the Cauchy pulls in the noise-driven estimates while leaving genuinely large fold changes where they are— “Here, we propose the use of a heavy-tailed Cauchy prior distribution for effect sizes, which avoids the use of filter thresholds or pseudocounts.”
mechanismswapping a Normal prior for a Cauchy gives heavy tails, so small effects are still shrunk while genuinely large ones survive - shrinkage without flattening— “We extend the basic framework of DESeq2 , a Negative Binomial (NB) generalized linear model (GLM) ( McCullagh and Nelder, 1989 ) with moderated dispersion parameter, by exchanging the Normal distribution as a prior on LFCs with a heavy-tailed Cauchy distribution (a t distribution with 1 degree of freedom).”
mechanismthe prior's scale is fitted by subtracting each estimate's own error variance from its squared value, so the prior learns how much of the observed spread is real— “This equation is motivated by assuming that the MLE β ^ g k follows a Normal distribution around the true value β gk with variance e g k 2 , and that the β gk themselves follow a Normal distribution with mean zero.”
mechanismthe scale is set from a Normal assumption even though the prior itself is Cauchy - the two roles come apart and the authors say the mismatch works in practice— “Although we use a Cauchy prior in apeglm in order to accommodate large effect sizes from potentially only a few genes, we found that setting the scale of the prior by assuming the β gk follow a zero-centered Normal distribution performed well in practice.”
critiques count filtering as practised in edgeR and limma-voom, which removes genes before analysis and can lose those with sufficient signal— “The authors of edgeR ( McCarthy et al. , 2012 ) and limma-voom ( Law et al. , 2014 ) suggest using a filtering rule that removes genes with low scaled counts before statistical analysis”
critiques DESeq2's adaptive Normal prior, which shrinks true large fold changes too aggressively— “DESeq2 applies an adaptive Normally distributed prior, to produce a shrinkage estimator for the LFC for each gene.”
extends the empirical Bayes shrinkage tradition itself - the method is offered as a replacement for the prior, not for the framework— “The proposed method, Approximate Posterior Estimation for generalized linear model, apeglm , has lower bias than previously proposed shrinkage estimators, while still reducing variance for those genes with little information for statistical inference.”
senselogarithmic fold change - AS ESTIMATED, NOT AS DEFINED. The definition is a ratio of average expression between conditions; the estimate at low counts is `large ... not representative of true differences`. THE SAME SYMBOL NAMES A QUANTITY AND A NUMBER THAT DOES NOT MEASURE IT— “When the read counts are low or highly variable, the maximum likelihood estimates for the LFCs has high variance, leading to large estimates not representative of true differences, and poor ranking of genes by effect size.”
senseshrinkage - the family of corrections, and this paper's point is that the family members fail in OPPOSITE directions: a filter loses true signal, a Normal prior flattens true large effects. WHICH ERROR YOU GET IS A CHOICE, AND USUALLY AN UNEXAMINED ONE— “However, in our analysis, we found that filtering or application of Normal priors each can have drawbacks, either leading to loss of genes with sufficient signal, or overly aggressive shrinkage of true, large LFCs.”
sensepseudocount - a correction that `must be ADAPTED PER DATASET`. The paper's remedy is to remove the parameter rather than to tune it, which is the same move a field makes when it replaces an inferred name with a measured one— “One approach is to introduce filtering thresholds and pseudocounts to exclude or moderate estimated LFCs. Filtering may result in a loss of genes from the analysis with true differences in expression, while pseudocounts provide a limited solution that must be adapted per dataset.”
