phenomenonpriming of cooperation in a public goods game— “Using priming techniques from social psychology, we activate the concept of cooperation and explore the extent to which this intervention brings about changes in people’s voluntary contributions to the public good and self-reported emotional responses.”

transferfrom social psychology: the word-search priming procedure itself, lifted from psychology experiments and applied inside an incentivised economic game where money is at stake— “Using priming techniques from social psychology, we activate the concept of cooperation and explore the extent to which this intervention brings about changes in people’s voluntary contributions to the public good and self-reported emotional responses.”
mechanismPriming works in two steps: the cooperation words first raise women's beliefs about how much the other group members will give, and those raised beliefs then drive their own contribution decisions— “priming works in our subject pool is in two steps: first, positive primes shift females‟ beliefs about the contributions of the other counterparts and then, these beliefs shape their motivation and contribution decisions.”
mechanismThe primes serve as anchors or focal points on which subjects can coordinate, which lowers the strategic risk of contributing and so releases more generous behaviour, particularly among women— “regard primes as anchors or focal points, inducing them to coordinate on more”
mechanismThe word-search puzzle acts as a subconscious reminder of cooperation, raising the accessibility of the concept at the moment of choice— “contributions towards the common resource, as priming would act a subconscious”
builds-on Bargh et al. (2001) word-search priming methodology— “Our procedure of priming subjects follows a methodology commonly used in other psychological experiments (see, for instance, Bargh, et al, 2001).”
builds-on Croson and Gneezy (2009) on gender differences in social preferences— “This is in agreement with the explanation suggested by Croson and Gneezy (2009) that women‟s behaviour is more context dependent than that of men (see also Gilligan (1982)).”
sensepriming - the same formal definition this group uses in the 2015 publication, taken from Hertel & Fiedler: a PROCEDURAL feature, previously activated information acting on the processing of subsequent information. The definition is what makes the import from psychology into economics arguable at all— “Priming, which is formally defined as “the procedural feature that some previously activated information impacts on the processing of subsequent information” (see Hertel & Fiedler, 1998), is a prominent topic in social and cognitive psychology”

no type for thispriming works through a shift in females’ beliefs (this paper, IZA DP 4963, May 2010) → priming does not affect beliefs and works by changing preferences (the same authors' published version, Theory and Decision 2015, in this corpus as drouvelis2015-cooperation-priming) — SAME AUTHORS, SAME EXPERIMENT, OPPOSITE MECHANISM - AND THE SCHEMA HAS NO TYPE FOR IT. The 2015 paper reports two waves, the first run in 2009 with 51 and 54 subjects, which is this paper's N. So this working paper is wave one and the publication is wave one plus wave two. In wave one the raw contribution difference is NOT significant (Wilcoxon two-sided p = 0.249) and the effect appears only with a gender control at the 10% level, carried by a four-cell split of 105 subjects (19, 25, 32, 29); the mechanism is stated as two steps through female beliefs. **In the pooled publication the raw difference IS significant (p = 0.044 one-sided), there is `insufficient evidence to suggest that the effect of priming varies significantly across.. gender`, and `priming does not affect subjects' beliefs`, which is precisely what licenses its claim that priming works through PREFERENCES.** So the whole two-step belief mechanism, and the gender effect that motivated it, are absent from the published version of the same experiment. Neither paper is being called wrong here: more data arrived and a subgroup result did not survive it.
