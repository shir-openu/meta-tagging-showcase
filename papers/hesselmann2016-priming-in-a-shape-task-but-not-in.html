phenomenonunconscious priming under continuous flash suppression— “Here we tested the hypothesis that the potentially dorsal-stream-based analysis of prime-stimulus elongation during CFS affects the categorization of manipulable target objects.”
claim-typenull-result— “In two behavioral experiments, we found evidence for priming in a shape task, but none for priming in a category task, when prime stimuli were rendered invisible using CFS.”
method — evidence— “We determined sample size based on a recent study by Almeida et al. (2014).”
mechanismThe task set tunes unconscious processing: a category task desensitizes the pathways coding prime elongation while a shape task, needing only attention to basic visual features, sensitizes them and so lets the priming effect emerge— “Speculatively, the category task might have desensitized perceptual pathways related to the processing of prime elongation and thus diminished category-by-shape priming; by contrast, the shape task—requiring only attention to basic visual features—sensitized perceptual pathways and boosted category-by-shape priming.”
mechanismAttentional sensitization by task set raises the sensitivity of task-relevant pathways and lowers that of task-irrelevant ones, which is the process offered for why priming appears in one task and not the other— “According to this model, attentional sensitization of unconscious processing by task sets is achieved by enhancing the sensitivity of task-relevant pathways and reducing the sensitivity of task-irrelevant pathways (Kiefer & Martens, 2010).”
contradicts Almeida et al. (2014) elongation-based category priming— “As in Experiment 1, we found no evidence supporting the notion of elongation-based unconscious priming in a category task, as proposed by Almeida et al. (2014).”
contradicts Almeida et al. (2008), Almeida et al. (2010) and Sakuraba et al. (2012) tool-priming effects— “In contrast to previous CFS studies (Almeida et al., 2008; Almeida et al., 2010; Sakuraba et al., 2012), we observed no priming effects in the tool-versus-animal categorization task.”
builds-on Gayet et al. (2014) feature-level account of interocular suppression— “From a broader perspective, our finding that prime shape can escape CFS suppression and affect subsequent manual responses to target stimuli appears to support the notion that interocularly suppressed stimuli are processed at the level of features and coarse feature configurations (Gayet et al., 2014).”

findingIn Experiment 1 neither elongated tool primes nor elongated animal primes speeded categorization of elongated tool targets relative to nonelongated animal primes; the 5 ms and 4 ms differences ran in the predicted direction but were not significant.— “While the observed RT differences were numerically in the predicted direction (5 and 4 ms, i.e., facilitatory), they were not significant (one-sided paired t tests), t(22) = 0.56, p = 0.290, and t(22) = 0.52, p = 0.305.”
findingIn Experiment 2 the category task again showed no elongation-based priming, and this time the reaction-time differences ran opposite to the predicted direction.— “The observed RT differences were not in the predicted direction and not significant (one-sided paired t tests), t(26) ¼ (cid:3)0.01, p = 0.498, and t(26) ¼ (cid:3)0.82, p = 0.209.”
findingWith the same stimuli and the same observers, a shape-discrimination task did produce priming: shape-congruent primes speeded responses to elongated tool targets, with a reversed and less robust difference for nonelongated tool targets.— “We found facilitatory priming by shape-congruent versus shape-incongruent primes for elongated tool targets, and a reversed (albeit not robust) RT difference for nonelongated tool targets.”
findingAcross both experiments there is no support for unconscious category-by-shape priming under CFS, but suppressed prime shape does affect shape discrimination of tool targets.— “In two experiments, we found no evidence in support of the hypothesis that elongated prime stimuli, unconsciously presented under CFS, facilitate the…
findingThe primes were genuinely invisible: forced-choice category discrimination of primes rated invisible was at chance level, 48.87% in Experiment 1.
findingA control analysis found the correlation between prime-target orientation difference and reaction time to be larger for tool than for animal targets, so stimulus orientation may have modulated the shape-task result.
findingThe pattern is read as evidence that stimuli suppressed by CFS are represented only as features and coarse feature configurations, not at a category or semantic level.
findingBecause earlier studies used only elongated tool targets and only nonelongated animal targets, participants in those studies could have solved the task by shape rather than by category, which would explain their apparent category priming.
