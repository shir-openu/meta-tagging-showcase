phenomenoncategory-specific semantic feature processing in cross-modal priming— “The current paper aimed to address the role of feature-based processing in the categorization of fruits/vegetables compared to the well-investigated artificial category of tools.”
claim-typemechanism— “These findings provide strong evidence that color is an integral property to the categorization of fruits/vegetables, thus substantiating the claim that feature-based processing guides as a function of semantic category.”
method — evidence— “We report the results of two cross–modal priming experiments in which object images are used to prime lexical decisions on written words.”
mechanismAltering a feature that is constitutive of a category's semantic representation is processed as a semantic violation, which in turn increases N400 negativity and reduces priming— “We hypothesized that a modification of a feature integral to the representation of a given category would be processed as a semantic violation, thus eliciting an increase in negativity in the N400 time window.”
mechanismRetrospective semantic matching of target to prime operates late in the response distribution, producing the delayed condition effect for tools— “Such a shift in RT distribution could potentially be attributed to retrospective retrieval processes, where the target is semantically matched to the prime”
builds-on prior report (ref. 56) that N400 amplitude is graded with prime-target relatedness— “In addition, a graded modulation of the N400 component was reported to reflect a similarly graded modulation of prime-target relatedness [ 56 ] this finding is particularly pertinent to the current study, which aims to compare the effect of a prime subjected to visual feature modification on subsequent target processing.”
senseTHE MEASURE IS A LOSS OF PRIMING, AND THAT IS THE INSTRUMENT. Modify a feature of the prime image and see how much priming survives: `a REDUCTION IN PRIMING for COLOR-modified NATURAL entities and ORIENTATION-modified ARTIFICIAL entities`. The size of the loss reads off how integral the broken feature is to the concept - so the informative quantity is the effect's ABSENCE, and a corpus that records only that priming occurred would lose the finding entirely— “Reaction time analysis indicated a reduction in priming for color-modified natural entities and orientation-modified artificial entities. Standard event-related potentials (ERP) analysis was performed, in addition to linear classification. For natural entities, a N400 effect at central channel sites was observed for the color-modified…
sensePRIMING AS AN INSTRUMENT, IN A LITERATURE THAT IS NOT ABOUT PRIMING. The question is which features a semantic category depends on - a question about conceptual structure and about category-specific deficits in patients - and priming is borrowed to answer it. This is the distinction pickering2008 asks its own field to keep (Q-744): an investigation USING priming rather than ABOUT it. Here the borrowing is complete enough that the conclusion mentions categorization and not priming at all— “These findings provide strong evidence that color is an integral property to the categorization of fruits/vegetables, thus substantiating the claim that feature-based processing guides as a function of semantic category”
senseTHE HYPOTHESIS IS WRITTEN WITH BOTH ARMS AND ITS ALTERNATIVE, BEFORE THE RESULTS: `if color and orientation are asymmetrically important for natural and manmade objects, respectively, then color-modified primes should render a more severe priming reduction for fruits/vegetables, while orientation-modified primes would shrink priming for tools` - and then the rival, that if fruits and vegetables overlap with the artificial category, orientation-modified primes should ALSO reduce priming there. Two distinguishable predictions, stated in advance, with the data able to separate them— “Our hypothesis is as follows: if color and orientation are asymmetrically important for natural (fruits/vegetables) and manmade (tools) objects, respectively, then color-modified primes should render a more severe priming reduction for fruits/vegetables, while orientation-modified primes would shrink priming for tools”
senseTHE NEURAL INDEX IS A SEMANTIC-VIOLATION MARKER, WHICH IS A STRONGER CLAIM THAN A SLOWDOWN: a colour-modified fruit `should yield a SEMANTIC VIOLATION as indexed by an increase in N400 amplitude`, and it does. The behavioural half says the concept is harder to reach; the N400 says the modified image is treated as semantically WRONG for its category, which is what distinguishes an integral feature from a merely useful one— “These findings are in line with our hypothesis that a color-modification for fruits/vegetables should yield a semantic violation as indexed by an increase in N400 amplitude”
controla CROSS-MODAL design, so that prime and target never share a surface form — held — rules out that the priming effect is form-based - orthographic or phonological overlap between prime and target rather than access to shared meaning— “Also, the cross-modal design was meant to ensure that any generated effect would be genuinely semantic in nature, engaging access across domains.”
controlthe keys assigned to word and nonword responses were counterbalanced across participants — held — rules out that the lexical decision effect is a hand or key preference— “Keys associated with word and nonword trials were counterbalanced across participants.”
controlsix practice trials, plus the first experimental trial treated as practice — held — rules out that the first responses reflect unfamiliarity with the task rather than the manipulation— “Participants were given six practice trials to familiarize themselves with the task, in addition to the first trial being considered as a practice trial.”

findingModifying a prime image's color reduced priming for fruits/vegetables, while modifying its orientation reduced priming for tools, producing a category-by-feature crossover in reaction times.— “Reaction time analysis indicated a reduction in priming for color-modified natural entities and orientation-modified artificial entities.”
findingFor natural targets, color-modified primes gave significantly less priming than both orientation-modified and normal primes.— “For the natural category, color–modified primes generated a significant reduction in priming compared to orientation–modified (t (56) = -2.12, p = .034) and normal primes (t (56) = -3.87, p < .001)”
findingFor artificial (tool) targets the pattern reversed: orientation-modified primes penalised priming more than color-modified or normal primes.
findingIn the EEG experiment, color-modified primes elicited a larger N400-window negativity than normal primes for fruits/vegetables over left fronto-central channels.
findingNo ERP condition difference emerged for the artificial category, contrary to the behavioural result.
findingMultivariate linear classification nonetheless separated all three prime conditions for the natural category at left fronto-central searchlight channels.
findingThe condition effect for tools appeared only in the slower reaction-time quartiles, a distributional shift not seen for fruits/vegetables.
