phenomenonmasked response (action) priming— “In the experiments reported here, we measured the amount of priming from metacontrast-masked stimuli while manipulating the temporal dynamics of conscious perception.”
claim-typeexistence— “We observed priming with identical time course for reportable and invisible prime stimuli, despite qualitative changes in the masking time course.”
method — evidence— “Masking and priming were studied in tasks that differed only with respect to which stimulus serves as target, keeping stimulus conditions identical.”
extends the neural accumulator model of Hanes and Schall— “It is similar to the neural accumulator model of Hanes and Schall (26) but includes saturation in the stochastic accumulation process”
contradicts Milner and Goodale's theory of separate perception and action systems— “These findings do not agree with central notions of Milner and Goodale ’s theory (3), such as the role of color processing in the action system and the importance of hard-wired connections to effectors”
builds-on Macknik and Livingstone's single-cell observation of V1 after-discharge— “In single-cell recordings, Macknik and Livingstone (40) have observed an after-discharge of cells in V1 some 100 ms after prime offset, which seems crucial for con- scious perception because it is suppressed when a mask follows”
senseTHE DOUBLE DISSOCIATION ITSELF, and it is two CURVES rather than two conditions: `within the SOA range from 42 to 98 ms: the amount of priming INCREASED, but recognition accuracy DETERIORATED with SOA`. No level of invisibility is asserted anywhere - the argument is carried entirely by the opposite slopes, which is the criterion Schmidt2011 later proposed as a replacement for requiring zero sensitivity— “For 42-ms primes and masks, there was a remarkable double dissociation within the SOA range from 42 to 98 ms: the amount of priming increased, but recognition accuracy deteriorated with SOA”
sense`DIFFERENT TEMPORAL LAWS` - the general form of the claim, and it is a statement about SHAPES rather than about magnitudes: conscious perception and unconscious behavioural effects of the same masked stimuli do not merely differ in size, they are governed by different functions of time. Seventh record this sitting where a time course is the load-bearing variable, and the first where two time courses ARE the finding— “Over a wide range of time courses, we show that conscious perception and unconscious behavioral effects of masked visual stimuli obey different temporal laws”
senseSUBLIMINAL IN WHICH RESPECT - a precision most records in this corpus do not make: `prime stimuli were NOT subliminal in the sense that their presence or absence could not be detected, but the CRITICAL PRIME FEATURE remained perfectly invisible`. Something was plainly there to be seen; the attribute that did the priming was not. `Subliminal` is a predicate of a FEATURE here, not of a stimulus, and the difference decides what the effect is evidence for— “Note that prime stimuli were not subliminal in the sense that their presence or absence could not be detected, but the critical prime feature remained perfectly invisible”
sensethe priming time course is INDIFFERENT to whether the prime could be reported: `We observed priming with IDENTICAL TIME COURSE for reportable and invisible prime stimuli, despite qualitative changes in the masking time course.` The masking function changes shape - type A to type B - and the priming function does not follow it. That is a stronger result than a difference in size at one delay, because it survives a change in the shape of the other curve— “We observed priming with identical time course for reportable and invisible prime stimuli, despite qualitative changes in the masking time course”
controlthe slope prediction from the mean-process approximation was re-derived by MONTE CARLO SIMULATION of the stochastic process — held — rules out that the analytic result is an artefact of the mean-process approximation and does not hold for the stochastic process it approximates— “Because there is no guarantee that results based on the mean process approximation are close to those for the stochastic process (27), we checked the slope prediction by Monte Carlo simulation.”
controlpriming time courses were compared under two different masking types — held — rules out that masking and priming reflect the SAME underlying process - a dissociation between the two time courses cannot be produced by a single mechanism— “Such a dissociation cannot be accounted for by assuming that masking and priming reflect the same underlying process (see Supporting Text II).”
