claim-typemechanism— “The results of both experiments support a retrieval model involving a dependence between separate successive decisions about whether each of the two strings is a word”
method — evidence— “Two experiments are reported in which 5s were presented two strings of letters simultaneously, with one string displayed visually above the other”
contradicts Schaeffer and Wallace (1969, 1970)— “Our reasoning suggests, furthermore, that the findings of Schaeffer and Wallace (1969, 1970) may not have resulted solely from a comparison of word meanings”

sense**THE FIRST MEASUREMENT OF THE EFFECT, WITH ITS ERROR BAR:** `"Yes" responses averaged 85 ± 19 msec, faster for pairs of ASSOCIATED words than for pairs of UNASSOCIATED words, F (1, 11) = 20.6, p < .001.` Two strings shown at once, one above the other, decide whether both are words. **There is no prime and no target** - the design is simultaneous and symmetric, which is worth holding onto, because everything downstream assumes a sequence and an asymmetry that this experiment did not have— “"Yes" responses averaged 85 ± 19 msec, faster for pairs of associated words than for pairs of unassociated words, F (1, 11) = 20.6, p < .001”
sense**AND THE PAPER DECLINES THE INFERENCE ITS OWN NAME WOULD LATER REST ON:** `The effect of association on RT DOES NOT NECESSARILY IMPLY THAT THE MEANING OF A WORD IS RETRIEVED to make a lexical decision.` The founding result of semantic priming, and its authors say in the discussion that it does not establish semantic retrieval. **The word `semantic` in `semantic priming` is a claim this sentence explicitly withholds** - and the withholding is not hedging, it is followed by a worked alternative in which association affects RT through the organisation of memory locations rather than through meaning— “The effect of association on RT does not necessarily imply that the meaning of a word is retrieved to make a lexical decision”
sense**TWO MECHANISMS, NO TEST, AND THE PAPER SAYS SO RATHER THAN PICKING:** `The present data do not provide a direct test between this location-shifting model and the spreading-excitation model.` It then does something better than choosing - it asks what survives the choice: `REGARDLESS OF WHETHER spreading excitation, location shifting, comparison of meanings, or some other process is involved, the effects of association appear limited neither to semantic decisions nor to same-different judgments.` **The result is stated at the level the data supports and the mechanism is left open**, which is exactly what neely1977 spends 28 pages building an experiment to settle six years later— “Regardless of whether spreading excitation, location shifting, comparison of meanings, or some other process is involved, the effects of association appear limited neither to semantic decisions nor to same-different judgments”
sense**AND THE LIMITATION IS STATED IN ONE FLAT SENTENCE WITH NO SOFTENING:** `At present we do not have ways to test all the possible explanations of these effects.` No `future work will`, no `it remains to be seen`. **This corpus has 539 records and I have read few limitation sentences this short** - and it belongs to the paper that started the literature, which is the opposite of the order one would expect confidence to arrive in— “At present we do not have ways to test all the possible explanations of these effects”
controlhalf of the practice and test trials required a `yes` response — held — rules out that a reaction-time difference is a response bias - subjects favouring one key because it is used more often— “Half of the practice trials and test trials therefore required "yes" responses.”
controleach subject was randomly assigned one of the lists in one of the orders, so that every list-order combination was used exactly once — held — rules out that the effect depends on a particular list or a particular presentation order— “Each S was then randomly assigned one of the lists presented in one of the orders, so that each list in each order was used for exactly one 5.”

conceptassociation — the manipulated variable - **`degree of association`, taken from free-association norms**, not from any theory of meaning. That is deliberate and it is why the caveat later in the paper is available to be made: an associative norm is a fact about what people say, and nothing about it requires that meaning was retrieved
conceptdependence — the paper's actual thesis, and the word in its title: the two word/nonword decisions are **not independent**. Recognising one string changes what happens when the other is recognised. Note how much weaker and how much more defensible this is than `meaning spreads` - it claims only that the operations interact
