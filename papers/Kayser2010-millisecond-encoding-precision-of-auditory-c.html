transferfrom information theory: Shannon's mutual information, defined over a set of stimuli and a set of responses, is imported as the direct measure of what a spike train tells an observer about the sound — “We thus used Shannon information [ I ( S ; R ); Eq. 1 ] to directly quantify the information carried by temporal spike patterns sampled at different temporal precision.”
transferfrom information theory: Shannon mutual information estimated directly, chosen because it bounds all the information a response could carry rather than what a particular decoder can extract — “Shannon information measures the reduction of uncertainty about the stimulus gained by observing a single trial response (averaged over all stimuli and responses) and was computed from many (average 55) repeats of the same stimulus sequence by using the direct method”
transferfrom visual neurophysiology: the finding that response precision scales with stimulus speed, imported as the prediction that the auditory answer should depend on stimulus dynamics — “Our results reveal a dependency of neural response precision on the prominent stimulus time scales: The average PSTH-derived response precision ( Figs. 1 D and 2 D ) was finer, and the information loss with decreasing response precision ( Figs. 1 E and 2 E ) was larger, for the chord stimulus, which had a much shorter autocorrelation time scale than the natural sounds (compare Figs. 1 and 2 B ).”
mechanismFaster stimulus dynamics drive more rapid membrane depolarizations, whose steeper threshold crossings produce more precisely timed spikes, which in turn carry stimulus information at finer temporal precision. — “On the basis of this hypothesis, our results suggest that stimuli with faster dynamics elicit more rapid membrane depolarizations and, hence, more precisely timed spikes that, in return, then encode stimulus information at high temporal precision.”

mechanism — “Yet, the width of PSTH peaks does not fully establish the temporal scale at which spikes carry information, because, for example, spikes might encode stimulus information depending on where they occur within a PSTH peak. findingRegistering auditory cortex spike times at a resolution coarser than a few milliseconds significantly reduces the stimulus information that can be recovered. — “We investigated the dependence of stimulus information on the temporal precision at which spike times were registered and found that registering spikes at a precision coarser than a few milliseconds significantly reduced the encoded information.”
findingThe effective coding resolution in this dataset lies between 1 ms and roughly 4-6 ms, since degrading precision from 1 to 2 ms cost no information but 4-6 ms did. — “This result demonstrates that for the present dataset the temporal precision of information coding is coarser than one, but finer than ≈4–6 ms.”
findingNearly all of the stimulus information resides in the finely timed modulation of firing rate, with higher-order correlations between spike times contributing negligibly. — “Our results also revealed that basically the entire information in the spike train is provided by the time-dependent firing rate and that higher order correlations between spike times add negligibly to this.”
findingResponse precision and the cost of coarsening it were greater for the fast-varying random chords than for natural sounds, so coding precision tracks the stimulus's own time scale. — “Our results reveal a dependency of neural response precision on the prominent stimulus time scales: The average PSTH-derived response precision ( Figs. 1 D and 2 D ) was finer, and the information loss with decreasing response precision ( Figs. 1 E and 2 E ) was larger, for the chord stimulus, which had a much shorter autocorrelation time scale than the natural sounds (compare Figs. 1 and 2 B ).”
findingLinear stimulus decoding underestimates temporal coding precision relative to direct mutual-information estimates, which may explain earlier reports of coarse cortical coding. — “We found that linear decoding resulted in smaller losses of stimulus information when degrading temporal precision and, hence, would have indicated a coarser encoding time scale than that found with direct estimates of the stimulus information ( SI Results and Fig. S3 ).”

findinginformation loss was 5%, 11% and 20% at effective precisions of 4, 6 and 12 ms — “Across neurons the information loss amounted to 5, 11, and 20% (median) for effective precisions of 4, 6, and 12 ms, respectively, demonstrating that a considerable fraction of the encoded stimulus information is lost when sampling responses at coarser resolution.”
