phenomenoncontinuous learning intention in STEAM education— “Therefore, this study aims to explore the latent factors that can enhance STEAM continuous learning intention.”
claim-typemechanism— “The results showed that perceived usability directly influenced learning intention and strengthened the relationship between learning attitudes and intention.”
method — evidence— “The data collection period was from February to May 2021, and 145 questionnaire responses were collected, yielding 141 valid responses.”
transferfrom cognitive psychology (short-term memory research): cognitive load theory, originally developed from short-term memory experiments, is imported as the cognitive-domain measure in a STEAM education acceptance model— “Cognitive load theory was first proposed by a cognitive psychologist based on empirical research on short-term memory ability”
mechanismsuccessfully carrying out the hands-on STEAM activity in the psychomotor phase raises the learner's attitude and motivation, which in turn produces the intention to keep learning— “In the third phase (affective domain), the successful implementation experience of STEAM activity in the second phase would increase the positive attitude and enhance the motivation towards continuous learning intention in the fourth phase.”
mechanisma heavy cognitive load depresses learning performance because a learner cannot process the complexity and volume of the incoming information all at once— “In the second phase (psychomotor domain), a heavy cognitive load may negatively affect learning performance because individuals cannot handle the complexity and the infinity of the abundant information at once as an input”
mechanismhigh demand from cognitive processes such as recall, comprehension, application and analysis consumes capacity and thereby lowers the learner's perception of the material's usability— “The high-level load of cognitive processes (such as knowledge recall, comprehension of understanding, application, and analysis) decreases perception”
extends Weng et al. (2018)— “The results show that cognitive load enables learners to easily understand and implement the STEAM task, which extends the findings of Weng et al.”
replicates Davis, 1989; Davis et al., 1989 (TAM)— “The research results confirmed the relationship in Davis’s TAM constructs (Davis, 1989; Davis et al., 1989) that were applied to explain the behavioral intention of STEAM education (Mutambara & Bayaga, 2021; Su, 2019).”

findingPerceived usability had a direct positive effect on continuous learning intention and also strengthened the link between learning attitude and intention.— “The results showed that perceived usability directly influenced learning intention and strengthened the relationship between learning attitudes and intention.”
findingCognitive load had a significant negative direct effect on perceived usability (B = -0.32), so lower load meant higher perceived usability.— “Analysis of the effects of cognitive load (H4) (cognitive load → perceived usability) showed that the variable had significant negative direct effects on perceived usability (B = − 0.32, p < 0.01).”
findingARCS motivation had a positive and significant effect on learning attitude (B = 0.48).— “Regarding the effects of Keller’s ARCS theory, H5 (ARCS → learning attitude) showed a positive and significant effect (B = 0.48, p < 0.01).”
findingBoth moderation terms were significant: perceived usability moderated attitude to intention (B = 0.104) and ARCS moderated perceived usability to attitude (B = 0.082).— “the interaction term “perceived usability x attitude” is positive and significant for intention (B = 0.104, t-value = 3.13, p = 0.002), and the interaction term “ARCS x perceived usability” is positive and significant for attitude (B = 0.082, t-value = 2.03, p = 0.042).”
findingThe attitude-to-intention relationship was steeper for learners with high perceived usability than for those with low perceived usability.— “Perceived usability strengthens the positive relationship between attitude and intention.”
findingAll three core TAM paths were supported: perceived usability to attitude (0.41), attitude to intention (0.34), and perceived usability to intention (0.55).— “H1 (perceived usability → learning attitude) path showed a significant positive effect (B = 0.41, p < 0.005), H2 (learning attitude→ learning intention) path showed a strong significant effect (B = 0.34, p < 0.01)”
findingNone of the model's paths differed significantly between the elementary-school and university subsamples, so the model held for both age groups.— “The PLS-GMA result showed that all hypotheses in our structural model have no significant difference between elementary student group and university group”
findingThe structural model fitted the data well, with SRMR of 0.069 and RMS_theta of 0.16.— “The SRMR value for the saturated model was 0.069, and the RMS_theta value was 0.16 close to 0, indicating a well-fitting model in this study.”
