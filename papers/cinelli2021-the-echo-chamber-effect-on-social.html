# Abstract

### Significance

We explore the key differences between the main social media platforms and how they are likely to influence information spreading and the formation of echo chambersterm. To assess the different dynamics, we perform a comparative analysis on more than 100 million pieces of content concerning controversial topics (e.g., gun control, vaccination, abortion) from Gabterm, Facebookterm, Redditterm, and Twitter. The analysis focuses on two main dimensions: 1) homophilyterm in the interaction networks and 2) bias in the information diffusion toward like-minded peers. Our results show that the aggregation in homophilic clusters of users dominates online dynamics. However, a direct comparison of news consumption on Facebookterm and Redditterm shows higher segregation on Facebookterm.

Social media may limit the exposure to diverse perspectives and favor the formation of groups of like-minded users framing and reinforcing a shared narrative, that is, echo chambersterm. However, the interaction paradigms among users and feed algorithms greatly vary across social media platforms. This paper explores the key differences between the main social media platforms and how they are likely to influence information spreading and echo chambersterm’ formation. We perform a comparative analysis of more than 100 million pieces of content concerning several controversial topics (e.g., gun control, vaccination, abortion) from Gabterm, Facebookterm, Redditterm, and Twitter. We quantify echo chambersterm over social media by two main ingredients: 1) homophilyterm in the interaction networks and 2) bias in the information diffusion toward like-minded peers. Our results show that the aggregation of users in homophilic clusters dominate online interactions on Facebookterm and Twitter. We conclude the paper by directly comparing news consumption on Facebookterm and Redditterm, finding higher segregation on Facebookterm.

Social media radically changed the mechanism by which we access information and form our opinions. We need to understand how people seek or avoid information and how those decisions affect their behavior, especially when the news cycle—dominated by the disintermediated diffusion of information—alters the way information is consumed and reported on. A recent study limited to Twitter claimed that fake news travels faster than real news. However, a multitude of factors affects information spreading on social media platforms. Online polarizationterm, for instance, may foster misinformation spreading. Our attention span remains limited, and feed algorithms might limit our selection process by suggesting contents similar to the ones we are usually exposed to. Furthermore, users show a tendency to favor information adhering to their beliefs and join groups formed around a shared narrative, that is, echo chambersterm. We can broadly define echo chambersterm as environments in which the opinion, political leaning, or belief of users about a topic gets reinforced due to repeated interactions with peers or sources having similar tendencies and attitudes. Selective exposureterm and confirmation bias (i.e., the tendency to seek information adhering to preexisting opinions) may explain the emergence of echo chambersterm on social media.

According to group polarizationterm theory, an echo chamber can act as a mechanism to reinforce an existing opinion within a group and, as a result, move the entire group toward more extreme positions. Echo chambersterm have been shown to exist in various forms of online media such as blogs, forums, and social media sites. Some studies point out echo chambersterm as an emerging effect of human tendencies, such as selective exposureterm, contagion, and group polarizationterm. However, recently, the effects and the very existence of echo chambersterm have been questioned. This issue is also fueled by the scarcity of comparative studies on social media, especially concerning news consumption. In this context, the debate around echo chambersterm is fundamental to understanding social media’s influence on information consumption and public opinion formation. In this paper, we explore the key differences between social media platforms and how they are likely to influence the formation of echo chambersterm or not. As recently shown in the case of selective exposureterm to news outlets, studies considering multiple platforms can offer a fresh view on long-debated problems. Different platforms offer different interaction paradigms to users, ranging from retweets and mentions on Twitter to likes and comments in groups on Facebookterm, thus triggering very different social dynamics. We introduce an operational definitionterm·mth of echo chambersterm to provide a common methodological ground to explore how different platforms influence their formation. In particular, we operationalize the two common elements that characterize echo chambersterm into observablesterm that can be quantified and empirically measured, namely, 1) the inference of the user’s leaning for a specific topic (e.g., politics, vaccines) and 2) the structure of their social interactions on the platform. Then, we use these elements to assess echo chambersterm’ presence by looking at two different aspects: 1) homophilyterm in interactions concerning a specific topic and 2) bias in information diffusion from like-minded sources. We focus our analysis on multiple platforms: Facebookterm, Twitter, Redditterm, and Gabterm. These platforms present similar features and functionalities (e.g., they all allow social feedback actions such as likes or upvotes) and design (e.g., Gabterm is similar to Twitter) but also distinctive features (e.g., Redditterm is structured in communities of interest called subreddits). Redditterm is one of the most visited websites worldwide (https://www.alexa.com/siteinfo/redditterm.com) and is organized as a forum to collect discussions on a wide range of topics, from politics to emotional support. Gabterm claims to be a social platform aimed at protecting freedom of speech. However, low moderation and regulation on content has resulted in widespread hate speech. For these reasons, it has been repeatedly suspended by its service provider, and its mobile app has been banned from both App and Play stores. Overall, we account for the interactions of more than 1 million active users on the four platforms, for a total of more than 100 million unique pieces of content, including posts and social interactions. Our analysis shows that platforms organized around social networks and news feed algorithms, such as Facebookterm and Twitter, favor the emergence of echo chambersterm.

We conclude the paper by directly comparing news consumption on Facebookterm and Redditterm, finding higher segregation on Facebookterm than on Redditterm.

## Characterizing Echo Chambersterm in Social Media

### Operational Definitions.

First, we need to identify the attitude of users at a microlevel. Then the individual leaning of user can be defined as the average of the leanings of produced contents,Once individual leanings have been inferred, polarizationterm can be defined as a state of the system such that the distribution of leanings, , is concentrated in one or more clusters. A possible example is the case of a single cluster, distinguishable by a single, extreme peak in . Another example is the typical case of topics characterized by positive versus negative stances, in which a bimodal distribution can describe polarizationterm. For instance, if opinions are assumed to be embedded in a one-dimensional space, without loss of generality, as usual for controversial topics, then polarizationterm is characterized by two well-separated peaks in , for positive and negative opinions. In contrast, neutral ones are absent or underrepresented in the population. Note that polarizationterm can happen independently from the structure or the very presence of social interactions. Homophilyterm in social interactions can be quantified by representing interactions as a social network and then analyzing its structure concerning the opinions of the users. Social networks can be reconstructed in different ways from online social media, where links represent social relationships or interactions. Since we are interested in capturing the possible exchange of opinions between users, we assume links as the substrate over which information may flow. For instance, if user follows user on Twitter, user can see tweets produced by user , and there is a flow of information from node to node in the network. When the reconstructed network is directed, we assume the link direction points to potential influencers (opposite of information flow). Actions such as mentions or retweets may convey similar flows. In some cases, direct relations between users are not available in the data, so one needs to assume some proxy for social connections, for example, a link between two users if they comment on the same post on Facebookterm. Crucially, the two elements characterizing the presence of echo chambersterm, polarizationterm and homophilic interactions, should be quantified independently.

### Facebookterm.

We quantify the individual leaning of users considering endorsements in the form of likes to posts. Posts are produced by pages that are labeled in a certain number of categories, and, to each category, we assign a numerical value (e.g., Anti-Vax [+1] or Pro-Vax [–1]). Thus, the user’s leaning is defined as the average of the content leanings of the posts liked by the user, according to Eq. 1.

We analyze three different datasets collected on Facebookterm regarding a specific topic of discussion: vaccines, science versus conspiracy, and news. The interaction networkterm is defined by considering comments. In such an interaction networkterm, two users are connected if they cocommented on at least one post. ### Effects on Information Spreading.

Simple models of information spreading can gauge the presence of echo chambersterm: Users are expected to be more likely to exchange information with peers sharing a similar leaning. Classical epidemic models such as the susceptible–infected–recovered (SIRterm·def) model have been used to study the diffusion of information, such as rumors or news. In the SIRterm·def model, each agent can be in any of three states: susceptible (unaware of the circulating information), infectious (aware and willing to spread it further), or recovered (knowledgeable but not ready to transmit it anymore). Susceptible (unaware) users may become infectious (aware) upon contact with infected neighbors, with a specific transmission probability . Infectious users can spontaneously become recovered with probability . To measure the effects of the leaning of users on the diffusion of information, we run the SIRterm·def dynamics on the interaction networks, by starting the epidemic process with only one node infected, and stopping it when no more infectious nodes are left.

The set of nodes in a recovered state at the end of the dynamics started with user as a seed of infection, that is, those that become aware of the information initially propagated by user , form the set of influence of user , . Thus, the set of influence of a user represents those individuals that can be reached by a piece of content sent by him/her, depending on the effective infection ratio . ## Conclusions

Social media platforms provide direct access to an unprecedented amount of content. Platforms originally designed for user entertainment changed the way information spread. Indeed, feed algorithms mediate and influence the content promotion accounting for users’ preferences and attitudes. Such a paradigm shift affected the construction of social perceptions and the framing of narratives; it may influence policy making, political communication, and the evolution of public debate, especially on polarizing topics. Indeed, users online tend to prefer information adhering to their worldviews, ignore dissenting information, and form polarized groups around shared narratives. Furthermore, when polarizationterm is high, misinformation quickly proliferates.

Some argued that the veracity of the information might be used as a determinant for information spreading patterns. However, selective exposureterm dominates content consumption on social media, and different platforms may trigger very different dynamics. In this paper, we explore the key differences between the leading social media platforms and how they are likely to influence the formation of echo chambersterm and information spreading. To assess the different dynamics, we perform a comparative analysis on more than 100 million pieces of content concerning controversial topics (e.g., gun control, vaccination, abortion) from Gabterm, Facebookterm, Redditterm, and Twitter. The analysis focuses on two main dimensions: 1) homophilyterm in the interaction networks and 2) bias in the information diffusion toward like-minded peers. Our results show that the aggregation in homophilic clusters of users dominates online dynamics. However, a direct comparison of news consumption on Facebookterm and Redditterm shows higher segregation on Facebookterm. Furthermore, we find significant differences across platforms in terms of homophilic patterns in the network structure and biases in the information diffusion toward like-minded users. A clear-cut distinction emerges between social media having a feed algorithmterm tweakable by the users (e.g., Redditterm) and social media that don’t provide such an option (e.g., Facebookterm and Twitter). Our work provides important insights into the understanding of social dynamics and information consumption on social media. The next envisioned step addresses the temporal dimension of echo chambersterm, to understand better how different social feedback mechanisms, specific to distinct platforms, can impact their formation.
