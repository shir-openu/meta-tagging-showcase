phenomenonemotional influence on value-based decision making
claim-typemechanism
mechanisminduced moods set appraisal tendencies - a sad or disgusted state makes the person appraise unrelated options in a mood-congruent way, which shifts the subjective value assigned to them — “To explain this striking effect, Lerner and colleagues (2004) suggested that moods inspire appraisal tendencies, or tendencies to appraise unrelated events, such as the subjective value of a choice option, in a manner consistent with the mood.”
mechanismstress impairs prefrontal function and enhances striatal/dopaminergic habit circuits, so the decision process falls back on automatic or habitual responding — “The research on stress suggests that the decision process may become more habitual or automatic under stress, perhaps because of the effect of stress on PFC function, the striatum and/or the dopaminergic system.”
mechanismamygdala input to the striatum is proposed as the circuit that folds an emotional reaction into the subjective-value computation — “Although the neural systems mediating the incorporation of emotional reactions into the calculation of subjective value are not clearly delineated as of yet, there is evidence that one circuit might involve the amygdala’s input to the striatum.”
mechanisma mismatch between behaviour and attitude produces negative affect, and preference is revised after the fact to become consonant with the behaviour so that the negative affect is removed — “To reduce this negative affect, after we have engaged in some behavior that is inconsistent with our preferences, we have a tendency to change our preference so that it is consonant with that behavior.”
relation — “For these reasons, affective neuroscientists now often argue that the limbic system concept is obsolete and should be abandoned (Dagleish, 2004; Gazzaniga, 2004; LeDoux, 2000; Phelps, 2009).”
relation — “A similar idea was proposed with the Risk-As-Feelings Hypothesis (Loewenstein et al., 2001), which suggested that affective responses to risk in decisions may drive choices.”
replicationuntested
limitationThe stress-to-habit account reviewed lacks direct neural evidence in humans. — “However, it is important to note that as of yet, there is no direct neuroscientific evidence of diminished PFC involvement in these tasks.”
limitationThe stress and decision-making literature reviewed is not yet conclusive. — “The collection of findings on decision making under stress conducted to date presents intriguing ideas, but leaves many open questions.”
limitationThe choice-blindness studies reviewed cannot separate preference change from preference revelation because preferences were not rated before and after. — “These studies on choice blindness present intriguing results, but they are limited in that participants did not systematically rate their preferences for objects both before and after they made their choices.”
limitationThe inference from brain activation to a psychological construct is named and reported as questioned, especially for regions with multiple functions. — “Inferring the presence of a psychological construct from brain activation is known as reverse inference. The success of reverse inference as a scientific technique to investigate psychological phenomena has been questioned, especially for brain regions that are known to have multiple functions”
limitationThe review deliberately excludes an entire class of evidence: findings that rest only on reverse inference are not reviewed at all. — “For this reason, in this chapter we only discuss findings that measure and/or manipulate affective processes. Statements about emotion that derive exclusively from reverse inference form no part of the review”
limitationThe chapter describes the earlier practice it is correcting as assuming its conclusion a priori. — “Instead, it was often assumed, a priori, that “irrational” choices were linked to emotion and that activation of brain regions previously linked to emotional responses verified the emotional quality of these decisions.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingThe emotion/cognition division inherited from the limbic system concept fails anatomically: no boundary separates emotional from cognitive brain regions, and no single unified system drives emotion. — “More recently, however, it has become apparent that there is no clean dividing line in the brain between regions underlying emotion and cognition, and there is no evidence for a single unified “system” that drives emotion.”
findingEmotion influences choice by two routes - incidental affect that carries over from an unrelated state, and emotional reactions to the choice options or outcomes that enter the value calculation directly. — “The second primary means by which emotion can influence decisions is thus by incorporating the emotional reaction to the choice options or outcomes into the value calculation.”
findingThe chapter's synthesis is that emotion is not a competing system but a modulator of the subjective-value computation, and that shift in the computation is what changes the choice. — “Much like other cognitive functions, these studies suggest that emotion modulates the value computation, thus changing the choice.”
findingThere is no single mechanism by which incidental affect shifts choices: stress and mood act through different routes rather than one common pathway. — “Importantly, there does not seem to be a single mechanism by which incidental affect can influence choices.”
findingReappraising an unfair ultimatum offer changes decisions, whereas merely suppressing the outward expression of emotion does not - so it is the reinterpretation, not the display, that carries the effect. — “These findings suggest that simply suppressing the expression of emotion is not sufficient to change decision-making patterns, but reinterpreting an emotional response to the offer of an unfair split can lead to a change in decision tendencies.”
findingThat cognitive reappraisal of a choice option's emotional significance changes decisions is treated as strong evidence that emotion enters the subjective-value calculation itself. — “The evidence showing an impact of cognitive strategies to reinterpret or reappraise the emotional significance of the choice option or outcomes on decisions provides strong support for a role of emotion in the calculation of subjective value.”
findingCausation also runs backwards from choice to preference: participants who merely believed they had chosen an option rated it higher afterwards, even though the choices were random. — “Simply believing that they had chosen an item increased participants’ preference for that item.”
findingThe chapter restricts itself to studies that measure or manipulate an affective process, excluding claims about emotion resting only on reverse inference from brain activation. — “For this reason, in this chapter we only discuss findings that measure and/or manipulate affective processes.”
quantityLess than 30% — proportion of participants who noticed that the face they had chosen had been covertly swapped, in the choice-blindness study the chapter reports — “Less than 30% of the subjects noticed that their choices had been changed.”
variablerisk taking measured as behavior in the Balloon Analogue Risk Task (BART) (dependent) — “Lighthall et al. (2009) conducted a study of risk taking under stress, using behavior in the Balloon Analogue Risk Task (BART) as the dependent variable.”
variablegender (mediator) — “This budding literature on decision strategies under stress has suggested that gender may also be an important mediating variable.”
definitionincidental affect — “We define incidental affect as a baseline affective state that is unrelated to the decision, but can, nevertheless, shift choices.”

definitionmood — “The term mood is generally used to describe a relatively lasting state that is predominantly defined by subjective feelings that may or may not be linked to a specific event.”

definitionphysiological arousal — “Physiological arousal refers to an increased sympathetic nervous system response, usually in response to highly emotional or salient stimuli in the environment.”
definitionskin conductance response (SCR) — “The skin conductance response (SCR) is an objective, transient indication of autonomic nervous system arousal in response to a stimulus.”
definitiontemporal discounting — “Temporal discounting refers to the tendency for individuals to prefer immediate rewards to rewards received after a delay, even if the magnitude of the delayed reward is larger”
