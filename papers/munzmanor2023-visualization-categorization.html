mechanisma tagset too large and too hierarchical to grasp in the annotation tool itself is given an interactive network-oriented representation in the separate visualisation tool — “Large, hierarchical tagsets are hard to grasp and visualize in CATMA, and ViS-À-ViS offers an interactive representation of the tagset in a network-oriented chart”
mechanismthe chart collapses and uncollapses level by level, and a level's size is drawn as the number of children it has, which is what makes the inner hierarchy of the tagset readable as shape — “Here a representation of the tagset is visible and the interactive chart enables one to collapse or uncollapse the various levels. The size of each level relates to the number of children it has, which once more offers a useful insight into the structure and inner hierarchy of the tagset.”
mechanismthe annotations of two or more annotators are viewed side by side, which is what turns the scholar's attention to the individual and subjective interpretation of each annotator instead of to a single reading of the text — “As was previously noted, CATMA, and thus also ViS-À-ViS, support multiple annotations and annotators. This way the user can view side-by-side the annotations of two or more annotators in order to compare and analyze them. This turns the attention of the scholar to the individual and subjective interpretation of each annotator and presents therefore another…”
mechanismthe variation between annotators is not collapsed into a gold or a silver standard; the multiplicity is visualised instead, so the disagreement is left as a question about the correct annotation rather than answered — “Instead of collapsing the variation into a gold or a silver standard, ViS-À-ViS advocates for the visualization of multiplicity. In other words, instead of providing an answer concerning the “correct” annotation, it raises a question about it and promotes once more literary speculation.”
Content tags
content observations: finding · quantity · definition · variable · statistic · key-term — each grounded in a verbatim quotation from the paper
findingVisualising the annotations repeatedly forced the categories to be revised. — “after visualizing the material in this way, it often turns out that the categories themselves need to be re-assessed”
findingCategorisation and visualisation form a hermeneutic circle: each changes the other. — “the categorization and visualization in themselves create a sort of hermeneutical circle in which both parts influence one another reciprocally”
findingThe first annotation pass had a working notion of categories but no defined tagset — the tagset came later. — “although there existed a notion of a clear-cut categorization of figurative language in the corpus, no well-defined tagset was created”
findingThe initial scheme was flat: four devices with no hierarchy between them. — “The major figurative devices were annotated in an entirely flat hierarchy of metaphor, metonymy, synecdoche and simile.”
quantity25% — figurative density of the Hosha'not genre: one word in four — “Most notably is the genre of the Hosha’not (hosanna), which ranked at 25% of figurative density. That is to say, in general, every fourth word is figurative.”
quantity11% — figurative density of the whole corpus, the comparison the 25% stands out against — “If we compare this phenomenon to the overall figurative density of the corpus, which is 11%, it really stands out.”
