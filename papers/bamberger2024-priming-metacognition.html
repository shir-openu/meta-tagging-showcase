This finding likely indicates that THE PRIME HAD NO EFFECT.` The study powered on two published effects - Swami et al. 2014 d = .46, Gervais & Norenzayan 2012 d = .44 - required N = 119 and recruited N = 184. **Every other priming record I have tagged reports an effect; this one reports its absence.** For a corpus about when priming works, a clean negative is worth more than another positive— “Hence, the first hypothesis was not supported: the priming of introductory psychology students’ metacognition and critical thinking did not positively predict their ability to dispel psychological misconceptions. This finding likely indicates that the prime had no effect”
sense**AND THE TWO ESCAPE HATCHES ARE NAMED BY THE AUTHORS THEMSELVES, WHICH IS THE PART WORTH RECORDING:** `This finding was surprising after Swami et al.s (2014) found that priming analytic thinking led to less conspiracy theory endorsement. THIS DIFFERENCE COULD BE BECAUSE DIFFERENT WORDS WERE USED in the treatment prime in this study versus Swami et al.'s study.` A null attributed to word choice **cannot be distinguished from a null attributed to the effect not existing** - and that is the move which makes a priming literature hard to falsify. It is stated here by people running an honest study and reporting their own failure, not by a critic, which is exactly why it is worth having in the corpus verbatim— “This finding was surprising after Swami et al.s (2014) found that priming analytic thinking led to less conspiracy theory endorsement. sense**SUPRALIMINAL PRIMING DEFINED ON THE AXIS OF INTENT RATHER THAN VISIBILITY:** `nonconscious activation can be achieved via SUPRALIMINAL PRIMING which involves participants' CONSCIOUS AWARENESS OF THE STIMULI, BUT NOT OF ITS INTENT.` **The participant sees the words and does not know what they are for.** dehaene1998 and holcomb2005 argue about whether the stimulus was SEEN at all; this names the other axis entirely, and a corpus that holds both can tell the two kinds of `unconscious` apart instead of pooling them— “nonconscious activation can be achieved via supraliminal priming ( Chartrand & Bargh, 1996 ) which involves participants’ conscious awareness of the stimuli, but not of its intent”
sense**AND THE LIMITATION SECTION PUTS THE SAME HATCH IN ONE SENTENCE:** `THERE MAY BE BETTER WORDS to elicit metacognition and critical thinking.` The words came from the Affective Norms for English Words, chosen to be emotionally neutral, and the authors name that choice as the first limitation of the study. **This is the unfalsifiable residue of any priming null** - the manipulation can always have been the wrong manipulation - and it belongs in the corpus as a tagged limitation rather than as a reason to discount the record— “There may be better words to elicit metacognition and critical thinking”
