phenomenonunconscious semantic processing and information integration
claim-typemethod
mechanismA generic same-different algorithm is applied unconsciously to the prime pair whatever its format, and the response it evokes then facilitates or interferes with the response demanded by the conscious target pair. — “the response evoked by the unconscious same-different judgment on the primes facilitates or interferes with the response required for the same-different judgment on the targets.”
mechanismIf 'same' judgments are holistic and parallel while 'different' judgments are serial, then global-workspace theory predicts that only the parallel 'same' route can run without conscious access, so priming should come from same primes. — “According to the global neuronal workspace theory of consciousness, parallel processes can operate non-consciously, but serial processing requires conscious access [22,50–52].”
mechanismUnconscious integration succeeds in this task because the stimuli fall within a sufficiently small spatial (and temporal) window and are of limited complexity. — “It could be that also the spatial window should be sufficiently small to make unconscious integration possible [25] or that the complexity of the information should be limited.”
relation — “If the dual process model of same-different judgements is correct, it could thus be hypothesized that priming in the subliminal SD task would work for primes that are the same, but less so for primes that are different.”
differs inprimes → targets — THE FORMAT THEY ARE PRESENTED IN, required to differ so that an effect cannot be explained by visual similarity between the two — “presented in different formats”
replicationreplicated
limitationThe review cannot say why this task succeeds where other unconscious-integration designs are unreliable. — “Although the evidence reviewed here demonstrates that the subliminal SD task has already been proven a successful paradigm to investigate unconscious cognition, it remains unclear why this is the case.”
limitationIn the word version of the task primes and targets were not cleanly separated, so part of that effect may be a conscious stimulus-response mapping. — “It should, however, be noted that in this study the prime and target words were not clearly separated which leaves open the possibility that the observed unconscious priming effect was at least partially caused by the conscious formation of stimulus-response mappings.”
Content tags
content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingIn the subliminal same-different task, the time to judge whether two target colours matched was determined by whether two invisible digit primes matched, so the same-different judgment was carried out on the unseen primes as well. — “Participants were faster in judging if the two colors were the same (different) if they were preceded by two prime numbers that were the same (different) [15(cid:1)].”
findingThe unconscious same-different priming effect is short-lived: it appeared at a 100 ms prime-target interval and was gone at 316 ms. — “was short (i.e. 100 ms) but not when this time period was longer (i.e. 316 ms), indicating that it is rather short-lived [16].”
findingBecause the prime set (Arabic digits) and target set (colour patches) had nothing in common, the effect cannot be attributed to visual overlap or to consciously prepared action triggers, the two standard objections to subliminal semantic priming. — “The clear separation between the prime set (Arabic digits) and the target set (colored patches) in this experiment ensured that the subliminal priming effect could not be caused by a visual overlap between the primes and targets or by the potential to consciously create action triggers.”
findingWhen the primes were an upper- and lower-case letter matched for visual similarity, the same-different judgment could only rest on letter identity, which the author treats as strong evidence for unconscious semantic processing. — “could only be done based on their meaning, thus providing strong evidence for unconscious semantical processing.”
findingThe unconscious same-different judgment is context-relative: the pair 'a A' primed a 'same' response when surrounded by dissimilar primes but a 'different' response when surrounded by very similar primes. — “dissimilar both physically and semantically) it primed a ‘same’ response, but when the same prime was presented in a context with high similarity (i.e. embedded in primes that are very similar) it primed a ‘different’ response [20].”
findingIn a multimodal version pairing a visual and an auditory stimulus, unconscious integration across the senses appeared only for participants who had been given conscious training beforehand. — “Results showed that unconscious multisensory integration can occur, but only when subjects received conscious training before the experiment.”
findingThe subliminal version of the task reproduces the fast-same effect, in which 'same' responses are faster than 'different' responses. — “Similar to other same-different tasks, the subliminal SD task often shows the fastsame effect (i.e. the — counterintuitive — observation that same responses are faster than different responses) [47,48].”
findingThe prediction that priming should be confined to 'same' primes is not borne out consistently: some experiments show priming only for same primes and others only for different primes. — “the evidence until now is very mixed (with opposite effects, i.e. priming only for different primes, in Experiment 2 in Ref. [45(cid:1)] and Experiment 2 in Ref. [15(cid:1)]).”
findingWith pairs of emotional faces as primes the effect reversed sign, responses being slower when the prime relation matched the target relation. — “In two experiments they showed a negative priming effect, indicating that reaction times were slower on trials where the relation of the primes faces (same/ different) was the same as the relation of the target faces (same/different).”
quantityabout 10 ms — size of the subliminal same-different priming effect on reaction time — “Participants were about 10 ms faster to respond to the targets when the response evoked by the primes matched that of the one to the targets (Figure 2a).”
quantity100 ms / 316 ms — prime-target intervals at which the priming effect was present versus absent — “was short (i.e. 100 ms) but not when this time period was longer (i.e. 316 ms), indicating that it is rather short-lived [16].”
quantity50 ms or less — prime duration below which participants have no awareness of the prime — “When the prime stimulus is presented for 50 ms or less and immediately followed by mask stimuli, participants have no awareness of the presence”
definitionsubliminal priming — “researchers have investigated the topic by presenting a stimulus below the threshold of conscious perception and measuring whether this unconscious stimulus affects a behavioral response (i.e. subliminal priming) [1,2].”
definitionsubliminal same-different (SD) task — “Instead of centrally presenting a single prime and a single target on every trial, in the subliminal SD task two masked primes and two targets are presented side by side (Figure 1c & d) and participants are required to judge if the two target stimuli are the same or different.”
definitioninformation integration — “can be defined as ‘the generation of a non-perceptual, abstract representation by associating distinct signals into a new one’ (see Ref. [25], p. 488), and consciousness would thus be a requirement to successfully perform this process.”
definitionfast-same effect — “Similar to other same-different tasks, the subliminal SD task often shows the fastsame effect (i.e. the — counterintuitive — observation that same responses are faster than different responses) [47,48].”
definitioncriterion 1 for genuine unconscious processing (primes from large or novel sets) — “First, primes should come from large categories or from a novel set outside of the task set so that subliminal priming effects cannot be attributed to the conscious preparation of response links.”
definitioncriterion 2 for genuine unconscious processing (different prime and target formats) — “Second, primes and targets should be presented in different formats to avoid that any priming effect is caused by visual similarities [10,11].”
