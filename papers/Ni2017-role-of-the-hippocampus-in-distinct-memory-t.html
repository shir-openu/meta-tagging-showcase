findingGoal match enhancement appeared only at contacts in the left posterior hippocampus, whereas perceptual mismatch enhancement appeared bilaterally across both anterior and posterior hippocampus. — “Consistent with our proposal and a previous study [ 29 ], we found goal match enhancement at electrode contacts #3 and #4, which were located on the left posterior hippocampus, and perceptual mismatch enhancement at electrode contacts #1, #2, #3, #4, and #9, #10, #11, #12, which included the anterior and posterior hippocampi bilaterally.”
findingThe perceptual mismatch signal began before the goal match signal in every one of the eight participants. — “The perceptual mismatch enhancement began earlier than the goal match enhancement for all participants”
findingThe two hippocampal signals divide by content: match enhancement tracks the match to the maintained goal while mismatch enhancement tracks perceptual mismatch, dissociating components of working memory within the hippocampus. — “The dissociation of different components of working memory at hippocampus revealed more information beyond previous electrophysiological research [ 18 , 19 ], demonstrating that match enhancement is more related with goal match at the hippocampus and mismatch enhancement is more related with perceptual mismatch.”
findingParticipants whose right posterior hippocampus produced a later perceptual mismatch enhancement were more accurate, a positive correlation between latency and memory performance. — “The accuracy was found to be positively correlated with the perceptual mismatch enhancement latency on the right posterior hippocampus.”
findingThat latency-accuracy link is read as evidence that the right posterior hippocampal mismatch latency indexes the quality of memory retrieval. — “This suggested that the latency of the perceptual mismatch enhancement at the right posterior hippocampus could indicate the performance of memory retrieval.”
findingIn six of the eight patients, single-trial intracranial field potentials from the right hippocampus were sufficient to classify whether the trial was a match task or a switch task. — “Six out of 8 participants showed that the task demand could be decoded from the IFP response on the right hippocampus.”
findingBehaviourally, responses were faster in the match task than in the switch task. — “Paired sample t tests revealed that the responses in the match task were faster than those in the switch task”
findingHippocampal engagement here is attributed to the task requiring object identity and location to be bound together, in both the match and the switch task. — “Our results agree with these studies, in that object and location information are combined to finish the task requirements, no matter whether it is in a match task trial or a switch task trial.”
findinggoal match enhancement appeared in the left posterior hippocampus between about 520 and 735 ms after probe onset — “Two similar time intervals were found (535 ms to 735 ms on electrode contact #3, and 520 ms to 590 ms on electrode contact #4) to be significant (Fig. 3 ).”
findingperceptual mismatch enhancement appeared bilaterally in anterior and posterior hippocampus, averaging 376 to 555 ms — “A perceptually driven mismatch enhancement was revealed bilaterally in the hippocampus including both the anterior and posterior areas (electrode contacts #1, #2, #3, #4 and #9, #10, #11, #12). This enhancement effect occurred at different intervals on different electrode contacts, averaging from 376 ms to 555 ms (Fig. 4 ).”
findingmismatch preceded match in every participant, by about 150 ms — “The perceptual mismatch enhancement began earlier than the goal match enhancement for all participants (latency of match enhancement vs latency of mismatch enhancement in left posterior hippocampus: t (7) = 2.43, P < 0.05).”
findingregion latencies were 524 ms for goal match and about 405 to 430 ms for perceptual mismatch across the four subdivisions — “The goal match enhancement was found in the left posterior hippocampus with a latency of 524 ms. The latency of the perceptual mismatch enhancement varied across areas: left anterior 411 ms, left posterior 405 ms, right anterior 430 ms, and right posterior 409 ms.”
findinga late perceptual match effect appeared on two electrodes at 717 and 900 ms, fitting neither canonical pattern — “A late perceptual match enhancement was found on electrodes #2 and #3 with a latency of 717 ms and 900 ms, which fitted a pattern of BOTH > NEITHER and GOAL < PERCEPT.”
findingevery observed latency survived a 1000-permutation test at p below 0.05 — “For each electrode and each significant effect, the probability of the observed latency was <0.05.”
findingmismatch latency in the right posterior hippocampus correlated positively with memory accuracy — “The latency of the perceptual mismatch enhancement effect found in the right posterior hippocampus was significantly correlated with the accuracy of memory performance (Fig. 5 ) (ρ = 0.74, P < 0.05).”
findingtask demand was decodable from single trials in 6 of 8 patients, always from the right hippocampus — “With the TTL 2 datasets, 6 out of 8 patients consistently showed a significantly reliable response in the hippocampus representing the task demand. More interestingly, we found that for all 6 patients, the IFP signal in the right hippocampus efficiently discriminated the task demand (Table 3 ).”
findingresponses were faster in the match task than in the switch task — “Paired sample t tests revealed that the responses in the match task were faster than those in the switch task ( t (7) = −3.608, P < 0.01).”
findingover 80% of trials survived artifact rejection — “We managed to retain >80% of all trials after artifact rejection.”
