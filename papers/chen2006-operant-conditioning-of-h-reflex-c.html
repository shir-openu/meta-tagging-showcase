mechanismUp-conditioning strengthens the motoneuron response to primary afferent input, recruiting more motoneurons into the locomotor burst (and making each fire more), which enlarges and broadens the soleus burst and thereby lengthens right stance until symmetry is restored— “The increases in burst amplitude and width are likely to reflect an increase in the number of motoneurons responding to this input.”
mechanismThe enlarged locomotor burst produced by up-conditioning is the proposed origin of the restored right/left symmetry— “The probable origin is the marked increase in locomotor burst amplitude that results from H-reflex up-conditioning (i.e., Fig. 2 ).”
mechanismIn normal animals compensatory adjustments by other muscles cancel the conditioning-induced change; in lesioned animals those compensatory adjustments are impaired or absent, so the conditioning effect is allowed to alter locomotion— “In contrast, when a locomotor asymmetry is already present before H-reflex conditioning, as it is in the LC rats of this study, the same automatic adjustments and the same impetus for adaptation to eliminate the impact of conditioning on locomotion are likely to be impaired or entirely absent.”
mechanismup-conditioning raises the locomotor burst, probably by recruiting more motoneurons to primary afferent input, and the burst also broadens— “On average, an increase in the stance H-reflex was accompanied by an increase 0.32 times as large in soleus burst amplitude ( r = 0.60; p = 0.04).”
