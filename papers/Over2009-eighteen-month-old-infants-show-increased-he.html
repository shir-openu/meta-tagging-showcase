phenomenonsocial priming of prosocial helping in infants
claim-typeexistence
mechanismThe affiliation photographs activate a general prosocial orientation, which is then expressed in an unrelated helping act, rather than being copied from what was depicted — “Infants did not directly reproduce the situations depicted in the photographs; instead, the photographs triggered a general prosocial orientation that manifested itself in increased helping.”
mechanismAn implicit, non-conscious link between affiliation to the group and helping is engaged by the prime — “Thus, infants made the subconscious connection between affiliation and helping behavior.”
relation — “We know from previous studies that, if an adult is unable to reach an object he wants, 18-month-old infants will often pick up the object themselves and offer it to the adult (Warneken & Tomasello, 2006).”
effect directionaffiliation → helping behavior — increases — “increased helping behavior”
effect directionindividuality → helping behavior — no-change — “rather than the individuality primes that decreased it”
effect directionaffiliative stance → helping — increases — “that increased helping”
effect directionprosocial orientation → helping — increases — “manifested itself in increased helping”
effect directionmood → helping — no-change — “cannot be explained by differences in attentiveness or mood”
replicationuntested
limitationBaseline helping rates were lower than in an earlier helping study, leaving open the alternative reading that the affiliation condition is the true baseline and the other three conditions suppressed helping. — “This raises the possibility that our together condition represents a true baseline and the other three conditions depressed helping below baseline levels.”
limitationProcedural choices (a single helping opportunity, and a different experimenter interacting with the infant beforehand) probably lowered helping in every condition, so rates are not comparable across studies. — “In contrast, infants in our study were presented with only one chance to help and had been interacting with a different experimenter immediately before the test phase, which likely reduced their tendency to help across all four conditions.”
Content tags
content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingEighteen-month-old infants who saw photographs with an affiliation prime (two dolls facing each other) helped an adult in need more often and more spontaneously than infants primed with individuality (a single doll). — “Eighteen-month-old infants helped a person in need more often, and more spontaneously, when primed with photographs evoking affiliation than when primed with photographs evoking individuality.”
findingSpontaneous helping was three times as frequent after the affiliation prime as in each of the alone, baseline and back-to-back control conditions. — “Three times as many infants helped spontaneously in the together condition as in each of the other three conditions.”
findingComparison with the non-social baseline shows the direction of the effect: the affiliation prime raised helping rather than the individuality prime lowering it. — “By comparing the results of the together condition to the baseline control condition, we can conclude that it was the affiliation primes that increased helping behavior rather than the individuality primes that decreased it.”
findingWhat drove the increase was the affiliative stance of the two dolls toward each other, not the mere presence of two dolls, since the back-to-back condition contained the same two dolls but did not raise helping. — “Moreover, by comparing the results of the together condition to the back-to-back condition, we can conclude that it was the affiliative stance depicted in the photographs, rather than the mere presence of two dolls, that increased helping.”
findingNeither how long infants looked at the photographs nor their rated mood differed across the four conditions, so attentiveness and mood cannot account for the helping difference. — “Thus, infants’ increased helping in the together condition cannot be explained by differences in attentiveness or mood.”
findingHelping itself was never depicted in the primes - the dolls only stood beside one another - so the effect is not imitation of a modelled action. — “It is important to note that helpfulness itself was not primed: The dolls simply stood next to one another.”
quantitysixty — number of 18-month-old infants in the study — “Participants were sixty 18-month-old infants (age range 5 18 months 0 days to 18 months 29 days; 27 boys, 33 girls) recruited from a database of parents who had volunteered to participate in child development studies.”
quantityFifteen — infants per condition (four conditions) — “Fifteen infants participated in each condition.”
quantity7 — additional infants tested but excluded from analysis, for parental interference, crying, experimenter error and refusal to look — “An additional 7 infants were tested but excluded from analyses for parental interference (n 5 3), crying during the test (n 5 2), experimenter error (n 5 1), and refusal to observe some of the photographs (n 5 1).”
quantityThree times — ratio of infants helping spontaneously in the together condition to each control condition — “Three times as many infants helped spontaneously in the together condition as in each of the other three conditions.”
quantityw2(1, N 5 60) 5 8.57, two-tailed p 5 .003 — chi-square test of spontaneous helping, together versus pooled controls — “Infants were significantly more likely to spontaneously help the experimenter in the together condition than in the pooled control conditions, w2(1, N 5 60) 5 8.57, two-tailed p 5 .003, prep 5 .97, j 5 .38.”
quantityw2(1, N 5 60) 5 6.43, two-tailed p 5 .011 — chi-square test of overall helping (including after prompting), together versus pooled controls — “Again, infants were significantly more likely to help in the together condition than in the pooled control conditions, w2(1, N 5 60) 5 6.43, two-tailed p 5 .011, prep 5 .95, j 5 .33.”
quantityCohen’s k 5 1.00 — interrater agreement on both helping measures for the 20% of tapes double-coded — “Agreement was perfect for both measures (Cohen’s k 5 1.00).”
quantity102.2 / 92.8 / 89.6 / 101.4 — mean seconds spent looking at the photographs in the together, alone, baseline and back-to-back conditions — “The average number of seconds spent looking at the photographs was 102.2 in the together condition, 92.8 in the alone condition, 89.6 in the baseline condition, and 101.4 in the back-to-back condition.”
variableprime type (together / alone / baseline / back-to-back photographs) (independent) — “In the current study, we primed 18-month-old infants with photographs evoking affiliation (two small dolls standing next to each other in the background of photographs of other objects) and measured the influence of the primes on infants’ tendency to be helpful.”
variablespontaneous helping within the first 10 s (dependent) — “The main dependent measure was whether infants helped the experimenter spontaneously, that is, whether they picked up at least one stick and offered it to her within the first 10 s of the test phase (before she spoke to the infants or reached for the sticks herself).”
variableinfants' mood (parent-rated) (control) — “There were also no significant differences in infants’ mood across the four conditions, F(3, 56) 5 0.155, p 5 .93, prep 5 .15.”
variableproportion of the dolls' faces visible (low-level social stimulus content) (control) — “Furthermore, the angle at which the dolls were placed meant that infants in these two conditions were exposed to exactly the same proportion of the dolls’ faces (that is, they saw the same number of eyes, and the same proportion of the dolls’ smiles), thus controlling for possible effects of lower-level social stimuli on helping behavior.”
variableattention to the primes (seconds spent looking at the photographs) (control) — “To ascertain whether infants paid equal attention to the presentation of the primes in each condition, we randomly selected a third of the infants from each condition and coded the number of seconds they spent looking at the photographs.”
definitionspontaneous helping (main dependent measure) — “The main dependent measure was whether infants helped the experimenter spontaneously, that is, whether they picked up at least one stick and offered it to her within the first 10 s of the test phase (before she spoke to the infants or reached for the sticks herself).”
definitiongeneral measure of helping — “In addition, a more general measure of helping was scored if infants picked up at least one stick and offered it to the experimenter at any point during the test phase (i.e., including after she asked for help).”
definitionprosocial behavior — “Most notably, it has created a tendency for us to engage in behaviors that benefit our group members, that is, prosocial behaviors such as helping and sharing (Gintis, Bowles, Boyd, & Fehr, 2003).”
definitiontogether (affiliation) prime condition — “In the together condition, each prime consisted of two small wooden dolls facing each other in close proximity.”
definitionback-to-back condition (its diagnostic purpose) — “This condition was designed to determine whether it is the mere presence of two dolls or their affiliative interaction that increases helpfulness.”
