phenomenonmasked repetition priming of pictures— “This experiment examined invariance in object representations through measuring event-related potentials (ERPs) to pictures in a masked repetition priming paradigm.”
claim-typeexistence— “This experiment found that both early (N190/P190 effects) and later effects (N400) were invariant to size, whereas only the N190/P190 effect was invariant to mirror reflection.”
method — evidence— “The EEG (250 Hz sampling rate, bandpass 0.01 and 40 Hz) was recorded continuously (SA Instruments amplifier, San Diego, CA) and ERPs were averaged time-locked to the onset of targets.”
mechanismObject recognition proceeds hierarchically: perceptual overlap between prime and target must first be established before a higher-level semantic representation can be activated, so blocking the early overlap blocks the later effect.— “Taken together, the results of this set of experiments suggests that object recognition, more specifically, activating an object representation, occurs in a hierarchical fashion where overlapping perceptual information between the prime and target is necessary, although not always sufficient, to activate a higher level semantic…
mechanismInsufficient pooling: many neurons coding low-level features may fire to both prime and target, yet not enough of their activity converges to drive the sparser, more complex intermediate representation.— “It is possible that a large number of neurons are active to encode perceptual features of the prime object that overlap with the target object, but there is not sufficient activation to pool together to activate an intermediate representation that relies on a more complex integration of these features.”
mechanismReceptive-field displacement: mirror reflection moves the prime-activated features outside the receptive fields of the cells that the target activates, so no priming benefit accrues.— “If the mirror reflection is placing visual features activated by the prime outside the receptive field of a cell activated for the target, then no benefit in priming would be expected.”
mechanismCompensation cost: reconciling a prime and target that differ in both size and left-right orientation demands extra processing effort, which shows up as a reduced (here absent) N190/P190 priming effect.— “If a compensation process must equate the prime and target as the same when they are different sizes and mirror reflected, an increase in processing effort (as indicated by greater amplitude for the repeated condition and less of an N190/P190) would be expected, like the effect observed in the current experiment.”
contradicts Fiser and Biederman (2001)— “This finding of an attenuated N400 effect for the mirror reflected condition is inconsistent with the findings of Fiser and Biederman (2001) who found intact priming effects regardless of left-right orientation.”
extends Chauncey et al. (2008) masked size-priming study with words— “This finding is consistent with the findings of intact series of priming effects with changes in word size by Chauncey et al. (2008) as well as findings in neurophysiological studies (e.g., Ito et al., 1995 ) where a significant change in size still led to invariant firing the inferior temporal (IT) cortex of monkey.”
builds-on Eddy et al. (2006) masked repetition priming of pictures— “The aim of this experiment was to address how the series of ERP components observed in masked repetition picture priming ( Eddy et al., 2006 ) are affected by changes in physical features of the stimuli.”
sensePRIMING IS THE INSTRUMENT AND INVARIANCE IS THE SUBJECT, stated in the abstract's first sentence: `This experiment examined INVARIANCE IN OBJECT REPRESENTATIONS through measuring event-related potentials (ERPs) to pictures in a masked repetition priming paradigm.` Whether priming SURVIVES a transformation is the measurement of whether the representation is invariant to it - so the experiment is about object representation, and priming is the apparatus. The pickering2008 distinction (Q-744) in a third literature, after Argiris2021— “This experiment examined invariance in object representations through measuring event-related potentials (ERPs) to pictures in a masked repetition priming paradigm”
senseNECESSARY, ALTHOUGH NOT ALWAYS SUFFICIENT - an explicit logical qualifier, which is rare enough in this corpus to be worth a row of its own. `Overlapping perceptual information between the prime and target is NECESSARY, ALTHOUGH NOT ALWAYS SUFFICIENT, to activate a higher level semantic representation.` Necessity and the denial of sufficiency in one clause, and the design earns both halves separately: the mirror condition gives early overlap WITHOUT the later effect (not sufficient), and the size-mirror condition removes the early effect and the later one with it (necessary). The record's mechanisms[0] paraphrases only the necessity half— “Taken together, the results of this set of experiments suggests that object recognition, more specifically, activating an object representation, occurs in a hierarchical fashion where overlapping perceptual information between the prime and target is necessary, although not always sufficient, to activate a higher level semantic…
senseTHE LATE STAGE IS MORE INVARIANT THAN THE EARLY ONE, which is the opposite of the obvious ordering. `The N400 unlike the N190/P190 appears to be FULLY invariant to size, since the repetition effect for the small prime condition DID NOT DIFFER from the repetition effect for the identity priming condition` - a null stated with its statistical basis attached. The early perceptual effect survives the same manipulation but pays for it: it `compensates for differences in size, BUT NOT WITHOUT A SMALL PROCESSING COST`. So invariance is not a property a representation has or lacks; it is graded, and it is cheaper further up— “Importantly though, the N400 unlike the N190/P190 appears to be fully invariant to size, since the repetition effect for the small prime condition did not differ from the repetition effect for the identity priming condition”
senseTHE ERP CASCADE TURNS `IS IT INVARIANT?` INTO `WHEN DOES IT STOP BEING INVARIANT?` The conclusion says exactly this: the results `help elucidate the TIME COURSE of previous fMRI findings of size invariance in adaptation paradigms and also WHEN during object processing left-right arrangement of objects becomes important for activating an object representation.` fMRI adaptation can say a region is invariant; a component sequence can say at which stage the invariance ends. That is the methodological reason this paper exists, and it is a claim about what its instrument can resolve rather than about objects— “The results of this study help elucidate the time course of previous fMRI findings of size invariance in adaptation paradigms and also when during object processing left-right arrangement of objects becomes important for activating an object representation”
controltwelve counterbalanced lists, so each repeated and unrepeated object appears an equal number of times across participants while appearing in only one condition for any single participant — held — rules out item effects - that a difference between repeated and unrepeated objects is a property of which objects happened to fall in which condition— “Twelve counterbalanced lists resulted in each repeated and unrepeated object being presented an equal number of times across participants, but each object being presented in only one trial for each participant.”

findingMasked repetition priming of pictures produced ERP effects that survived a halving of prime size at both the early (N190/P190) and late (N400) stages, but survived mirror reflection of the target only at the early stage.— “This experiment found that both early (N190/P190 effects) and later effects (N400) were invariant to size, whereas only the N190/P190 effect was invariant to mirror reflection.”
findingCombining the two transformations, a small prime followed by a mirror-reflected target, abolished priming entirely, in every time window.
findingEven in the earliest window, the small-prime-plus-mirror condition produced no reliable repetition effect (F<3, p>0.065).
findingNo significant N300 repetition effect appeared in any condition, which the authors attribute to the rapid presentation rate smearing the N300 into the N400.
findingThe N400 was fully size-invariant: the small-prime repetition effect was statistically indistinguishable from the identity repetition effect.
findingThe early N190/P190 was only partially size-invariant: the effect was reliably present but smaller with a small prime, implying a processing cost for compensating the size difference.
