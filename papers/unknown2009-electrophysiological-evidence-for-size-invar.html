phenomenonmasked repetition priming of pictures
claim-typeexistence
mechanismObject recognition proceeds hierarchically: perceptual overlap between prime and target must first be established before a higher-level semantic representation can be activated, so blocking the early overlap blocks the later effect. — “Taken together, the results of this set of experiments suggests that object recognition, more specifically, activating an object representation, occurs in a hierarchical fashion where overlapping perceptual information between the prime and target is necessary, although not always sufficient, to activate a higher level semantic representation.”
mechanismInsufficient pooling: many neurons coding low-level features may fire to both prime and target, yet not enough of their activity converges to drive the sparser, more complex intermediate representation. — “It is possible that a large number of neurons are active to encode perceptual features of the prime object that overlap with the target object, but there is not sufficient activation to pool together to activate an intermediate representation that relies on a more complex integration of these features.”
mechanismReceptive-field displacement: mirror reflection moves the prime-activated features outside the receptive fields of the cells that the target activates, so no priming benefit accrues. — “If the mirror reflection is placing visual features activated by the prime outside the receptive field of a cell activated for the target, then no benefit in priming would be expected.”
mechanismCompensation cost: reconciling a prime and target that differ in both size and left-right orientation demands extra processing effort, which shows up as a reduced (here absent) N190/P190 priming effect. — “If a compensation process must equate the prime and target as the same when they are different sizes and mirror reflected, an increase in processing effort (as indicated by greater amplitude for the repeated condition and less of an N190/P190) would be expected, like the effect observed in the current experiment.”
relation — “This finding of an attenuated N400 effect for the mirror reflected condition is inconsistent with the findings of Fiser and Biederman (2001) who found intact priming effects regardless of left-right orientation.”
relation — “This finding is consistent with the findings of intact series of priming effects with changes in word size by Chauncey et al. (2008) as well as findings in neurophysiological studies (e.g., Ito et al., 1995 ) where a significant change in size still led to invariant firing the inferior temporal (IT) cortex of monkey.”
relation — “The aim of this experiment was to address how the series of ERP components observed in masked repetition picture priming ( Eddy et al., 2006 ) are affected by changes in physical features of the stimuli.”
differs inN190/P190 → N400 — WHICH TRANSFORMATION EACH TOLERATES. Both survive a halving of the prime; only the early one survives mirror reflection of the target. The two manipulations are therefore indistinguishable at the perceptual stage and separate at the semantic one, which is how the paper locates where left-right arrangement starts to matter — “only the N190/P190 effect was invariant to mirror reflection”
effect directionmirror reflection → N400 — reduces — “appears to be enough to attenuate the N400 effect”
replicationcontested
limitationObject symmetry was not controlled, so the surviving early mirror effect may be an artefact of symmetric items. — “One factor that was not controlled for was how symmetrical objects were when mirror reflected.”
limitationThe early mirror-reflection priming effect may reflect global shape overlap rather than internal object features. — “It is possible this effect was mainly driven by overlap in overall shape of the prime and target, rather than internal features.”
limitationNo N300 effect was obtained in any condition, possibly because the rapid presentation rate merged it with the N400. — “One possibility for this could be the rapid presentation rate of the objects, leading to a streamlined process where the N300 effect is somehow combined or smeared with the N400.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingMasked repetition priming of pictures produced ERP effects that survived a halving of prime size at both the early (N190/P190) and late (N400) stages, but survived mirror reflection of the target only at the early stage. — “This experiment found that both early (N190/P190 effects) and later effects (N400) were invariant to size, whereas only the N190/P190 effect was invariant to mirror reflection.”
findingCombining the two transformations, a small prime followed by a mirror-reflected target, abolished priming entirely, in every time window. — “The combination of a small prime and a mirror reflected target led to no significant priming effects.”
findingEven in the earliest window, the small-prime-plus-mirror condition produced no reliable repetition effect (F<3, p>0.065). — “While significant repetition effects are observed for the small prime-identity target condition (see results above), when investigating repetition effects for the small prime – mirror reversal condition alone, no repetition effects are observed (F<3, p > 0.065) within this early time window (see Figure 6 and 7D ).”
findingNo significant N300 repetition effect appeared in any condition, which the authors attribute to the rapid presentation rate smearing the N300 into the N400. — “In terms of higher level processing, no significant N300 effects were observed for any of the conditions.”
findingThe N400 was fully size-invariant: the small-prime repetition effect was statistically indistinguishable from the identity repetition effect. — “Importantly though, the N400 unlike the N190/P190 appears to be fully invariant to size, since the repetition effect for the small prime condition did not differ from the repetition effect for the identity priming condition.”
findingThe early N190/P190 was only partially size-invariant: the effect was reliably present but smaller with a small prime, implying a processing cost for compensating the size difference. — “Therefore, this early effect is indexing processing of object features and shape in a way that compensates for differences in size, but not without a small processing cost associated with this compensation.”
findingMirror-reflecting the target eliminated the N400 repetition effect altogether. — “In our experiment when objects were mirror reflected, no significant N400 effect was observed.”
findingThe failure of priming in the combined small-prime/mirror-target condition is attributable to the mirror reflection rather than to the prime size, since small primes alone still primed and mirror targets alone did not. — “Since a repetition priming effect was found for small primes-identity targets (see results above) and no repetition priming effect was found for the identity prime-mirror reflected target condition (see statistics above), the lack of repetition effect for the small prime-mirror flipped target condition is primarily driven by the mirror reflection of the target.”
findingPrime d' values near 1.7-1.8 versus target d' of 4.45-4.75 confirm that the masking kept primes below identification threshold at both prime sizes. — “The average d’ for food items appearing in the target position was 4.45 (significantly different from the prime items (t(23) = −9.436, p = 0.000) when preceded by a normal size non-food filler prime and 4.75 when preceded by a small non-food filler prime (significantly different from the prime items (t(23) = −10.275, p = 0.000).”
