mechanismMoving the criterion shifts hit and false-alarm rates together, so a liberal criterion inflates both; only reducing the overlap of the signal and noise distributions can raise hits while lowering false alarms. — “The only way to increase the hit rate while reducing the false-alarm rate is to reduce the overlap between the signal and the noise distributions.”

findingFitting ROC data by ordinary least squares gives biased slope and intercept estimates, because in rating tasks both the hit-rate and the false-alarm-rate z scores carry sampling error rather than only one of them. — “In rating tasks, the empirically determined hit and false-alarm rates both contain sampling error, so OLS provides biased estimates of the slope and intercept.”
findingIn the worked example OLS wrongly implied that the signal and noise distributions had equal standard deviations, whereas the specialised ROC program showed the noise distribution varied far more. — “Furthermore, OLS gives the misleading impression that the signal and noise distributions have equal standard deviations”
findingBecause the equal-variance assumption was violated in the example data, the d' values obtained from the five different criteria varied widely, so d' should not be used before evidence that its assumptions hold. — “This violation explains why the d' values in Table 6 each of which results from a different criterion-vary so widely.”
findingLookup tables are an inaccurate route to SDT measures because they force hit and false-alarm rates to be rounded, usually to two significant digits, and some published tables contain wrong entries. — “Tabular methods have relatively poor accuracy. Some tables contain incorrect entries, but even error-free tables can be used only after the hit and false-alarm rates are rounded off (usually to two significant digits).”

definitionresponse bias — “Clearly, the hit and false-alarm rates reflect two factors: response bias (the general tendency to respond yes or no, as determined by the location of the criterion) and the de gree of overlap between the signal and the noise distribu tions.”
definitiond' — “One measure that attempts to do this is d', which mea sures the distance between the signal and the noise means in standard deviation units.”
definitionthe d' assumptions — “SDT states that d' is unaffected by response bias (i.e., is a pure measure of sensitivity) if two assumptions are met regarding the decision variable: (1) The signal and noise distributions are both normal, and (2) the signal and noise distributions have the same standard deviation. definitionAz (ROC area estimated under normality) — “When this procedure is used, the ROC area is called A z , where the z (which refers to z scores) indicates that the noise and signal dis tributions are assumed to be normal.”
