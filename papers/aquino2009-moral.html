phenomenonmoral identity accessibility and moral behavior— “This article proposes and tests a social-cognitive framework for examining the joint influence of situational factors and the centrality of moral identity on moral intentions and behaviors.”
claim-typemechanism— “One important contribution of our research relates to its specification and testing of a mediating mechanism—the current accessibility of moral identity within the working self-concept—through which situational factors and moral identity centrality influence moral intentions and behaviors.”
method — evidence— “A two-group design with a control condition and a moral prime condition was used in the in-lab priming experiment.”
mechanismmoral behavior depends on the situational accessibility of moral identity: cues that make moral identity salient in the working self-concept increase moral motivation, while cues that suppress it (e.g. money/material primes) reduce moral behavior— “if a situational factor increases the current accessibility of moral identity within the working self-concept, then it strengthens the motivation to act morally”
builds-on social-cognitive theory (Bandura, 2001)— “Our theoretical framework is grounded in social-cognitive theory (Bandura, 2001) and uses concepts and psychological mechanisms from social cognition to offer specific predictions about when and how the influence of situational factors on moral behaviors will be moderated by the centrality of moral identity.”
builds-on Aquino and Reed's (2002) moral identity Internalization measure— “The Internalization subscale of Aquino and Reed’s (2002) moral identity measure was used to assess centrality in all studies reported herein.”
builds-on Muller, Judd, and Yzerbyt's (2005) mediated moderation procedure— “Therefore, the present analysis followed established prescriptions for testing mediated moderation hypotheses (see Muller, Judd, & Yzerbyt, 2005).”
extends Walker and Frimer (2007), whose call for systematic exploration of person-situation interaction in the moral domain the framework answers— “In response to their plea, we propose a theoretical framework that explains how situational factors and a relatively stable individual characteristic that we refer to as the centrality of moral identity (Aquino & Reed, 2002) jointly influence moral behavior.”
contradicts research conceptualising moral identity as enduring and stable (Blasi; Colby & Damon; Damon & Hart; Walker & Frimer; Walker & Hennig)— “At first glance, the observation that situational cues can promote self-interested behavior even among people for whom moral identity has high centrality (Studies 3 and 4) appears to contradict a large body of research that conceptualizes moral identity as enduring and stable (cf. sense**THE PRIME WORKS LEAST ON THE PEOPLE IT IS ABOUT, AND THIS IS PREDICTED IN ADVANCE RATHER THAN EXPLAINED AFTERWARDS:** `the prime is expected to activate an otherwise DORMANT aspect of the self-concept for people LOW in moral identity centrality but HAVE LITTLE INFLUENCE on people with highly central moral identities BECAUSE THEIR MORAL SELF-SCHEMAS ARE ALREADY ACCESSIBLE.` higgins1977 raises exactly this variable in ONE CONJECTURAL SENTENCE - `There may even be individual differences in the relative accessibility of different categories` - and does not test it. **This paper builds four studies on it and finds it REVERSES the priming effect**, so the same trait that makes someone morally consistent is the one that makes them unprimeable. A 1977 aside and its test 32 years later, both in this corpus. (The term `chronic accessibility` is Higgins, King & Mavin 1982 and appears nowhere in higgins1977.) A study sampling only committed people would find nothing and conclude the effect is not real— “In other words, the prime is expected to activate an otherwise dormant aspect of the self-concept for people low in moral identity centrality but have little influence on people with highly central moral identities because their moral self-schemas are already accessible”
sense**PRIMING PLACED AS ONE ROUTE AMONG THREE TO THE SAME STATE:** `The activation potential of any particular knowledge structure can be the result of several factors ranging from FREQUENT PRIMING, to INHERITED PERSONALITY ORIENTATIONS, to REPEATED ACTS OF self-definition (Higgins, 1989).` Three roads to accessibility, of which the experimental one is only the fastest. **That sentence does more for a corpus about priming than most results do, because it says what priming is an INSTANCE OF** - and it makes the individual-difference moderator in this paper a consequence of the theory rather than a nuisance variable. (The quote stops before `self-definition`, which the staged text renders as a hyphen-loss fusion.)— “The activation potential of any particular knowledge structure can be the result of several factors ranging from frequent priming, to inherited personality orientations”
sense**AND STUDY 4 REVERSES THE MODERATION OVER TIME, WHICH A ONE-SHOT STUDY WOULD HAVE SCORED AS A NULL:** initially the prime does little for high-centrality people, exactly as the ceiling account predicts. But under repeated feedback that others are behaving selfishly, `WITHOUT A MORAL PRIME, participants for whom moral identity was highly central DID NOT SUSTAIN an increased level of cooperative behavior in the face of selfish behavior by others.` **The prime's value is not the initial push but the resistance to decay.** A corpus that records only peak effect sizes cannot represent this finding at all— “Without a moral prime, participants for whom moral identity was highly central did not sustain an increased level of cooperative behavior in the face of selfish behavior by others”
sense**THE MODERATION IS STATED TWICE AND THE SECOND TIME IT IS A RESULT:** `However, the influence of moral priming IS NOT UNIFORM ACROSS PEOPLE. Rather, moral priming appears to have STRONGER effects on people for whom moral identity has relatively LOW centrality because such priming has GREATER POTENTIAL to increase the accessibility of moral identity within the working self-concept.` The mechanism is a headroom argument, and it is testable: it predicts that any manipulation raising a construct's accessibility will show the same inverse relation to that construct's chronic strength. **This record and higgins1977 are the two ends of that claim in this corpus, and the two ends are not equal** - higgins1977 says only `THERE MAY EVEN BE individual differences in the relative accessibility of different categories`, a speculation with no test attached, while this paper makes that variable its moderator and predicts where a prime will fail— “However, the influence of moral priming is not uniform across people. Rather, moral priming appears to have stronger effects on people for whom moral identity has relatively low centrality because such priming has greater potential to increase the accessibility of moral identity within the working self-concept”
