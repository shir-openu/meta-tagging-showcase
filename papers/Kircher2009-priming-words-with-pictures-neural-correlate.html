phenomenoncross-modal semantic priming (picture-word)— “The aim of the current study was to investigate between‐ (picture–word) and within‐modality (word–word) neural processing using semantic priming.”
claim-typeexistence— “Our results indicate that cross‐modal and unimodal processing share a common neural network within the bilateral fronto‐temporal cortex.”
method — evidence— “The present study examined the neural correlates of uni‐ and cross‐modal priming using fMRI with a word–word and picture–word semantic priming task.”
mechanismPresentation of a prime activates its concept, and that activation spreads automatically along associative pathways in the semantic network to related concepts, so the related target is already pre-activated when it appears.— “The priming effect is explained by an automatic “spread of activation” between related word representations within the semantic network that occurs at short stimulus onset asynchrony [SOA < 400 ms; see Neely, 1991 for a review].”
mechanismThe haemodynamic decrease for related pairs arises because pre-activation lowers the demand on semantic memory retrieval and selection, so less neural work is needed than for unrelated pairs.— “Hence, the response suppression is caused by reduced demands for the semantic memory retrieval or is the result of selection caused by the automatic spread of activation [Matsumoto et al., 2005 ; Tivarus et al., 2006 ].”
mechanismThe shared right fronto-temporal deactivation is attributed to the easier semantic processing of associatively related concepts, an ease that does not depend on which modality delivered the prime.— “we assume that the deactivation of the right fronto‐temporal regions for related pairs reflects the ease of semantic processing for associatively related concepts that is independent of modality”
builds-on Caramazza et al. [1990] (Organized Unitary Conceptual Hypothesis)— “Thus, our data support an amodal semantic system based on the hypothesis of Caramazza et al. [ 1990 ] rather than the hypothesis of multiple semantic systems [e.g., Paivio, 1991 ].”
contradicts Paivio, 1991 (multiple semantic systems)— “Thus, our data support an amodal semantic system based on the hypothesis of Caramazza et al. [ 1990 ] rather than the hypothesis of multiple semantic systems [e.g., Paivio, 1991 ].”
replicates Caramazza et al., 1990; Vandenberghe et al., 1996— “First of all, similar to other studies we found no difference between cross‐modal and unimodal priming on a behavioral or neural level [Caramazza et al., 1990 ; Vandenberghe et al., 1996 ].”
senseA NULL WHERE THE DESIGN EXPECTED A DIFFERENCE: `The comparison of both modalities revealed NO DIFFERENCES between uni‐ and cross‐modal semantic priming.` A picture priming a word behaves like a word priming a word. Set beside Chen2018, tagged earlier tonight - which separated two cross-modal primes by their time courses - this says the modality BOUNDARY itself costs nothing, while what crosses it still can— “The comparison of both modalities revealed no differences between uni‐ and cross‐modal semantic priming. In contrast, common regions of deactivation were found within bilateral fronto‐tempo‐parietal regions. The extensive involvement of the right‐lateralized fronto‐temporal clusters during semantic processing has been demonstrated in some…
senseTHE SHORT SOA IS A CONTROL AND THE ALTERNATIVE IS NAMED: `In order to avoid STRATEGIC INFLUENCE OR EXPECTANCY which can affect priming‐specific patterns of activation ... we wanted to use fast, automatic, implicit processing, i.e., semantic priming with an short stimulus onset asynchrony.` The SOA is not a parameter, it is a fence - it makes strategy impossible rather than unlikely. Eighth record this sitting where a time course is load-bearing, and the first where it is used as a CONTROL rather than reported as a result— “In order to avoid strategic influence or expectancy which can affect priming‐specific patterns of activation [Grossman et al., 2006 ], we wanted to use fast, automatic, implicit processing”
sense`response suppression` DEFINED BY SCOPE rather than by mechanism: it is `a special form of BOLD response deactivation, response enhancement a special form of BOLD activation IN THE CONTEXT OF SEMANTIC PRIMING EXPERIMENTS IN AN fMRI SETTING`. The definition carries its own boundary - the same neural word means this only here - which is exactly the move this corpus exists to record— “Thus, response suppression is a special form of BOLD response “deactivation”, response enhancement a special form of BOLD “activation in the context of semantic priming experiments in an fMRI setting”
senseA SAMPLING DECISION WITH ITS REASON, AND ITS COST: `To minimize the variance and increase homogeneity within our sample, ONLY MALE SUBJECTS WERE MEASURED.` The rationale is stated and is statistical rather than theoretical - homogeneity bought at the price of generalising to half the population. Worth a row because the trade is made explicit rather than left to be noticed by a reader— “To minimize the variance and increase homogeneity within our sample, only male subjects were measured. Three experimental conditions were compared while brain activation was measured with a 3 T fMRI scanner”
controla PRE-TEST established that the word pairs really were strongly related — held — rules out that a null or weak priming effect is a failure of the materials rather than of the hypothesis— “To ensure that the word‐pairs had a strong relationship a pre‐test was conducted.”
controlfour pseudo-randomised versions of the experiment counterbalanced across subjects — held — rules out that the conditions systematically influence one another through their order— “Four pseudo randomized versions of the experiment were counterbalanced across subjects to avoid a systematic effect of conditions on each other.”
differs incross‐modal —distinct-from→ unimodal — WHERE IN THE BRAIN, NOT HOW MUCH - both produce a behavioural priming effect and the comparison of the two revealed NO behavioural difference, while the neural suppression sits in bilateral fronto-parietal regions for unimodal and in the right inferior frontal gyrus for cross-modal— “Behavioral data revealed a priming effect for cross‐modal and unimodal word‐pairs. On a neural level, the unimodal condition revealed response suppression in bilateral fronto‐parietal regions”— “a priming effect for cross‐modal and unimodal word‐pairs”

notecross‐modal —operationalised-as→ response suppression — The neural effect is a DEACTIVATION - less BOLD signal for related than for unrelated pairs - so the priming benefit shows up as the brain doing less. Recorded because the sign is counterintuitive and a reader meeting `suppression` in a priming context needs it.— “Cross‐modal priming led to response suppression within the right inferior frontal gyrus. limitationThe sample was restricted to male subjects, so gender effects on the reported lateralisation and language processing are untested here.— “We restricted our sample to male subjects due to the ongoing debate about gender specific effects for almost all types of cognitive demands, including language processing and lateralization [Baxter et al., 2003 ; Bermeitinger et al., 2008 ; Laws, 1999 ; Lloyd‐Jones and Humphreys, 1997 ].”
