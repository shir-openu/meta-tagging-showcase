mechanismThe reward contingency induces plasticity in the brain, which in turn guides and sustains the spinal cord plasticity that directly produces the reflex change — a top-down hierarchy rather than a single site of change— “Taken together, these studies showed that H-reflex conditioning depends on a hierarchy of plasticity (i.e., Figure 2C), in which the reward contingency produces plasticity in the brain that guides and maintains the plasticity in the spinal cord that is directly responsible for the H-reflex change”
mechanismAcquiring a new spinal behaviour disturbs the equilibrium serving existing behaviours, which triggers widespread adaptive change until a new equilibrium serving the enlarged repertoire is reached — and that renegotiation can repair a behaviour that trauma had impaired— “When the acquisition of a new behavior (e.g., a larger or smaller H-reflex) changes the spinal cord and disturbs older behaviors, it triggers widespread adaptive changes that arrive at a new equilibrium that serves the expanded repertoire.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingAcross all five model versions — monkey SSR, monkey and rat and mouse H-reflex, and human H-reflex — conditioning worked in 75-80% of subjects and left the reflex near its starting size in the rest.
findingIn rats and monkeys the reflex settled at roughly 175% of baseline for up-conditioning and 55% for down-conditioning.
findingHumans reached 140% (up) and 69% (down) of baseline despite completing only 3-5% as many trials as the animals.
findingThe conditioning-induced reflex asymmetry outlasted removal of all descending input, proving the change resides in the spinal cord itself.
findingDown-conditioning raises the motoneuron firing threshold and slows axonal conduction velocity.
findingThe threshold shift together with a small reduction in the Ia afferent EPSP accounts for most of the smaller reflex.
findingSuccessful down-conditioning markedly increases the number of GABAergic terminals on the motoneuron.
findingLesion work shows the corticospinal tract and sensorimotor cortex are necessary for reflex conditioning while other descending and ascending tracts are not.
findingThe cerebellum is required to keep the cortical plasticity alive beyond about 40 days.
findingIn normal rats the conditioned reflex change leaves right/left symmetry and step length intact.
findingUp-conditioning abolished the limp of rats with a lateral-column lesion while reflex-measurement-only controls stayed asymmetrical.
findingIn people with chronic incomplete SCI, 30 down-conditioning sessions reduced the soleus H-reflex in two-thirds of subjects and produced faster, more symmetrical walking with better bilateral EMG modulation, with the reflex still reduced months later.
