mechanismTHE DIFFICULTY IS ATTRIBUTED TO THE STRUCTURE OF THE OBJECT, NOT TO THE METHOD: analysing figurative RATHER THAN LITERAL language POSES CERTAIN DIFFICULTIES DUE TO THE VERY STRUCTURE OF FIGURATIVE EXPRESSIONS - so the obstacle is located in what is being measured before any tool is named — “The analysis of figurative language, rather than literal language, poses certain difficulties due to the very structure of figurative expressions. The suggested method in this articles offers a way to overcome these difficulties.”
mechanismA PROHIBITION IS SHOWN VIOLATING ITSELF IN ITS OWN WORDING: the Areopagite judges CONDEMN the use of figurative language AS DECEPTIVE, and THEY THEMSELVES USE A METAPHOR OF A CARPENTER IN THEIR CONDEMNATION. The rule against a class of language is stated in that class of language — “Ironically, although the Areopagite judges condemn the use of figurative language as deceptive, they themselves use a metaphor of a carpenter in their condemnation”
mechanismAND THE SHARPER POINT IS THAT THE HOMONYMY DOES THE WORK, NOT THE METAPHOR: the influence is NOT ONLY evident through the carpenter - RATHER IT IS THE MORE SUBTLE HOMONYM SENSES OF THE WORDS UPRIGHT AND RULE THAT TRULY REVEAL THE PERVASIVE POWER OF THE FIGURATIVE. Two words carrying two senses each, unnoticed by the speakers, are what the argument turns on — “The influence of figurative language is not only evident here through the metaphor of the carpenter, but rather it is the more subtle homonym senses of the words upright and rule that truly reveal the pervasive power of the figurative.”
mechanismA CLASS OF METAPHOR IS DEFINED BY WHAT WOULD BE IMPOSSIBLE WITHOUT IT: the word is catalogued as part of the ORIENTATIONAL class of metaphors, which RELY ON OUR MOST BASIC SENSE OF SPACE AND PHYSICAL CONCEPTS, so much so that WITHOUT WHICH WE COULD NOT FUNCTION IN THE WORLD - COULD NOT REASON OR COMMUNICATE. The category's members are identified by the cost of their absence — “In Metaphors We Live By, Lakoff and Johnson catalogue the word upright as part of the orientational class of metaphors, which rely on our most basic sense of space and physical concepts, so much so that “without which we could not function in the world – could not reason or communicate””
mechanismA METHOD'S OUTPUT AND THE CONCEPT IT IS MEANT TO MEASURE ARE REPORTED NOT TO CORRESPOND: after comparing a computational reading with a thematic one, the conclusion quoted is WE CANNOT FIND A CLEAR OR CONSTANT RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN AN LDA-TOPIC AND A THEME. The instrument produces something, and it is not the thing the word names — “he concluded that “we cannot find a clear or constant relationship between an LDA-topic and a theme out of this comparison” [Navarro-Colorado 2018, 7].”
mechanismTHE METHOD IS SAID TO WORK ON ONE TEXT TYPE AND FAIL ON ANOTHER, AND THE REASON GIVEN IS CONTEXT-EXCLUSIVITY OF VOCABULARY: topic modelling applied to poetry DOES NOT EXTRACT CLEAR AND DEFINED THEMES AS IT DOES WHEN APPLIED TO SCIENTIFIC TEXTS, because IN SCIENTIFIC TEXTS THERE ARE WORDS THAT ARE USED EXCLUSIVELY IN VERY SPECIFIC CONTEXTS - and THE LINGUISTIC USES IN POETRY WORK IN THE EXACT OPPOSITE WAY. **The property that makes the method work is a property of the vocabulary, not of the algorithm** — “LDA topic modeling applied to poetry does not extract clear and defined themes as it does when applied to scientific texts. […] In scientific texts there are words that are used exclusively in very specific contexts, related to specific topics. […] The linguistic uses in poetry work in the exact opposite way.”
mechanismTHE NAMING STEP IS IDENTIFIED AS THE MANUAL AND SUBJECTIVE ONE: in topic modelling, DISCERNING THE TITLES of the groups of words REMAINS A MANUAL TASK THAT REQUIRES ACTIVE AND OFTEN SUBJECTIVE INTERPRETATION. The algorithm delivers groups; a person supplies the words that say what the groups are — “In topic modeling, however, discerning the titles of the groups of words remains a manual task that requires active and often subjective interpretation.”
mechanismA NAMED DEFECT OF AUTOMATIC GROUPING IS CITED, AND IT IS A DISAGREEMENT BETWEEN MACHINE AND HUMAN CATEGORIES: a list of words treated as one topic MAY INCLUDE WORDS THAT HUMAN INTERPRETERS RECOGNIZE AS BELONGING TO DISTINCT TOPICS - a problem with its own name, WORD INTRUSION. The defect is defined by the mismatch, and the mismatch is what the name records — “A list of words considered as a topic by a topic modeling may include words that human interpreters recognize as belonging to distinct topics, a problem described by Chang et al. as “word intrusion””
mechanismTHE RIGHT RELIABILITY MEASURE IS NAMED, ITS PURPOSE STATED, AND IT IS EXPLICITLY NOT DONE, WITH THE REASON: given the SAME set of topics and instances, TWO INTERPRETERS MIGHT STILL CHOOSE DIFFERENT TOPICS for a word - INTER-ANNOTATORS AGREEMENT is the method by which that agreement can be measured, PROVIDING A STATUS OF CONSENSUS, and the procedure REQUIRES CERTAIN RESOURCES AND HENCE WILL BE IMPLEMENTED later. **The measure that would qualify the result is named and deferred in the same breath** — “Another subjective aspect pertains to the classifying process itself. Given the same set of topics and figurative instances, two interpreters might still choose different topics for a word in certain cases. The inter-annotators agreement (IAA) is a method by which the agreement between interpreters can be measured, providing a status of consensus, in varying degrees, to the classifications. Tthis procedure requires certain resources and hence will be implemented in a future study.”
mechanismTHE IMPROVEMENTS PROPOSED ARE PROPERTIES OF THE SETUP RATHER THAN OF THE ANALYSIS: the recommendations begin with REDUCING THE NUMBER OF TOPICS AND ENLARGING THE DATASET, which will allow the algorithm to have MORE DATA AND LESS AMBIGUITY REGARDING TOPICS - so the ambiguity is treated as a quantity the design can lower rather than a feature of the object — “Several recommendations can be made based on training the classifier to reach better results, starting with reducing the number of topics and enlarging the dataset, which will allow the algorithm to have more data and less ambiguity regarding topics.”

findingFigurative language resists computational analysis because of how figurative expressions are structured, not because of the size of the text. — “The analysis of figurative language, rather than literal language, poses certain difficulties due to the very structure of figurative expressions”
findingThe method combines two techniques rather than one: topic classification to label the figures, network analysis to relate them. — “In this article I suggest a computational approach for the analysis of figurative language, which employs two main methods: topic classification and network analysis”
findingFigurative analysis is not confined to literary criticism; it matters wherever the object of study is text. — “The analysis of figurative language plays a major role in literary criticism and, to varying degrees, in other fields of the humanities and social sciences whose orientation is textual”
findingThe method is judged by whether it can support a close reading, not only by whether it runs. — “the article examines the method's ability to provide insights into the thematic concerns of a text, assess existing interpretations, and develop a close reading”
