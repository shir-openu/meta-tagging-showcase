phenomenonsemantic priming
claim-typemechanism
mechanismActivation spreads from the prime's concept along indirect links to concepts it is not directly associated with, producing mediated priming. — “According to spreading activation models, when a concept (e.g., LION) is activated, this activation spreads not only to directly associated concepts (e.g., TIGER), but also to indirectly related concepts (e.g., STRIPES).”
mechanismParticipants notice that related pairs predict word targets and check back from target to prime, so relatedness biases the lexical decision after the target has been accessed. — “Participants who become aware of this contingency may begin to use prime–target relatedness strategically to facilitate their lexical decisions by checking back after the presentation of a target to see if it is related to the prime”
mechanismAn auditory prime leaves an echoic trace that survives into target presentation, letting participants integrate or strategically match prime and target and so inflating auditory priming effects. — “Alternatively, because auditory primes leave a longer echoic trace that persists after the presentation of the target, an equally plausible explanation is that such a trace allows for some type of strategic matching or prime–target integration by participants.”
mechanismIn distributed recurrent networks a featurally similar prime leaves the network nearer the target's attractor basin, so it settles into the target pattern in fewer iterations. — “If the previous word was semantically similar to the new word, the network will settle faster to the attractor basin of the new stimulus than if the previous word was dissimilar.”
relation — “Finally, whereas Lucas (2000) concluded that there is little to no evidence of pure associative priming, the present review leads to the opposite conclusion.”
relation — “First, although Lucas (2000) claims that automatic priming does occur for category coordinates, close inspection of her studies reveals that priming from these items is not automatic.”
relation — “It is hoped that the inclusion of pure associative studies and the present, more careful analysis of pure semantic priming, when combined with Lucas’s (2000) more global analysis of pure semantic priming, will provide a clearer overall picture of the evidence for and against both associative and featural priming.”
replicationcontested
limitationThe status of several relation types (collocates, episodic relatives, script relations) is left unresolved and needs further experiments. — “The status of other relations, such as collocates, episodic relatives, and script relations, is unclear and requires further experimentation.”
limitationRestricting the review to published studies leaves the direction of the resulting bias in effect-size estimates unknown. — “In the present case, it is unclear whether this should increase or decrease one’s estimates of effect size.”
limitationThe review cannot say why auditory presentation yields so much larger priming estimates than visual presentation. — “It is unclear why presentation modality should make such a large difference in effect size estimates.”
limitationThe available evidence cannot decide whether the associative boost reflects combined lexical and semantic connections or simply greater featural overlap. — “However, one cannot tell, on the basis of the available evidence, whether this associative boost is due to combined lexical and semantic connections, as Collins and Loftus (1975) predicted, or simply to a greater overlap in semantic features between these items (Chiarello et al., 1990; Lucas, 2000; McRae & Boisvert, 1998).”

findingAutomatic semantic priming is produced by both association strength and feature overlap, not by either alone. — “The most important conclusion is that automatic priming appears to be due to both association strength and feature overlap.”
findingMediated associates (e.g. lion-stripes) give the strongest evidence for automatic purely associative priming, while functional associates, synonyms and antonyms instead support priming based on feature overlap. — “Mediated associates provide the strongest evidence of automatic associative priming, whereas functional associates, synonyms, and antonyms instead support priming based on feature overlap.”
findingCategory coordinates and perceptually similar items show no automatic priming when the stimuli are presented visually. — “In contrast, automatic priming does not occur for category coordinates or perceptually similar items, at least when presented in the visual modality.”
findingPriming from category coordinates collapses to about 4 msec in the pronunciation task, indicating that the large effects seen in the standard lexical decision task come from task-dependent strategies. — “By contrast, only 4 msec of priming from these items occurs in the pronunciation task.”
findingIn the standard lexical decision task at long SOA, backward priming (mean 28 msec) was statistically equivalent to forward priming (mean 34 msec) in every experiment, contradicting the directional prediction of spreading activation. — “Moreover, averaged across all experiments using the standard LDT and a long SOA, the size of the backwardpriming effect (M 5 28 msec) is comparable to the size of the forward-priming effect (M 5 34 msec), and in every experiment the two priming effects were statistically equivalent.”
findingMediated priming is largest when strategic processing is minimised and vanishes when strategic processing is encouraged, which the author treats as the signature of automaticity. — “In fact, mediated priming is strongest when strategic processing is minimized and disappears when strategic processing is encouraged.”
findingThe author's own LSA analysis of Balota and Lorch's items found mediated pairs significantly more similar (.20) than the same items randomly re-paired as controls (.10), so mediated items are not contextually unrelated after all. — “The mean LSA similarity value of .20 for Balota and Lorch’s mediated items was significantly greater than the .10 value given to these items when they were randomly re-paired to form unrelated controls [t(47) 5 4.27, p , .001].”
findingStudies using auditory primes produced roughly twice the effect size of studies using visual presentation (Cohen's d of .51 versus .25), so presentation modality alone drives much of the reported pure-semantic priming. — “In fact, if one simply averages her unweighted effect sizes from the five studies in which auditory presentation was used, the resulting Cohen’s d of .51 is more than twice as large as the d of .25 obtained from the remaining visual presentation studies.”
findingPublished studies gave a mean effect size of .35, almost twice the .18 of unpublished studies, indicating publication bias inflates pure-semantic priming estimates. — “An informal examination of the effect sizes appears to favor the first explanation, since the unweighted mean effect size from published studies was .35, an estimate almost twice as large as the .18 effect size from unpublished studies.”
findingContrary to Lucas's meta-analysis, priming from category coordinates is not automatic; her significant effect size came from studies using moderately-to-strongly associated items and strategy-encouraging methods. — “First, although Lucas (2000) claims that automatic priming does occur for category coordinates, close inspection of her studies reveals that priming from these items is not automatic.”

definitionsemantic priming effect — “The semantic priming effect refers to the consistent observation that people respond faster to a target word (e.g., cat) when it is preceded by a semantically related prime (e.g., dog) rather than by an unrelated prime (e.g., table).”
definitionautomatic (as used of a priming process) — “In line with Posner and Snyder, the term automatic here suggests that the process occurs quickly and unintentionally, even if one is simultaneously engaged in other activities.”
definitionassociation strength — “The association strength of a given pair (e.g., dog–cat) refers to the percentage of people responding with the target word (cat) when given the cue (dog).”
definitionexpectancy generation — “The expectancy generation component involves the use of the context provided by the prime to anticipate consciously the approximate area in the network (or collection of features, if a distributed representation is assumed) containing the target word.”
definitionfour-level classification of association strength (none / weak / moderate / strong) — “In this analysis, the association strength was defined as one of the following: (1) no association, with an average strength of less than 1% in both the forward and backward directions; (2) weak association, with an average strength between 1% and 10%; (3) moderate association, with an average strength between 10% and 20%; and (4) strong association, with an average strength of greater than 20%.”
definitioninstrument relations — “Instruments (e.g., hammer–nail) are related in that the intended function of one of the items is to perform some action on the other.”
definitionscript relations — “Script relations (e.g., restaurant–wine) involve items that are part of the same script or schema”
definitionattractor basins — “These learned patterns are referred to as attractor basins, because the network is biased to settle into one of these known patterns when given partial information.”
definitionsemantic distance (as distinct from semantic relatedness) — “According to Collins and Loftus (1975), semantic distance is the “shortest path [direct or indirect] between two nodes” (p. 412, note 3).”
definitionHyperspace Analogue to Language (HAL) — “The HAL model of semantic memory assesses a word’s meaning through the construction of a co-occurrence matrix based on a sample of 160 million words taken from Usenet newsgroups.”
