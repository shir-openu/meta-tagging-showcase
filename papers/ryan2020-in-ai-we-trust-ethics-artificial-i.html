mechanismTHE ARGUMENT TURNS ENTIRELY ON TWO OF THE FIVE CHARACTERISTICS. AI can satisfy confidence, competence and vulnerability; it cannot satisfy `there is a possibility that B will BETRAY A's trust` nor `B is MOTIVATED to do X for affective or normative reasons`, because it has no emotive states and cannot be held responsible— “Section Three establishes a definition of trust based on five characteristics: A has confidence in B to do X; A believes in the competence of B to do X; A is vulnerable to the actions of B; there is a possibility that B will betray A’s trust; and A believes that B is motivated to do X for affective or normative reasons.”
mechanismand the consequence is a transfer of responsibility, which is the practical stake: `in the normative account, the trustee MUST BE HELD RESPONSIBLE for its actions, WHICH AI CANNOT. Whereas, RELIABLE AI PLACES THE BURDEN OF RESPONSIBILITY ON THOSE DEVELOPING, DEPLOYING, AND USING these technologies`— “In the normative account, the trustee must be held responsible for its actions, which AI cannot. Whereas, reliable AI places the burden of responsibility on those developing, deploying, and using these technologies.”
mechanismthe three accounts share confidence, competence and vulnerability and diverge only on betrayal and motive - so the disagreement is located in two components, not in the whole concept— “The definitions include three characteristics: A has confidence in B to do X; A believes B is competent to do X; A is vulnerable to the actions of B.”
mechanismeach component is a sliding scale rather than a threshold, so trust can be high on vulnerability and low on confidence at the same time— “Each of these distinctions is based on a sliding-scale, where there are varying degrees and contexts of trust.”
mechanismAI satisfies the first three components and fails the last two, which is why what looks like trust in AI is reliance— “The following sections will outline the different components within trust definitions and will propose that AI can meet the first three requirements, but fails to meet the last two.”
mechanismthere can be no breach of trust where there was no confidence to begin with - pessimism about B makes trust impossible rather than merely weak— “One must have a sense of confidence in the person doing X, because there can be no breach of trust if I am pessimistic that B will do X.”
