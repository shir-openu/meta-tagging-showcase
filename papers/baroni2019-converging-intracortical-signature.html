transferfrom dynamical systems theory and control theory: The fast-to-slow negative feedback loop is adopted as a circuit motif because control-theoretic arguments identify it as a building block giving both sensitivity and stability.— “This choice is motivated by dynamical systems and control theoretical arguments indicating that negative feedback loops from fast to slow subsystems constitute dynamical building blocks for fast and stable responses to inputs”

mechanismSynaptic interactions between excitatory and inhibitory populations operating at theta and gamma timescales, coupled by a negative feedback loop, generate the two-peaked (delta-theta and gamma) spectral profile of information content.— “Hence, this profile can be explained as a consequence of the synaptic interactions between excitatory and inhibitory neural populations at theta and gamma timescales, as captured in our model.”
mechanismShort-term synaptic depression of the bottom-up pathway depletes available neurotransmitter as the speech envelope arrives, producing large onset responses and attenuated responses to later syllables.— “Synaptic depression qualitatively reproduces the large responses to speech onsets and attenuated responses to subsequent syllables observed in the iEEG recordings.”
mechanismThe beta peak in early auditory cortex is inherited top-down from higher cortical areas rather than generated locally, which is why a single-area microcircuit model cannot reproduce it.— “lending strong support to the hypothesis that beta oscillations as recorded in early auditory cortical areas might be largely inherited from higher cortical areas.”
mechanismExcessive self-excitation drives the network into strong baseline synchronisation and saturating oscillatory responses, so different speech segments elicit near-identical patterns and stimulus information is lost.— “excess of baseline synchronization in the corresponding frequency, and, perhaps more critically, a strong oscillatory responsiveness to inputs impairing selective (hence informative) modulations by the speech input”
