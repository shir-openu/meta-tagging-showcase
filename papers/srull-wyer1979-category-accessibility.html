senseTHE AMBIGUITY CONDITION, FOR THE FOURTH TIME AND FROM A FOURTH DIRECTION: `the effect of category accessibility on the encoding of behavioral information is MUCH MORE PRONOUNCED WHEN THE IMPLICATIONS OF THIS INFORMATION ARE RELATIVELY AMBIGUOUS.` With higgins1977 (`at least in ambiguous situations`), north1999 (`only when there is an element of UNCERTAINTY`) and money2013 (`a situation that does not leave much room for interpretation`), **that is four papers across 1977-2015 and three fields.** And here it is not a qualifier in passing: it is one of four summarised findings, and the target behaviours were SELECTED for ambiguity by a separate rating study of 43 subjects before the experiment began— “In addition, the effect of category accessibility on the encoding of behavioral information is much more pronounced when the implications of this information are relatively ambiguous”
senseFREQUENCY IS A DOSE, AND THE PAPER SAYS SO AS A GENERAL LAW RATHER THAN A RESULT: `The accessibility of these concepts, and therefore the likelihood that they are subsequently used, INCREASES WITH THE NUMBER OF TIMES that instances of them have been activated IN THE PAST.` Not `in this experiment` - in the past. **The claim is that ordinary life is continuously setting accessibility**, and the laboratory manipulation is only a controlled instance of something always running. That is a much larger claim than the experiment tests, and it is the one the field inherited— “The accessibility of these concepts, and therefore the likelihood that they are subsequently used, increases with the number of times that instances of them have been activated in the past”
senseTHE DECAY IS SLOW AND INCOMPLETE: `although these effects decrease with the time interval between their activation and the acquisition of information to be interpreted or encoded, they are SOMETIMES DETECTABLE EVEN AFTER 24 HOURS.` A priming effect surviving a day, in a corpus whose other records measure the same word in MILLISECONDS - vorberg2003's time courses, Holcomb2005's 40 ms primes, Huang2018's 16 ms exposure. **The same term spans six orders of magnitude of persistence**, and `sometimes detectable` is the hedge that keeps this one honest— “Moreover, although these effects decrease with the time interval between their activation and the acquisition of information to be interpreted or encoded, they are sometimes detectable even after 24 hours”
senseA GRADIENT USED TO RULE OUT A RIVAL ACCOUNT: the priming generalised to traits related to the primed one only indirectly, but LESS strongly - `The fact that these effects were typically LESS on judgments of the latter than of the former traits suggests that this generalizability is NOT SIMPLY DUE TO A HALO EFFECT produced by exposure to `good` or `bad` concepts on the priming task.` **A halo would not respect the distance between traits.** The rival is excluded by the SHAPE of the effect rather than by a control condition, which is a cheaper and less common move— “The fact that these effects were typically less on judgments of the latter than of the former traits suggests that this generalizability is not simply due to a halo effect”
