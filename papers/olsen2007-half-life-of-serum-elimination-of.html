phenomenonserum elimination of perfluoroalkyl acids that is slow enough in humans, and fast enough in every other species measured, that the interspecies gap is itself the finding— “Nevertheless, the present study data strongly indicate that humans are very slow eliminators of these three perfluoroalkyl acids compared with other species.”
claim-typeexistence— “Based on these data, humans appear to have a long half-life of serum elimination of PFOS, PFHS, and PFOA. Differences in species-specific pharmacokinetics may be due, in part, to a saturable renal resorption process.”

contribution — evidence— “Twenty-six (24 male, 2 female) retired fluorochemical production workers, with no additional occupational exposure, had periodic blood samples collected over 5 years, with serum stored in plastic vials at −80°C.”
transferfrom pharmacokinetics: first-order elimination kinetics and the half-life construct, carried from single-dose animal pharmacokinetics onto an observational human cohort with no known dose and no defined time zero— “We used WinNonlin software, version 4.1 (Pharsight Corporation, Mountain View, CA) to calculate the half-life of elimination based on the mean value of the split analyses. The data were linear when plotted as the logarithm of serum concentration versus time. Therefore, we assumed a first order model.”
transferfrom renal physiology: the renal transporter explanation, carried from rodent sex-difference studies to account for a between-species difference in humans— “The diversity of proximal tubular organic anion transporters and potential for genetic variation have indicated that the long elimination half-life in humans compared with that in other species may be due to differential expression of organic anion transporters and could be linked to either low-level transport into urine or increased tubular…
mechanismsaturable renal resorption a high-affinity transporter returns filtered perfluoroalkyl acid from the renal tubule to the blood; because it saturates, the elimination rate depends on concentration and on how much transporter a species expresses— “Based on these data, humans appear to have a long half-life of serum elimination of PFOS, PFHS, and PFOA. Differences in species-specific pharmacokinetics may be due, in part, to a saturable renal resorption process.”
mechanismdifferential expression of organic anion transporters species and sexes differ in which proximal-tubule anion transporters they express, so the same molecule is returned to the blood at different rates - offered as the reason the human half-life is years and the rat's is days— “The diversity of proximal tubular organic anion transporters and potential for genetic variation have indicated that the long elimination half-life in humans compared with that in other species may be due to differential expression of organic anion transporters and could be linked to either low-level transport into urine or increased tubular…
mechanismmetabolic stability of the carbon-fluorine bond the C-F bond's stability and the high electronegativity of perfluorinated alkyl acids make metabolism unfavourable, so elimination is the only route of loss and half-life is set entirely by excretion— “Because of the stability of the carbon–fluorine bond and the high electronegativity of perfluorinated alkyl acids, metabolism would not be favored; thus, perfluorohexanesulfonate (PFHS) is also not expected to be metabolized.”
builds-on Calafat et al.'s NHANES estimates of general-population serum concentrations, used here as the background floor the retirees had to stay above— “Furthermore, only four study subjects (subjects 1–4) had end-of-study PFOS concentrations that were below the upper 95% CI of the geometric mean of the 95th percentile as reported by Calafat et al. (97.5 ng/mL).”
extends intravenous and repeated-oral-dose pharmacokinetic studies in cynomolgus monkeys, from which human persistence had previously been extrapolated— “The serum elimination half-life of PFOA in the present study’s 26 retired fluorochemical production workers is considerably longer than the elimination half-life in cynomolgus monkeys (approximately 14–40 days) after either repeated daily oral doses or a single intravenous injection.”
qualifies one-compartment first-order pharmacokinetic modelling of perfluoroalkyl acids in humans— “The actual pharmacokinetics of PFOS, PFHS, and PFOA in humans is not likely to be consistent with a one-compartment distribution in spite of the present data fit to a…
