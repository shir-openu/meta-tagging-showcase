phenomenonsubliminal priming— “A subliminal priming effect is obtained when undetectable prime stimuli modulate targetrelated processes in some way.”
claim-typeexistence— “Thus, we conclude that subliminal priming effects can be observed even when taking the steps necessary to guard against the task-difficulty artifact.”
method — evidence— “To address the concerns with Pratte and Rouder’s (2009) procedure for controlling task difficulty, the primes in the present experiment were presented in color (red or green) in both the targetand prime-classification tasks.”
mechanismusing a backward mask that is visually similar to the prime (a letter string masking a letter-string prime) suppresses prime identity strongly enough to reach chance-level identification at durations that still produce priming, whereas dissimilar and repeated masks let the prime pop out— “This decision, along with their use of identical forward and backward masks, which causes the prime to “pop out” or appear in relief, contributed to the lack of effective masking in their experiment.”
mechanismdifferences in masking procedure, rather than differences in control of task difficulty, are what drive the opposite conclusions of the two studies— “We suggest that our findings stand in contrast to those of Pratte and Rouder (2009) because of important differences in the masking procedures between the two studies.”
mechanismwhen primes and targets share a stimulus set, learned stimulus-response mappings can be triggered automatically by an unseen prime, so apparent above-chance prime classification may reflect nonconscious motor activation rather than awareness— “then above-chance performance in the prime-classification task may index nonconsciously elicited motor processes— not visual awareness.”
contradicts Pratte and Rouder (2009)— “But Pratte and Rouder’s conclusion that controlling for the task-difficulty artifact eliminates subliminal priming lacks empirical support.”

builds-on Morey et al. (2008) mass-at-chance (MAC) model— “The prime-classification data from the present experiment were analyzed using the one-parameter MAC model (Morey et al., 2008), which is available as a package for the R language (http://r-forge.r-project.org/projects/massatchance/).”
sense**THE CRITERION STATED AS AN IF-THEN, WHICH IS WORTH HOLDING VERBATIM:** `If, in a separate task that assesses PRIME AWARENESS, subjects are not able to identify the prime stimuli at BETTER THAN CHANCE levels (i.e., d' is not statistically different from zero), then SUBLIMINAL PRIMING IS SAID TO HAVE OCCURRED.` **`is said to have occurred`** - the phenomenon is the conjunction of two measurements, and the paper is explicit that the word is licensed by the second one. dehaene1998 and holcomb2005 argue about WHAT was processed without awareness; this record is about what licenses the word `subliminal` at all, which is the prior question and the corpus had no clean statement of it— “If, in a separate task that assesses prime awareness, subjects are not able to identify the prime stimuli at better than chance levels (i.e., d′ is not statistically different from zero), then subliminal priming is said to have occurred”
sense**AND THE CRITIQUE IT ANSWERS IS AN ATTACK ON THE MEASUREMENT, NOT ON THE EFFECT:** `In a recent critique of subliminal priming, Pratte and Rouder (2009) suggested that previous claims of subliminal priming may have been due to A FAILURE TO CONTROL THE TASK DIFFICULTY between the experiment proper and the prime-classification task.` **If the certifying task is simply harder, chance performance on it certifies nothing.** That is the strongest form of methodological criticism - it does not dispute a result, it disputes what the result is evidence OF - and a corpus that records priming findings without recording this cannot represent why the literature is contested— “In a recent critique of subliminal priming, Pratte and Rouder (Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics, 71, 1276–1283, 2009) suggested that previous claims of subliminal priming may have been due to a failure to control the task difficulty between the experiment proper and the prime-classification task”
sense**THE ANSWER IS BUILT INTO THE STIMULUS RATHER THAN ARGUED IN A DISCUSSION - PRINT THE PRIMES IN COLOUR:** `In the prime-classification task, subjects reported the color of the primes VERY ACCURATELY, indicating ALMOST PERFECT CONTROL OF TASK DIFFICULTY, but they COULD NOT IDENTIFY the primes.` 92.1% on colour, chance on identity, **same stimulus, same trial, within subject.** The colour report proves the task was easy and the subject was attending, so the identity failure cannot be blamed on difficulty. **A control made of the stimulus, not of an argument** - and the cleanest reply to an artifact objection I have tagged— “In the prime-classification task, subjects reported the color of the primes very accurately, indicating almost perfect control of task difficulty, but they could not identify the primes”
sense**AND A FOOTNOTE CARRIES THE STRONGEST SENTENCE IN THE PAPER:** `The primes that were presented for 40 to ~60 ms were VISIBLE (d' > 2.0); the primes presented for LESS THAN 40 ms were NOT VISIBLE (d' not different from zero), AND YET THEY PRODUCED RELIABLE PRIMING EFFECTS.` **Visible primes prime and invisible primes prime.** So the priming function and the awareness function are separable, and a study that finds priming has learned nothing about awareness until it measures awareness separately - which is the whole argument of this paper, stated in a footnote about a different experiment— “The primes that were presented for 40 to ~60 ms were visible (d′ > 2.0); the primes presented for less than 40 ms were not visible (d′ not different from zero), and yet they produced reliable priming effects”
controlthe letters in the backward mask were randomly repositioned on every trial — held — rules out that the masking effect is specific to one mask arrangement rather than to masking as such— “The forward mask always appeared as it is depicted in Table 1; the letters in the backward mask were randomly assigned to different positions on each trial.”
