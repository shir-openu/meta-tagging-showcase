phenomenonsubliminal priming
claim-typeexistence
mechanismusing a backward mask that is visually similar to the prime (a letter string masking a letter-string prime) suppresses prime identity strongly enough to reach chance-level identification at durations that still produce priming, whereas dissimilar and repeated masks let the prime pop out — “This decision, along with their use of identical forward and backward masks, which causes the prime to “pop out” or appear in relief, contributed to the lack of effective masking in their experiment.”
mechanismdifferences in masking procedure, rather than differences in control of task difficulty, are what drive the opposite conclusions of the two studies — “We suggest that our findings stand in contrast to those of Pratte and Rouder (2009) because of important differences in the masking procedures between the two studies.”
mechanismwhen primes and targets share a stimulus set, learned stimulus-response mappings can be triggered automatically by an unseen prime, so apparent above-chance prime classification may reflect nonconscious motor activation rather than awareness — “then above-chance performance in the prime-classification task may index nonconsciously elicited motor processes— not visual awareness.”
relation — “But Pratte and Rouder’s conclusion that controlling for the task-difficulty artifact eliminates subliminal priming lacks empirical support.”

limitationEven with 240 prime-classification trials the design remains open to the low-power criticism levelled at subliminal priming studies. — “But the present study, despite including 1.5 times as many trials in the prime-classification task (N = 240) as the targetclassification task (N = 160), is still susceptible to the criticism that Rouder, Morey, and colleagues have persuasively leveled against many subliminal priming studies (cf. Morey, Rouder, & Speckman, 2008, 2009; Rouder, Morey, Speckman, & Pratte, 2007).”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingReliable masked congruence priming was obtained at both 40 ms and 50 ms prime durations. — “The planned comparisons revealed significant priming effects with both 40-ms primes [t(20) = 2.5, p < .05] and 50-ms primes [t(20) = 2.2, p < .05].”
findingPresenting primes in colour let subjects report the prime's colour almost as accurately as they classified targets, which the author takes as successful equating of difficulty across the two tasks. — “These high accuracy scores, which approximate the accuracy scores in the target-classification task, indicate near-perfect control of task difficulty.”
findingEven though colour was reported near ceiling, prime identity could not be identified above chance at 40 ms, while identity identification did exceed chance at 50 ms. — “With 40-ms primes, the mean accuracy rate was 51.8% (d′ = 0.098; one-sample t test: t(20) = 1.67, p = .19), and with 50-ms primes it was 55.6% (d′ = 0.308; one-sample t test: t(20) = 2.43, p = .02).”
findingA Bayesian mass-at-chance analysis confirmed genuine subliminality for most subjects at 40 ms but almost none at 50 ms. — “As is clear, subliminality was confirmed for only 1 subject in the condition with 50-ms primes. In contrast, 16 of the 21 subjects were judged to be at chance with the 40-ms primes.”
findingRestricting the priming analysis to only those subject-by-duration cells whose subliminality was confirmed still yielded a significant priming effect. — “The results of this t test indicated a significant subliminal priming effect [t(16) = 2.79, p = .01].”
findingThe size of the priming effect did not shrink as the evidence for subliminality got stronger, arguing that the priming is not carried by partially visible primes. — “As is clear from the fitted polynomial (LOESS), the magnitude of the priming effect does not decrease as the criterion for subliminality increases.”
findingThe study therefore rejects Pratte and Rouder's claim that controlling the task-difficulty artifact eliminates subliminal priming. — “Thus, we conclude that subliminal priming effects can be observed even when taking the steps necessary to guard against the task-difficulty artifact.”
