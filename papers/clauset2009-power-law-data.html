sensepower law - a hypothesis one can only fail to reject; the paper says an observed quantity can rarely if ever be certified as power-law distributed— “In practice, we can rarely, if ever, be certain that an observed quantity is drawn from a power-law distribution. sensepower law - a property of the tail only, holding above a chosen minimum rather than across the whole range— “In practice, few empirical phenomena obey power laws for all values of x. More often the power law applies only for values greater than some minimum xmin. In such cases we say that the tail of the distribution follows a power law.”
sensescaling parameter - a quantity whose published values for one data set disagree, because they depend on the estimator used— “the citation distribution data of [48] have been reported to have a scaling parameter of either 2.9 [62] or 2.5 [33], neither of which are compatible with our maximum likelihood figure of 3.16”
controla goodness-of-fit p-value computed for the power law AND for each plausible competing distribution, and the two compared — held — rules out that the power law fits because heavy-tailed data fits almost any heavy-tailed form - a log-normal or a stretched exponential may fit as well or better, and only comparing them shows which— “We can eliminate this possibility by using a goodness-of-fit test again—we can simply calculate a p-value for a fit to the competing distribution and compare it to the p-value for the power law.”
controlan explicit rejection rule fixed in advance: p greater than 0.1 keeps the power-law hypothesis, otherwise it is rejected — held — rules out that the decision to accept a power law is made after seeing how good the fit looks— “If the resulting p-value is greater than 0.1 the power law is a plausible hypothesis for the data, otherwise it is rejected.”
controlthe whole procedure was run on SYNTHETIC data drawn from known distributions — held — rules out that the method itself is wrong - synthetic data has a known answer, so the procedure can be checked against ground truth before it is trusted on real data— “We evaluate the effectiveness of the approach with tests on synthetic data and give critical comparisons to previous approaches.”
