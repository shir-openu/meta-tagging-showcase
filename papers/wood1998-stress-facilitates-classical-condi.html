mechanismStress impairs female conditioning by acting through circulating estrogen binding to available estrogen receptors, so removing either the hormone or the receptor blocks the impairment — “In combination with the ovariectomy results, these data suggest that the stress-induced impairment of classical conditioning in females is dependent on the presence of estrogen and the availability of estrogen receptors.”

limitationSex and stress each independently affect performance in learning tasks, which complicates attributing the observed differences to learning. — “Evaluating the contribution of sex and stress to learning is complicated by the fact that both variables alter performance in learning tasks.”
limitationThe ovariectomy result may reflect progesterone rather than estrogen, since removal of the hormone and blockade of its receptor gave different outcomes. — “Alternatively, the results may suggest a critical role for progesterone in the impaired performance associated with ovariectomy.”
limitationHormone removal and receptor blockade did not have equivalent consequences for learning, so the cellular route of the effect is unresolved. — “These results suggest that removal of estrogen hormone and blockade of its receptor do not necessarily have similar consequences on learning and may activate different cellular processes.”
limitationthe two hormones cannot be separated by ovariectomy, which removes both estrogen and progesterone — “Alternatively, the results may suggest a critical role for progesterone in the impaired performance associated with ovariectomy.”
limitationthe antagonist is not clean at the receptor-subtype level — “Estrogen has at least two types of nuclear receptors (α and β receptors) ( 40 ), and TAM has binding affinity for both types of receptors to varying degrees ( 45 ).”
limitationthe estrous stage was not controlled in the main experiments, so the effect may be carried by one phase of the cycle — “These results raise the possibility that the effects of stress on acquisition of the CR are mediated by the stage of estrous and levels of circulating estrogen.”
limitationovariectomy itself was not inert — it raised sensitized responding, and the paper has to argue that the increase is too small to matter — “Ovariectomy alone enhanced the number of sensitized responses from <1 of 10, to 2 of 10 responses to a white noise stimulus alone [ F (1, 34) = 5.96; P < 0.05]. This minor increase in sensitized responses cannot account for the finding that OVX prevented the stress-induced impairment of CRs.”
limitationsex and stress are entangled as explanatory variables because each alters performance on its own — “Evaluating the contribution of sex and stress to learning is complicated by the fact that both variables alter performance in learning tasks.”
limitationthe mechanism linking stress to the hormones is proposed, not shown — “The stress-induced impairment of learning in females may be mediated by a stress-induced disruption of ovarian hormones.”

findingThe same stressor had opposite effects by sex: it speeded acquisition of the conditioned eyeblink response in males and slowed it in females, each relative to its own unstressed controls. — “Post-hoc analysis using a Newman–Keuls comparison test indicated that exposure to the stressor facilitated acquisition of the CR in males ( P < 0.001), whereas exposure to the same stressor impaired acquisition in females ( P < 0.001), relative to their same-sex unstressed controls (Fig. 1 ).”
findingWithout stress, females acquired the conditioned response faster than males. — “Females outperformed males under unstressed conditions ( P < 0.01).”
findingRemoving the ovaries abolished the stress-induced impairment: stress impaired acquisition in females with intact ovaries but not in ovariectomized females. — “A post-hoc Newman–Keuls comparison test revealed that acquisition was impaired by stress in females with intact ovaries ( P < 0.01), but not in females without ovaries ( P = 0.44).”
findingBlocking estrogen receptors with tamoxifen also abolished the impairment: stress impaired acquisition in vehicle-injected females but not in tamoxifen-injected females. — “Post-hoc analysis using a Newman–Keuls comparison test revealed that exposure to the stressor impaired acquisition in the VEH ( P < 0.05), but did not alter acquisition in females injected with the estrogen antagonist TAM ( P = 0.44).”
findingThe opposite effects of stress on the two sexes were not caused by changes in spontaneous blinking, sensitization or pseudoconditioning. — “Therefore, the sexually opposed effects of stress on acquisition of the CR was not attributable to nonspecific effects of stressor exposure on spontaneous blinking, sensitization, or pseudoconditioning.”
findingLowering the CS intensity so that unstressed females started at a male-like acquisition rate did not remove the impairment, ruling out a ceiling effect as its cause. — “After stressor exposure, however, females were still significantly impaired relative to the unstressed females [ F (1, 16) = 10.33; P < 0.005] (Fig. 4 ).”
findingStressed males and stressed females did not differ in pain sensitivity as measured by tailflick latency, so the sex-opposite learning effects were not caused by differences in analgesia. — “stressed males and stressed females did not differ in their tailflick response to a painful photobeam of increasing intensity [ F (1, 40) = 0.35; P = 0.55] (Fig. 3 ).”
findingLate in training, stressed ovariectomized females actually responded more than unstressed ovariectomized females, that is, they showed the male-like facilitation once ovarian hormones were gone. — “In fact, during the last 100 trials, OVX females exposed to the stressor responded more than the unstressed OVX females [ F (2, 68) = 3.34; P = 0.04], suggesting that the performance of OVX females can be facilitated in response to the stressor”
findingOvariectomy on its own impaired learning in unstressed females during the later part of acquisition. — “The loss of ovarian hormones also resulted in a learning impairment among unstressed females during late acquisition (last 100 out of 300 trials).”
findingThe unstressed female advantage disappeared with continued training, whereas the stress effect persisted, showing the two differences have different durabilities. — “During the last 100 trials, unstressed males were performing at a similar rate as unstressed females ( P = 0.26).”
findingthe same stressor had opposite effects by sex — facilitating acquisition in males and impairing it in females — “Exposure to the stressor had diametrically opposed effects on acquisition of the CR in males vs. females [ F (1, 36) = 14.28; P < 0.001]”
findingunstressed females outperformed unstressed males — “Females outperformed males under unstressed conditions ( P < 0.01).”
findingthe baseline sex difference disappeared by the last 100 trials while the stress effect persisted across 600 trials — “During the last 100 trials, unstressed males were performing at a similar rate as unstressed females ( P = 0.26).”
findinglowering the CS to 80 dB brought unstressed females down to the male starting rate and the stress impairment remained, so it is not a ceiling effect — “After stressor exposure, however, females were still significantly impaired relative to the unstressed females [ F (1, 16) = 10.33; P < 0.005]”
findingovariectomy prevented the stress-induced impairment; sham surgery did not — “A post-hoc Newman–Keuls comparison test revealed that acquisition was impaired by stress in females with intact ovaries ( P < 0.01), but not in females without ovaries ( P = 0.44).”
findingovariectomised females went the other way late in training, responding more after stress — the male pattern — “In fact, during the last 100 trials, OVX females exposed to the stressor responded more than the unstressed OVX females [ F (2, 68) = 3.34; P = 0.04]”
findingblocking estrogen receptors with tamoxifen prevented the stress-induced impairment, while the injections alone had no effect on acquisition — “Post-hoc analysis using a Newman–Keuls comparison test revealed that exposure to the stressor impaired acquisition in the VEH ( P < 0.05), but did not alter acquisition in females injected with the estrogen antagonist TAM ( P = 0.44). findingno sex difference in spontaneous blink rate, in responses to the tone before training, or to explicitly unpaired stimuli — “There were no sex differences in spontaneous blink rate prior to stressor exposure [ F (1, 40) = 0.00; P = 1.0], eyeblink responses to the auditory stimulus prior to paired training [ F (1, 40) = 1.14; P = 0.29] or responses to explicitly unpaired stimulus presentations [ F (1, 12) = 0.22; P = 0.65]”
