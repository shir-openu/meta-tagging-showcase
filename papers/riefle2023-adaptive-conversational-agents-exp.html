phenomenonadaptive conversational agent design
claim-typeexistence
transferfrom communication science / social psychology (communication accommodation theory): the theory of how humans accommodate their speech to an interlocutor, re-applied to design a chatbot that accommodates its style to the user — “Our study will further contribute to research by adapting communication accommodation theory (Giles et al., 2010) to the context of human-AI interaction, thereby showing that adapting the CA’s communication style to users has the potential to positively influence their user experience.”
mechanismAccommodating one's communication style to the interlocutor signals an attitude toward them and thereby steers how the speaker and the interaction are evaluated — “The theory suggests that humans adapt their communication style to show their attitude toward the interlocutor and steer the evaluation of the speaker and the interaction (Giles et al., 2010).”
mechanismAn interaction partner that accommodates the user's needs signals attention and caring, which raises perceived social presence, which in turn produces enjoyment — “When a (human or technology) interaction partner considers the needs and preferences of the interlocutor during the interaction, this signals attention and caring (Bitner et al., 1990; Giles et al., 2010), thereby enhancing the perceived social presence (Hess et al., 2009; Van Pinxteren et al., 2020).”
mechanismMatching the chatbot's communication style to the user's cognitive abilities lowers the cognitive effort the interaction demands and raises its efficiency, which drives re-use intention — “For example, if the CA’s communication style matches the user's cognitive abilities, this will likely decrease the required cognitive effort and improve interaction efficiency (Giles et al., 2010; Goodhue and Thompson, 1995).”
mechanismThe unequal split between rational and intuitive participants left that group underpowered, producing the absence of significant differences — “A possible explanation could be the imbalance of the number of participants that were classified as rational or intuitive (10 compared to 4)—which will be addressed in the main study.”
relation — “Drawing on communication accommodation theory (Giles et al., 2010), we derive a research model, that is tested in an online experiment.”
relation — “However, the majority of prior studies on the individualization of CAs or AI-based systems has two crucial shortcomings: First, they do not explicitly adapt the system design to user characteristics (e.g., Altmeyer et al., 2020; Danckwerts et al., 2019; Gnewuch et al., 2020), yet only measure perceived individualization (i.e., instead of actively presenting different levels of individualization of the CA, they only ask users in how far they think the given system was adapted to them).”
replicationuntested
limitationThe pilot's sample was small and the authors treat the whole design as provisional pending a larger main study. — “Despite its limited sample size, our pilot study offers valuable insights into the effect of individualized CAs on the user experience:”
limitationThe within-subjects design may bias later user-experience responses; a between-subjects design is being considered instead. — “First, it is worth considering a between-subjects design instead of a within-subjects design, as this prevents participants from being biased in their responses to the user experience questionnaire by previous questions.”
limitationThe cognitive-style group was imbalanced (10 rational vs 4 intuitive), which may explain the absence of significant effects there. — “A possible explanation could be the imbalance of the number of participants that were classified as rational or intuitive (10 compared to 4)—which will be addressed in the main study.”
limitationOnly two user characteristics were studied, and separately; other characteristics and their combinations remain unexamined. — “Furthermore, we have considered two user characteristics (separately) so far, but there are more worth investigating (e.g., other cognitive styles).”
limitationThe summarised benefit does not carry the exceptions the results report: in one group the adapted agent was no better on satisfaction, and the UN-adapted agent was rated higher on enjoyment. — “The user experience with the individualized CA was rated better in both experimental groups, with two exceptions—in the rational/intuitive cognitive style group, service satisfaction did not differ for the individualized and the not adapted CA and enjoyment was rated better for the not adapted CA.”
limitationThe design used may bias the responses it collects, and the authors say a different design would prevent it. — “First, it is worth considering a between-subjects design instead of a within-subjects design, as this prevents participants from being biased in their responses to the user experience questionnaire by previous questions.”
limitationTwo user characteristics were examined, separately, and the authors state the interplay between them was not investigated. — “Furthermore, we have considered two user characteristics (separately) so far, but there are more worth investigating (e.g., other cognitive styles). Likewise, the interplay of combinations of different user characteristics as well as the influence of different CA design characteristics on the user experience might be interesting to explore.”
limitationThe constructs are measured entirely by item sets borrowed from other studies, so the measurement inherits whatever those instruments assume. — “To measure the variables, we adopted established measures from prior literature. Users’ characteristics ‘need for interaction’ and ‘rational/intuitive cognitive style’ are measured using four items based on Dabholkar (1996) and ten items based on Hamilton et al. (2016).”

findingIn the need-for-interaction group, both enjoyment and re-use intention were significantly higher for the chatbot adapted to the user than for the non-adapted chatbot. — “The analysis results show that in the experimental group “need for interaction” both enjoyment (V = 72.5, p < .05) and re-use intention (V = 74, p < .05) were significantly higher for the individualized CA.”
findingIn the rational/intuitive cognitive-style group, none of the user-experience differences between the adapted and non-adapted chatbot reached statistical significance. — “In addition, for the experimental group “rational/intuitive cognitive style”, no evidence for statistically significant differences in the user experience could be demonstrated.”
findingThe improvement in service satisfaction from individualization was not statistically significant. — “For service satisfaction (V = 57, p = .22) the observed improvement was not statistically significant.”
findingIndividualization improved ratings in both groups except that, for cognitive-style adaptation, service satisfaction was unchanged and enjoyment was actually rated higher for the non-adapted chatbot. — “The user experience with the individualized CA was rated better in both experimental groups, with two exceptions—in the rational/intuitive cognitive style group, service satisfaction did not differ for the individualized and the not adapted CA and enjoyment was rated better for the not adapted CA.”
findingEnjoyment of the chatbot adapted to the user's need for interaction averaged 5.70 against 4.90 for the one-size-fits-all chatbot. — “Whereas the enjoyment with the CA that was adapted to users’ need for interaction was rated substantially better (mean = 5.70 compared to mean = 4.90)”

definitionrational cognitive style — “By contrast, a rational cognitive style is characterized by a deliberate and analytical decision-making process (Epstein et al., 1996; Hamilton et al., 2016).”
definitionneed for interaction — “The user characteristic ‘need for interaction’ captures users’ fundamental desire to retain personal contact with others (Dabholkar, 1996).”

definitionenjoyment — “Enjoyment refers to “the extent to which the activity of interacting […] is perceived to be enjoyable in its own right” (Qiu and Benbasat, 2009, p. 154).”
