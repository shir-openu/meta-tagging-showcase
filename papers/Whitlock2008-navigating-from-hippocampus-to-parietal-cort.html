transferfrom primate electrophysiology of reaching and saccades: the multiplicative gain-field mechanism for coordinate transformation, imported as the account of how a rodent map becomes motor output — “Such a multiplicative mechanism is exactly what is needed to translate the allocentric entorhinal representation into goal-directed movements.”
transferfrom human neuropsychology: the dorsal and ventral stream distinction and the deficits of parietal patients, used to motivate PPC's spatial role — “The dorsal stream, which provides the PPC with its visual input, has been described as being critical for spatial perception, enabling the representation of the locations of objects in space”
mechanismOutput of the medial entorhinal grid-cell circuit is transformed into body-based coordinates in parietal cortex, and parietal projections to motor and premotor cortex then convert those coordinates into locomotor commands. — “We propose here that this translation involves a transformation of output from the MEC circuit into body-based coordinates in parietal cortex, and that this transformation, aided by strong connections from parietal cortex to motor and premotor cortex ( 33 , 34 ), is necessary for guiding locomotor output during goal-oriented navigation.”
mechanismWorld-referenced and body-referenced signals converge multiplicatively on single PPC neurons, and this conjunctive (gain-field) coding is what performs the allocentric-to-egocentric conversion. — “These studies indicate that world-referenced and body-referenced signals come together in PPC neurons that respond specifically to conjunctions of the two types of input. Visual representations of objects and scenes in PPC thus contain spatial information that can be used in accurately guiding specific actions, such as targeting and grasping. Such a multiplicative mechanism is exactly what is needed to translate the allocentric entorhinal representation into goal-directed movements.”

limitationWhether PPC deficits actually reflect a failure to transform self-location into locomotor action is not established by the existing evidence. — “Whether they do remains open; at present, the evidence is ambiguous.”
limitationThe direct medial entorhinal projection to PPC is weak and may not carry usable location information. — “This connection is relatively weak, and it is uncertain whether it is able to convey location information to possible action-preparing systems in PPC.”
limitationIt is unknown whether medial entorhinal output actually targets the PPC neurons involved in action preparation. — “In principle, position can be reconstructed from the spike patterns of a very low number of grid cells ( 15 , 17 ), but it has not been determined whether the medial entorhinal outputs target those PPC neurons that are involved in action preparation.”
limitationWhich of the candidate hippocampal-entorhinal to PPC routes actually carries the signal is unresolved. — “The relative contributions of the various pathways remain uncertain.”
limitationThe whole account is at an early stage. — “We are just beginning to scratch the surface of this multitude of navigational control pathways.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingThe paper's central claim is that the posterior parietal cortex converts the world-centred position signal carried by place cells and grid cells into the body-centred coordinates that locomotion requires. — “We hypothesize that the essential contribution of the PPC is the translation of the animal's position from a world-centered coordinate system generated by place cells and grid cells into the body-based coordinates of locomotor actions.”
findingRats with PPC lesions cannot use their outbound self-motion to return to a refuge on a curtained circular arena, so the PPC deficit is not confined to vision. — “For example, when such animals leave a refuge to search for a randomly located food reward on a circular arena surrounded by a curtain, they are not able to return directly to the refuge based on their outbound movements”
findingRat PPC cells fire for conjunctions of a particular movement with a particular place - for instance only during outward forward running, or only during left turns at the end of an arm. — “For example, a PPC cell would fire during forward motion, but only when the animal was running outward from the center of the maze, whereas another cell would fire exclusively during left turns executed at the end of a maze arm”
findingPPC firing fields rescale with the size of a maze segment even when hippocampal place fields do not, a dissociation implying PPC does not code perceived space. — “but the scaling occurred even under conditions where the size of hippocampal place fields remained constant. This dissociation suggests that PPC neurons do not express perceived space.”
findingAnatomically there is no direct hippocampal or subicular input to PPC; the only direct route is a weak projection from the dorsal lateral band of medial entorhinal cortex. — “There are no direct connections to PPC from the dentate gyrus, hippocampus, or subiculum, but the dorsal part of the lateral band of the MEC, adjacent to the postrhinal cortex, has a direct PPC projection”
findingRetrosplenial cortex damage, unlike postrhinal or prefrontal damage, produces lasting water-maze and path-integration deficits, making it a strong candidate relay from entorhinal cortex to PPC. — “damage to the retrosplenial cortex causes strong and lasting disruption of spatial navigation, both in the water maze ( 81 , 91 – 93 ) and in a path-integration task testing the ability to return directly to a home base in darkness”
findingthe entorhinal-hippocampal circuit alone cannot convert spatial information into goal-oriented motor output — “However, the entorhinal-hippocampal circuit alone cannot be sufficient for implementing the full array of computations necessary for spatial behavior. In addition to possessing a universal map of space, animals must also be able to convert the spatial information into goal-oriented motor output.”
findingPPC neurons combine world-referenced and body-referenced signals multiplicatively — “Such a multiplicative mechanism is exactly what is needed to translate the allocentric entorhinal representation into goal-directed movements.”
findingPPC delay activity follows the movement to be made, switching when the required effector changes — “These data, along with others, have led to the idea that cells in the PPC form a distributed representation of movement plans or intentions to make movements to specific goal locations”
finding77% of neurons in a medial PPC region responded to conjunctions of movement and location during virtual navigation — “In one such study, 77% of neurons recorded from a medial region of PPC responded to conjunctions of specific movements and specific locations during virtual navigation ( 52 ). In another study, approximately one quarter of the PPC cells were spatially tuned to path direction in a computerized maze task”
findinggrid fields keep their relative shift and rotation across environments, unlike place fields — “Unlike place cells in the hippocampus, grid cells are activated in a stereotypic manner across environments, irrespective of the particular landmarks of the environment. Two cells in different parts of MEC whose grid fields are shifted and rotated 30° relative to each other in one environment will show the same relative shift and rotation in a different environment.”
findingplace and grid firing persists without external cues but is anchored by them — “The continued firing of place cells and grid cells in the absence of environmental cues does not mean they are not normally anchored to extrinsic inputs. Spatial representations in both the hippocampus and MEC rotate in accordance with the displacement of distal visual cues ( 6 , 7 , 16 ), firing fields in both regions can be transformed by extending or contracting the geometric boundaries of the recording enclosure ( 23 , 24 ), and self-motion-driven firing patterns in the hippocampus can be overridden by input from external landmarks ( 22 , 25 ). These observations demonstrate that, although the discharge patterns of place cells and grid cells may be generated primarily by self-motion, the locations of firing must be influenced to a great extent by external sensory cues.”

The authors suggested that the hippocampal part of the circuit is not necessary for path integration.”
