phenomenonspatial navigation in rodents— “The navigational system of the mammalian cortex comprises a number of interacting brain regions.”
claim-typemechanism— “We hypothesize that the essential contribution of the PPC is the translation of the animal's position from a world-centered coordinate system generated by place cells and grid cells into the body-based coordinates of locomotor actions.”
method — evidence— “A large body of work, involving rats, monkeys, and humans, has consistently supported the conclusion that PPC is essential for key aspects of spatial representation ( 35 ).”
object of study — evidence— “To traverse the environment, animals must be able to translate coordinate information from spatial maps in the entorhinal cortex and hippocampus into body-centered representations that can be used to direct locomotion. How this is done remains an enigma.”
research question — evidence— “However, the entorhinal-hippocampal circuit alone cannot be sufficient for implementing the full array of computations necessary for spatial behavior. In addition to possessing a universal map of space, animals must also be able to convert the spatial information into goal-oriented motor output.”
contribution — evidence— “We propose here that this translation involves a transformation of output from the MEC circuit into body-based coordinates in parietal cortex, and that this transformation, aided by strong connections from parietal cortex to motor and premotor cortex ( 33 , 34 ), is necessary for guiding locomotor output during goal-oriented navigation.”
transferfrom primate electrophysiology of reaching and saccades: the multiplicative gain-field mechanism for coordinate transformation, imported as the account of how a rodent map becomes motor output— “Such a multiplicative mechanism is exactly what is needed to translate the allocentric entorhinal representation into goal-directed movements.”
transferfrom human neuropsychology: the dorsal and ventral stream distinction and the deficits of parietal patients, used to motivate PPC's spatial role— “The dorsal stream, which provides the PPC with its visual input, has been described as being critical for spatial perception, enabling the representation of the locations of objects in space”
mechanismOutput of the medial entorhinal grid-cell circuit is transformed into body-based coordinates in parietal cortex, and parietal projections to motor and premotor cortex then convert those coordinates into locomotor commands.— “We propose here that this translation involves a transformation of output from the MEC circuit into body-based coordinates in parietal cortex, and that this transformation, aided by strong connections from parietal cortex to motor and premotor cortex ( 33 , 34 ), is necessary for guiding locomotor output during goal-oriented navigation.”
mechanismWorld-referenced and body-referenced signals converge multiplicatively on single PPC neurons, and this conjunctive (gain-field) coding is what performs the allocentric-to-egocentric conversion.— “These studies indicate that world-referenced and body-referenced signals come together in PPC neurons that respond specifically to conjunctions of the two types of input. Visual representations of objects and scenes in PPC thus contain spatial information that can be used in accurately guiding specific actions, such as targeting and grasping.…
mechanismPPC neurons multiply world-referenced by body-referenced signals, which is what a coordinate transformation requires— “Such a multiplicative mechanism is exactly what is needed to translate the allocentric entorhinal representation into goal-directed movements.”
mechanismgrid cells are argued to rest on path integration because a periodic firing pattern persists through constantly changing speed and direction— “The expression of a strongly periodic spatial firing pattern in the presence of constantly changing running speed and running direction suggests that the grid must rely on path integration computations ( 16 , 19 , 20 ), where changes in velocity and direction are integrated over time to allow a constant representation of space”
mechanismgrids keep their relative geometry across environments, unlike place fields which remap— “Unlike place cells in the hippocampus, grid cells are activated in a stereotypic manner across environments, irrespective of the particular landmarks of the environment. Two cells in different parts of MEC whose grid fields are shifted and rotated 30° relative to each other in one environment will show the same relative shift and rotation in…
