mechanismdefinitional dependence of a ratio the B/H ratio is a quotient whose numerator is fixed but whose denominator depends on whether enucleated cells are counted; because red blood cells dominate the human count, that single definitional choice moves the published value by a factor of ten— “This is because the dominant population of non-nucleated red blood cells is not included in the calculation.”
mechanismrepetition without warrant a value stated without a primary reference is re-cited by papers that cite the re-citation, so the chain grows while the evidence at its root stays a single order-of-magnitude calculation, and the value acquires standing from the citation count rather than from measurement— “Reported values in the literature on the number of cells in the body differ by orders of magnitude and are very seldom supported by any measurements or calculations.”
mechanismconcentration times volume dominance multiplying a measured bacterial concentration by organ volume makes the colon exceed every other site by orders of magnitude, which collapses a body-wide estimation problem into a single-organ one— “Table 1 reveals that the bacterial content of the colon exceeds all other organs by at least two orders of magnitude. Importantly, within the alimentary tract, the colon is the only substantial contributor to the total bacterial population, while the stomach and small intestine make negligible contributions.”
contradicts the widely-repeated 10:1 ratio of bacterial to human cells in the body— “This B/H value of about 1:1 (with the associated uncertainty range) should replace the 10:1 or 100:1 values that are stated in the literature until more accurate measurements become available.”
