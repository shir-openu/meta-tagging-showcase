method — evidence— “In short, structural priming is an informative and heavily investigated phenomenon and therefore warrants an extensive and directed review.”
transferfrom psycholinguistics of comprehension (into production): priming between production and comprehension, and between writing and speaking, is read as evidence that the two processes draw on the SAME representations - a claim that carries structure across the production/comprehension divide— “Indeed, priming between production and comprehension, just like priming between writing and speaking, supports the claim that both processes draw on common representations of linguistic information, particularly ones associated with syntax.”
mechanismResidual activation (the one-locus account): using a construction leaves its combinatorial node and the lemma linked to it partly activated, so the same structure is cheaper to produce for a while. The lexical boost falls out of the same machinery - a repeated verb re-activates both the lemma AND the strengthened link to the node - which is what makes it ONE locus.— “On this account, priming is due to residual activation of combinatorial and lemma nodes, as well as the links between the two (as illustrated in Panel C of Figure 2 ). More specifically, lexically independent priming occurs as a result of the residual activation”
mechanismImplicit learning: producing or understanding a structure requires having learned how meaning relationships map onto functional elements and onto constituent order, and every sentence experienced re-weights those mappings. On this account priming is not a trace that decays but the visible edge of the same learning that acquired the grammar.— “The core idea is that for people to produce and comprehend language, they must learn how their different linguistic and language-related representations relate or map onto one other. To produce or understand the word cat , a language user must learn that the meaning of domestic feline maps onto the word cat (which is a noun), which in turn…
contradicts functionalist approaches to syntactic structure (Bates & MacWhinney, 1982) and connectionist views that eschew traditional syntactic representations (McClelland, St. John, & Taraban, 1989)— “This runs against more functionalist approaches to syntactic structure (e.g., Bates & MacWhinney, 1982 ), as well as other views that eschew traditional representations of syntactic knowledge (e.g., McClelland, St. John, & Taraban, 1989 ).”

sensestructural priming - THE LINGUISTICS SENSE, and it shares almost nothing with the social-psychology sense this corpus is otherwise full of except the word. The prime is a SENTENCE, the primed thing is its STRUCTURE, the response is another sentence, and the effect is defined disjunctively: to repeat OR BETTER PROCESS a current sentence because of its structural similarity to a previously experienced one. No concept is activated, no behaviour is influenced, no attitude is reached— “In the language sciences, a ubiquitous form of such repetition is structural priming , a tendency to repeat or better process a current sentence because of its structural similarity to a previously experienced (“prime”) sentence ( Bock, 1986 ). sensestructural priming versus syntactic priming - A NAME CHOSEN AGAINST A THEORY, and the clearest instance of it in this corpus. The review drops `syntactic priming` (Pickering's own term from 1999) for `structural priming` on the explicit ground that the old name PRESUPPOSES THE EXISTENCE OF SPECIFICALLY SYNTACTIC REPRESENTATIONS - the very thing the method is used to test. A term retired because it smuggles in the conclusion, with the reason stated in the sentence that retires it— “Some researchers use the term syntactic priming (e.g., Pickering & Branigan, 1999 ), but we shall use structural priming because it encompasses abstract linguistic priming that need not be syntactic (e.g., Bock, Loebell, & Morey, 1992 ), and indeed does not presuppose the existence of specifically syntactic representations”
sensepriming as a DIAGNOSTIC INSTRUMENT - the reason the field runs these experiments at all, and the review states the logic in general form before applying it: because being affected by repetition depends on how knowledge is represented, the pattern of what does and does not prime is used to READ OFF the representations. The effect is evidence about something else— “the tendency to be affected by the repetition of aspects of knowledge can be used to diagnose the nature of that knowledge. So in this case, the tendency to repeat aspects of sentence structure helps researchers identify some of the representations that people construct”

limitationObserving structural priming does not license the inference that abstract syntax caused it.— “However, this does not mean that whenever structural priming is observed, it is always caused by abstract syntactic knowledge.”
limitationThe evidence on whether thematic roles affect priming is contradictory and unresolved by this review.— “In sum, the effects of thematic roles on priming are mixed, and more work is needed to determine how they fit into the mapping from message to syntax.”

conceptrepetition — the superordinate this field places priming under - and the review's opening move is that repetition is interesting precisely because it is NOT diagnostic on its own: learning, imitation and failure of inhibition all produce it— “Repetition is central because it can reflect the operation of many different underlying psychological mechanisms.
