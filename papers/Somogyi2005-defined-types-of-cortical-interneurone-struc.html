transferfrom nineteenth-century histology: Ramon y Cajal's and Lorente de No's cell descriptions, most of which were then ignored or collapsed into one name — “Although the identity of the basket cell has not been questioned, the many other cell types described by Ramon y Cajal (1893) and Lorente de No (1934) have either been ignored and all non-pyramidal cells called basket cells, or non-pyramidal cells were named by the laminar location of their soma, or some other simple feature, as if that would define a cell type as outlined above.”
transferfrom immunocytochemistry: molecular markers as class criteria - adopted, and then shown to be insufficient on their own — “With the cited evidence we hope to convince our readers that some frequently used criteria about the identity of interneurones are unsustainable. (i) Somatic location in a layer does not identify an interneurone class, as the same class may have somata in several layers and all layers contain several distinct classes. (ii) Dendritic shape does not necessarily identify an interneurone class, as indistinguishable axonal output can be made by cells having diverse dendritic fields. (iii) A single molecular cell marker, such as a calcium binding protein or a neuropeptide, does not identify an interneurone class on its own, as most molecules have been shown to be expressed in several different cell types with distinct inputs and spatio-temporal outputs. For example, parvalbumin is expressed by at least four distinct classes of interneurone (see below).”
transferfrom isocortical anatomy: the homologous cell classes, and the axo-axonic cell which was discovered there first — “Most of the GABAergic cell types defined in the CA1 area have homologues in the isocortex, which is particularly apparent in the supragranular layers. For example, the somata of supragranular pyramidal cells are innervated by basket cells immunopositive for PV, or CCK, or CCK and VGLUT3 ( Kawaguchi & Kubota, 1997 ; Somogyi et al. 2004 ) as in the hippocampus, and the axo-axonic cell was discovered in the rat visual cortex.”
transferfrom in vivo electrophysiology: oscillation-phase firing, imported as the fourth axis of the definition rather than as a result — “Probably all neurones of the cortex can be defined, recognized and compared on the comprehensive application of the following measures: (i) brain area- and cell domain-specific distribution of input and output synapses, (ii) expression of proteins involved in cell signalling, (iii) intrinsic membrane properties reflecting the expression of ion channel proteins, and (iv) temporal distribution of firing in vivo . Based on the above range of parameters, each cell will fall into a tight cluster, the cell type , in this multidimensional space.”
mechanismBecause each interneurone class terminates on a different pyramidal-cell compartment and each class fires at a different phase, the compartments of one pyramidal cell receive GABAergic input separated in time, which structures where and when that cell can integrate its inputs. — “Because the different interneurones innervate distinct domains of the pyramidal cells, the different compartments will receive differential GABAergic input in time.”
mechanismDuring ripples the interneurones act not by shunting spikes but by delivering hyperpolarization timed to de-inactivate voltage-dependent ion channels, which then promotes the pyramidal cells' next discharge. — “This specific spike timing indicates that the role for the interneurones during ripple episodes lies not in the shunting of action potentials, but, more likely, in providing hyperpolarization well-timed to de-inactivate voltage-dependent ion channels and therefore promote subsequent firing of pyramidal cells.”
mechanismO-LM cells go silent during ripples partly because their pyramidal input has a very low resting release probability that only repetitive presynaptic firing raises, and pyramidal cells emit only one or two spikes per ripple. — “The pyramidal input to O-LM cells has a very low resting release probability, which is increased only by repetitive firing ( Ali et al. 1999 ).”
mechanismBistratified cells firing at ripple frequency before the pyramidal cells could structure dendritic oscillations so that accumulating excitation triggers fast dendritic spikes that propagate to the soma. — “In particular, the early ripple frequency firing of bistratified cells, before the pyramidal cells, could structure dendritic oscillations in the pyramidal cell dendrites and with the build-up of excitation lead to fast dendritic spikes actively propagating to the soma”
mechanism — “And finally, interneurones belonging to different connectivity classes may fire preferentially at distinct time points during a given oscillation. Because the different interneurones innervate distinct domains of the pyramidal cells, the different compartments will receive differential GABAergic input in time. Such a dynamic spatio-temporal GABAergic control, which evolves distinct patterns during different brain states, is ideally suited to regulating the input integration of individual pyramidal cells and contribute to the formation of cell assemblies and representations in the hippocampus.”
mechanism — “If a single basket cell innervating up to 2000 pyramidal cells ( Sik et al. 1995 ; Halasy et al. 1996 ) can reset the rhythmic firing of the cells and synchronize them in interaction with intrinsic membrane oscillations, how would the approximately 25 basket cells converging on a single pyramidal cell ( Buhl et al. 1994 a ) affect firing in different brain states?”
mechanism — “These results indicate that interneurones within a connectivity class (nos (1), (2), (5) and (6) reported so far) exhibit similar firing patterns during a given network oscillation. In addition, during different network oscillations representing two distinct brain states, interneurones of the same connectivity class show different firing activities and therefore modulate their specific postsynaptic target-domain in a brain-state-dependent manner.”
mechanism — “The silence of O-LM cells during ripple episodes is surprising because they receive their major glutamatergic input from local CA1 pyramidal cells, which are most active as a population during ripples. The pyramidal input to O-LM cells has a very low resting release probability, which is increased only by repetitive firing ( Ali et al. 1999 ). Because pyramidal cells rarely fire more than one or two action potentials, this network state is not optimal for O-LM cell activation. However, the specific depression of firing of O-LM cells during ripple episodes cannot be explained by weak excitation alone. Additional mechanisms for silencing must be present in the network.”

findingThe review counts 12 interneurone types that innervate specific domains of CA1 pyramidal cells plus four types that innervate mainly other interneurones, i.e. at least 16 distinct GABAergic cell types in CA1. — “Currently, we recognize 12 types of interneurone innervating mainly specific domains of pyramidal cells and four types of interneurone innervating mostly other interneurones ( Fig. 1 ).”
findingInterneurones belonging to one connectivity class fire alike during a given oscillation, but the same class fires differently during theta than during ripples, so its target pyramidal-cell domain is modulated in a brain-state-dependent way. — “During different network oscillations representing two distinct brain states, interneurones of the same class show different firing patterns modulating their postsynaptic target-domain in a brain-state-dependent manner.”
findingDuring theta oscillations the four identified cell types fire at different phases: PV basket cells on the descending phase, axo-axonic cells around the peak, and bistratified and O-LM cells at the trough. — “During theta oscillations the PV basket cell fired at the descending phase, the axo-axonic cell around the peak and the bistratified and the O-LM cell discharged mainly at the trough of theta wave.”
findingDuring sharp-wave ripples PV basket cells fire at the ripple's highest amplitude, axo-axonic cells fire before the ripple then are depressed, bistratified cells fire throughout, and O-LM cells fall silent. — “During ripple episodes the PV basket cell fired preferentially at the highest amplitude of the ripple, the axo-axomic cell sometimes fired before the ripple but was depressed afterwards, the bistratified cell fired throughout, before and after the ripple episode and the O-LM cell was silent.”
findingThe layer in which an interneurone's soma sits does not identify its class, because one class can have somata in several layers and every layer holds several classes. — “(i) Somatic location in a layer does not identify an interneurone class, as the same class may have somata in several layers and all layers contain several distinct classes.”
findingA single molecular marker such as a calcium-binding protein or neuropeptide cannot identify an interneurone class, since most such molecules are expressed by several cell types with different inputs and outputs (parvalbumin alone marks at least four classes). — “(iii) A single molecular cell marker, such as a calcium binding protein or a neuropeptide, does not identify an interneurone class on its own, as most molecules have been shown to be expressed in several different cell types with distinct inputs and spatio-temporal outputs.”
findingBistratified cells and parvalbumin-positive basket cells both discharge on the ascending phase of each ripple cycle, roughly 1-2 ms after the pyramidal cells rather than before them. — “The analysis of the exact spike timing during single ripple cycles indicated that both bistratified and also parvalbumin expressing basket cells fire at the ascending phase of the ripple cycle ( Csicsvari et al. 1999 ; Klausberger et al. 2004 ), on average 1–2 ms after the firing of pyramidal cells.”
findingIn vitro, a single basket or axo-axonic cell can entrain and synchronize tonically firing pyramidal cells at theta frequency by resetting the phase of their intrinsic membrane potential oscillations. — “Tests conducted in vitro ( Cobb et al. 1995 ) demonstrate that single basket and axo-axonic cells are able to entrain and synchronize tonically discharging hippocampal pyramidal cells by resetting the phase of intrinsic membrane potential oscillations at the physiologically relevant theta (4–7 Hz) frequency ( Fig. 2 ).”
findingfour measures together define a neurone - synaptic input and output distribution, signalling molecules, membrane properties, and in vivo firing — “Neurones of the cortex can be defined, recognized and compared on the comprehensive application of the following measures: (i) brain area- and cell domain-specific distribution of input and output synapses, (ii) expression of molecules involved in cell signalling, (iii) membrane and synaptic properties reflecting the expression of membrane proteins, (iv) temporal structure of firing in vivo , resulting from (i)–(iii).”
findingSTRUCTURE AND FUNCTION ARE DECLARED INSEPARABLE: an indivisible unity of design, and neurones do not have separate structure or function — “Spatial and temporal measures of neurones in the network reflect an indivisible unity of evolutionary design, i.e. neurones do not have separate structure or function.”
findingat least 16 distinct GABAergic types support one relatively uniform population of pyramidal cells — “Reviewing the cell types and their spatio-temporal interactions, we suggest that CA1 pyramidal cells are supported by at least 16 distinct types of GABAergic neurone. During a given behaviour-contingent network oscillation, interneurones of a given type exhibit similar firing patterns.”
findingA PROPERTY OF THE TYPE IS STATE-DEPENDENT: cells of the same class show different firing patterns in two distinct brain states — “During different network oscillations representing two distinct brain states, interneurones of the same class show different firing patterns modulating their postsynaptic target-domain in a brain-state-dependent manner. These results suggest roles for specific interneurone types in structuring the activity of pyramidal cells via their respective target domains, and accurately timing and synchronizing pyramidal cell discharge, rather than providing generalized inhibition.”
findingTHERE IS NO AGREEMENT ON THE CRITERIA, so different authors use partial criteria or non-overlapping data for the same or a mixture of populations — “There is no agreement on the number and identity of neuronal species in the cerebral cortex. This is partly due to the lack of agreement on criteria of what is necessary to define a cell type, and consequently different authors use partial criteria or sets of non-overlapping data for studying the same or a mixture of several neuronal populations.”
findingand the reason is that little effort has been devoted to defining them rigorously, not that the methods are missing — “The lack of adequate definition of cell types mainly results from the small number of cells and cortical areas studied in a comprehensive manner, despite the availability of long established methods of proven value. In other words, relative to the challenge of defining the likely number of cell types that occupy a distinct position in the spatio-temporal structure of the cortex, little effort has been devoted to defining them in a rigorous manner.”

For example, as the axon initial segment is thought to generate the action potential, its GABAergic regulation by the axo-axonic cell must be independent of the distal dendrite innervated by the O-LM cells, due to the different times that GABA is needed there. But even the same domain may receive input from several distinct cell types with similar specialization, as the bistratified and Schaffer collateral-associated cells converging on pyramidal dendrites in stratum radiatum and oriens, or CCK or parvalbumin expressing basket cells converging on pyramidal somata, the domain of integration. Why these inputs to the same postsynaptic domain need to be independent is not understood, but a temporal division of labour is a possibility.”
findingone basket cell innervates up to 2000 pyramidal cells and about 25 converge on each — “If a single basket cell innervating up to 2000 pyramidal cells ( Sik et al. 1995 ; Halasy et al. 1996 ) can reset the rhythmic firing of the cells and synchronize them in interaction with intrinsic membrane oscillations, how would the approximately 25 basket cells converging on a single pyramidal cell ( Buhl et al. 1994 a ) affect firing in different brain states?”
findingEVEN THE POPULATION TAKEN AS UNIFORM IS NOT: the pyramidal cells may be three groups that do not share inputs or response properties — “The pyramidal cells are generally considered to form a single population, but there may be at least three distinct groups, which do not necessarily share the same inputs and response properties.”
findingone interneurone draws at least 70% of its excitation from a source that supplies far fewer of the synapses around it — “The so called O-LM GABAergic interneurone with dendrites restricted to stratum oriens ( Fig. 1 ) receives at least 70% of its glutamatergic input from CA1 pyramidal cells ( Blasco-Ibanez & Freund, 1995 ), although CA3 pyramidal boutons probably form a much larger fraction of synapses in this layer.”

findingand in the other state the same four reorder completely, with one specifically suppressed — “During sharp wave-associated ripple episodes ( Fig. 5 ) parvalbumin-expressing basket cells and bistratified cells increase their discharge frequency, but there are subtle yet significant differences between them ( Klausberger et al. 2003 , 2004 ). On average, basket cells fired preferentially at the highest amplitude of the ripple and bistratified cells fired with high frequency throughout the whole ripple episodes. Furthermore, unlike basket cells, bistratified cells increased their firing rate even before ripples can be detected in the extracellular field potential. Axo-axonic cells slightly increased their firing activity at the beginning of the ripples, but became silent after the highest amplitude of the ripple episode, and appeared suppressed even after the ripple episodes. In contrast, O-LM cell firing was specifically suppressed for the duration of ripple episodes.”
findingTHE CELL FALLS SILENT EXACTLY WHEN ITS MAIN EXCITATORY INPUT IS MOST ACTIVE, which weak excitation alone cannot explain — “The silence of O-LM cells during ripple episodes is surprising because they receive their major glutamatergic input from local CA1 pyramidal cells, which are most active as a population during ripples. The pyramidal input to O-LM cells has a very low resting release probability, which is increased only by repetitive firing ( Ali et al. 1999 ). Because pyramidal cells rarely fire more than one or two action potentials, this network state is not optimal for O-LM cell activation. However, the specific depression of firing of O-LM cells during ripple episodes cannot be explained by weak excitation alone. Additional mechanisms for silencing must be present in the network.”
findingand a falsifiable prediction is made from connectivity alone about a class that had not been recorded — “From the major, and apparently independent, GABAergic input of O-LM cells from IS-III cells (see no. (16)), we predict that IS-III cells fire strongly during ripples. The consequences of the depressed firing of O-LM cells during ripple episodes remain unknown.”
findingcells within a connectivity class fire alike in a given oscillation, and differently between states — “These results indicate that interneurones within a connectivity class (nos (1), (2), (5) and (6) reported so far) exhibit similar firing patterns during a given network oscillation. In addition, during different network oscillations representing two distinct brain states, interneurones of the same connectivity class show different firing activities and therefore modulate their specific postsynaptic target-domain in a brain-state-dependent manner.”
findingso the compartments of the pyramidal cell receive GABAergic input differentiated in time rather than generalized inhibition — “And finally, interneurones belonging to different connectivity classes may fire preferentially at distinct time points during a given oscillation. Because the different interneurones innervate distinct domains of the pyramidal cells, the different compartments will receive differential GABAergic input in time. Such a dynamic spatio-temporal GABAergic control, which evolves distinct patterns during different brain states, is ideally suited to regulating the input integration of individual pyramidal cells and contribute to the formation of cell assemblies and representations in the hippocampus.”
findingTHE MEASURABLE LOCAL PROPERTY DOES NOT PREDICT THE BEHAVIOUR: firing is not simply determined by the main excitatory drive as predicted from where the dendrites sit — “Another fascinating question is how the different firing patterns of distinct interneurones are generated. The results so far indicate that the in vivo firing of a cell is not simply determined by its main excitatory drive as predicted from the laminar location of the dendrites, but is governed by complex network phenomena”
findingand the next test is named - record classes with the same target preference and see whether the firing follows the target or the class — “Does this mean that the postsynaptic target of an interneurone (e.g. the specific subcellular domain of a pyramidal cell) determines the firing patterns of this interneurone? This can be tested by recording additional interneurone classes with target preference similar to those already presented, such as basket cells expressing CCK and innervating the soma similar to PV expressing basket cells, or Schaffer collateral-associated cells innervating the same dendritic domain as the reported bistratified cells.”
findinga better spatio-temporal DEFINITION of the network is what is needed, the same word used for the cell types — “Therefore, we need a much better knowledge of which types of cells are connected with GABAergic synapses and gap junctions, what the different sources of excitation and modulation are and how accurately timed input from different sources is integrated into a specific firing output. In other words, a much better spatio-temporal definition of the network is needed to understand the processing of information in the cerebral cortex.”
findingmost of these types have homologues in the isocortex, where the axo-axonic cell was discovered first — “Most of the GABAergic cell types defined in the CA1 area have homologues in the isocortex, which is particularly apparent in the supragranular layers. For example, the somata of supragranular pyramidal cells are innervated by basket cells immunopositive for PV, or CCK, or CCK and VGLUT3 ( Kawaguchi & Kubota, 1997 ; Somogyi et al. 2004 ) as in the hippocampus, and the axo-axonic cell was discovered in the rat visual cortex.”
findingand the isocortical picture emerges slowly because of partial data sets, untested connections, INCONSISTENT DELINEATION OF CELL IDENTITY, and missing firing patterns — “A great deal of information is available on interneurones of the isocortex, but a comprehensive picture is only slowly emerging ( Kawaguchi & Kubota, 1997 ; Somogyi et al. 1998 ; Thomson et al. 2002 ; Markram et al. 2004 )) due to partial data sets, the lack of directly tested synaptic connections, the inconsistent delineation of cell identity and the limited information on in vivo firing patterns of defined cell types.”
findingthe area was chosen deliberately as the least heterogeneous population with the fewest extrinsic inputs — “The cortical area with the least heterogeneous neuronal population and the smallest number of extrinsic inputs is probably the CA1 area, one reason for its popularity for studying the cortical network. The alignment of the somata and dendrites of pyramidal cells into defined layers and the laminar segregation of much of the extrinsic and intrinsic inputs provide the best chance for defining the synaptic relationships of distinct cell types and the basic cortical circuit.”
findingthe shape hypothesis is the oldest criterion, supported first by neurotransmitter identification and then by the selective location of output synapses — “The hypothesis that neurones with different shapes have distinct roles in the cortex was implicit in the earliest studies of Ramon y Cajal and was later elaborated by many, most elegantly by Janos Szentagothai (1975) . Key evidence for neuronal shape being indicative of role came from neurotransmitter identification, e.g. GABA via the immunocytochemical demonstration of glutamate decarboxylase (GAD) in smooth dendritic neurones and its absence from pyramidal cells ( Ribak, 1978 ). A further useful predictor for distinct roles was found in the differential and highly selective location of output synapses on target neurones ( Ramon y Cajal, 1893 ; Szentagothai, 1975 ; Somogyi et al. 1998 ). The use of biophysical measures, expression patterns of proteins involved in cell signalling, and, more recently, in vivo firing patterns have all proved useful in defining cell types”
