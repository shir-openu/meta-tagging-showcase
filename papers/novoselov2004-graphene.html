mechanismatomically-thin (few-layer) graphene is stable and metallic under ambient conditions, and its carrier density is tunable by an electric field (ambipolar field effect), enabling ballistic transport — “monocrystalline graphitic films, which are just a few atoms thick but nonetheless stable under ambient conditions, metallic and of remarkably high quality”
relation — “Contrary to the common belief, we have been able to prepare graphitic sheets of thicknesses down to a few atomic layers, including single layer graphene, and succeeded in making devices from them and studying their electronic properties.”

limitationan observed shift could be due to unintentional doping by absorbed gas molecules — “this shift could be due to an unintentional doping of the films by absorbed gas molecules”
limitationThe zero-gate-voltage hole doping may be an artefact of adsorbed gas molecules rather than intrinsic. — “However, this shift could be due to an unintentional doping of the films by absorbed gas molecules [16,17].”
limitationThe authors cannot explain why the measured behaviour fits a simple free-electron model when graphene is expected to have linear dispersion and massless carriers. — “Note that graphene is expected to have the linear energy 2 dispersion and carriers with zero mass, and the reason why the observed behavior is so well described by the simplest free-electron model remains to be understood.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
limitationThe transistor's on-off resistance ratio is modest and this is a fundamental limit for a gapless material. — “Graphene transistors show a rather modest on-off resistance ratio (less than ≈30 at 300K; limited because of thermally excited carriers) but this is a fundamental limitation for any material without a band gap exceeding kBT.”
limitationThe devices differ in the one variable that defines the material - the number of layers - and that number is inferred from the measurement rather than known. — “which varied from 4 to 20meV for different samples, presumably indicating a different number of graphene layers involved”
limitationThe headline mobility is the top of a sample-to-sample range, and mobility is stated to be limited by defect scattering rather than intrinsic. — “In both cases, we obtained the same values of µ, which varied from sample to sample between 3,000 and 10,000 cm2/V⋅s. The mobilities were practically independent of temperature T, indicating that even being so high they were still limited by scattering on defects”
limitationWhat is measured is few-layer graphene of one, two or three atomic layers reported together, not single-layer graphene. — “electronic properties of our thinnest (FLG) devices, which contained just 1, 2 or 3 atomic layers [15]. All FLG devices exhibited essentially identical electronic properties”

findingAtomically thin graphitic carbon films — graphene — are stable at ambient conditions, metallic, and behave as a 2D semimetal with a strong ambipolar field effect, switchable between electron and hole gases by gate voltage. — “The films are found to be a twodimensional semimetal with a tiny overlap between valence and conductance bands and to exhibit a strong ambipolar electric-field effect” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
findingGraphene forms an all-metallic field-effect transistor exhibiting ballistic transport at submicron distances even at room temperature. — “demonstrate all-metallic field-effect transistor, which uniquely exhibits ballistic transport at submicron distances even at room temperature”
