phenomenonvisuospatial attention and primary visual consciousness
claim-typeexistence
mechanismrTMS spreads transsynaptically into two networks whose interaction is nonlinear, so perturbing ventral PPC dissociates attention from consciousness instead of moving them together — “Interaction of these networks might be nonlinear and too complex to cause simple relationships between visuospatial attention and primary consciousness, at least due to the focal and week TMS-induced perturbation of ventral PPC.”
mechanisma focal coil at near-threshold intensity limits the induced neural interference within a widely distributed network, which is why the behavioural effect is only about 10% — “The small but significant magnitude of rTMS effects on ventral PPC (about 10%) is likely due to the use of focal coil and near-threshold intensity of rTMS.”
mechanismthe stimulation disrupts the spatial disengagement operation of Posner's attention system, which then lengthens reaction time — “It is likely that the present rTMS of ventral PPC mainly effected spatial disengagement, in agreement with previous clinical studies showing that ventral PPC lesions are related to prominent spatial disengagement deficits”
relation — “The length of the rTMS train corresponded to the activation timing of PPC previously found in an electroencephalography (EEG) study carried out with the same paradigm (Babiloni and others 2006).”
relation — “These results corroborate the well-known notion that ventral PPC is a putative substrate of neglect and other visuospatial deficits (e.g., Vallar 2001; Corbetta and Shulman 2002; Karnath and others 2003; Corbetta and others 2005).”
relation — “In this sense, the present findings challenge the idea that visuospatial attention and primary consciousness are subserved by strictly interdependent networks.”
replicationuntested
limitationThe behavioural effect of the stimulation was small, attributed to the focal coil and near-threshold intensity used. — “The small but significant magnitude of rTMS effects on ventral PPC (about 10%) is likely due to the use of focal coil and near-threshold intensity of rTMS.”
limitationrTMS effects are not confined to the targeted region, so the locus of the effect is not purely ventral PPC. — “As a premise, it should be remarked that the effect of rTMS does not remain limited to the targeted brain region but spread transynaptically.”
limitationA motor-output contribution to the reaction-time slowing cannot be excluded. — “It is reasonable that the slowing of the reaction time is mainly due to visuospatial sensory processing, although a slight influence on motor output cannot be excluded.”
limitationThe paper's consciousness measure is the percentage of trials a subject reported seeing. — “Trials associated with the conscious perception of cue stimuli were defined as ‘‘seen trials”
limitationThe stimulus duration was calibrated per subject to about 50% detection, so the baseline rate is a design target. — “The threshold time was defined as the duration of the cue stimulus determining about 50% of correct stimulus detections within a series of 10 trials”
Content tags
content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingRepetitive TMS over either left or right ventral posterior parietal cortex significantly reduced the number of cue stimuli that subjects consciously reported seeing, compared with sham stimulation. — “Compared with the sham condition, the number of ‘‘seen’’ trials was significantly reduced after rTMS at both left (P < 0.02) and right PPC (P < 0.04), reflecting the reduction of primary visual consciousness.”
findingThe same parietal stimulation slowed reaction times to go stimuli relative to sham, indicating an impairment of covert visuospatial attention. — “With respect to the sham, the real rTMS of right or left ventral PPC slowed reaction time to go stimuli.”
findingThe size of the consciousness impairment and the size of the attention impairment were not correlated across individuals. — “First row of Figure 4 shows no statistically significant correlation (rTMS at left PPC: r = –0.36, P = 0.13; rTMS at right PPC: r = –0.18, P = 0.46).”
findingDelivering any rTMS, real or sham, speeded reaction times relative to the no-stimulation baseline, so the attentional cost had to be measured against sham rather than baseline. — “The presence of rTMS, either real or sham, generally shortened reaction times: 418 ms (±10 SE) in the sham condition (pseudo rTMS), 446 ms (±12 SE) after rTMS at left PPC, and 438 ms (±11 SE) after rTMS at right PPC.”
findingA control analysis validated the subjective reports: reaction times were faster on trials the subject called 'seen' than on trials called 'not seen'. — “According to the control hypothesis, mean reaction time was shorter in the ‘‘seen’’ trials than in the ‘‘not seen’’ ones regardless the spatial congruency between cue and go stimuli, thus demonstrating the global positive effect of conscious visual perception.”
quantity18 — number of healthy adult volunteers stimulated — “Experiments were performed on 18 healthy adult volunteers (with ages spanning from 20 to 40 years).”
quantityabout 10% — drop in the number of consciously perceived visual stimuli after parietal rTMS versus sham — “In ‘‘Consciousness’’ experiment, number of consciously perceived visual stimuli was lower by about 10% after rTMS (300 ms, 20 Hz, motor threshold intensity) on left or right PPC than after sham (pseudo) rTMS.”
quantityabout 25 ms — slowing of reaction time to go stimuli caused by parietal rTMS relative to sham — “Compared with sham condition, parietal rTMS slowed of about 25 ms reaction time to go stimuli, thus disclosing effects on endogenous covert spatial attention.”
quantity72 ms (±7.9 SE) — mean individually determined perceptive threshold duration of the cue stimulus — “Subject-by-subject, the mean threshold time of the cue stimulus was 72 ms (±7.9 SE).”

quantityF1,18 = 5.84; P < 0.025 — control-ANOVA main effect of seen/not-seen condition on reaction time — “The results showed statistically significant main factors Condition (F1,18 = 5.84; P < 0.025) and Congruence (F1,18 = 5.16; P < 0.035).”
