phenomenonvisuospatial attention and primary visual consciousness— “In the present study, we wondered whether human ventral PPC plays a causal role on visuospatial attention and primary consciousness and whether these 2 functions are correlated with each other.”
claim-typeexistence— “To our knowledge, this is the first direct experimental evidence of functional effects of bilateral ventral PPC on both visuospatial attention and primary consciousness.”
method — evidence— “In this paper, we used repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) in 18 normal subjects to investigate whether the ventral posterior parietal cortex (PPC) plays a causal role on visuospatial attention and primary consciousness and whether these 2 functions are linearly correlated with each other.”
mechanismrTMS spreads transsynaptically into two networks whose interaction is nonlinear, so perturbing ventral PPC dissociates attention from consciousness instead of moving them together— “Interaction of these networks might be nonlinear and too complex to cause simple relationships between visuospatial attention and primary consciousness, at least due to the focal and week TMS-induced perturbation of ventral PPC.”
mechanisma focal coil at near-threshold intensity limits the induced neural interference within a widely distributed network, which is why the behavioural effect is only about 10%— “The small but significant magnitude of rTMS effects on ventral PPC (about 10%) is likely due to the use of focal coil and near-threshold intensity of rTMS.”
mechanismthe stimulation disrupts the spatial disengagement operation of Posner's attention system, which then lengthens reaction time— “It is likely that the present rTMS of ventral PPC mainly effected spatial disengagement, in agreement with previous clinical studies showing that ventral PPC lesions are related to prominent spatial disengagement deficits”
builds-on Babiloni and others 2006— “The length of the rTMS train corresponded to the activation timing of PPC previously found in an electroencephalography (EEG) study carried out with the same paradigm (Babiloni and others 2006).”
replicates Vallar 2001; Corbetta and Shulman 2002; Karnath and others 2003; Corbetta and others 2005— “These results corroborate the well-known notion that ventral PPC is a putative substrate of neglect and other visuospatial deficits (e.g., Vallar 2001; Corbetta and Shulman 2002; Karnath and others 2003; Corbetta and others 2005).”
contradicts the idea that visuospatial attention and primary consciousness are subserved by strictly interdependent networks— “In this sense, the present findings challenge the idea that visuospatial attention and primary consciousness are subserved by strictly interdependent networks.”
senseprimary consciousness, as measured - THE PERCENTAGE OF `SEEN TRIALS`: the title claims a functional role in primary consciousness and the experiment records how often a subject reported perceiving a cue at their own threshold duration, so a term from consciousness theory and a detection rate occupy the same slot— “Trials associated with the conscious perception of cue stimuli were defined as ‘‘seen trials”
sensethe threshold time - EACH SUBJECT'S OWN 50% POINT: defined as the cue duration giving about 50% correct detections over 10 trials, repeated 7 times, so the quantity is fitted per person and every later percentage is measured relative to that fit— “The threshold time was defined as the duration of the cue stimulus determining about 50% of correct stimulus detections within a series of 10 trials”
sensethe 52.9% baseline - NEAR THE DESIGN TARGET BY CONSTRUCTION: the calibration sets each subject near 50% detection, so the baseline seen-trial rate is close to the number the procedure aimed at; the rTMS effect is a departure from a value the method placed there, not from a natural rate— “Across subjects mean percentage of the ‘‘seen trials’’ was 52.9% (±0.8 SE) in the baseline condition (no rTMS)”
controlSHAM rTMS delivered at the same sites, and the effect measured as the difference between real and sham — held — rules out that the effect is the coil's noise, scalp sensation or the expectation of stimulation rather than the magnetic pulse reaching cortex— “In ‘‘Consciousness’’ experiment, number of consciously perceived visual stimuli was lower by about 10% after rTMS (300 ms, 20 Hz, motor threshold intensity) on left or right PPC than after sham (pseudo) rTMS.”
controlthe same sham-difference correlation was computed ACROSS the two experiments as a control comparison — held — rules out that a correlation between the consciousness and attention measures is a general property of the difference scores rather than a shared mechanism— “As a control, the correlation was also calculated between the difference (sham-rTMS at left or right PPC) of ‘‘seen’’ trials in the ‘‘Consciousness’’ experiment and the difference (sham-rTMS at left or right PPC) of reaction times to valid trials in the ‘‘Attentional’’ experiment.”
