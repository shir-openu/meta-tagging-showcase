Introduction

The Women Film Pioneers Projectterm·dom (WFPP) is an established online resource for research on women in early cinema. The project focuses on telling individual stories of women film pioneers and making blind spotsterm·dom visible in order to draw attention to the major role played by women in the early days of film history (Gaines et al. n.d.). Initially conceived in the early 1990s as a classic book project, in 2013 the WFPP was published as a digital platform with three primary goals: to make research findings available, to stimulate further research, and to challenge traditional film historical narratives that fail to mention women's contributions. However, although structured metadataterm·mth is central to making research data and digital publications visible, accessible, and reusable (Baca 2016; Flanders / Jannidis 2018), WFPP's application of metadataterm·dom has been very limited. Therefore, my project aims to create structured metadataterm·mth for the WFPP, enrich it with authority dataterm·mth, and link it in a sustainable way to other datasets on women film pioneers.

Related work

In addition to feminist film historiographyterm·dom, the digital humanities are a central point of reference for this project, particularly approaches that address accessibility of text as data, digital publishing, categorizationterm·dom practices, and metadataterm·dom (Blaney et al. 2021; Flanders / Jannidis 2018). In order to make metadataterm·dom interoperable in the sense of the FAIR data principlesterm·mth, it can be made available as Linked Open Dataterm·dom (Schmidt et al. 2022). In the field of film heritage institutions, the modeling of film data as Linked Open Dataterm·dom has been discussed for several years (Heftberger 2019), including case studies with Wikidataterm·mth (Heftberger / Duchesne 2020).

Methodology

While the initial goal of the project was to develop a thesaurusterm·dom tailored to the needs of WFPP’s staff and users, it became clear as the project progressed that a more promising approach would be to focus on reusing existing thesauri to make the data FAIRterm·key. This conceptual shift from developing a project-specific thesaurusterm·dom to opening up the collection by sustainably linking disparate datasets on women film pioneers had several implications for the project's methodological design. First, the nine categories initially identified for modeling the thesaurusterm·dom (person, film work, occupation, place, technique, institution, film genre, theme, and publication) were reduced to four categories relevant to the structured collection of filmographiesterm·dom: person, film work, institution, and place. By creating FAIRterm·key metadataterm·dom using the WFPP filmographiesterm·dom as an example, the project explores how the FAIR principlesterm·dom can be applied in feminist film historiographyterm·dom. Second, whereas the initial concept included a manual text analysis and annotation with CATMAterm·mth to build the thesaurusterm·dom in a bottom-up approach, the filmographic data is now extracted using Named Entity Recognitionterm·mth and then modeled as LOD. Third, an important step in the methodology is to analyze and evaluate existing thesauri and authority files such as the Getty Art & Architecture Thesaurusterm·mth (AAT), Friend of a Friendterm·mth (FOAF), Virtual International Authority Fileterm·mth (VIAF), Gemeinsame Normdateiterm·mth (GND), and Wikidataterm·mth regarding their usability for feminist film historiographyterm·dom.

Critical reflection

Furthermore, it also involves an examination of the epistemic conditions of a structured data approach and a reflection on the specific requirements of feminist film historiographyterm·dom for applying the FAIR data principlesterm·mth.
