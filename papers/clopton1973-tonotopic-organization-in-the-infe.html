phenomenontonotopic organization in the inferior colliculus
claim-typeexistence
mechanismBest frequencies are organised as partially overlapping bandpass filters spaced one-third octave apart, so near threshold the filter-centre frequencies are favoured over the overlap frequencies and produce the saw-toothed, multimodal frequency distribution — “Thus, near threshold, frequencies at the center of the filters are favored over those at the overlapping regions, and it results in the multi-modal, saw-toothed pattern of frequency distribution.”
mechanismA fixed slab of collicular tissue is devoted to each frequency band, so advancing an electrode through a lamina yields no frequency change until the next lamina is entered, producing a step — “At the IC level, best frequencies may be organized as partially overlapping bandpass filters with a center-to-center separation of one-third octave.”
relation — “Previous studies (Rose et al., 1966; Clopton and Winfield, 1973; Merzenich and Reid, 1974) used larger steps in their electrode mapping and thus are likely to have sampled across the stepwise organization.”
relation — “Together, both types of data argue against the general view that the tonotopic organization in the CNIC forms a continuous frequency gradient.”
relation — “These steps were larger than those found in the present study of the rat, and the size differences may be related to species differences and the larger brain of the cat.”

findingMultiunit recordings sampled every 25-50 micrometres revealed a stair-step, not smooth, progression of best frequency in every penetration through the central nucleus of the inferior colliculus. — “However, when the BF was recorded every 25–50 (cid:1)m (Fig. 2), we found a distinct stair-step pattern in every penetration (n (cid:1) 16 animals, 45 penetrations).”
findingBest frequency stayed nearly constant over roughly 151 micrometres of electrode travel and then jumped by about a third of an octave, giving about 14 steps per track. — “The BF remained nearly constant (changing (cid:7)0.1 octaves) for an average of 151 (cid:3) 41 (cid:1)m (mean (cid:3) SD) and then jumped 0.34 (cid:3) 0.11 octaves (n (cid:1) 460 steps).”
findingAn independent single-unit dataset showed the same discontinuity: pooled best frequencies formed a multimodal distribution with 16 peaks spaced about one-third of an octave apart. — “When the BFs of all 604 units in this sample were pooled and displayed as a histogram with 0.1 octave bins, we observed a multimodal distribution with 16 peaks occurring at 0.32 (cid:3) 0.11 octave intervals (Fig. 6 A).”
findingStep thickness and step size in best frequency did not differ between low- and high-frequency regions of the central nucleus, indicating uniform laminar dimensions across the tonotopic axis. — “When we compared low-frequency versus high-frequency regions, neither the thickness of the constant pattern nor the change in BF at the steps were significantly different (Mann– Whitney tests, p (cid:6) 0.05).”
findingThe thinnest anatomically labelled axonal laminae averaged about 104 micrometres in diameter, which after shrinkage correction matches the 140-150 micrometre physiological step size. — “Seven axonal laminae from the four cases with the thinnest laminae have a mean diameter of 104 (cid:3) 21 (cid:1)m (Table 1).”
findingThe physiological frequency steps correspond to the anatomical fibrodendritic laminae, so the tonotopic map in the rat central nucleus is built from discrete laminar modules rather than a smooth gradient. — “Thus, our anatomical and physiological data suggest that the tonotopic map in the CNIC in the rat is composed of discrete physiological laminae that correspond to the anatomical fibrodendritic laminae of the CNIC.”
findingReanalysis of 198 previously published rat primary auditory cortex single units showed the same multipeaked best-frequency distribution at about 0.31 octave intervals, suggesting the discontinuity persists from midbrain to cortex. — “The cortical units showed a similar multipeaked distribution (Fig. 9B), with the peaks occurring at 0.31 (cid:3) 0.10 octave intervals.”
findingEarlier mapping studies missed the stepwise pattern because electrode steps larger than 50 micrometres undersample frequency changes that occur about every 150 micrometres. — “Electrode steps of 50 (cid:1)m or less were necessary to observe the stepwise organization because the changes in frequency occurred at (cid:4)150 (cid:1)m.”
findingBest frequency at a fixed recording site was stable over 23-80 minutes, drifting on average only 0.006 octaves, about fifty times less than a staircase jump. — “We found that BF remains virtually constant, changing on average 0.006 octaves (i.e., 50 times smaller than the jumps of BF, 0.34), with a variance of 0.0002.”
findingThe number and width of the inferior colliculus laminae implied by the data (about 8-12 laminae of 0.29-0.36 octaves) coincides with independent estimates of the number of critical bands in the rat. — “Interestingly, our data in the IC suggest that there may be 8 –12 laminae, each covering 0.29 – 0.36 octaves.”
