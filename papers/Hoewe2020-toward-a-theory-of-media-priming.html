phenomenonmedia priming— “The argument presented here is that media priming occurs when the response to some aspect of media content – the influence of the prime – happens outside the media consumer’s awareness.”
claim-typemechanism— “Media priming operates entirely within System 1 processing (fast, automatic, and with little effort) and should only include effects of a prime that are beyond the conscious awareness of the media consumer.”
method — evidence— “This paper begins by detailing the existing definitions of priming, focusing on the different uses of ‘priming’ within media research and its overlap with other media theories.”
transferfrom cognitive and social psychology (dual-process models: System 1 / System 2, ELM, HSM): The paper imports the dual-process account of cognition wholesale and uses it as the machinery for distinguishing media priming from political priming in communication research.— “It then applies dualprocess models to the study of priming to illustrate the cognitive processing that occurs within media priming (and political priming).”
transferfrom psychology: Chooses Kahneman's and Stanovich and West's System 1 / System 2 vocabulary, in preference to the communication field's own ELM and HSM, as the framework for the new theory.— “Given that the System 1 and System 2 description encapsulates many of the propositions put forth in the ELM, the HSM, and other dual-process models, it will be utilized here.”
mechanismA prime spreads activation through an associative memory network, and because only a few activated nodes reach conscious awareness the resulting judgment cannot be traced back to its source.— “Priming operates through the associative activation of memory, where information can trigger the activation of other ideas from memory in a ‘cascade of activity’ (Kahneman, 2011, p. 51).”
mechanismMedia priming works by activating nodes or networks of nodes in associative memory outside the media consumer's immediate awareness.— “Media priming then relies on the use of associative memory, where nodes (or networks of nodes) are activated outside the media consumer’s immediate awareness.”
mechanismAwareness cancels the effect: once people notice the prime or deliberate about the source of their decision they correct for it, which is why the effect requires unawareness.— “Providing further evidence of this proposition, prior research has found that when participants carefully consider the impact of information, the source of their decisions, and/or are made aware of the priming mechanism, they are less likely to be influenced by the prime because they will correct for it (Lepore & Brown, 2002; Lombardi et…
mechanismA gap between desired and actual confidence in a political judgment motivates a switch from System 1 to System 2, which is why evaluations of political actors can be made within awareness.— “If the desired level of confidence is not reached, System 2 would be activated, as System 2 is necessary when one’s desired level of confidence in their judgement is not the same as their actual level of confidence.”

sensepriming, at its most basic - the presentation of a stimulus and the effects it causes, with the PRIME separated out as the portion of the stimulus responsible. The paper starts from the least contested version and then shows it does not survive contact with the field— “At its most basic level, priming is the presentation of a stimulus and the effects caused by that stimulus, which can include subsequent changes in beliefs, attitudes, and behaviors. A prime, then, is the portion of the stimulus that leads to these effects.”
sensepriming per Bargh 2016, quoted at length as the best available clarification - PASSIVE, UNINTENDED, and not accurately reportable. The definition works by exclusion: an explicit instruction is not priming because the person intends it, and presenting new information is LEARNING rather than passive activation of what is already in the head— “Priming effects of recent experience on presently made responses to that environment (judgments, verbal and nonverbal behavior, actions) are passive effects that the individual did not intend to occur, and of which influence the individual is not aware and cannot accurately verbally report upon.
