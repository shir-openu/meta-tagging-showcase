claim-typemechanism
transferfrom cognitive and social psychology (dual-process models: System 1 / System 2, ELM, HSM): The paper imports the dual-process account of cognition wholesale and uses it as the machinery for distinguishing media priming from political priming in communication research. — “It then applies dualprocess models to the study of priming to illustrate the cognitive processing that occurs within media priming (and political priming).”
transferfrom psychology: Chooses Kahneman's and Stanovich and West's System 1 / System 2 vocabulary, in preference to the communication field's own ELM and HSM, as the framework for the new theory. — “Given that the System 1 and System 2 description encapsulates many of the propositions put forth in the ELM, the HSM, and other dual-process models, it will be utilized here.”
mechanismA prime spreads activation through an associative memory network, and because only a few activated nodes reach conscious awareness the resulting judgment cannot be traced back to its source. — “Priming operates through the associative activation of memory, where information can trigger the activation of other ideas from memory in a ‘cascade of activity’ (Kahneman, 2011, p. 51).”
mechanismMedia priming works by activating nodes or networks of nodes in associative memory outside the media consumer's immediate awareness. — “Media priming then relies on the use of associative memory, where nodes (or networks of nodes) are activated outside the media consumer’s immediate awareness.”
mechanismAwareness cancels the effect: once people notice the prime or deliberate about the source of their decision they correct for it, which is why the effect requires unawareness. — “Providing further evidence of this proposition, prior research has found that when participants carefully consider the impact of information, the source of their decisions, and/or are made aware of the priming mechanism, they are less likely to be influenced by the prime because they will correct for it (Lepore & Brown, 2002; Lombardi et al., 1987; Thompson et al., 1994).”
mechanismA gap between desired and actual confidence in a political judgment motivates a switch from System 1 to System 2, which is why evaluations of political actors can be made within awareness. — “If the desired level of confidence is not reached, System 2 would be activated, as System 2 is necessary when one’s desired level of confidence in their judgement is not the same as their actual level of confidence.”

Content tags
content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingThe paper proposes that media priming, properly defined, occurs only within System 1 processing, when a media stimulus produces a reaction whose source the consumer cannot trace. — “Given these considerations, the theory of media priming put forth here posits that media priming can only occur within System 1, where it takes place when a media stimulus causes some reaction that occurs for reasons outside the media consumer’s awareness.”
findingMedia priming and political priming are separated by awareness rather than by accessibility: the first works outside awareness, the second may work inside it. — “While both media priming and political priming could operate through cognitive accessibility, as previous research has suggested, media priming involves processing that occurs outside the individuals’ awareness, while political priming could involve processing that takes place within the individual’s awareness.”
findingPolitical priming is argued to be plausibly a System 2 phenomenon and therefore a different psychological process from media priming. — “Therefore, while it is possible that political priming could occur through System 1 processing, it is also entirely plausible that it could occur through System 2 processing.”
findingThe review identifies two divergent research camps using the same word 'priming' with different operationalizations and different effect patterns. — “Beyond these basic definitional similarities, two broad camps have developed within media research with quite different operationalizations and findings.”
findingThe theory yields a testable criterion: a manipulation check establishing that the consumer cannot identify the prime is required before the label media priming applies. — “If the media consumer is able to identify the prime within the media stimulus that influenced their response to it, then it would not be considered media priming.”
findingApplying the criterion to an existing study of terrorism news framing shows it does not qualify as media priming because participants demonstrably noticed the manipulated phrase. — “Due to participants’ clear awareness of the information included in the news story and its application to their judgments, this example would not be illustrative of media priming (appropriately, the authors discuss their findings in terms of equivalence framing).”
definitionmedia priming — “This manuscript puts forth a theory of media priming, where media priming occurs when information from media content is utilized outside the media consumer’s awareness.”
definitionpriming (basic definition) — “At its most basic level, priming is the presentation of a stimulus and the effects caused by that stimulus, which can include subsequent changes in beliefs, attitudes, and behaviors.”
definitionprime — “A prime, then, is the portion of the stimulus that leads to these effects.”
definitionpolitical priming — “More simply put, political priming is the process them all through which media coverage of an issue increases the weight placed upon it by the media consumer, which then is used in evaluating a politician or political party (Iyengar & Kinder, 1987).”
definitionSystem 1 — “Stanovich and West (2000) defined System 1 as ‘automatic, largely unconscious, and relatively undemanding of computational capacity’ (p. 658).”
definitionSystem 2 — “Kahneman (2011) defined System 2 as allocating ‘attention to the effortful mental activities that demand it’ (p. 21).”
definitionlaw of least effort — “Given this law of least effort (Bohner et al., 1995; Kahneman, 2011), System 1 processing is more likely to occur than System 2 processing.”
definitionsufficiency principle — “This proposition fits within the sufficiency principle, where individuals seek balance between using the least amount of effort and having confidence in their attitudes and decisions (Bohner et al., 1995).”

conceptmedia priming — “the theory of media priming put forth here posits that media priming can only occur within System 1, where it takes place when a media stimulus causes some reaction that occurs for reasons outside the media consumer’s awareness. Media priming then relies on the use of associative memory, where nodes (or networks of nodes) are activated outside the media consumer’s immediate awareness. The media consumer is influenced by some aspect of that media content, but they are unable to mentally trace the source of said influence.”
