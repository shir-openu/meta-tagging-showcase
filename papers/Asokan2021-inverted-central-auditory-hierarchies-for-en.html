mechanismCorticothalamic feedback recruits thalamic reticular nucleus inhibition, which dampens feedforward thalamocortical drive during rhythms — “These effects hint at an additional (or alternative) circuit mediated through corticothalamic feedback, which could dampen feedforward thalamocortical synaptic inputs via inhibition from the thalamic reticular nucleus.”

limitationCortical sensitivity to regularity did not extend to longer or more complex cycles, so the integration window demonstrated is bounded at roughly 0.8-1.6 s. — “Therefore, at least for A1 in passively listening mice, single unit sensitivity to regularity is limited to stimulus timescales on the order of 4-8 repeating elements or 0.8 – 1.6s.”
limitationOnly A1 in passively listening mice was studied; higher-order fields and behaviourally engaged listening remain to be tested. — “In future work, it would be important to test whether even more protracted spiking timescales and more integrative processing of slower developing regularities are encoded in candidate higher-order areas of the mouse auditory cortex, such as the dorsal-posterior and ventral-posterior fields, particularly when mice are behaviorally engaged in active listening tasks.”

findingNeural timescales, measured as the autocorrelation decay constant, roughly double from auditory midbrain to cortex. — “Adapting this approach to our data, we confirmed a progression of neural timescales in the auditory system that approximately doubled from midbrain to cortex ( Figure 1D ).”
findingAccuracy of decoding the interval between two noise bursts falls progressively from midbrain to thalamus to cortex. — “From midbrain to cortex, decoding accuracy was reduced overall and restricted to a limited range of longer intervals ( Figure 1G ), such that the median threshold of interval decoding rose from 1.4ms for IC units, to 16ms for MGB units, and 64ms in A1 ( Figure 1H )”
findingOnly cortical ensemble activity, not thalamic or midbrain activity, allowed a classifier to tell rhythmic from random interval sequences. — “We observed that the changes in ensemble spiking were sufficient to classify the change from rhythmic to random sequences in A1, but not in MGB or IC ( Figure 2C - D ).”
findingRhythmic context shortened neural timescales strongly and reliably in A1, weakly in MGB, and not at all in IC. — “We found that neural timescales were unaffected by random or rhythmic sequences in the IC, were slightly and inconsistently dampened in the MGB, but were strongly and reliably dampened in A1 during rhythmic patterns ( Figure 3C - D ).”
findingRegularity was encoded by the arrangement of spikes in time rather than by any change in mean firing rate. — “Presenting the intervals in rhythmic patterns had no consistent effect on mean firing rate within the 100ms response window ( Figure 3E - F ), but instead only on how the spikes were arranged withing the 100ms response window, both in terms of shorter decay constants and less variation in first spike latency ( Figure S1 ).”
findingCortical sensitivity to regularity was bounded, disappearing for rhythmic cycles longer than roughly 4 to 8 repeating intervals. — “Therefore, at least for A1 in passively listening mice, single unit sensitivity to regularity is limited to stimulus timescales on the order of 4-8 repeating elements or 0.8 – 1.6s.”
findingThe timescale compression occurred in putative pyramidal (regular-spiking) neurons across layers but not in putative parvalbumin interneurons. — “Neural timescales were equivalently compressed for regular spiking putative pyramidal neurons across A1 layers ( Figure S2 ), yet no effect of context was observed in fast spiking putative parvalbumin-expressing interneurons ( Figure S3 ).”
