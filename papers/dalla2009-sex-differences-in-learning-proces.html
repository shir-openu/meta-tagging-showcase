phenomenonsex differences in classical and operant conditioning
claim-typeexistence
transferfrom human cognitive psychology: the verbal-versus-spatial pattern of human sex differences, used to motivate the rodent work — “For example, women tend to outperform men on tasks that involve verbal or semantic rules and memories related to personal experience, whereas men perform better on tasks that require the manipulation of complex spatial information, such as the manipulation of shapes in space”
transferfrom clinical psychiatry: the sex-skewed prevalence of specific disorders, used as the applied justification for the whole review — “For example, alcoholism, autism and attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) are much more common in men, whereas Alzheimer's disease, social phobias, eating disorders, post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) and major depression are more common in women”
mechanismThe amount of fear generated by the task determines which sex performs better: high-fear paradigms favour males, low-fear paradigms favour females — “These effects seem to depend on the amount of fear that is generated and expressed during the learning experience”
mechanismMales acquire and retain the conditioned fear more strongly, and this stronger fear is what drives their superior performance in fear conditioning — “It is possible that males perform better than females, because they acquire and retain the conditioned fear in a higher degree.”
mechanismDivergent coping strategies to shock — active responding in females versus passive behavioural inhibition and freezing in males — produce the operant-conditioning sex difference — “Female rats respond actively to aversive stimulation, such as the footshock, whereas male rats exhibit behavioural inhibition by reacting passively and freeze”
mechanismAdult testosterone acts anxiolytically and thereby pushes males toward the passive strategy that impairs their avoidance learning — “Testosterone has been proposed to have anxiolytic properties and this might contribute to the passive strategy that male rats choose in response to the footshock”
mechanismDendritic spine density on CA1 pyramidal cells tracks learned-response performance across hormonal and stress manipulations, and is proposed as the anatomical substrate of the sex difference — “Thus, under all these various circumstances, performance of the learned response relates in a positive way to the presence of dendritic spines in the CA1 area of the hippocampus”
mechanismGonadal hormones act twice — organizing the developing brain and then activating circuits in puberty and adulthood — and the two effects interact to produce adult sex differences — “Thus, sex differences in learning are influenced by both organizational and activational effects of sex hormones, but in a complex and probably interactive way.”
mechanism — “Most sex differences in conditioning are dependent on organizational effects of gonadal hormones during early development of the brain, in addition to modulation by activational effects during puberty and adulthood.”
mechanism — “When females are masculinized by exposure to testosterone on the day of their birth, they do not perform like males as adults [ 84 , 87 ]. However, if they are also treated with testosterone in adulthood, then they exhibit male-like escape avoidance latencies”
mechanism — “It seems that in active avoidance tasks testosterone plays a more important role than estrogens in the emergence of sex differences, while in eyeblink and fear conditioning estrogens and progesterone in females seem to be more important. These tasks differ in the fact that during operant conditioning, the animal must emit a voluntary motor response in order to learn the contingency, whereas during classical (i.e. Pavlovian) conditioning, the animal emits an obligatory unconditioned response to the unconditioned stimulus, irrespective of volition. Thus, the two types of training procedures are vastly different in terms of their dependence on volitional activity and the learning processes involved. Therefore, different hormonal factors seem to mediate each task.”
mechanism — “However, the sex difference is apparent even in females that are trained during diestrus, a phase of the estrous cycle characterized by lower levels of estrogens and progesterone ( Figure 1b ) [ 47 ]. Thus, sex differences are evident across the estrous cycle and are not solely dependent on the presence of estrogens in females.”
mechanism — “It is noted that one would not expect a total dependence of basic learning processes on one or even a few hormones, because the adaptive value of learning is too great. Males and females of all species undergo massive changes in sex hormone availability across their lifespan, so learning cannot be massively influenced by their levels.”
mechanism — “Studies with exogenous estrogens administration showed that low doses of estrogens disrupt, while high doses facilitate acquisition of the active avoidance behaviour”
relation — “Minimally, the presentation of sex differences in learning underscores the need to include females in basic research studies [ 30 ]. They are often not included either because of the problems inherent to monitoring the estrous cycle, but also because it is assumed that their behaviours will be similar to males, albeit more variable.”
relation — “Oddly enough, in human studies, men tend to express more variance on test scores of cognitive and intellectual performance”
relation — “Also, some sex differences probably do not reflect sex differences in learning, but rather sex differences in strategies. This has been suggested before for spatial learning paradigms, in which male rats usually perform better than females by using a more direct strategy”
relation — “Because so many mental disorders are more prevalent on one sex than the other, it is important to consider sex differences in learning when applying animal models of learning for these disorders.”
differs intrace conditioning → delay conditioning — THE AXIS IS THE TRACE INTERVAL, AND IT IS GIVEN AS A NUMBER. 'This task DEPENDS ON THE HIPPOCAMPUS and REQUIRES MULTIPLE TRIALS to learn BECAUSE THE CS IS SEPARATED WITH THE US WITH A TRACE INTERVAL OF 500 MS.' The two procedures are identical except for a gap, and the sentence states the gap AS THE CAUSE of both the hippocampal dependence and the slower learning - so the axis is not a description of the difference, it is the explanation of the consequences. Q-471. — “the CS is separated with the US with a trace interval of 500 ms”
replicationreplicated
limitationThe neural substrate of the reviewed sex differences is not established; hippocampal CA1 spine density is offered only as a parallel correlate. — “The neuroanatomical substrates that might account for sex differences have not been identified”
limitationPerformance factors can never be entirely excluded even in the eyeblink paradigm the authors regard as least contaminated by them. — “That said, one can never rule out performance effects entirely.”
limitationSex differences in spatial learning are excluded from the review's scope. — “Sex differences in spatial learning, although important, will not be discussed in this review, because they have been reviewed elsewhere”
limitationDevelopmental hormone effects on conditioned taste aversion are absent from the literature reviewed. — “To our knowledge, there is no report in the literature concerning developmental effects of sex hormones.”
limitationConditioned taste aversion is confounded by sex differences in sweet preference and in the rewarding/aversive properties of the compounds used, making learning-level conclusions hard to draw. — “As a result, it is difficult to study sex differences in conditioned learning in this paradigm, although they exist.”
limitationFindings on retention and extinction of cue fear conditioning are inconsistent across studies. — “However, conflicting results have been obtained on whether sex differences exist during testing (retention) and extinction of cue fear conditioning, when rats are placed in a different context (CR: time spend freezing)”
limitationsex differences in performance account for some of the reported learning effects, a confound running through the whole literature — “Critically, sex differences in performance account for some of the reported effects on learning and these are discussed throughout the review.”
limitationsome differences may be differences in strategy rather than in learning — “Also, some sex differences probably do not reflect sex differences in learning, but rather sex differences in strategies. This has been suggested before for spatial learning paradigms, in which male rats usually perform better than females by using a more direct strategy”
limitationlever-pressing differences are attributable to general activity and to what females do instead of pressing — “Sex differences in lever-pressing have been attributed to higher general activity of females than males and to the fact that during testing females engage to different activities than pressing the lever”
limitationconditioned taste aversion is declared difficult to interpret for this question because taste preference, drug reward and the hormones themselves all confound it — “To make the issue more complicated, exogenous sex hormone's administration also has illness-inducing properties, which can in turn influence conditioned taste aversion [ 121 ]. As a result, it is difficult to study sex differences in conditioned learning in this paradigm, although they exist.”
limitationdrug-based aversion data cannot be evaluated from a learning perspective because of the rewarding properties of the drugs — “Although these data are very interesting, it is difficult to evaluate them from a learning perspective, because of the potent rewarding properties of the different classes of drugs of abuse.”
limitationhuman sex differences are small, task-dependent and hard to interpret even when observed — “That said, many of the reported sex differences are relatively small in magnitude and their emergence may only arise as a function of the task, its difficulty or the specific experimental design [ 14 ]. For these reasons, sex differences in human cognition are difficult to be observed and once observed, even more difficult to interpret.”
limitationspatial learning is excluded from the review despite being central to the topic — “Sex differences in spatial learning, although important, will not be discussed in this review, because they have been reviewed elsewhere”
limitationno study exists on developmental hormone effects in conditioned taste aversion — “To our knowledge, there is no report in the literature concerning developmental effects of sex hormones. Given the time course, such studies would be difficult to do, although not impossible.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingFemale rats emit more conditioned eyeblink responses than males in both the hippocampus-dependent trace version and the hippocampus-independent delay version of the task. — “During both trace and delay conditioning, females outperform males”
findingThe direction of the sex difference reverses in fear conditioning: males acquire and express conditioned fear better than females. — “Thus, in general, males outperform females in fear conditioning paradigms.”
findingOn the harder two-way active avoidance task females learn to escape within the first trials while males need many more, and some males never learn at all. — “Although the task is more difficult, most females readily learn to escape the stimulus on the first trials of testing, whereas males require more trials.”
findingRemoving the ovaries in adulthood abolishes the female advantage in eyeblink conditioning, showing the effect depends on circulating ovarian hormones. — “Also, removal of ovarian hormones prevents the sex difference in performance”
findingThe sex difference in eyeblink conditioning is absent before and during puberty and only appears in adulthood, so it cannot be explained by plasma estrogen levels alone. — “Interestingly, the sex differences in learning emerge after puberty, whereas no sex differences are observed before or during puberty”
findingGiving testosterone to newborn females impairs their adult classical conditioning to male-like levels, evidencing an organizational hormone effect. — “On the other hand, classical conditioning is decremented in adult females if they are treated with testosterone on the day of their birth.”
findingIn conditioned taste aversion acquisition does not differ by sex, but females extinguish the aversion faster, drinking the sweet solution again sooner and in larger amounts. — “Females typically extinguish sooner than males; they will again consume the sweet solution in less time and do so in greater quantities”
findingUncontrollable stress makes male rats fail to escape shock in active avoidance (learned helplessness) while similarly stressed females continue to escape. — “When male rats are exposed to uncontrollable stress, they do not learn to escape the shock in the active avoidance task and thus they become helpless.”
findingBecause females learn trace eyeblink conditioning better, more of the newly born hippocampal cells survive in females than in males. — “In a recent study we found that female rats learn better than males in trace eyeblink conditioning and as a consequence a greater proportion of new neurons are rescued in their hippocampus than in males”
findingCastrating adult males improves their operant avoidance learning, implicating adult testosterone in the male deficit. — “In contrast, castration of adult males facilitates learning in operant conditioning”
findingfemales outperform males in eyeblink conditioning, fear-potentiated startle and most operant tasks — “In general, females perform better than males in the classical eyeblink conditioning, in fear-potentiated startle and in most operant conditioning tasks, such as the active avoidance test. However, in the classical fear conditioning paradigm, in certain lever-pressing paradigms and in the conditioned taste aversion males outperform females or are more resistant to extinction.”
findingmales outperform females in classical fear conditioning, some lever-pressing tasks and taste aversion — “In general, females perform better than males in the classical eyeblink conditioning, in fear-potentiated startle and in most operant conditioning tasks, such as the active avoidance test. However, in the classical fear conditioning paradigm, in certain lever-pressing paradigms and in the conditioned taste aversion males outperform females or are more resistant to extinction.”
findingthe direction reverses with task difficulty within lever-pressing — “Thus, sex differences exist in lever-pressing paradigms with males outperforming females in simple tasks, while females performing better in more difficult tasks, such as the differential reinforcement of low rate responding.”
findingthe eyeblink difference persists in diestrus, so it is not solely an oestrogen effect — “However, the sex difference is apparent even in females that are trained during diestrus, a phase of the estrous cycle characterized by lower levels of estrogens and progesterone ( Figure 1b ) [ 47 ]. Thus, sex differences are evident across the estrous cycle and are not solely dependent on the presence of estrogens in females.”
findingfemales retain the conditioned response better weeks later, which argues the difference is learning and not performance — “Most convincing is our recent data on memory. In this study, females that are trained and weeks later they are re-exposed to the CS, respond with many more CRs than do males. These data indicate that female rats actually learn the CR better than males and thus they retain it better”
findingno sex difference exists in spontaneous blinking, in response to the noise alone, or in the unconditioned response — “In terms of general responsivity, we have never seen any sex difference in spontaneous blinking before training. Neither have we observed any sex differences in eyeblink response to the white noise alone (the eventual CS) before training or in the magnitude of the UR. Also, both males and females do not respond to the first trial, before any US has occurred”
findingmales exposed to uncontrollable stress become helpless while females do not — “When male rats are exposed to uncontrollable stress, they do not learn to escape the shock in the active avoidance task and thus they become helpless. On the contrary, females exposed to the same regime of uncontrollable stress learn to escape and thus they do not express learned helplessness behaviour”
findingthe helplessness difference survives gonadectomy and neonatal masculinisation, unlike the other effects — “However, gonadectomy of either sex in adulthood or masculinization of the female brain with testosterone on the day of birth does not prevent sex differences in learned helplessness behaviour”
findingoestrogen effects on avoidance are non-monotonic - low doses disrupt, high doses facilitate — “Studies with exogenous estrogens administration showed that low doses of estrogens disrupt, while high doses facilitate acquisition of the active avoidance behaviour”
findingphysiological oestrogen replacement does not restore the eyeblink advantage; only non-physiological doses do — “On the other hand, estrogens replacement at physiological doses does not enhance conditioning in ovariectomized females. Only very high non-physiological doses of estradiol are effective.”
