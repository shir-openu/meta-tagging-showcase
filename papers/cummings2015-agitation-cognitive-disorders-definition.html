Discussion Elements of the consensus definitionterm·key The provisional consensus definitionterm·key uses the DSM style for defining a disorder as one that produces disability (American Psychiatric Association, 2013 ). The definitionterm is limited to patients with cognitive impairmentterm·key, requires evidence of emotional distressterm·key, requires one of the three observable types of behavior (excessive motor activity, verbal aggressionterm·dom, or physical aggressionterm·dom), specifies that the behavior causes excess disabilityterm·key, and notes that the behaviors cannot be solely attributable to a suboptimal care environment or another disorder such as psychiatric illness, medical illness, or effects of a substance.

Provisional consensus definitionterm·key The definitionterm is labeled as “provisional” because it may evolve as it is subjected to prospective validation. It represents an important starting point in a dynamic dialogue that will evolve with clinical application, research, and review. Validation studies are expected to show that some elements are better suited to clinical and research applications than others.

The definitionterm was labeled as a “consensus” because a majority of stakeholders involved in the process concurred with the current definitionterm. Not all elements were unanimously endorsed; a consensus was achieved on all aspects of the definitionterm.

Limitation to syndromes with cognitive impairmentterm·key Agitationterm·dom occurs in many disorders and is not limited to conditions with cognitive impairmentterm·key (Ballard and Corbett, 2010 ; Manoochehri and Huey, 2012 ; Bruns and Josephs, 2013 ; Gonzalez et al. , 2013 ; Swann, 2013 ). The ADWGterm·dom and the participating members of the surveys favored limiting the definitionterm to disorders with cognitive impairmentterm·key. Sixty-seven percent of Survey 2 respondents agreed with this approach.

There are currently separate definitionsterm for psychosis of ADterm·dom and psychosis in schizophrenia even though they have shared elements such as delusions and hallucinations (Jeste and Finkel, 2000 ); similarly there are definitionsterm of major depression and depression in ADterm·dom that are overlapping but not identical (Olin et al. , 2003 ). Similar considerations may apply to agitationterm·dom, and we chose to develop a definitionterm explicitly for patients with cognitive impairmentterm·key. Further research may lead to modifications of this approach.

The FDA has expressed concern about “pseudo-specificityterm·dom” of syndromes that are artificially assigned to one disorder when they represent general syndromes for which drugs are already approved (CummingspersName and Jeste, 2007 ), and the ADWGterm·dom wished to avoid constructing a definitionterm that might raise this issue. Our definitionterm applies specifically to the type of agitationterm·dom observed in patients with cognitive impairmentterm·key based on the unique relationships of agitationterm·dom with aspects of cognition (Senanarong et al ., 2004 ); a distinct pathophysiology (Bruen et al. , 2008 ; Trzepacz et al. , 2013 ); and possibly a differential response to treatment compared with syndromes such as psychosis of ADterm·dom (Schneider et al. , 2006 ).

Subjective aspect The definitionterm requires that there be observed or inferred evidence of emotional distressterm·key. Examples of behaviors indicative of emotional distressterm·key are provided, including rapid changes in mood, irritability, or emotional outbursts. The ADWGterm·dom required this subjective element because agitationterm·dom as conceived by the panel includes the concept that the patient is upset or distressed, and the agitated behavior is an expression of this emotional state. A person could exhibit purposeful aggressionterm·dom with behaviors overlapping with the definitionterm but would not be classified as “agitated” if there was no associated distress. It is also important that patients not be treated when their behavior is upsetting to someone else (a family member or caregiver) but not to the patient. Requiring the subjective element of the definitionterm makes this less likely. It is not possible to directly observe emotional states, and they must be inferred from observable behaviors. In all, 78.2% of the Survey 2 respondents strongly (42.8%) or somewhat (35.4%) agreed that this aspect of agitationterm·dom was captured appropriately by the proposed definitionterm.

The definitionterm applies to a chronic or at least long-lasting state that has been sustained, persisted, or been frequently recurrent for at least two weeks. This aspect of the definitionterm will assist with intervention studies (pharmacologic and non-pharmacologic) where interventional groups are compared and sustained behavioral changes are required to see a difference in the study outcomes.

Change from previous behavior The ADWGterm·dom limited the definitionterm of agitationterm·dom to patients with cognitive impairmentterm·key. Behaviors that preceded the onset of the associated cognitive disorder would not be included in this definitionterm. This aspect of the consensus definitionterm·key is similar to the precedent adapted in the definitionterm of psychosis of ADterm·dom (Jeste and Finkel, 2000 ).

The behaviors can include excessive motor activity (such as pacing, rocking, gesturing, pointing fingers, restlessness, performing repetitious mannerisms), verbal aggressionterm·dom (e.g. yelling, speaking in an excessively loud voice, using profanity, screaming, shouting), or physical aggressionterm·dom (e.g. grabbing, shoving, pushing, resisting, hitting others, kicking objects or people, scratching, biting, throwing objects, hitting self, slamming doors, tearing things, and destroying property).
