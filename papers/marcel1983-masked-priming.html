sense**THE ORDERING RESULT, AND IT RUNS THE WRONG WAY FOR THE STANDARD ACCOUNT:** `As SOA is reduced, DETECTION judgments suffer first, GRAPHIC similarity judgments fall next, and SEMANTIC judgments fall last.` The received view was that masking interrupts the icon and that without the icon there is nothing left to analyse for meaning - which predicts semantic judgments failing first, or at latest with the rest. **They are the most robust of the three.** Three measures, one manipulation, a fixed order, and the order is the argument— “As SOA is reduced, detection judgments suffer first, graphic similarity judgments fall next, and semantic judgments fall last.”
sense**AND THE CONTROL IS WHAT MAKES IT A DISSOCIATION RATHER THAN A FAILED MASK:** `while NO SUBJECT WAS AWARE of the first letter string under EITHER masking condition, all subjects showed a substantial advantage for associated words when the first was CENTRALLY masked while NONE did when it was PERIPHERALLY masked.` `They could not see it and were primed anyway` invites `then your mask did not work`. **Marcel's reply is a second mask that also abolishes awareness and also abolishes the priming** - awareness held constant at zero, priming moved from 56 msec to 4. Compare erb2019 and eckstein2007 in this corpus: the same manoeuvre, thirty years later, still being reinvented— “while no subject was aware of the first letter string under either masking condition, all subjects showed a substantial advantage for associated words when the first was centrally masked while none did when it was peripherally masked.”
sense**THE POSITION BEING OVERTURNED, STATED IN ITS OWN WORDS BEFORE IT IS DISMANTLED:** `The currently held interpretation of masking (Sperling, 1967; Turvey, 1973) is that it disrupts a relatively raw representation of visual input (ICONIC MEMORY), WITHOUT WHICH input cannot be processed to achieve semantic or phonological coding.` Worth holding as a record of what the field believed in 1983, and worth marking as a view the paper REJECTS - a triple built from this sentence would enter the graph asserting the opposite of the record's finding, which is the fault I dropped an edge for on eckstein2007 this afternoon— “The currently held interpretation of masking (Sperling, 1967; Turvey, 1973) is that it disrupts a relatively raw representation of visual input (iconic memory), without which input cannot be processed to achieve semantic or phonological coding.”

conceptdetection — can the subject say ANYTHING was there. **The weakest question you can ask, and it fails first** - which is the whole point: a measure that dies before the others cannot be the gateway the others depend on— “As SOA is reduced, detection judgments suffer first, graphic similarity judgments fall next, and semantic judgments fall last.”
conceptsemantic judgments — which of two probe words is closer in MEANING to the thing you could not see. **Falls last of the three**, so meaning is recoverable at exposures where presence is not— “As SOA is reduced, detection judgments suffer first, graphic similarity judgments fall next, and semantic judgments fall last.”
conceptmasking — the instrument, and this paper's real subject. **It has two kinds and they behave differently while producing the same absence of awareness** - which is what turns a null-awareness claim into a dissociation instead of an untested assumption— “while no subject was aware of the first letter string under either masking condition, all subjects showed a substantial advantage for associated words when the first was centrally masked while none did when it was peripherally masked.”
concepticonic memory — the raw visual store masking was thought to destroy. **The standard account made it the gateway**: no icon, no semantic coding. If that were right, semantic judgments should fail FIRST as SOA drops. They fall last— “The currently held interpretation of masking (Sperling, 1967; Turvey, 1973) is that it disrupts a relatively raw representation of visual input (iconic memory), without which input cannot be processed to achieve semantic or phonological coding.”
