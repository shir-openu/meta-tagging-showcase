phenomenonsensory discrimination testing
claim-typemethod
mechanismchoosing an irrelevant attribute in the self-defined test adds irrelevant variation, which degrades the panellist's later discrimination — “As shown by the group analysis, choosing a ‘‘wrong” dimension, i.e. attending to some aspect of irrelevant variation, will degrade the subject’s performance.”
mechanismwider discriminal dispersion in some subjects makes rank-order reversals (inversions) of the two products more likely, so their responses oppose the majority — “If the discriminal dispersions are greater for some subjects than others, the possibility of such reversals becomes more likely and thus the choice of a response that is at odds with the ‘‘correct” or majority opinion.”
mechanismthe triangle test requires three implied paired comparisons, whose combined variability lowers its power relative to a directional AFC test — “Part of the difficulty with the triangle procedure arises from the inherent variability of the three implied paired comparisons that must be performed in order to select the ‘‘odd” or outlying (most different) sample.”
relation — “Such a testing procedure was mentioned by Pfaffmann in 1953 who discussed both the benefits of incidental warm-up in repeated testing and the possibility of using a personally defined attribute in a two-sample choice test.”
relation — “This statement thus foreshadowed the warmed-up paired comparison method of O’Mahony and Goldstein (1986) and O’Mahony et al. (1988) as well as the SD-2-AFC used here.”
relation — “However, examination of the d-prime values shows that the increase in d-prime was not significant (.52–.98 for N = 9, z = 0.56).”
replicationuntested
limitationRe-tasting during the preview was permitted but not recorded, so the procedure may have drifted toward a full warm-up protocol for some subjects. — “Re-tasting was allowed during the preview phase, but was not recorded.”
limitationVariation between the food systems may reflect differences between the judges who happened to participate rather than the methods. — “As pointed out by one reviewer, some of the variation among the different foods was undoubtedly contributed by differences among judges, as further analysis below will examine.”
limitationParticipants were not excluded from later product tests, so the same people could recur across product systems. — “No attempt was made to exclude earlier participants from a subsequent food product test, although they were of course restricted to participation in only one condition per product.”
limitationThe two measures of the same thing disagree in direction: the method with the statistical advantage returns the LOWER estimate of absolute sensory difference, and the paper calls this paradoxical. — “In summary, the SD-AFC showed a statistical advantage over the triangle procedure in these studies. Paradoxically, the method showed lower absolute discrimination as measured by Thurstonian scale values.”
limitationBelow-chance performance was not recorded as below chance: those groups were assigned a d-prime of zero so that the comparison could be run. — “Groups with performance below chance were assigned a d-prime value of zero for purposes of statistical comparisons.”
limitationWhat counts as the CORRECT attribute is set by the majority of subjects, not by the manipulation, and a subject can be scored as atypical for disagreeing with the plurality. — “For all tests, a plurality of subjects used attributes that were similar enough to be grouped. These attributes were categorized as typical. All other subjects were categorized as atypical.”
limitationWho turns up decides part of the result, and the authors say so, crediting a reviewer. — “As pointed out by one reviewer, some of the variation among the different foods was undoubtedly contributed by differences among judges, as further analysis below will examine. The ‘‘luck of the draw” in terms of who shows up on any given day in a company lab for the day’s discrimination tests may certainly have some effect on the outcome.”
limitationThe central question the paper set out to inform is left open. — “Whether a simple inspection phase is advantageous remains an open question and may depend on the product tested.”
limitationA result relied on in this literature is reported as untested: an overall difference was claimed by taking three non-significant comparisons together, with no test of that claim reported. — “None of the d-prime values were significantly different when comparing each of the three tests, but ‘‘taken together”, the group with familiarization was reported to have an overall higher d-prime value (no statistical test of that fact was reported, however”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingThe three AFC formats, including the new self-defined 2-AFC, detected the sensory difference more reliably than the triangle test did. — “The AFC tests (including the SD-2-AFC) performed better than the triangle on a statistical basis.”
findingPoorer discrimination in the self-defined 2-AFC went with panellists naming the wrong critical attribute or judging the difference in the reverse direction during the inspection phase. — “Correlates of poorer discrimination with the SD-2-AFC included miss-identification of the critical attribute (as determined by majority response) or reversal of the direction of the difference during the inspection phase.”
findingLetting panellists preview both samples before a triangle or 2-AFC test brought no measurable benefit. — “No advantage of adding a preview to the triangle or 2-AFC was observed.”
findingAlthough statistically more powerful, the self-defined 2-AFC produced smaller Thurstonian d-prime values than the triangle test, i.e. less absolute sensory differentiation. — “Paradoxically, the method showed lower absolute discrimination as measured by Thurstonian scale values.”
findingPanellists who chose the same attribute as the majority reached higher d-prime values than those who chose an atypical attribute, significantly so for the fruit drink. — “For the fruit drink, subjects with ‘‘typical” responses had higher d-prime values than those with atypical responses (p < .01), and in the other product systems the d-primes were directionally higher but not significantly different.”
findingPanellists who reversed the majority direction of the attribute (calling the sweeter sample less sweet) discriminated worse than typical non-inverted panellists in the fruit drink and tea. — “Typical, non-inverted subjects had significantly higher d-prime values that the groups that were inverted, for the fruit drink and the tea (p < .01) and a marginal effect for the orange beverage group (p = 0.0586).”
findingThe attribute a panellist stated during the preview predicted whether that panellist would answer correctly on later trials. — “Taken together, these results indicate that the stated criterion of a person in the SD-AFC was a predictor of future performance.”
findingSelf-reported confidence after a preview barely predicted subsequent discrimination performance. — “Confidence was only a modest predictor of performance in the tests with a preview.”
findingPresentation-order effects appeared only in the orange beverage, where presenting the weaker sample before the stronger one gave more correct 2-AFC answers than the reverse order. — “For all 2-AFC tests, the order W–S was higher than the order S–W.”
