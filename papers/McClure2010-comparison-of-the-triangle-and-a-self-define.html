phenomenonsensory discrimination testing— “The triangle test and the 2-AFC test or paired comparison are commonly used discrimination tests.”
claim-typemethod— “The purpose of this study was to provide a direct comparison of a self-defined 2-AFC test to the non-directional triangle procedure.”
method — evidence— “Over 600 tests were conducted in both simple and complex food and beverage systems.”
transferfrom psychophysics / signal detection theory (Thurstonian scaling): d-prime as the measure of sensory difference, so that food-discrimination tests are scored on a psychophysical sensitivity scale rather than by proportion correct— “However, in some cases the SD-2- AFC produced lower d-prime values indicating lower absolute levels of sensory differentiation.”
mechanismchoosing an irrelevant attribute in the self-defined test adds irrelevant variation, which degrades the panellist's later discrimination— “As shown by the group analysis, choosing a ‘‘wrong” dimension, i.e. attending to some aspect of irrelevant variation, will degrade the subject’s performance.”
mechanismwider discriminal dispersion in some subjects makes rank-order reversals (inversions) of the two products more likely, so their responses oppose the majority— “If the discriminal dispersions are greater for some subjects than others, the possibility of such reversals becomes more likely and thus the choice of a response that is at odds with the ‘‘correct” or majority opinion.”
mechanismthe triangle test requires three implied paired comparisons, whose combined variability lowers its power relative to a directional AFC test— “Part of the difficulty with the triangle procedure arises from the inherent variability of the three implied paired comparisons that must be performed in order to select the ‘‘odd” or outlying (most different) sample.”
builds-on Pfaffmann (1953)— “Such a testing procedure was mentioned by Pfaffmann in 1953 who discussed both the benefits of incidental warm-up in repeated testing and the possibility of using a personally defined attribute in a two-sample choice test.”
extends O'Mahony and Goldstein (1986); O'Mahony et al. (1988) warmed-up paired comparison— “This statement thus foreshadowed the warmed-up paired comparison method of O’Mahony and Goldstein (1986) and O’Mahony et al. (1988) as well as the SD-2-AFC used here.”
contradicts Angulo et al. (2007)— “However, examination of the d-prime values shows that the increase in d-prime was not significant (.52–.98 for N = 9, z = 0.56).”
sensediscrimination - as STATISTICAL SIGNIFICANCE: how reliably a method returns a significant result, on which the SD-AFC beats the triangle— “Together these results support the suitability of the SD-AFC as an alternative to the triangle test in terms of statistical differentiation.”
sensediscrimination - as ABSOLUTE SENSORY DISTANCE: the Thurstonian d-prime, on which the same SD-AFC comes out LOWER than the triangle for the same products— “However, when comparing the d-prime values, a different pattern emerged. For the soup, the SD-AFC had a lower d-prime than the triangle (z = 2.04, p < .05) and for the tea, it had a lower d-prime than the triangle with preview”
sensepreview - a single presentation of both test stimuli before testing, explicitly contrasted with the more extensive warm-up— “A preview sample was defined as a presentation of both test stimuli before beginning the actual testing procedure.”
sensed-prime - stated here as the Thurstonian measure of sensory difference, and used precisely because it is claimed to allow comparison ACROSS methods— “Thurstonian distance estimates (d-prime values) allow direct comparisons across different methods for the degree of sensory difference that is perceived.”
senseinversion - a subject using a relevant attribute but ranking the two products the wrong way round, which the paper distinguishes from choosing an irrelevant attribute— “Another problem arises when the subject attends to a relevant dimension, but has difficulty rank ordering the stimuli correctly, which we have termed here ‘‘inversion” in our data.”
controlsubjects were randomly assigned across the five discrimination procedures for simple stimuli — held — rules out that a difference between procedures is a difference between the subjects who happened to use each— “Subjects testing simple stimuli were randomly assigned to one of the following five procedures: triangle, triangle with preview, 2-AFC, 2-AFC with preview, or self-defined 2-AFC (SD-2-AFC).”

edgepreview samples —renamed-across-fields→ familiarization samples— “Rousseau, Rogeaux, and O’Mahony (1999) showed that preview samples (called familiarization samples) function differently than full warm-up procedures.”
