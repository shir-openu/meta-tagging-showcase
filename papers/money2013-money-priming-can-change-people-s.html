method — evidence— “This article discusses the factors that predict priming effects, and particularly those pertinent to differences between Caruso et al. and Rohrer et al.”
transferfrom economics (into the psychology of money): the paper claims the subject of money has been left to economists and argues that the psychological side of it explains events economics could not— “For decades, economists have had the last (and often the only) word on money, and yet some of the biggest worldwide events of recent history stem from a failure to understand the psychology of money.”
mechanismPriming works by supplying an interpretation for an ambiguous outcome situation, so the more constrained and unambiguous the outcome, the less room the prime has to shift the response and the smaller the effect— “The more specific and less ambiguous the outcome (such as when responding to the word doctor after seeing the word nurse), the smaller the expected priming effect because there is less wiggle room for the prime to alter the response (e.g., Loersch & Payne, 2011).”
mechanismFrequent real-world contact with money keeps the concept chronically accessible, so that even subtle cues suffice to activate it and change behaviour— “When the content of a prime is easily accessible, it can take only subtle cues reminiscent of it to alter outcomes (Higgins & Brendl, 1995).”
mechanismMoney cues set two opposing forces in motion - one routing from money to free markets to endorsement of the systems supporting them, the other routing from money to personal agency and self-sufficiency, which removes the need to endorse those systems - and the two cancel— “Integrating the two lines of reasoning suggests that there might be countervailing forces when people are primed with money and asked to contemplate the righteousness of existing social systems.”
mechanismMoney primes act by activating the phenomenological self as an owner of money, which is why primes tying money to the participant personally produce parallel and stronger effects— “Parallels between general money prime effects and primes that highlight the self owning money suggest that self activation might be an important contributor.”
builds-on Caruso, Vohs, Baxter, and Waytz (2013)— “Thus began our investigation of whether money priming could alter responses related to system justification, social dominance, belief in a just world, and free market ideology.”
contradicts Rohrer, Pashler, and Harris (2015)— “In summary, there are two theoretically derived reasons why Rohrer et al.’s (2015) results were inconsistent with Caruso et al.’s (2013).”
senseA THEORY OF WHEN PRIMING WORKS AT ALL, OFFERED IN REPLY TO A FAILED REPLICATION. `Variations in a prime's MEANING, the EASE with which primed content comes to mind, the prime's MOTIVATIONAL IMPORTANCE, and the AMBIGUITY OF THE OUTCOME SITUATION influence the impact of the prime.` Four moderators, stated in the abstract of a paper whose third sentence is that Rohrer, Pashler and Harris failed to replicate. **The provenance matters as much as the content**: these are conditions named AFTER a null, which is the weakest position from which to name them and also the position in which the field most needs them named— “Variations in a prime’s meaning, the ease with which primed content comes to mind, the prime’s motivational importance, and the ambiguity of the outcome situation influence the impact of the prime”
senseAMBIGUITY OF THE OUTCOME IS OFFERED AS THE REASON COGNITIVE PRIMING EFFECTS ARE SMALLER THAN SOCIAL ONES, WHICH INVERTS THE USUAL READING. `The more specific and LESS AMBIGUOUS the outcome (such as when responding to the word doctor after seeing the word nurse), THE SMALLER the expected priming effect because there is less wiggle room for the prime to alter the response.` The usual account of that same effect-size gap is that social priming effects are inflated. This says they are large because the outcome is loose - a property of the MEASURE, not of the phenomenon - and it makes the gap a prediction rather than an embarrassment. **Both readings fit the same numbers, and this corpus holds papers from both sides of the gap**— “The more specific and less ambiguous the outcome (such as when responding to the word doctor after seeing the word nurse), the smaller the expected priming effect because there is less wiggle room for the prime to alter the response”
senseAND IT IS FALSIFIABLE, WITH THE TEST SUPPLIED: `A situation that does not leave much room for interpretation IS UNLIKELY TO SHOW A CONSISTENT OR LARGE PRIMING EFFECT.` The same outcome task was given two NAMES - Kay et al. (2004) called it either a bland `situation` or a `community game` - and `business priming led to more competitive behaviors than neutral primes when the task was blandly called a situation, THERE WAS NO EFFECT of prime condition when the task was called the community game`. **Ambiguity manipulated by renaming, with a null in the constrained arm.** That is a much stronger form of the claim than a post-hoc moderator, and it is the paper's best evidence— “Whereas business priming led to more competitive behaviors than neutral primes when the task was blandly called a “situation,” there was no effect of prime condition when the task was called the “community game”
