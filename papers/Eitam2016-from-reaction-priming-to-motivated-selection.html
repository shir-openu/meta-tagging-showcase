claim-typemechanism
transferfrom classic cognitive psychology and psycholinguistics: the priming method and the surrounding cluster of concepts (availability, accessibility, activation, applicability) were imported into social psychology, where their nature was altered to fit social-cognitive phenomena — “Although the idea of priming per se originated within classic cognitive psychology and psycholinguistics, its nature and the set of related ideas changed when they were applied to phenomena that interested social psychologists.”
mechanismMotivational relevance gates the activation of semantic knowledge, so a stimulus that is not relevant to truth, control or value fails to make its corresponding knowledge functionally available — “Given that ROAR stipulates that the effect of relevance is on the activation of knowledge, we hypothesized that people were not ‘phenomenally blind’ to the irrelevant percept but rather, could not report what it was”
mechanismFeedback that a shared reality failed retroactively lowers the truth relevance of the tuned message, which lowers its accessibility and hence its influence on recall — “This effect is consistent with the ROAR framework because the later feedback about a failure to create shared reality can still reduce the truth relevance of the message, thereby reducing its accessibility and thus its impact on recall”
relation — “Building on Higgins’ [24] parsing of motivation we also offered three possible sources of motivational relevance; namely, the mind’s concern with ‘truth’ (what is real), ‘control’ (what I can make happen), and ‘value’ or ‘outcome’ (what is desired/ undesired).”
effect directionrelevance → accessibility — increases — “A relevance-based framework for mental accessibility”
replicationuntested
limitationThe paper deliberately restricts itself to how accessibility has been conceptualised and does not assess whether the hypotheses it reviews are empirically supported. — “Please note that we focus here on the changing understanding of accessibility that the above hypotheses reflect, which is a separate issue from their empirical verification.”
limitationThe authors characterise their own mechanistic model of accessibility as crude. — “The above examples show that even a crude mechanistic model of mental accessibly that interacts with the motivational relevance of stimuli (from truth, control, or value motivation) can be a promising framework for both predicting and explaining social-cognitive phenomena, as well as cognitive phenomena more generally.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingMerely designating one of two locations as task-relevant produces a blindness to information at the other location, as ROAR predicts. — “Consistent with the ROAR prediction, a series of experiments [37] showed that merely instructing people that one of two locations is task relevant (e.g. the outer of two differently colored concentric circles) led to ‘blindness’ to the information appearing in the second location (task location).”
findingParticipants who cannot report irrelevant Kanizsa inducers nonetheless see the illusory shape, showing that relevance-based selection acts on knowledge activation rather than on phenomenal experience. — “This prediction was confirmed in a study in which participants that were ostensibly ‘blind’ (in the sense of non-accessibility) to the inducers of a Kantiza illusion (see Figure 2) nonetheless displayed the illusion by ‘successfully’ judging the orientation of the (relevant) illusory shape”
findingThe shared-reality memory bias is abolished by post-encoding feedback that the shared reality failed, even though encoding conditions were untouched. — “the biased memory effect disappears when participants are notified just before recall that their reality was not in fact shared by the audience”
findingAudience tuning alone does not distort recall — the memory bias requires a shared-reality goal, not politeness or ingratiation, despite equal tuning. — “It does not occur when participants merely tune to be polite or ingratiate their audience [29], despite the degree of audience tuning being at least as great in these cases.”
quantityup to 25% — share of participants who could not report the colour of the task-irrelevant inner circle — “For example, up to 25% percent of the participants could not report the color of the (irrelevant) inner circle that was presented for 500 ms without any masking”
quantity500 ms — unmasked presentation duration of the irrelevant concentric circle that participants still failed to report — “A first demonstration of ‘irrelevance induced blindness’ up to 25% of the participants could not report the color of the single irrelevant concentric circle they had just seen for 500 ms.”
definitionROAR (Relevance of Accessible Representations) — “About six years ago, we outlined a framework that we called ‘ROAR’ for Relevance of Accessible Representations”
definitionaccessibility (as functional availability) — “The proposed solution was rather simple: knowledge becomes accessible, which we defined as being ‘functionally available’ to cognitive processes, to the degree that it is motivationally relevant.”
definitionapplicability — “Applicability was the match between a (select) set of the stimulus’ and the activated concepts’ features”
definitionsaying-is-believing effect — “The ‘saying-is-believing’ phenomenon was discovered by Higgins and Rholes in 1978 and has been recently systematically studied by Higgins, Echterhoff and their colleagues”
definitionirrelevance induced blindness — “A first demonstration of ‘irrelevance induced blindness’ up to 25% of the participants could not report the color of the single irrelevant concentric circle they had just seen for 500 ms.”
definitionthree sources of motivational relevance (truth, control, value) — “Building on Higgins’ [24] parsing of motivation we also offered three possible sources of motivational relevance; namely, the mind’s concern with ‘truth’ (what is real), ‘control’ (what I can make happen), and ‘value’ or ‘outcome’ (what is desired/ undesired).”
