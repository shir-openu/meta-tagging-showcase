mechanismOUTPUT CANNOT EXCEED INPUT: a conclusion inherits the reliability and the non-neutrality of the data it was computed from, so an algorithm cannot repair evidence that was misguided before it arrived— “the informal ‘garbage in, garbage out’ principle clearly illustrates what is at stake here, namely that conclusions can only be as reliable (but also as neutral) as the data they are based on.”
mechanismA LEARNING ALGORITHM REWRITES ITS OWN DECISION STRUCTURE WHILE RUNNING, so the rationale for any particular classification is not fixed at design time and cannot be read off the program— “The algorithm modifies its behavioural structure during operation ( Markowetz et al., 2014). This alteration of how the algorithm classifies new inputs is how it learns”
mechanismONCE TRAINED, CLASSIFICATION PROCEEDS WITHOUT AN OPERATOR - the structure produced by training is what new inputs meet, which is why oversight has no natural point of entry— “Training produces a structure (e.g. classes, clusters, ranks, weights, etc.) to classify new inputs or predict unknown variables. Once trained, new data can be processed and categorised automatically without operator intervention”
mechanismPOPULATION KNOWLEDGE APPLIED TO AN INDIVIDUAL: correlations hold of groups while the action lands on a person, and even population-level action spills over - an insurance premium set for a sub-population is paid by each member— “even if both actions and knowledge are at the population-level, our actions may spill over into the individual level. For example, this happens when an insurance premium is set for a sub-population, and hence has to be paid by each member.”
mechanismVALUES ENTER THROUGH PARAMETERS: developers specify and users configure operational parameters with desired outcomes in mind, so the algorithm privileges some interests before it processes anything— “Operational parameters are specified by developers and configured by users with desired outcomes in mind that privilege some values and interests over others”
mechanismSECRECY IS COMMERCIALLY MOTIVATED, NOT INCIDENTAL: disclosure would enable gaming and threaten viability, so inaccessibility is a position taken in a conflict of interests rather than a technical limit— “This struggle is marked by information asymmetry and an “imbalance in knowledge and decision-making power” favouring data processors”
mechanismCORRELATIONS ARE ACTED ON WITHOUT CAUSAL KNOWLEDGE because a sufficient volume of data is treated as credible enough to drive action - the paper's name for that lowered bar is `actionable insights`— “In this sense data mining and profiling algorithms often need only establish a sufficiently reliable evidence base to drive action, referred to here as actionable insights.”
critiques the treatment of distinct epistemic and normative concerns as a single cluster, so that fixing one is taken to address the rest— “the map demonstrates that solving problems at one level does not address all types of concerns; a perfectly auditable algorithmic decision, or one that is based on conclusive, scrutable and well-founded evidence, can nevertheless cause unfair and transformative effects, without obvious ways to trace blame among the network of contributing…
critiques the assumption that better evidence reduces discrimination - the paper holds that it can be used to discriminate more efficiently— “Better methods to produce evidence for some actions need not rule out all forms of discrimination for example, and can even be used to discriminate more efficiently.”
predicts the expectation that increasingly better algorithmic tools will eliminate the ethical problems of algorithms— “we cannot in principle avoid epistemic and ethical residues.”
critiques the naive treatment of transparency as a panacea for ethical issues arising from new technologies— “transparency is often naïvely treated as a panacea for ethical issues arising from new technologies.”
qualifies the assumption that a more discerning algorithm is the ethically preferable one— “one may even conceive of situations where less discerning algorithms may have fewer objectionable effects.”
sensealgorithm - THE PAPER HOLDS TWO SENSES AT ONCE AND SAYS SO. The formal sense is Hill's mathematical construct; the sense it actually maps is the public-discourse one, which includes the implementation into a technology and the configuration of that technology to a task. Its own point is that a map of the ethics cannot use the formal sense alone— “Lay usage of ‘algorithm’ also includes _implementation_ of the mathematical construct into a technology, and an application of the technology _configured_ for a particular task.”
sensealgorithm - AND THE CONFLATION IS NAMED AS THE OBSTACLE, not glossed over: the same word carries a mathematical construct and 'any procedure or decision process, however ill-defined', and the paper says the mapping exercise must address that before it can begin— “Any attempt to map an ‘ethics of algorithms’ must address this conflation between formal definitions and popular usage of ‘algorithm’.”
sensetransparency - NOT ONE PROPERTY BUT TWO CONDITIONS THAT COME APART: information can be accessible and still not comprehensible, which is exactly the case machine learning creates— “The primary components of transparency are _accessibility_ and _comprehensibility_ of information.”
sensefairness - EXPLICITLY OBSERVER-DEPENDENT HERE, a generic label for whatever ethical criteria an observer brings to the action rather than a standard the paper supplies— “ethical evaluation of algorithms can also focus solely on the _action_ itself. Actions driven by algorithms can be assessed according to numerous ethical criteria and principles, which we generically refer to here as the observer-dependent ‘fairness’ of the action and its effects.”
senseinconclusive evidence - THE PAPER'S OWN CATEGORY, defined as the uncertainty that is inherent rather than accidental: inferential and machine-learning methods yield knowledge that is probable and inevitably uncertain, so the deficiency cannot be engineered away— “When algorithms draw conclusions from the data they process using inferential statistics and/or machine learning techniques, they produce probable6 yet inevitably uncertain knowledge.”
senseinscrutable evidence - defined by a REASONABLE EXPECTATION THAT IS NOT MET: the link between data and conclusion ought to be intelligible and open to scrutiny, and given how algorithms operate that is not automatically satisfied— “When data are used as (or processed to produce) evidence for a conclusion, it is reasonable to expect that the connection between the data and the conclusion should be accessible (i.e. intelligible as well as open to scrutiny and perhaps even critique).”
sensemisguided evidence - defined from a limit shared by ALL data processing rather than anything specific to algorithms: the output can never exceed the input— “Algorithms process data and are therefore subject to a limitation shared by all types of data-processing, namely that the output can never exceed the input.”
sensetransformative effects - the category for harms with NO IDENTIFIABLE VICTIM AND NO OBVIOUS FAILURE: effects that look ethically neutral because nothing was broken, yet change how the world is conceptualised and organised— “algorithms can affect how we conceptualise the world, and modify its social and political organisation”
controlthe paper marks its own normative judgements as observer-dependent rather than claiming a standard: whether evidence counts as misguided, and whether an outcome counts as unfair, are both said to depend on who is evaluating — held— “Evaluations of the neutrality of the process, and by connection whether the evidence produced is misguided, are of course observer-dependent.” — rules out a reader taking the map's normative labels as the authors' own ethical standard - `misguided` and `unfair` are marked observer-dependent, so the framework cannot be read as smuggling in a particular ethics— “Evaluations of the neutrality of the process, and by connection whether the evidence produced is misguided, are of course observer-dependent.”
controlthe map is declared not to derive from any particular ethical theory, which is what lets readers who disagree about ethics still use it — held— “The map is not proposed from a particular theoretical or methodological approach to ethics, but rather is intended as a prescriptive framework of types of issues arising from algorithms owing to three aspects of how algorithms operate.” — rules out the objection that the map only works for readers who already share the authors' ethical theory - it is declared to derive from none— “The map is not proposed from a particular theoretical or methodological approach to ethics, but rather is intended as a prescriptive framework of types of issues arising from algorithms owing to three aspects of how algorithms operate.”

limitationthe authors accept there may be gaps in coverage for topics discussed only in relation to specific algorithm types rather than to algorithms as such— “We must therefore accept that there may be gaps in coverage for topics discussed only in relation to specific types of algorithms, and not for algorithms themselves.”
limitationsummarising every field in which algorithms are used is stated to be beyond reach, and the narrowing is justified on that ground— “While it would be ideal to summarise work in all the fields represented in the reviewed literature, and thus in any domain where algorithms are in use, the scope of such as an exercise is prohibitive.”
limitationdiscussion of 'algorithm' runs into a level-of-abstraction problem: without fixing the level, parties talk past each other and the set of relevant observables is never bounded
limitationthe authors state that a mature ethics of algorithms does not yet exist, and attribute that partly to the breadth of the concept itself
limitationconfiguration is excluded from changing the object: tuning an algorithm to a task is said not to alter its mathematical representation or its implementation, which bounds what the map is about
limitationalgorithms that automate mundane tasks are excluded by declaration rather than by a criterion, and the class of interest is given without a precise definition
