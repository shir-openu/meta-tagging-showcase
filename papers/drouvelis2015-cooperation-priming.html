phenomenoncooperative priming in a public goods game— “In our paper, we focus on cooperative priming in a standard linear public goods game in which randomly selected participants make decisions in situations where personal and group interests are misaligned.”
claim-typeexistence— “Our findings indicate that cooperative priming increases contributions in a one-shot public goods game from approximately 25–36 % compared with the non-primed group.”
method — evidence— “The priming manipulation was carried out through an initial word-search puzzle that each participant self-completed at the beginning of each experimental session (e.g. Bargh et al. 2001).”
mechanismTHE PRIME IS ARGUED TO WORK THROUGH PREFERENCES BECAUSE IT DEMONSTRABLY DID NOT WORK THROUGH BELIEFS - a null result carrying the mechanism claim: priming does not affect subjects' beliefs about other group members' contributions, **INDICATING THAT PRIMING PRIMARILY SEEMS TO WORK BY CHANGING SUBJECTS' PREFERENCES ABOUT HOW MUCH TO CONTRIBUTE**. The absent effect is the evidence, not a caveat about it— “Our results also suggest that priming does not affect subjects’ beliefs about other group members’ contributions, ceteris paribus, indicating that priming primarily seems to work by changing subjects’ preferences about how much to contribute.”
mechanismA ONE-SHOT GAME IS CHOSEN TO BUY A CLEAN CAUSE RATHER THAN A REALISTIC SETTING: the reason for choosing a one-shot interaction over repeated interactions is that we wanted to investigate **THE PURE EFFECTS OF OUR PRIMING PROCESS**, without these being confounded with **STRATEGIC CONSIDERATIONS THAT MAY ARISE FROM REPEATED INTERACTION**. The design gives up the question of persistence to isolate the question of existence, and says so— “The reason for choosing a one-shot interaction over repeated interactions is that we wanted to investigate the pure effects of our priming process on subjects’ cooperative behaviour, without these being confounded with strategic considerations that may arise from repeated interaction.”
mechanismEXPOSURE TO THE PRIME IS GUARANTEED RATHER THAN ASSUMED, by handing out the puzzle's answers: at the end of the allotted time, the participants received the solutions to the word-search puzzle **TO ENSURE THAT EVERYONE IN THE TREATMENT GROUP HAD BEEN PRIMED** with words associated with cooperation. A subject who found none of the fifteen words still meets them— “At the end of the allotted time, the participants received the solutions to the word-search puzzle to ensure that everyone in the treatment group had been primed with words associated with cooperation.”
mechanismTHE DEMAND-EFFECT OBJECTION IS ANSWERED BY ASKING THE SUBJECTS AND THEN CONTROLLING FOR THEIR ANSWER: **NONE of the subjects in the non-priming treatment and 16 OUT OF 75 in the priming treatment** said the first task had affected the second, and with a dummy for those subjects the priming coefficient stays significant for contributions and insignificant for beliefs. The control is reported with its own numbers rather than asserted— “None of the subjects in the non-priming treatment and 16 out of 75 subjects in the priming treatment answered this question positively. When we include a dummy variable controlling for those who reported that the priming task affected their subsequent behaviour, the estimated coefficient on the variable “Priming” remains statistically…

sensepriming - taken by these authors from psychology in a FORMAL definition and then used in economics: previously activated information impacting the processing of subsequent information. The definition is procedural, which is why it can be transplanted across fields— “It is formally defined as “the procedural feature that some previously activated information impacts on the processing of subsequent information”
sensepriming, as distinguished from framing and anchoring - the distinction is drawn on WHAT IS LEFT UNTOUCHED: priming reinforces a pathway to a stimulus without re-describing the decision problem, whereas framing re-describes an equivalent problem and anchoring redirects attention— “Priming, on the other hand, involves exposing a subject’s mind to a stimulus, concept, or memory—without requiring the re-description of the decision problem—so that his or her pathways to that particular stimulus, concept, or memory are reinforced and would also later be reflected on the processing of subsequent information (Kolb and Wishaw…
sensecooperative priming, as delivered here - fifteen of the twenty hidden words carry the concept and five are neutral in both conditions, so the two treatments differ only in the fifteen. The construct is a WORD LIST, chosen by pre-test rating— “Each list contained the same set of five neutral words to be found (carpet, lamp, plant, shampoo, window), and the remaining 15 words being either associated with the concept of cooperation or completely neutral, depending on the treatment.”
sensebeliefs about others’ contributions - measured, unincentivised, and elicited AFTER the contribution decision, which the authors say makes them endogenous to the thing they are meant to explain
