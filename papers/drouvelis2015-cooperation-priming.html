phenomenoncooperative priming in a public goods game
claim-typeexistence
mechanismTHE PRIME IS ARGUED TO WORK THROUGH PREFERENCES BECAUSE IT DEMONSTRABLY DID NOT WORK THROUGH BELIEFS - a null result carrying the mechanism claim: priming does not affect subjects' beliefs about other group members' contributions, **INDICATING THAT PRIMING PRIMARILY SEEMS TO WORK BY CHANGING SUBJECTS' PREFERENCES ABOUT HOW MUCH TO CONTRIBUTE**. The absent effect is the evidence, not a caveat about it — “Our results also suggest that priming does not affect subjects’ beliefs about other group members’ contributions, ceteris paribus, indicating that priming primarily seems to work by changing subjects’ preferences about how much to contribute.”
mechanismA ONE-SHOT GAME IS CHOSEN TO BUY A CLEAN CAUSE RATHER THAN A REALISTIC SETTING: the reason for choosing a one-shot interaction over repeated interactions is that we wanted to investigate **THE PURE EFFECTS OF OUR PRIMING PROCESS**, without these being confounded with **STRATEGIC CONSIDERATIONS THAT MAY ARISE FROM REPEATED INTERACTION**. The design gives up the question of persistence to isolate the question of existence, and says so — “The reason for choosing a one-shot interaction over repeated interactions is that we wanted to investigate the pure effects of our priming process on subjects’ cooperative behaviour, without these being confounded with strategic considerations that may arise from repeated interaction.”
mechanismEXPOSURE TO THE PRIME IS GUARANTEED RATHER THAN ASSUMED, by handing out the puzzle's answers: at the end of the allotted time, the participants received the solutions to the word-search puzzle **TO ENSURE THAT EVERYONE IN THE TREATMENT GROUP HAD BEEN PRIMED** with words associated with cooperation. A subject who found none of the fifteen words still meets them — “At the end of the allotted time, the participants received the solutions to the word-search puzzle to ensure that everyone in the treatment group had been primed with words associated with cooperation.”
mechanismTHE DEMAND-EFFECT OBJECTION IS ANSWERED BY ASKING THE SUBJECTS AND THEN CONTROLLING FOR THEIR ANSWER: **NONE of the subjects in the non-priming treatment and 16 OUT OF 75 in the priming treatment** said the first task had affected the second, and with a dummy for those subjects the priming coefficient stays significant for contributions and insignificant for beliefs. The control is reported with its own numbers rather than asserted — “None of the subjects in the non-priming treatment and 16 out of 75 subjects in the priming treatment answered this question positively. When we include a dummy variable controlling for those who reported that the priming task affected their subsequent behaviour, the estimated coefficient on the variable “Priming” remains statistically significant for the regressions of contributions (Models 1, 2 and 3) and statistically insignificant for the regressions of beliefs about others’ contributions (Models 4, 5 and 6).”
relation — “By contrast, we explore the effect of a significantly more subtle intervention on the incentive to free ride in social dilemmas.”

differs inpriming → framing — WHETHER THE DECISION PROBLEM IS RE-DESCRIBED: framing restates a logically equivalent problem in a different light; priming leaves the problem alone and reinforces a pathway to a concept — “the re-description of a logically equivalent decision problem”
differs inpriming → anchoring — WHAT IS REDIRECTED: anchoring fixes the decision-maker's attention on a single trait or piece of information, leaving the problem and the concepts alone; priming reinforces a pathway to a concept without directing attention to it — “eliciting participants to focus on one trait or piece of information”
effect directioncooperative priming → contributions — increases — “cooperative priming increases contributions”

effect directionpriming → beliefs — no-change — “priming does not affect subjects”
replicationuntested
limitationOnly a one-shot game was run, so nothing can be said about whether the priming effect persists over repeated decisions. — “We have focused only on a one-shot public goods game, so we can say nothing about the long-term impacts of priming on decisions that people will need to make repeatedly over time.”
limitationThe result does not generalise beyond the public goods game to other economically relevant behaviours. — “Moreover, we are not able to address the issue of whether priming may affect economically relevant behaviours other than those observed in the public goods game.”
limitationA single priming method (word-and-association semantic priming) was used, so the effect of other priming modes, including negative priming, is unknown. — “Another caveat is that only one method of priming—i.e. words and associations—was used in our experiment.”
limitationBeliefs were elicited after contribution decisions, making the belief measure endogenous to the behaviour it was meant to explain. — “However, recall that we elicited beliefs after subjects had made their contribution decisions, and thus, beliefs are endogenous to contributions.”
limitationThe priming manipulation is open to an experimenter demand interpretation, which the authors could only address post hoc with a questionnaire control. — “It is important to note that our priming intervention can be criticized as being potentially an experimenter demand effect (Zizzo 2010).”
