phenomenonsubliminal affective priming from facial expressions— “Is this subliminal affective priming merely a transient phenomenon manifested in fleeting perceptual changes, or are long-lasting effects also induced?”
claim-typeexistence— “We conclude that behavioral biases induced by masked emotional expressions are not ephemeral, but rather can last at least 24 hours.”
method — evidence— “To address this question, we investigated memory for surprise faces 24 hours after they had been shown with 30-ms fearful, happy, or neutral faces.”
mechanismUnconsciously processed happy primes are proposed to shift attention toward the global configuration of the following face, which improves encoding and hence next-day recognition.— “In line with this reasoning, we speculate that happy primes may have led to better encoding and subsequent memory by preferentially engaging an attention focus on the global configuration of facial features, consistent with findings that happy mood can increase the use of global features to classify visual figures ( Bridge et al., 2009…
mechanismThe prime's expression is proposed to alter encoding and/or later rehearsal of the target face, rather than the memory difference arising from the conscious affective judgment the participant made about that face.— “Therefore, we suggest that the observed differences in memory were due to subliminal priming that led to differential processing at encoding and/or subsequent rehearsal as a function of facial expression.”
mechanismAffect elicited by a masked stimulus is assumed to be diffuse and therefore to spill over onto whatever stimulus follows it in time, producing assimilation of prime valence into the target judgment.— “Presumably, the affect from a subliminal stimulus is diffuse in the sense that it can spill over onto a temporally adjacent stimulus.”
replicates Li, Zinbarg, Boehm, & Paller (2008)— “In particular, our findings replicate and extend the findings of Li and colleagues (2008) , though several design features differed between these two studies.”

senseA TACIT ASSUMPTION NAMED AND TESTED - that subliminal affective priming is `a transient phenomenon manifested only in fleeting perceptual changes`. The paper does not dispute that the effect exists; it disputes how long it is entitled to last, and it says the short-lived reading was never argued for, only assumed. Second record this sitting whose target is a belief its field holds without evidence— “Although subliminal affective priming in these cases may be regarded as a transient phenomenon manifested only in fleeting perceptual changes, here we question the tacit assumption that such phenomena are short-lived. Might unconscious affective processing also have long-lasting effects”
sense`long-lasting` OPERATIONALISED AS AN INTERVAL, not as a rhetorical claim: memory for the primed faces is tested 24 HOURS LATER, with the 30-ms primes absent at test and the target faces intermixed with new ones. The conclusion is stated as a floor - biases `are not ephemeral, but rather CAN LAST AT LEAST 24 HOURS` - which is the honest form for a study that measured one interval— “We conclude that behavioral biases induced by masked emotional expressions are not ephemeral, but rather can last at least 24 hours. Introduction The influence of affective information on behavior is notable”
senseA TARGET CHOSEN FOR ITS AMBIGUITY, with the reasoning written out: surprise `derives its affective valence primarily through CONTEXT` - the same expression reads positive at a surprise birthday party and negative in a horror film. Because the target has no fixed value on the measured dimension, any value it takes can be attributed to the prime. The stimulus choice is doing the work a control usually does— “Emotionally ambiguous facial expressions, such as surprise, derive their affective valence primarily through context. The effect belongs to the unaware group and vanishes in the aware one - whereas Van den Bussche, holding signal strength constant, found conscious trials strongest. Two records, opposite orderings, and the designs differ in exactly the way that matters— “Participants likely to have processed primes supraliminally did not respond differentially as a function of expression.
