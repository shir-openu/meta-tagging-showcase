transferfrom psycholinguistics / speech-production research: cascade models of language production are imported wholesale as a model for how parallel primed responses become serial social behaviour — “Accordingly, recent work in our lab has begun to apply cascade models of language production (e.g., Dell, 1986 ; Morsella & Miozzo, 2002 ; Navarette & Costa, 2004 ) to these next-generation questions concerning social behavior production ( Morsella, Levine, & Bargh, 2005 ).”
transferfrom cognitive linguistics and the study of language use: Lakoff and Johnson's conceptual-metaphor theory, Clark's analysis of conversational language use, and Dell's speech-production model are proposed as the solution to social psychology's own two problems — “It is suggested that models of complex conceptual structures ( Lakoff & Johnson, 1980 ), language use in real-life conversational settings ( Clark, 1996 ), and speech production ( Dell, 1986 ) might hold the key for solving these two important ‘second-generation’ research problems.”
transferfrom language-comprehension research: the paper traces the concept of priming itself as an import into social cognition from Lashley's analysis of language — “The notion of priming originated in Karl Lashley’s (1951) analysis of language comprehension; how we need to hold some sentential information temporarily in mind in order to comprehend it.”
mechanismAn active goal raises the accessibility of goal-relevant representations, so attention selects only a fraction of the environment and only those selected stimuli can prime a response. — “An active goal (e.g., to find something to eat) caused the mental representations relevant to attaining the goal (e.g., restaurants, bodegas) to become somewhat more active than usual and thus more ready to become activated by corresponding stimuli in the environment.”
mechanismActivation cascades from the conceptual-semantic level onto the action-planning level, so perceiving an object automatically brings up the action plans associated with it. — “A cascade cognitive architecture suggests that the semantic activation arising from perceiving an object automatically leads to the activation of the various action plans with which the object is associated.”
mechanismInfants internalise both sides of routine interactions with caregivers, which is how complex role/perspective structures — the things later primed — come to be represented at all. — “According to this view, the experience of the infant and young child with its caregivers leads to the development of internalized roles or perspectives , such that participation with the care-taker in events such as taking a bath, or playing a new game, involves—indeed, requires—the ability to know both complementary roles”
mechanismLater, abstract concepts are scaffolded metaphorically onto earlier directly-experienced structures such as spatial orientation, so priming the earlier structure carries into the later one. — “Other conceptual structures are then built or scaffolded onto the direct structures in a metaphorical fashion (e.g., ‘I’m feeling up today’).”
relation — “It is suggested that models of complex conceptual structures ( Lakoff & Johnson, 1980 ), language use in real-life conversational settings ( Clark, 1996 ), and speech production ( Dell, 1986 ) might hold the key for solving these two important ‘second-generation’ research problems.”
relation — “The metaphor model also makes directional predictions that temporal-construal or construal-level theory does not (physical distance should prime psychological distance effects—e.g., time—but psychological distance priming should not affect physical distance judgments; see Boroditsky, 2000 ), so it should be possible to empirically assess these competing accounts.”
relation — “Finally, it should also be noted that Boroditsky’s (2000) findings can be seen as well as support for the idea that fundamental social perspectives or ‘deep ideologies’ as described by Tetlock (2003) do exist, as complex conceptual structures, and can be primed—that is, activated and put into operation nonconsciously by environmental stimuli.”
differs inconceptual structure → single concept — WHETHER ONE ACTIVATION CAN REACH SEVERAL PSYCHOLOGICAL SYSTEMS AT ONCE - a single concept cannot do the work required of it, a conceptual structure of the kind Lakoff & Johnson described can. The replacement is made on grounds of explanatory capacity, not on evidence that the structure was activated — “It is hard to see how a single concept”
differs ingeneration problem → reduction problem — DIRECTION - generation is one prime producing many simultaneous effects; reduction is many simultaneous effects having to become one action in real time. The same bottleneck seen from either side — “has to happen serially, in real time”
replicationuntested
limitationThe dependence of real-world goal pursuit on other people's cooperation is named as the largest threat to the generality of the laboratory findings. — “This seems to me to be the biggest challenge to the generality of our findings to the non-laboratory social world—and certainly a constraint on any complete theory of nonconscious social phenomena.”
limitationThe field's empirical demonstrations have outrun its ability to say what is being primed or how the effects are produced. — “To some, however, these impressive empirical demonstrations have become an embarrassment of riches; our empirical knowledge has outstripped our ability to understand and conceptualize just what is going on here—what exactly is being primed, and how are these impressive effects produced?”
limitationThe proposed role of selective attention in filtering potential primes is stated as expectation rather than established result. — “Although research in this area is just starting, one would expect that selective attention would serve to reduce the number of potential priming influences at any given moment, with priming influences more likely to occur among selected than nonselected information.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingThe paper's central claim is that priming manipulations have never been activating single concepts but whole conceptual structures — metaphors, roles, perspectives or mindsets. — “Thus, several disparate areas of research and social thought lead us to the same conclusion: that one reason for the multiple parallel effects of our priming manipulations is that we might not be priming single concepts, but rather conceptual structures, whether they be called metaphors, roles, perspectives, or mindsets.”
findingBecause a single prime produces its perceptual, evaluative, motivational and behavioural effects in parallel, which effect a study 'finds' is an artefact of the dependent variable the experimenter chose to measure. — “In other words, ‘the’ effect of the prime in these various types of studies just depends on which dependent variable the experimenter happens to be interested in.”
findingThe role/persona account is offered as the answer to what priming studies have actually been activating: not discrete acts but a whole perspective with its goals and values. — “Perhaps, then, what we have been priming all these years is a role, a conceptual structure that contains not only the nuts and bolts of how to act within that persona, but, at essence, the perspective a person in that role would have on the world—the purposes and goals and values that person, or animal, or even steel rod, would have.”
findingGoal-driven selective attention is proposed as the first stage of the solution to the reduction problem, filtering which of the many available environmental primes can act at all. — “One’s current goal, then, as it helps drive selective attentional processes, takes a big chunk out of the reduction problem.”
findingWhen primed responses conflict, the person's currently active goal or motivational state is said to prevail over the other activated tendencies. — “The general principle seems to be that one’s current goal or motivational state will ‘win out’ over other potential influences when they are in conflict.”
findingThe paper proposes that language production and social behaviour production may run on the very same underlying mechanism, so speech-production models should explain how parallel primes become serial action. — “Given this stellar track record it might be the case that the underlying mechanisms of language production and of social behavior production are one and the same”
quantity71% — proportion of participants in Boroditsky's (2000) study, as reported here, who interpreted ambiguous time information consistently with a preceding spatial prime — “The space primes strongly influenced participants’ interpretation of the ambiguous time information: 71% of the participants responded in a prime-consistent manner.”
quantity64% / 47% — the asymmetry the paper reports from Boroditsky's replication: space primes shifted thinking about time, but time primes did not shift thinking about space — “This effect was replicated in a second experiment: participants were influenced by spatial primes when thinking about time (64% consistent), but (as hypothesized) the reverse effect did not occur: participants were not influenced by temporal primes when subsequently thinking about space (47% consistent).”
quantity15% — the share of people the paper states are susceptible to post-hypnotic suggestion, used to argue priming is not simply hypnosis — “priming effects in social psychology experiments are too easy to obtain with just random samples of university students, whereas only 15% or so of us are capable of post-hypnotic suggestion ( Kihlstrom, 1998 )”
definitionpriming (nonconscious activation of social knowledge structures) — “Nearly all forms of social representation can be primed, it seems—activated incidentally or unobtrusively in one context, to influence what comes next without the person’s awareness of this influence.”
definitionthe generation problem — “But we may have reached childhood’s end, so to speak, and need now to move on to research questions such as how these multiple effects of single primes occur (the generation problem); next, how these multiple simultaneous priming influences in the environment get distilled into nonconscious social action that has to happen serially, in real time (the reduction problem).”
definitionthe reduction problem — “Understanding how many kinds of psychological effects can be produced by a single priming event is only half of the needed story, however—we must also seek to understand better how these multiple possibilities get reduced and channeled back into single, serial responses.”
definitionthe construal alternative — “the skeptical line moved again, to a position of ‘just social perception;’ that is, what might be called the construal alternative (see Dijksterhuis & Bargh, 2001 ), in which priming effects on behavior and goal pursuit were viewed as mediated by a change in the construal or interpretation of the experimental situation, with behavior and goal selection flowing naturally (and consciously) from that interpretation.”
definitionthe product tradition — “In the product tradition, concepts are studied for what they contain, how they define the external objects, events, and qualities they stand for.”
definitionthe action tradition — “By contrast, the action tradition has been concerned with the functions and purposes of language, how people use it to get things done in their daily lives.”
definitionexperiential gestalts — “They argue for the existence of experiential gestalts , or complex conceptual structures that ‘emerge from interaction, from constant negotiation with the environment and other people”
