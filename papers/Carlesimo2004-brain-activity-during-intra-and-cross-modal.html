phenomenonrepetition priming
claim-typeexistence
mechanismThe left inferior temporal/fusiform region mediates interaction between phonological and orthographic word codes, and this shared abstract code is what carries facilitation across modalities — “In particular, it suggests that this cortical region might be involved in the complex interactions between the phonological and orthographic codes of words which ultimately allow for the cross-modality facilitation in verbal stimulus re-processing.”
mechanismRight prefrontal increases arise from post-retrieval awareness that the generated word was recently encountered (involuntary conscious memory), not from deliberate episodic search driving the completions — “The substantially low level of the completion rate and the lack of an increase in priming magnitude across successive study-test blocks suggest that the contribution of such mechanisms is more in eliciting post-retrieval awareness of a recent encounter with the generated word than in actual support for active retrieval.”
mechanismLeft prefrontal increases reflect more efficient access to output lexical representations of recently encountered words rather than generic word-fluency demand — “Rather, rCBF changes in this region should be considered as the expression of more efficient access to output representations of recently encountered words prompted by either phonological or semantic cues.”
relation — “This is in contrast with previous reports in the literature which showed rCBF decreases in the lateral occipital cortex (BA 19) during auditory stem completion priming (Badgaiyan et al., 1999; Badgaiyan et al., 2001).”
relation — “Previous behavioural studies consistently reported significantly larger within-modality than cross-modality stem completion priming (e.g. Carlesimo, Marfia, Loasses, & Caltagirone, 1996; Graf et al., 1985).”
relation — “However, the conclusion that cross-modality priming is more exposed to contamination by explicit retrieval strategies than within-modality priming is not supported by neuropsychological evidence.”
relation — “Support for the view that regions in the left inferior temporal cortex can modulate their neural activity in response to verbal stimuli independent of stimulus-driven perceptual processes comes from an fMRI study by Buckner et al. (2000).”
replicationuntested
limitationThe cross-modality parietal and temporal activations cannot be attributed a specific role because the interaction contrasts were not significant. — “Also in this case, however, the lack of significance of the interaction contrasts suggests caution in attributing a peculiar role of this cluster of activations in the genesis of cross-modality priming.”
limitationThe left prefrontal activation specific to within-modality priming is only suggestive, again because the interaction failed. — “For this reason, the peculiarity of left prefrontal activation in the within-modality priming can be regarded as only suggestive.”
limitationThe comparability of the two priming effects may be an artefact of a chance difference in the auditory baseline completion rate. — “However, it should be noted that the stem completion rate with target words during auditory baseline scans (3.9) was (likely by chance) quite a bit higher than during visual baseline scans (3.2; P = 0.10).”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingAuditory-to-visual cross-modality priming produced a significantly larger blood-flow decrease at the left inferior temporal/fusiform boundary than within-modality auditory priming. — “Compared to the auditory-to-auditory priming condition, cross-modality priming was associated with a significantly larger regional cerebral blood flow (rCBF) decrease at the boundary between left inferior temporal and fusiform gyri, brain regions previously associated with modality independent lexical retrieval and reading.”
findingThe interaction analysis confirmed the deactivation difference was specific to the cross-modality condition in left BA 37/20. — “Conversely, there was an area of significantly larger rCBF decrease in the cross-modality condition at the junction between the left fusiform and inferior temporal gyrus (BA 37/20).”
findingWithin-modality auditory priming was accompanied by bilateral prefrontal blood-flow increases rather than posterior deactivations. — “Comparison of priming and baseline scans in the withinmodality auditory condition show a pattern of bilateral prefrontal rCBF increases in the left inferior frontal gyrus (BA 46) and in the right superior (BA 9) and middle (BA 10) frontal gyri.”
findingRight middle frontal activation was significantly greater for within-modality than for cross-modality priming, the reverse of what earlier studies had reported. — “The analysis showed a significantly greater activation in the right middle frontal gyrus (BA 9) in the within-modality than in the cross-modality condition.”
findingBehaviourally the within- and cross-modality priming effects did not differ in size, contrary to the usual finding of a within-modality advantage. — “The condition and modality effects did not interact significantly (F = 0.4; P = n.s. with d.f. = 1, 7), thus revealing comparably sized priming effects in the withinand cross-modality conditions (4.4 and 3.9).”
findingThe magnitude of the left inferior temporal deactivation tracked each subject's behavioural cross-modality priming, tying the imaging effect to behaviour. — “The rate of rCBF decrease in this area showed a significant negative correlation with the amount of cross-modality priming, as measured behaviourally (x = −50; y = −48; z = −20; Z = 2.0; P = 0.02) (Fig. 1).”
findingNo blood-flow decreases reached the conventional threshold for within-modality auditory priming, unlike previous auditory stem-completion PET reports. — “There were no significant rCBF decreases related to within-modality priming using this threshold.”
findingPriming magnitude did not grow across successive study-test blocks, arguing against subjects adopting explicit retrieval strategies. — “The Block effect was not significant (F = 0.7; P = n.s. with d.f. = 2, 14) and did not interact reliably with any of the other factors, thus demonstrating that the size of the priming effect was similar across replications.”
