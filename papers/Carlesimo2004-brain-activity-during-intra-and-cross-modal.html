phenomenonrepetition priming— “Repetition priming is a form of implicit memory that typically manifests as a facilitation in re-processing recently experienced stimuli or as a bias in completing fragments with recently encountered items when test instructions do not make any reference to prior events.”
claim-typeexistence— “A straightforward result of the present study is that auditory-to-auditory within-modality and auditory-to-visual cross-modality stem completion priming are associated with distinct patterns of rCBF changes as detected by PET.”
method — evidence— “A positron emission tomography (PET) study was conducted to investigate the neurofunctional correlate of auditory within-modality and auditory-to-visual cross-modality stem completion priming.”
mechanismThe left inferior temporal/fusiform region mediates interaction between phonological and orthographic word codes, and this shared abstract code is what carries facilitation across modalities— “In particular, it suggests that this cortical region might be involved in the complex interactions between the phonological and orthographic codes of words which ultimately allow for the cross-modality facilitation in verbal stimulus re-processing.”
mechanismRight prefrontal increases arise from post-retrieval awareness that the generated word was recently encountered (involuntary conscious memory), not from deliberate episodic search driving the completions— “The substantially low level of the completion rate and the lack of an increase in priming magnitude across successive study-test blocks suggest that the contribution of such mechanisms is more in eliciting post-retrieval awareness of a recent encounter with the generated word than in actual support for active retrieval.”
mechanismLeft prefrontal increases reflect more efficient access to output lexical representations of recently encountered words rather than generic word-fluency demand— “Rather, rCBF changes in this region should be considered as the expression of more efficient access to output representations of recently encountered words prompted by either phonological or semantic cues.”
contradicts Badgaiyan et al. (1999); Badgaiyan et al. (2001)— “This is in contrast with previous reports in the literature which showed rCBF decreases in the lateral occipital cortex (BA 19) during auditory stem completion priming (Badgaiyan et al., 1999; Badgaiyan et al., 2001).”
contradicts Carlesimo, Marfia, Loasses, & Caltagirone (1996); Graf et al. (1985)— “Previous behavioural studies consistently reported significantly larger within-modality than cross-modality stem completion priming (e.g. Carlesimo, Marfia, Loasses, & Caltagirone, 1996; Graf et al., 1985).”
contradicts Badgaiyan et al. (1999); Schacter et al. (1999)— “However, the conclusion that cross-modality priming is more exposed to contamination by explicit retrieval strategies than within-modality priming is not supported by neuropsychological evidence.”
builds-on Buckner et al. (2000)— “Support for the view that regions in the left inferior temporal cortex can modulate their neural activity in response to verbal stimuli independent of stimulus-driven perceptual processes comes from an fMRI study by Buckner et al. (2000).”
sense**THE ASYMMETRY IS STATED AS SETTLED BACKGROUND, AND THREE YEARS LATER IT IS AN ARTEFACT.** `In most cases, the magnitude of the priming effect is LARGER when the same modality of stimulus presentation is used for study and testing than when it differs (Graf, Mandler, & Haden, 1982).` Written as review, not as a claim under test - which is what makes it worth tagging. **lukatela2007 minimised study-test asymmetries across five experiments and found EQUAL magnitudes**, with the conventional advantage returning only in a within-subjects design. Kirsner1986 reasons from the asymmetry; this record goes looking for its brain basis; lukatela2007 dissolves it. **Three records, one day, none citing the others**— “In most cases, the magnitude of the priming effect is larger when the same modality of stimulus presentation is used for study and testing than when it differs (Graf, Mandler, & Haden, 1982).”
sense**AND THE NEURAL RESULT IS A DISSOCIATION IN DIRECTION, NOT JUST IN LOCATION:** cross-modal priming gave a larger rCBF DECREASE in left inferior temporal and fusiform cortex - regions tied to modality-independent lexical retrieval - while `WITHIN-MODALITY auditory priming was associated with a bilateral pattern of prefrontal rCBF INCREASE.` Different regions and opposite signs. **Whatever the behavioural asymmetry turns out to be, these two conditions are not one process running at two strengths in this data** - which is a constraint that survives even if lukatela2007 is right that the behavioural difference is a design artefact— “Instead, within-modality auditory priming was associated with a bilateral pattern of prefrontal rCBF increase.”
sense**THE DEFINITION PUTS THE CRITERION IN THE INSTRUCTIONS, WHICH IS EASY TO READ PAST:** repetition priming manifests as facilitation or a completion bias `WHEN TEST INSTRUCTIONS DO NOT MAKE ANY REFERENCE TO PRIOR EVENTS.` Not `when the subject is unaware`, not `when memory is not consciously used` - **when the experimenter did not mention it.** The construct is operationalised by what was said to the participant, and the 90%% discussion of this same paper concedes that `involuntary conscious memory` may occur anyway when the study context is reinstated. The definition and the caveat are in the same paper— “Repetition priming is a form of implicit memory that typically manifests as a facilitation in re-processing recently experienced stimuli or as a bias in completing fragments with recently encountered items when test instructions do not make any reference to prior events.”
controlthe 30-word stimulus sets were counterbalanced across subjects so each occurred equally often in the within- and cross-modality conditions and in the priming and baseline scans — held — rules out that a condition difference is a difference between the word sets— “The stimuli were counterbalanced across subjects so that each 30-word set occurred equally often in the within and cross-modality condition, priming and baseline scans.”
controlcondition and scan order counterbalanced across blocks and subjects in a LATIN SQUARE design — held — rules out that the effect is an order effect across the scanning session— “The order of within and cross-modality conditions as well as priming and baseline scans was also counterbalanced across blocks and subjects (Latin square design).”
controla postscan debriefing asked whether subjects had noticed that some stems came from the study list - and NEARLY ALL OF THEM HAD — failed — rules out that the priming is implicit - the control was run to establish that subjects were unaware, and it established the opposite— “Moreover, informal assessment of subjects during postscan debriefing revealed that nearly all subjects in both withinand cross-modality conditions were aware of the fact that some of the stems came from the study list.”
