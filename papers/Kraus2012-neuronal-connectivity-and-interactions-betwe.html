claim-typemechanism
mechanismNoise activates the amygdala, which drives the HPA axis to release glucocorticoids, which then act on receptors throughout the body including the inner ear — “Exposure to noise can trigger the release of glucocorticoids by activation of the HPA-axis via the amygdala (Fig. 4).”
mechanismNoise drives neural hyperactivity in the hippocampus, and this hyperactivity (rather than circulating stress hormone) suppresses neurogenesis over the long term — “Therefore it is conceivable that neural hyperactivity in hippocampus during noise exposure may play a role for altering neurogenesis long-term.”
mechanismFear conditioning is implemented by long-term potentiation of lateral amygdala pyramidal neurons via NMDA receptors and voltage-gated calcium channels — “The mechanism for fear conditioning is proposed to be long-term potentiation on pyramidal neurons in lateral amygdala, and formation of long-term memory by involvement of NMDA receptors together with voltage gated calcium channels (Blair et al., 2001).”
mechanismAcute stress protects the ear because released glucocorticoids activate glucocorticoid receptors in inner-ear tissue before the traumatic sound arrives — “It is believed that the protection against noise trauma is through the activation of glucocorticoid receptors in the inner ear (Canlon et al., 2007; Meltser and Canlon, 2011).”
mechanismAn amygdala-to-auditory feedback loop (via nucleus accumbens and thalamic reticular nucleus) gates the tinnitus signal out before it reaches cortex and awareness — “A feedback pathway from the amygdala to the auditory system may suppress the tinnitus signal at a subcortical level before it reaches auditory cortex and consciousness (Rauschecker et al., 2010).”

Content tags
content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingThe review concludes that the limbic system contributes to both the generation and the suppression of tinnitus, making it a target for treatment. — “In turn, the limbic system may play a role in the generation as well as the suppression of tinnitus indicating that the limbic system may be essential for tinnitus treatment.”
findingAmygdalar activation by emotionally meaningful sound is driven by the acoustic stimulus pattern itself rather than by the emotion the listener feels. — “These findings suggest that the stimulus pattern itself and not the resulting emotion is the crucial feature for amygdalar activation (Sander et al., 2003).”
findingAlthough chronic stress correlates with hearing problems, acute stress is protective, improving hearing sensitivity and reducing noise-induced hearing loss. — “In summary, while there are positive correlations between stress and hearing problems, a large body of studies provides evidence that acute stress can enhance hearing or mediate protection against noise-induced hearing loss.”
findingThe effect of stress hormones on hippocampal LTP follows an inverted-U dose relationship, facilitating potentiation at low levels and impairing it at high levels. — “Stress hormone effects on hippocampal plasticity were originally described as an “inverted U shaped curve”: At low stress hormone levels, a hormone increase facilitates LTP while at high stress hormone levels a further increase impairs LTP (Lynch, 2004; Maggio and Segal, 2010).”
findingBecause corticosterone returns to baseline soon after noise while neurogenesis stays suppressed for weeks, sustained stress-hormone elevation cannot be the cause of the long-term neurogenesis deficit. — “Thus the long-term neurogenesis reduction after noise cannot be mediated by sustained corticosterone elevation (Hayes et al., 2011).”
findingHippocampal phase-locking to auditory input is novelty-dependent, appearing for new stimuli and declining with repetition, which suggests a role in novelty detection. — “Furthermore, studies in guinea pig showed that phaselocking occurs when a stimulus is novel, but declines with repetition suggesting that phase-locking may play a role in the identification of novel stimuli (Liberman et al., 2009).”
findingStress has opposite effects on the two memory operations: it strengthens encoding of emotionally arousing events while suppressing retrieval and cognition. — “In summary, stress is capable of enhancing memory of emotionally arousing situations while it inhibits memory retrieval or cognition.”
quantity126 dB, 12 kHz narrowband, 2 h — the single acute noise exposure that produced a hippocampal neurogenesis reduction still visible ten weeks later — “Rats exposed to a high-intensity noise (126 dB, 12 kHz narrowband, 2 h) also showed a long-lasting reduction of hippocampal neurogenesis as seen 10 weeks after exposure (Kraus et al., 2010).”
quantity30 days / 40 days — recovery time of hippocampal monoamine levels and memory after 80 dB versus 100 dB chronic noise, showing recovery scales with exposure intensity — “The rate of recovery was dependent on the intensity of noise where rats exposed to 80 dB needed 30 days to recover while rats exposed to 100 dB needed 40 days.”
quantity2.1 kHz — the fundamental frequency of rat pain calls to which lateral amygdala neurons phase-lock — “Species-specific pain calls (tail-pain chatter) causes neuronal responses in the lateral amygdala that are phase-locked with the fundamental frequency (2.1 kHz) of the calls in anesthetized rats (Du et al., 2009).”
quantity80 dB, 2 h, 6 weeks — the moderate noise dose sufficient to impair learning and memory and cause oxidative damage — “Moreover, even moderately intense noise (80 dB, 2 h, 6 weeks) impaired learning and memory as assessed using the water maze and avoidance tasks.”
quantity122 dB — the noise level after which sympathectomy reduced permanent threshold shift in guinea pigs — “Sympathectomy was found to reduce a permanent threshold shift after 122 dB noise exposure in guinea pigs (Hildesheimer et al., 2002).”
definitionacoustic startle response — “The acoustic startle response is a rapid body twitch (startle) in response to a sudden loud sound and it is known that the amygdala can regulate the amplitude of the acoustic startle response.”
definitionpre-pulse inhibition — “Additionally, the basolateral amygdala can reduce the startle amplitude through “pre-pulse inhibition”, a suppression of the startle response by a weaker stimulus immediately before the startle-eliciting stimulus.”
definitionhippocampal inhibition or gating — “When identical auditory stimuli are repeated the response of the hippocampus decreases, a mechanism called hippocampal inhibition or gating.”
definitionneurogenesis — “Neurogenesis, the generation of new neurons, occurs in the subgranular zone of the DG and these new-born granule cells are then integrated into the existing circuits (Kempermann, 2002; Ge et al., 2008).”
definitionPavlovian fear conditioning — “Of particular importance is the Pavlovian fear conditioning (Maren, 2003, 2005; Sah et al., 2003; Rodrigues et al., 2004; Rosen, 2004) where a meaningless or neutral stimulus (conditioned stimulus) is coupled with the response network by an unpleasant stimulus (unconditioned stimulus) so that eventually the conditioned stimulus alone triggers the response.”
