phenomenonauditory mismatch responses to duration deviants — “The mismatch negativity (MMN) of event-related potentials (ERPs) reflects this initial stage of auditory change detection.”
claim-typeexistence — “We found differential LFPs (mismatch responses) to duration deviant tones in the auditory cortex and in the hippocampal system (CA1, dentate gyrus, and subiculum) in urethane-anesthetized rats.”
method — evidence — “In the present study, local-field potentials (LFPs) in urethane-anesthetized rats were recorded on the surface of the primary auditory cortex and in three locations of the hippocampal system (dentate gyrus, CA1, and subiculum, Figure 1 ).”
object of study — evidence — “Our interest was whether mismatch responses, whenever elicited, occupied both the auditory cortex and the hippocampus in a typical latency range of mismatch responses in urethane anesthetized rats or whether mismatch responses were only manifested in the auditory cortex and possibly only followed by later hippocampal activity.”
research question — evidence — “The evidence for this cortico-hippocampal dichotomy is scarce, however.”
contribution — evidence — “Most importantly, hippocampal and auditory cortical mismatch responses were found in the same latency range and in the same stimulus condition.”
transferfrom human event-related potential research: the entire oddball paradigm and the deviant-minus-standard subtraction logic, carried into rodent local field potentials with the polarity requirement dropped — “MMN is quantified by arithmetically subtracting ERPs to the standard tone from ERPs to the deviant tone.”
transferfrom human ERP research: the MMN-then-P3a sequence, imported as the prediction the animal data would have to match if the hippocampal response were merely downstream — “This would be analogous to a MMN-P3a sequence in humans that is only observable in full (i.e., also including P3a) when deviant tones of high magnitude attract involuntary switches of attention [1] , [4] .”
mechanismA long standard-to-deviant interval lets the transient memory trace of the standard decay, so the deviant can no longer be registered as mismatching it, abolishing the response — “Secondly, the long (500-ms) SDI might have led to a high degree of degradation of the memory trace of the standard tone, preventing the detection of the deviant tone as not matching this trace [1] , [7] .”
mechanismA short inter-deviant interval re-activates the mismatch generator too rapidly, diminishing the amplitude of the response it can produce — “First, the short inter-deviant interval (due to the 200-ms SSI) might have diminished mismatch responses by the fast-paced activation of their neural generator (in humans [43] – [45] , in cats [15] ).”
mechanismIn the responsive conditions the deviant differed from the standard on two features (duration and inter-stimulus interval) instead of one, making the change easier to detect — “The present pattern of observations may also reflect the fact that the deviant tone was distinguishable from the standard tone by two auditory features (tone duration and inter-stimulus interval) in the former pair of conditions as opposed to solely one feature (tone duration) in the latter pair [1] , [47] .”
mechanismthe animal analogue is given a deliberately looser definition than the human one, dropping the polarity requirement so a cross-species comparison is possible at all — “Mismatch responses (i.e., higher-amplitude brain responses to deviant tones than standard tones irrespectively of the polarity of these responses and despite these tones are only passively listened to)”
mechanismthat loosening turns out to be load-bearing rather than cautious: the cortical response is positive and the hippocampal one negative in the very same animals — “The responses - positive polarity in the auditory cortical and negative polarity in the hippocampal sites - appeared in the same latency range (between 51.5 and 89 ms post-change) and in all the recording sites, but only in the SSI 500 /SDI 200 -condition.”
mechanismco-occurrence in latency and in condition is the argument against a downstream account — if the hippocampus were merely triggered by cortex it should lag — “This correspondence suggests that the hippocampal responses reflect the initial detection of deviant tones similarly to auditory cortical mismatch responses.”
mechanismduration rather than frequency is chosen as the deviant feature specifically to avoid the refractoriness confound that a tonotopic dimension would introduce — “Sound frequency was not used due to its spatial coding from the cochlea [41] owing to a potential confounding effect of lower levels of refractoriness in afferent pathways activated by the rare deviant tone than the frequently occurring standard tones [1] .”
mechanismtwo intervals are crossed so that the memory-trace account and the generator-refractoriness account make different predictions across the four cells — “Two standard-to-standard (SSI) and standard-to-deviant (SDI) intervals (200 ms vs. 500 ms) were applied in different combinations to vary the observability of responses resembling MMN (mismatch responses).”
mechanismseries order is counterbalanced across animals specifically because anesthetic depth drifts with time on the rig — “was counterbalanced across the animals to control for effects of the duration of stimulus exposure and of the passage of time itself (e.g., through variations in the level of anesthesia)”
mechanismthe omnibus ANOVA is used as a licence for the post-hoc comparisons rather than as the result, and the parametric choice is stated as forced — “As there were no suitable non-parametric alternatives, ANOVA for repeated measures with stimulus (standard, deviant) and time-segment (first, second, third) as factors was used to justify post-hoc deviant-standard LFP amplitude comparisons for each time segment separately.”
mechanismelectrode positions are verified histologically after the fact and only confirmed placements enter the analysis, so the sample size differs by structure — “For the hippocampal recordings, the electrodes successfully implanted in the targeted areas were applied in the analysis (9 animals for the subiculum, 8 for the CA1 area, and 6 for the dentate gyrus, Figure 1 )”
mechanismthe baseline is taken from a window straddling tone onset, so that time zero is the moment of deviance rather than the moment of sound — “baseline corrected against the mean activity in the ±25 ms from tone onset and, hence in the 50-ms time window prior to duration deviance”
mechanismthe received dichotomy is attacked on three separate grounds — too little data, an independent hippocampal novelty mechanism, and existing animal evidence — rather than on the new result alone — “The MMN-hippocampus dissociation may, however, be premature for a number of reasons.”
critiques the received cortico-hippocampal dichotomy in which the hippocampus contributes to P3a but not to MMN — “The MMN-hippocampus dissociation may, however, be premature for a number of reasons.”
contradicts intracranial human studies reporting no hippocampal mismatch responses, or only longer-latency novelty responses — “Studies employing intracranial hippocampal recordings in neurological patients have reported no mismatch responses [33] or other, longer-latency differential responses to deviant tones as novel or salient stimuli rather than as changes in standards [34] .”
replicates previous cortical mismatch findings in urethane-anesthetized rats, in polarity and latency — “The polarity and latency range of auditory cortical mismatch responses in the SSI 500 /SDI 200 -condition correspond to previous findings in urethane anesthetized rats [18] , [19] , [22] , [23] .”
extends animal work already reporting hippocampal mismatch responses, some of them specific to change rather than rarity — “Finally, hippocampal mismatch responses have been observed in animals [14] , [16] , and some of these responses have, comparably to MMN [3] , even shown specificity to deviant tones as changes in standard tones [16] .”
builds-on the proposal that the hippocampus responds to rare stimuli by comparison against a model of past repetition — “Second, the hippocampus has been proposed, comparably to MMN [1] , to respond by itself to rare stimuli on the basis of their discrepancy from a neural model of past repetitive stimuli [35] – [38] .”
critiques the state of MMN generator theory generally, as contested and unresolved — “The generators of MMN are largely unknown. Different and partially contradictory explanations have been proposed [8] , [26] – [28] .”

findingMismatch responses to duration deviants were recorded not only in the auditory cortex but simultaneously in all three hippocampal-system sites (CA1, dentate gyrus and subiculum) of urethane-anesthetized rats. — “We found differential LFPs (mismatch responses) to duration deviant tones in the auditory cortex and in the hippocampal system (CA1, dentate gyrus, and subiculum) in urethane-anesthetized rats.”
findingThe cortical and hippocampal responses had opposite polarity (positive in cortex, negative in hippocampus) yet occupied the same 51.5-89 ms post-change latency window and appeared only in the condition combining a 500-ms standard-to-standard interval with a 200-ms standard-to-deviant interval. — “The responses - positive polarity in the auditory cortical and negative polarity in the hippocampal sites - appeared in the same latency range (between 51.5 and 89 ms post-change) and in all the recording sites, but only in the SSI 500 /SDI 200 -condition.”
findingOf the four interval combinations tested, only the 500-ms SSI paired with the 200-ms SDI elicited mismatch responses; the other three combinations elicited none. — “Mismatch responses were observed at 51.5–89 ms with the 500-ms SSI coupled with the 200-ms SDI but not with the three remaining combinations.”
findingBecause the hippocampal responses coincided with the cortical ones in both latency and stimulus condition, the hippocampal activity reflects the initial detection of the deviant rather than a later, attention-switch stage. — “Most importantly, hippocampal and auditory cortical mismatch responses were found in the same latency range and in the same stimulus condition.”
findingThe authors conclude that the hippocampus may contribute to the cortical manifestation of the mismatch negativity, contradicting the assumed cortico-hippocampal dichotomy. — “The findings suggest that the hippocampus may play a role in (cortical) manifestation of MMN.”
findingmismatch responses appeared in auditory cortex and all three hippocampal sites, at 51.5–89 ms post-change, but only in one of four conditions — “Mismatch responses were observed at 51.5–89 ms with the 500-ms SSI coupled with the 200-ms SDI but not with the three remaining combinations.”
findingthe cortical response was positive-going and the hippocampal responses negative-going, in the same animals and the same window — “The responses - positive polarity in the auditory cortical and negative polarity in the hippocampal sites - appeared in the same latency range (between 51.5 and 89 ms post-change) and in all the recording sites, but only in the SSI 500 /SDI 200 -condition.”
findingin auditory cortex the effect was a stimulus by time-segment interaction, present in the first segment only — “as reflected by a stimulus (deviant tone, standard tone)×time-segment (first, second, third) -interaction, in the SSI 500/ SDI 200 condition, F(2,18) = 5.13, P = 0.048”
findingin CA1 the effect was larger and lasted two segments rather than one — “Post-hoc pairwise comparisons further indicated that, in this condition, the displacement was present (P<0.05) in the first time segment (51.5–89 ms from duration change, P<0.001) and in the second time segment (89.5–127 ms from duration change, P<0.05).”
findingdentate gyrus and subiculum showed the same first-segment displacement as CA1 — “Furthermore, as in the CA1 and DG, the LFPs in the subiculum were displaced towards negative polarity in response to the deviant tone relative to the standard tone, as reflected by a significant stimulus×time-segment -interaction, F(2,16) = 8.18, P = 0.017, in the SSI 500/ SDI 200 condition.”
findingno main effect of stimulus in auditory cortex in any condition — “No main effect of stimulus was present in any condition (P≥0.211).”
findingthe tone-evoked waveform itself peaked at about 40 ms in cortex and had two negative peaks at about 20 and 40 ms in hippocampus, both timed from tone onset rather than from deviance — “In the CA1 area of the hippocampus, the tones evoked a two-peaked negative deflection, the first peak occurring at about 20 ms and the second at about 40 ms from stimulus onset (i.e., not from deviance which occurred at 25 ms after stimulus onset).”
findingin dentate gyrus one further condition gave a significant interaction that post-hoc testing reduced to a trend in the last segment — “However, post-hoc comparisons only showed a trend for the deviant-standard difference in the last (127.5 ms −165 ms) time segment (P = 0.09).”

Abstract

Any change in the invariant aspects of the auditory environment is of potential importance. The human brain preattentively or automatically detects such changes. The mismatch negativity (MMN)term·def of event-related potentialsterm (ERPs) reflects this initial stage of auditory change detection. The origin of MMNterm·def is held to be cortical. The hippocampus is associated with a later generated P3aterm of ERPs reflecting involuntarily attention switches towards auditory changes that are high in magnitude. The evidence for this cortico-hippocampal dichotomyterm is scarce, however. To shed further light on this issue, auditory cortical and hippocampal-system (CA1term, dentate gyrusterm, subiculumterm) local-field potentialsterm were recorded in urethane-anesthetized rats. A rare tone in duration (deviantterm) was interspersed with a repeated tone (standardterm). Two standard-to-standard (SSIterm) and standard-to-deviant (SDIterm) intervals (200 ms vs. 500 ms) were applied in different combinations to vary the observability of responses resembling MMNterm·def (mismatch responsesterm). Mismatch responsesterm were observed at 51.5–89 ms with the 500-ms SSIterm coupled with the 200-ms SDIterm but not with the three remaining combinations. Most importantly, the responses appeared in both the auditory-cortical and hippocampal locations. The findings suggest that the hippocampus may play a role in (cortical) manifestation of MMNterm·def.

Introduction

Any changes in the invariant attributes of the auditory environment are of potential importance for survival. The rapid, effortless, and sensitive detection of auditory changes is, therefore, of crucial importance for the organism.

In humans, the preattentiveterm detection of auditory changes is indexed by an electrical brain response with a fronto-central scalp distribution termed the mismatch negativity (MMN)term·def of event-related potentialsterm (ERPs) [1] – [3] . MMNterm·def can be elicited in the latency range of 150–250 ms even by changes perceptually too weak to attract involuntary attentionterm [1] . If the auditory changes are of high magnitude, they can capture involuntary attentionterm [4] and elicit a partly hippocampally-contributed P3aterm of ERPs in the later latency range of 250–280 ms [1] , [5] , [6] .

MMNterm·def is typically experimentally addressed by using the so-called oddball conditionterm. In this condition, a (‘deviantterm’) tone rarely replaces a frequently repeated (‘standardterm’) tone. MMNterm·def is elicited by the deviantterm tone only if the distinguishing feature(s) of the standardterm relative to the deviantterm are successfully represented neurally, stored in transient auditory memory, and compared to the auditory input by the deviantterm [1] , [7] , [8] . MMNterm·def is quantified by arithmetically subtracting ERPs to the standardterm tone from ERPs to the deviantterm tone. The specificity of MMNterm·def to deviantterm tones as changes in standardterm tones is reflected by the disappearance of these responses by the removal of standardterm tones from the series [3] .

Mismatch responsesterm (i.e., higher-amplitude brain responses to deviantterm tones than standardterm tones irrespectively of the polarity of these responses and despite these tones are only passively listened to) have been found in sleeping infants [9] – [11] and in awake, sleeping and anesthetized animals [12] – [23] (for negative findings in anesthetized rats, see, [24] , [25] ).

The generators of MMNterm·def are largely unknown. Different and partially contradictory explanations have been proposed [8] , [26] – [28] . The general consensus points to the generation of MMNterm·def in the temporal and, subsequently, frontal cortical areas [29] , [30] with a possible contribution from lower levels of the auditory pathway [31] , [32] .

No role in human MMNterm·def generation has been assigned to the hippocampus. Studies employing intracranial hippocampal recordings in neurological patients have reported no mismatch responsesterm [33] or other, longer-latency differential responses to deviantterm tones as novel or salient stimuli rather than as changes in standards [34] .

The MMN-hippocampus dissociation may, however, be premature for a number of reasons. First, too few hippocampal MMNterm·def data have been gathered in humans. Second, the hippocampus has been proposed, comparably to MMNterm·def [1] , to respond by itself to rare stimuli on the basis of their discrepancy from a neural model of past repetitive stimuli [35] – [38] . Finally, hippocampal mismatch responsesterm have been observed in animals [14] , [16] , and some of these responses have, comparably to MMNterm·def [3] , even shown specificity to deviantterm tones as changes in standardterm tones [16] .

Nevertheless, hippocampal mismatch responsesterm could be claimed to be secondary to and solely triggered by auditory cortical mismatch responsesterm in a limited set of circumstances. Namely, these responses could be argued to only emerge to high-magnitude deviantterm tones that capture involuntary attentionterm in contrast to auditory cortical mismatch responsesterm also observable to low-magnitude deviantterm tones close to their behavioral detection threshold. This would be analogous to a MMN-P3a sequence in humans that is only observable in full (i.e., also including P3aterm) when deviantterm tones of high magnitude attract involuntary switches of attention [1] , [4] .

In awake and anesthetized rats, cortical mismatch responsesterm have been found to occur in the latency range of 30–150 ms [18] , [19] , [21] – [23] (for 25-ms onset latency, see [20] ). In urethaneterm anesthetized rats, auditory cortical mismatch responsesterm have, comparable to MMNterm·def [1] , been observed to be specific to deviantterm tones as changes per se in standardterm tones. That is, mismatch responsesterm in these animals have been found to disappear when standardterm tones have either been completely removed from the series [18] or replaced by a set of heterogeneous tones of equal rarity to deviantterm tones [19] . Awake rats also show P3a-type responses to deviantterm tones of high magnitude. Similarly to P3aterm in humans [4] , [6] , these responses follow mismatch responsesterm at about 240 ms post-stimulus [39] .

In the present study, local-field potentialsterm (LFPs) in urethane-anesthetized rats were recorded on the surface of the primary auditory cortexterm and in three locations of the hippocampal systemterm (dentate gyrusterm, CA1term, and subiculumterm, Figure 1 ). The recordings were made during four oddball conditions with different stimulus-onset asynchronies between consecutive standardterm tones and between deviants tones and standardterm tones immediately preceding them adapted from [40] ( Figure 2 ). The deviantterm tone differed from the standardterm tone in duration (25 vs. 75 ms). Sound frequency was not used due to its spatial coding from the cochlea [41] owing to a potential confounding effect of lower levels of refractorinessterm in afferent pathways activated by the rare deviantterm tone than the frequently occurring standardterm tones [1] . Early neuronal selectivity to duration in the auditory system (the auditory midbrain) has been demonstrated but through active rather than fatigue-type of passive neural events [42] . We expected that some of the auditory oddball conditions would be more favorable for mismatch responsesterm than others. Our interest was whether mismatch responsesterm, whenever elicited, occupied both the auditory cortexterm and the hippocampus in a typical latency range of mismatch responsesterm in urethaneterm anesthetized rats or whether mismatch responsesterm were only manifested in the auditory cortexterm and possibly only followed by later hippocampal activity.

Discussion

We found differential LFPs (mismatch responsesterm) to duration deviantterm tones in the auditory cortexterm and in the hippocampal systemterm (CA1term, dentate gyrusterm, and subiculumterm) in urethane-anesthetized rats. The responses - positive polarity in the auditory cortical and negative polarity in the hippocampal sites - appeared in the same latency range (between 51.5 and 89 ms post-change) and in all the recording sites, but only in the SSIterm 500 /SDIterm 200 -condition. In the remaining three conditions, the findings were negative.

The polarity and latency range of auditory cortical mismatch responsesterm in the SSIterm 500 /SDIterm 200 -condition correspond to previous findings in urethaneterm anesthetized rats [18] , [19] , [22] , [23] . There are three possible reasons for the absence of mismatch responsesterm in the SSIterm 200 /SDIterm 500 -condition. First, the short inter-deviant interval (due to the 200-ms SSIterm) might have diminished mismatch responsesterm by the fast-paced activation of their neural generator (in humans [43] – [45] , in cats [15] ). Secondly, the long (500-ms) SDIterm might have led to a high degree of degradation of the memory traceterm of the standardterm tone, preventing the detection of the deviantterm tone as not matching this trace [1] , [7] . Third, the long SDIterm relative to the SSIterm might have been a less efficient additional temporal cue (inter-stimulus interval) for the detection of the (stimulus duration) deviantterm tone than the short SDIterm relative to the SSIterm [46] .

In the SSIterm 200 /SDIterm 200 - and SSIterm 500 /SDIterm 500 -conditions, the stimulus assignments (the 25-ms tone as the deviantterm tone and the 75-ms tone as the standardterm tone, but not vice versa) and numbers (a half) of sweeps per averaged waveform were different from those in the SSIterm 500 /SDIterm 200 - and SSIterm 200 /SDIterm 500 -conditions. Therefore, why mismatch responsesterm were absent precisely in the SSIterm 200 /SDIterm 200 - and SSIterm 500 /SDIterm 500 -conditions remains to be accounted for. However, some tentative explanations can be offered.

In the SSIterm 200 /SDIterm 200 -condition, the short inter-deviant interval (due to the 200-ms SSIterm) might have diminished the amplitude of mismatch responsesterm through the fast-paced activation of their neural generator [15] , [43] – [45] while the short decay time of the memory traceterm of the standardterm tone to be compared to the sensory input by the deviantterm tone (due to the 200-ms SDIterm, [1] , [7] ) might not have been sufficient to compensate for this diminution. In the SSIterm 500 /SDIterm 500 -condition, the decay time (the 500-ms SDIterm) might, in turn, have been too long as mismatch responsesterm were observed in the SSIterm 500 /SDIterm 200 -condition (with the same SSIterm but a shorter SDIterm).

Mismatch responsesterm in rats have been found to be higher in amplitude to deviantterm tones as lengthenings than shortenings of standardterm tones [21] . Therefore, mismatch responsesterm in the SSI500/SDI200-condition and a trend for a late differential response in the DG area in the SSI200/SDI500-condition may have reflected the fact that these conditions also involved the deviantterm tone as a lengthening of the standardterm tone as opposed to the SSI200/SDI200- and SSI500/SDI500-conditions which only included the deviantterm tone as a shortening of the standardterm tone. The present pattern of observations may also reflect the fact that the deviantterm tone was distinguishable from the standardterm tone by two auditory features (tone duration and inter-stimulus interval) in the former pair of conditions as opposed to solely one feature (tone duration) in the latter pair [1] , [47] .

Most importantly, hippocampal and auditory cortical mismatch responsesterm were found in the same latency range and in the same stimulus condition. This correspondence suggests that the hippocampal responses reflect the initial detection of deviantterm tones similarly to auditory cortical mismatch responsesterm. It remains to be resolved whether these responses of different anatomical origins recruit a single but spatially distributed neural mechanism, spatially separate and independent neural mechanisms, or spatially separate but interconnected neural mechanisms. The nature of the mechanism(s) should also be further explored. Namely, the mechanisms could, similarly to those for MMNterm·def in humans [1] , [8] , have treated the deviantterm tone as a change in the standardterm tone. Auditory cortical mismatch responsesterm in urethane-anesthetized rats [18] , [19] and hippocampal mismatch responsesterm in awake rabbits [16] to frequency deviantterm tones have been found to reflect such mechanisms. Nevertheless, the rarity of the deviantterm tone as such [26] , [27] also remains a possible explanation for our findings. Future studies with appropriate control procedures, such as deviant-aloneterm (e.g. [18] ) or equal-probabilityterm (e.g. [19] ) stimulus conditions, are needed to test the validity of the change-detection account. There is also need for deeper understanding of the registration and transient memory storage of repetitive standardterm tones at the neural level [1] , [7] . Stimulus-specific adaptationterm, a decrease in neural responses to a repeated tone which is specific to the physical features of this tone, observed in different stages of the auditory pathway [48] – [51] as functionally distinct from auditory cortical mismatch responsesterm [52] , [53] could provide a useful neurophysiological tool to increase this understanding.

To summarize, the brains of urethane-anesthetized rats were found to generate mismatch responsesterm to duration deviantsterm with the same set of stimulus parameters and in the same latency range in the auditory cortexterm and in three locations in the hippocampal systemterm (CA1term, dentate gyrusterm, subiculumterm). The findings prompt the question whether the hippocampal systemterm plays an active role in the auditory cortical manifestation of these responses and hence also possibly of MMNterm·def in humans.

Materials and Methods

Ethics Statement

The experiments were approved by the Finnish National Animal Experiment Board (Permit code: ESLH-2007-00662), and carried out in accordance with the European Communities Council Directive (86/609/EEC) regarding the care and use of animals for experimental procedures.

Animals and Surgery

Ten Spraque Dawley rats were used in the experiment (weight 305–375 g). The animals were housed as groups in cages with water and feed ad libitum. The animals were anaesthetized with urethaneterm (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) i.p. (1.2 g/kg). The level of anesthesia was controlled by pedal withdrawal reflex, and if necessary, extra doses of urethaneterm were given. The animal was positioned in a streotaxic instrument with blunt ear bars which afterwards were removed to allow auditory stimulation. Under lidocaine anesthesia (Lidocain 20%, Orion Pharma, Espoo, Finland) skin and muscle tissue were removed to expose unilaterally a 2×2 mm region over the left primary auditory cortexterm (from bregma anterior posterior (AP): −4.5– (−6.5) mm, dorsoventral (DV) 3–5 mm lateral to the bone edge of the upper skull surface).

A tip of a Teflon-insulated stainless steel wire (diameter 200 µm, A-M Systems, Carlsberg, WA, USA) was positioned on the surface of the dura above the auditory cortexterm on the basis of on-line recorded potentials. In addition, intracranial electrodes were implanted (Formwar® insulated stainless steel wire, diameter 100 µm, California Fine Wire Company Co, Grover Beach, CA, USA). Two electrodes with 400-µm tip separation were lowered to the ventral subiculumterm to coordinates AP: −6.5 mm, ML: 5.5 mm and DV 6.6. Three intermediate (caudal) hippocampal electrodes with 0.6 mm spacing between electrodes were lowered to coordinates AP: 6.0 mm, ML: 4.8, 5.4 and 6.0 mm, and DV: 4.6 mm. Based on histology, electrodes located in the area of interest were selected for further analysis. For the reference electrode, a hole was drilled in the scull over the right side of the cerebellum and a small insulin needle (BD Lo-Dose syringe, USA) was inserted in the cerebellum (AP −10 mm, ML: 2–3 mm and DV: 2 mm) and the animal was grounded by inserting a needle (18G, Terumo, Somerset, NJ, USA) subcutaneously into the neck.

Stimuli and Procedure

Sinusoidal 4000-Hz tones of 25 ms and 75 ms in duration, including 5-ms rise and fall times, were used as stimuli. The sound pressure level for each tone was 70 dB with C-weighting (optimized for 40–100 dB measurement), as measured with a sound level meter (type 2235, Bruel & Kjaer, Nærum Denmark), in the location of the animal’s right pinna during the recording.

The deviantterm (P = 0.1) and the standardterm (P = 0.9) occurred in a series of 1000 tones randomly with the restriction that consecutive deviants were separated by at least two standards.

Figure 2 illustrates the four stimulus conditions used in the study.

In two of the stimulus conditions, (SSIterm 200 /SDIterm 200 and SSIterm 500 /SDIterm 500 ), the SSIterm and the SDIterm was either 200 ms or 500 ms, respectively, and the deviantterm always 25 ms and standardterm 75 ms in duration. Each of these conditions comprised one stimulus series.

In the other two stimulus conditions (SSIterm 500 /SDIterm 200 and SSIterm 200 /SDIterm 500 ), the SSIterm and the SDIterm differed. In the SSIterm 500 /SDIterm 200 -condition, the SSIterm was 500 ms and the SDIterm 200 ms. In the and SSIterm 200 /SDIterm 500 -condition, SSIterm was 200 ms and SDIterm 500 ms. Each of these conditions comprised two stimulus series to counterbalance the stimulus assignments (both durations assigned to both stimulus types across the series).

The sequential order of the six series (one per SSIterm 200 /SDIterm 200 - and SSIterm 500 /SDIterm 500 -conditions, respectively, and two per SSIterm 200 /SDIterm 500 - and SSIterm 500 /SDIterm 200 - conditions, respectively) was counterbalanced across the animals to control for effects of the duration of stimulus exposure and of the passage of time itself (e.g., through variations in the level of anesthesia).

The stimulus presentation was controlled by E-prime software (Pittsburg, PA, USA), and the stimuli were delivered from a PC via an active loudspeaker system (Studiopro 3, M-audio, Irwindale, CA, USA). The stimulation was presented via a passive loudspeaker directed towards the right ear of the animal at a distance of 20 cm.

Electrocortical and Hippocampal Recordings

After surgery, the right ear bar was removed and recording started. Continuous electrocorticogram and hippocampal EEG were first 10-fold amplified using the MPA8I preamplifier (Multichannelsystems, Reutlingen, Germany), high-pass filtered at 0.1 Hz, 50-fold amplified, and low-pass filtered at 5000 Hz using an FA32I filter amplifier (Multichannelsystems), low-pass filtered at 400 Hz using a CyberAmp 380 filter amplifier (Molecular Devices Corporation), and finally sampled with 16-bit precision at 2 kHz (DigiData 1320A, Molecular Devices Corporation). The data were stored on a computer hard disk using Axoscope 9.0 data acquisition software (Molecular Devices Corporation).

Off-line Analysis

The data analyses were performed offline using a Vision Analyzer (Brain Products, Gilching, Germany), Matlab 7.5 (MathWorks Inc., Natick, MA, USA). For the hippocampal recordings, the electrodes successfully implanted in the targeted areas were applied in the analysis (9 animals for the subiculumterm, 8 for the CA1term area, and 6 for the dentate gyrusterm, Figure 1 ).

First, artifacts were removed from the data. Electrocardiogram epochs containing voltage steps larger than 300 µV/ms were deleted for 200 ms before and after the artifact. Visual inspection of the data revealed that no other types of artifacts were present in the data.

The data were then offline-filtered (0.1–30 Hz, 24 dB/octave, Butterworth Zero Phase filters), segmented (for each deviantterm and its immediately preceding standardterm), and baseline corrected against the mean activity in the ±25 ms from tone onset and, hence in the 50-ms time window prior to duration deviance.

Finally, the artifact-free segments were averaged for each animal separately for deviants and immediately preceding standards for each condition. The averaging was also made across two stimulus blocks (with the opposite stimulus assignments) in the SSI500/SSD200 and SSI200/SSD500 conditions and for one stimulus block in the SSI200/SSD200 and SSI500/SSD500 conditions. Not less than 67 out of 100 sweeps were included in calculating the average values per stimulus type and condition in any animal, the average number being 98.7 out of 100 sweeps. For the response amplitudeterm·var analysis, the response time of 187.5 ms, as calculated from tone onset, was divided into five 37.5-ms segments. The last three of these segments (starting at 50 ms from change) which coincided with mismatch-like waveforms observable in the signal (see also previous findings of mismatch responsesterm commencing at about 60 ms post-stimulus in urethaneterm anesthetized rats [18] , [19] ) were included in the statistical analyses.

Histology

After recording, the tips of the intracranial electrodes were marked in the tissue by anodal 30-µA 5-s current. The animal was sacrificed by cervical dislocation and the brain was moved from the skull and left for immersion post-fixation for 4 h in 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA) solution and after that in 30% sucrose solution for two days. The brains were stored in −20°C until slicing. Coronal sections (thickness 35 µm) were cut with a freezing slide microtome. The electrode locations were verified from the sections by cresyl violetterm·key staining and the exact locations of the electrode tips were confirmed by microscope observation.

Statistical Analysis

The statistical analyses were performed with SPSS for Windows (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). As there were no suitable non-parametric alternatives, ANOVA for repeated measuresterm·stat with stimulus (standard, deviant)term·var and time-segmentterm·var (first, second, third) as factors was used to justify post-hoc deviant-standard LFP amplitude comparisons for each time segment separately. Degrees of freedom were Greenhouse-Geisser correctedterm·stat whenever the sphericity assumption was violated, and corrected P values were reported. Pairwise deviant-standard comparison for each time segment was made using the paired t test (two-tailed)term·stat. An alpha level of 0.05 was used in all analyses.
