phenomenonauditory mismatch responses to duration deviants— “The mismatch negativity (MMN) of event-related potentials (ERPs) reflects this initial stage of auditory change detection.”
claim-typeexistence— “We found differential LFPs (mismatch responses) to duration deviant tones in the auditory cortex and in the hippocampal system (CA1, dentate gyrus, and subiculum) in urethane-anesthetized rats.”
method — evidence— “In the present study, local-field potentials (LFPs) in urethane-anesthetized rats were recorded on the surface of the primary auditory cortex and in three locations of the hippocampal system (dentate gyrus, CA1, and subiculum, Figure 1 ).”
object of study — evidence— “Our interest was whether mismatch responses, whenever elicited, occupied both the auditory cortex and the hippocampus in a typical latency range of mismatch responses in urethane anesthetized rats or whether mismatch responses were only manifested in the auditory cortex and possibly only followed by later hippocampal activity.”
research question — evidence— “The evidence for this cortico-hippocampal dichotomy is scarce, however.”
contribution — evidence— “Most importantly, hippocampal and auditory cortical mismatch responses were found in the same latency range and in the same stimulus condition.”
transferfrom human event-related potential research: the entire oddball paradigm and the deviant-minus-standard subtraction logic, carried into rodent local field potentials with the polarity requirement dropped— “MMN is quantified by arithmetically subtracting ERPs to the standard tone from ERPs to the deviant tone.”
transferfrom human ERP research: the MMN-then-P3a sequence, imported as the prediction the animal data would have to match if the hippocampal response were merely downstream— “This would be analogous to a MMN-P3a sequence in humans that is only observable in full (i.e., also including P3a) when deviant tones of high magnitude attract involuntary switches of attention [1] , [4] .”
mechanismA long standard-to-deviant interval lets the transient memory trace of the standard decay, so the deviant can no longer be registered as mismatching it, abolishing the response— “Secondly, the long (500-ms) SDI might have led to a high degree of degradation of the memory trace of the standard tone, preventing the detection of the deviant tone as not matching this trace [1] , [7] .”
mechanismA short inter-deviant interval re-activates the mismatch generator too rapidly, diminishing the amplitude of the response it can produce— “First, the short inter-deviant interval (due to the 200-ms SSI) might have diminished mismatch responses by the fast-paced activation of their neural generator (in humans [43] – [45] , in cats [15] ).”
mechanismIn the responsive conditions the deviant differed from the standard on two features (duration and inter-stimulus interval) instead of one, making the change easier to detect— “The present pattern of observations may also reflect the fact that the deviant tone was distinguishable from the standard tone by two auditory features (tone duration and inter-stimulus interval) in the former pair of conditions as opposed to solely one feature (tone duration) in the latter pair [1] , [47] .”
mechanismthe animal analogue is given a deliberately looser definition than the human one, dropping the polarity requirement so a cross-species comparison is possible at all— “Mismatch responses (i.e., higher-amplitude brain responses to deviant tones than standard tones irrespectively of the polarity of these responses and despite these tones are only passively listened to)”
mechanismthat loosening turns out to be load-bearing rather than cautious: the cortical response is positive and the hippocampal one negative in the very same animals— “The responses - positive polarity in the auditory cortical and negative polarity in the hippocampal sites - appeared in the same latency range (between 51.5 and 89 ms post-change) and in all the recording sites, but only in the SSI 500 /SDI 200 -condition.”
mechanismco-occurrence in latency and in condition is the argument against a downstream account — if the hippocampus were merely triggered by cortex it should lag— “This correspondence suggests that the hippocampal responses reflect the initial detection of deviant tones similarly to auditory cortical mismatch responses.”
mechanismduration rather than frequency is chosen as the deviant feature specifically to avoid the refractoriness confound that a tonotopic dimension would introduce— “Sound frequency was not used due to its spatial coding from the cochlea [41] owing to a potential confounding effect of…
mechanismtwo intervals are crossed so that the memory-trace account and the generator-refractoriness account make different predictions across the four cells
mechanismseries order is counterbalanced across animals specifically because anesthetic depth drifts with time on the rig

findingMismatch responses to duration deviants were recorded not only in the auditory cortex but simultaneously in all three hippocampal-system sites (CA1, dentate gyrus and subiculum) of urethane-anesthetized rats.
findingThe cortical and hippocampal responses had opposite polarity (positive in cortex, negative in hippocampus) yet occupied the same 51.5-89 ms post-change latency window and appeared only in the condition combining a 500-ms standard-to-standard interval with a 200-ms standard-to-deviant interval.
findingOf the four interval combinations tested, only the 500-ms SSI paired with the 200-ms SDI elicited mismatch responses; the other three combinations elicited none.
findingBecause the hippocampal responses coincided with the cortical ones in both latency and stimulus condition, the hippocampal activity reflects the initial detection of the deviant rather than a later, attention-switch stage.
findingThe authors conclude that the hippocampus may contribute to the cortical manifestation of the mismatch negativity, contradicting the assumed cortico-hippocampal dichotomy.
findingmismatch responses appeared in auditory cortex and all three hippocampal sites, at 51.5–89 ms post-change, but only in one of four conditions
findingthe cortical response was positive-going and the hippocampal responses negative-going, in the same animals and the same window
findingin auditory cortex the effect was a stimulus by time-segment interaction, present in the first segment only
findingin CA1 the effect was larger and lasted two segments rather than one
findingdentate gyrus and subiculum showed the same first-segment displacement as CA1
