senseTHE MECHANISM IS PROVED BY MISATTRIBUTION, WHICH IS THE STRONGEST FORM AVAILABLE FOR A FEELING: `Study 2 shows that, IF THE EXPERIENCED FLUENCY generated by repeated exposure CAN BE ATTRIBUTED TO ANOTHER SOURCE, then the effect WILL BE ATTENUATED.` Give the ease somewhere else to have come from and the preference goes with it. **That rules out the alternative that repetition simply builds a memory or a habit** - a memory would not care where you thought the ease came from— “Study 2 shows that, if the experienced fluency generated by repeated exposure can be attributed to another source, then the effect will be attenuated”
senseAND THE DOSE-RESPONSE IS NON-MONOTONIC: `Study 3 suggests that TOO MUCH FLUENCY CAN BACKFIRE, which is consistent with the SATURATION MODEL.` More exposure is not more effect. **This is the one finding here that a replication is most likely to invert by accident**: increasing the number of exposures to be safe would move the design past the peak and find less. A corpus that records `repeated exposure increases choice` without the saturation clause would license exactly that mistake— “Study 3 suggests that too much fluency can backfire, which is consistent with the saturation model proposed by Fazendeiro et al”
senseUNAWARENESS USED AS A CONTROL RATHER THAN REPORTED AS A FINDING: `the participants in study 4 WERE NOT AWARE of the brand and hence COULD NOT BE AWARE of its association with the in-group or out-group, SO CONSCIOUS CORRECTION WAS NOT POSSIBLE.` **This is higgins1977's discounting cue turned into a design feature.** Higgins found that noticing your own bias ATTENUATES it; here, showing that participants could not notice is what licenses reading the result as uncorrected. Same mechanism, opposite use - one paper measures it, the other rules it out by construction— “Importantly, the participants in study 4 were not aware of the brand and hence could not be aware of its association with the in-group or out-group, so conscious correction was not possible”
senseAN ANOMALY REPORTED AGAINST THEIR OWN ACCOUNT, IN THEIR OWN DISCUSSION: `While we found a positive increasing correlation between liking and choice of Dasani with increased exposure, REPORTED LIKING ITSELF DID NOT DIFFER ACROSS CONDITIONS. On the surface, this result seems INCONSISTENT WITH A FLUENCY ACCOUNT.` The exposure moved what people CHOSE without moving what they SAID they liked. **A measure and its supposed cause coming apart** - and the authors raise it themselves, under a heading of its own, rather than leaving it in a table— “While we found a positive increasing correlation between liking and choice of Dasani with increased exposure, reported liking itself did not differ across conditions”

conceptfluency — THE MECHANISM, and it is established by MISATTRIBUTION rather than asserted: give the feeling of ease another source to come from and the effect weakens, which is how you show a feeling rather than a memory is doing the work— “Study 2 shows that, if the experienced fluency generated by repeated exposure can be attributed to another source, then the effect will be attenuated”
concepttype of user — WHO WAS HOLDING THE BRAND - the context of the exposure, and the moderator that can bolster, dampen or overpower the fluency effect. It is what separates this from mere exposure, where the stimulus arrives without company— “When a type of user is discernible, however, this relationship is moderated by perceivers’ automatic responses to that group”
conceptmere exposure — the account this paper extends and departs from: mere exposure treats the stimulus as isolated, and the departure is that `the CONTEXT in which repeated exposure occurs matters`— “Our results go beyond an interesting application of mere exposure by showing that the context in which repeated exposure occurs matters”
