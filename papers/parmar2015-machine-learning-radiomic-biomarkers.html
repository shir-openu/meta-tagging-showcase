Methods Radiomicterm·dom Features A total of 440 radiomicterm·dom features were used in the analysis. These radiomicterm·dom features quantified tumor phenotypic characteristics on CT images and are divided into four feature groups: I) tumor intensity, II) shape, III) texture and IV) wavelet features. Tumor intensity based features estimated the first order statistics of the intensity histogram, whereas shape features described the 3D geometric properties of the tumor. Textural features, derived from the gray level co-occurrenceterm·mth (GLCMterm·mth) 21 and run length matrices (GLRLM) 22 , quantified the intra-tumor heterogeneityterm·dom. These textural features were computed by averaging their values over all thirteen directions. Wavelet features are the transformed domain representations of the intensity and textural features. These features were computed on different wavelet decompositions of the original image using a coiflet wavelet transformation. Matlab R2012b (The Mathworks, Natick, MA) was used for the image analysis. Radiomicterm·dom features were automatically extracted by our in-house developed radiomicsterm·dom image analysis software, which uses an adapted version of CERR (Computational Environment for Radiotherapy Research) 23 and Matlab for the preprocessing of medical images. Mathematical definitions of all radiomicterm·dom features, as well as the extraction methods, were previously described 8 .

Datasets In this study, we employed two NSCLC cohorts from the two different institutes of Netherlands: (1) Lung1:422 NSCLC patients treated at MAASTRO Clinic in Maastricht. (2) Lung2:225 NSCLC patients treated at Radboud University Medical Center in Nijmegen. CT-scans, manual delineations and clinical data were available for all included patients. More details on the included datasets are described in Supplementary-A . We dichotomized the censored continuous survival data using a cutoff time of 2 years. The patients who lived beyond the cutoff time were labeled as 1, whereas the deceased ones were labeled as 0. The objective of the study was to stratify patients into these two labeled survival classes. Two-years is considered as a relevant survival time for NSCLC patients and several other studies have designed their prediction models using a survival cutoff of 2 years 24 , 25 , 26 . We excluded the patients, which were followed for less than 2 years. It resulted in 310 patients in training cohort (Lung1) and 154 patients in validation cohort (Lung2). All the features were normalized using Z-score normalization.

Classifiers In machine-learningterm·mth, the classificationterm·mth is considered as a supervised learning task of inferring a function from labeled training data 16 . The training data consists of a set of examples, where each example is represented as a pair of an input vector (features) and a desired output value (target or category label). The classificationterm·mth algorithm (classifier) analyzes the training data and infers a hypothesis (function), which can be used for predicting the labels of unseen observations. Many classifiers belonging to different areas of computer science and statistics have been proposed in machine-learningterm·mth literature 29 . In our study, we used 12 machine-learningterm·mth classifiers arising from 12 classifier families (Bagging (BAG), Bayesian (BY), Boosting (BST), Decision trees (DT), Discriminant analysis (DA), Generalized linear models (GLM), Multiple adaptive regression splines (MARS), Nearest neighbors (NN), Neural networks (Nnet), Partial least square and principle component regression (PLSR), Random foreststerm·key (RF), and Support vector machines (SVM)). All classifiers were implemented using R package caret 30 , which provides a nice interface to access many machine-learningterm·mth algorithms in R. Classifiers were trained using the repeated (3 repeat iterations) 10 fold cross validation of training cohort (Lung1) and their predictive performance was evaluated in the validation cohort (Lung2) using area under ROC curve (AUC). Analysis Predictive Performance of Feature Selectionterm·mth and Classificationterm·mth Methods In order to investigate and compare different feature selectionterm·mth and classificationterm·mth methods, we created a three-dimensional parameter grid for the analysis. For each of the 14 feature selectionterm·mth methods, we incrementally selected features ranging from 5 up to 50, with an increment of 5 features (n = 5, 10, 15, 20, … , 50). Stabilityterm·key of Feature Selectionterm·mth and Classificationterm·mth Methods In order to assess the stabilityterm·key of feature selectionterm·mth methods, we used a stabilityterm·key measure proposed by Yu et al. 32 under the hard data perturbation settings 33 . We quantified the stabilityterm·key of a method as the similarity between the results obtained by the same feature selectionterm·mth method, when applied on the two non-overlapping partitions (of size N/2) of the training cohort (Lung1). To compute similarity between the two resultant feature sets, a weighted complete bipartite graph was constructed, where the two node sets corresponded to the two sets of selected features. The edge weights were assigned as the absolute Spearman correlation coefficient between the features at the nodes. We then applied the Hungarian algorithm 34 to identify the maximum weighted matching between the two node sets, and then similarity (stabilityterm·key) was quantified as the final matching cost. For each feature selectionterm·mth method, we computed the stabilityterm·key 100 times using a bootstrap approach and reported the median ± std values in the results.

Subsampling of the training cohort was done 100 times using a bootstrap approach. RSDterm·mth is the absolute value of the coefficient of variation and is often expressed in percentage. It should be noted that higher stabilityterm·key in the case of classifiers corresponds to lower RSDterm·mth values.

Stabilityterm·key and Predictive Performance In order to identify the highly reliable and accurate methods, we used the median values of AUC and stabilityterm·key as thresholds. We created two rank lists based on AUC & stabilityterm·key and cited the methods as highly accurate and reliable, which ranked in the top half of both the ranked lists. Feature selectionterm·mth methods having stabilityterm·key ≥0.735 (median stabilityterm·key of all feature selectionterm·mth methods) and AUC ≥ 0.615 (median AUC of all feature selectionterm·mth methods) are considered as highly reliable and accurate methods. Similarly, classificationterm·mth methods having RSDterm·mth ≤ 5.97 (median RSDterm·mth of all classifiers) and AUC ≥ 0.61 (median AUC of all classifiers) are considered as highly reliable and accurate ones.

Experimental Factors Affecting the Radiomicsterm·dom Based Survival Predictionterm·key There are three main experimental factors, which can potentially affect the prediction of radiomicsterm·dom based survival predictionterm·key: feature selectionterm·mth method, classificationterm·mth method and the number of selected features. Multifactor ANOVA was used to quantify the variability in AUC scores contributed by these factors and their interactions. In order to compare the variability contributed by each factor, the estimated variance components were divided by the total variance.

All the analysis was done using R software (R Core Team, Vienna, Austria) version 3.1.2 and Matlab R2012b (The Mathworks, Natick, MA) with Windows 7.

Discussion Medical imagingterm·mth is a routinely used and easily accessible source of information in clinical oncology. It serves as a non-invasive and cost-effective cancer diagnostic tool. Radiomicsterm·dom employs the medical imagingterm·mth data for the customization of cancer care and hence adds a new and promising dimension to precision oncologyterm·dom 2 , 3 , 8 . Moreover, it can also capture the intra-tumor heterogeneityterm·dom, which is often considered as an important biomarker in oncology 12 , 35 , 36 , 37 . A number of studies have built radiomicsterm·dom based predictive models for various clinical factors (tumor grades, survival outcomes, treatment response, etc.) 12 . For the successful realization of radiomicsterm·dom based predictive analyses, it is required to evaluate and compare different feature selectionterm·mth and predictive modeling methods, which was the primary objective of this study.

Various feature selectionterm·mth methods have been employed for high-throughput data mining problems 38 . In general, feature selectionterm·mth methods are categorized into three main categories: (1) filter methodsterm·mth (2) wrapperterm·mth methods and (3) embedded methods. In this study, we investigated 14 different filter based approaches for radiomicsterm·dom based survival predictionterm·key. We only used filter-based approaches because they are computationally more efficient and less prone to overfittingterm·dom than the wrapperterm·mth and embedded methods 18 , 27 . Furthermore, unlike wrapperterm·mth and embedded methods, filter methodsterm·mth are classifier independent. Thus, they allow separation of the modeling and feature selectionterm·mth component of the predictive analysis, which increases the generalizability of each component and hence the overall analysis.

We also investigated 12 machine-learningterm·mth classificationterm·mth methods belonging to 12 different classifier families. Many classifiers have been proposed in the machine-learningterm·mth literature. Theoretically speaking, these classifiers belong to different fields (classifier families) of computer science and statistics. Therefore, it could really be difficult to understand the underlying assumptions of each and every classifier and tune the parameters in an unbiased manner. The parameter tuning could be biased by user’s more (or lack of) expertise with some classifiers over the others. Usually, the studies, which propose a new classifier, only compare it to the reference classifiers of same family excluding the other classifier families. Even if classifiers belonging to different families are considered for comparison, these reference classifiers are usually implemented using simple tools and with limited parameter configurations while carefully tuning the proposed classifier. These could consequently bias the results in favor of the proposed classifiers 31 . In our study, we are not proposing any new classifier and we have used the same implementation tool (R package caret) for all the classifiers. Furthermore, to ensure unbiased usage of classifiers, we used parameter configurations that were previously defined by Fernandez-Delgado et al. 31 , in an exhaustive study of comparing 179 classifiers over 121 different datasets. These parameter configurations were selected from the literature and have been previously validated on a large number (121) of datasets belonging to different fields. Furthermore, in our study, the parameters were tuned using the repeated cross validation of training data only. Hence, our experimental design allowed us to evaluate different classificationterm·mth methods in an unbiased manner.

Our results show that the Wilcoxon test based feature selectionterm·mth method WLCX yields the highest predictive performance with the majority of classifiers. Interestingly, WLCX is a simple univariate method based on ranks, which does not take into account the redundancy of selected features during feature ranking. The majority of feature selectionterm·mth methods gave highest predictive performance when used with the random forestterm·key (RF) classifier. One could argue that with different parameter configurations, the performance of classificationterm·mth methods may improve further. An exhaustive parameter tuning could be investigated for evaluating the improvement of prediction performance. However, the required computational resources and high time complexity can hinder the exhaustive search. We expect that future radiomicterm·dom studies focusing on different clinical outcomes and similar analysis framework could provide better understanding in this regard. A limited number of methods, which are consistently high performing across different radiomicterm·dom studies, could be further assessed with an exhaustive parameter tuning. Nevertheless, It should be noted that random foreststerm·key (RF) have displayed high predictive performance in several other biomedical and other domain applications as well 31 . These results indicate that choosing the WLCX feature selectionterm·mth method and/or RF classificationterm·mth method increases predictive performance in radiomicsterm·dom.

Results related to our stabilityterm·key analysis provide another dimension for choosing the feature selectionterm·mth and classificationterm·mth methods. Depending upon the applications, one may give importance to the predictive performance or stabilityterm·key and accordingly opt for the required method. Results related to multifactor ANOVA indicated that the classificationterm·mth method is the most dominant source of variation in the prediction performance (AUC) and hence should be chosen carefully. Size of the selected feature subset contributed the least in the total variation of AUC.

Only few studies have investigated and compared different feature selectionterm·mth and machine-learningterm·mth modeling methods for radiomicsterm·dom based clinical predictions 19 , 20 . Recently, Hawkins et al. 19 have compared four different feature selectionterm·mth and classificationterm·mth methods for CT based survival predictionterm·key of NSCLC patients. This study, however, was limited by the small cohort size as the final results were obtained on only 40 patients. Furthermore, it also lacked an independent validation of the results. On the contrary, two independent radiomicterm·dom cohorts of sizes 310 and 154 patients were used in our analysis and an independent validation of the results was reported.

Our radiomicterm·dom analysis is focused on the prediction of two-year patient survival in NSCLC patients. It provides an unbiased evaluation of different machine-learningterm·mth methods of feature selectionterm·mth and classificationterm·mth. It could be considered as a reference for the future radiomicsterm·dom based predictive studies. Our results indicated that choosing Wilcoxon test based feature selectionterm·mth method WLCX and/or random forestterm·key (RF) classificationterm·mth method gives highest performance for radiomicsterm·dom based survival predictionterm·key. Furthermore, these methods also turned out reasonably stable against data perturbation and hence they could be preferred for radiomicsterm·dom based predictive studies. These results should be further tested in other radiomicsterm·dom based predictive studies, with different imaging modalities and in different cancer types.

It has been previously shown that for NSCLC patients, statistical models based on patient’s tumor and treatment characteristics provide significantly better predictions than the human expert 24 . Moreover, several other studies have highlighted the limitation of doctors’ prognostic capability for terminally ill cancer patients 39 , 40 , 41 . The predictions of human experts can suffer from inter-observer variability. On the contrary, statistical models could make the prediction system more deterministic if the parameter configurations and the training framework are fixed.

The potential clinical utility of radiomicsterm·dom based prognostic models has been stated in previous study 8 . With expanding radiomicsterm·dom cohorts and feature dimensions, we expect higher prediction performance in future radiomicterm·dom studies. Furthermore, the integrative studies like radiomics-genomics in combination with standard clinical covariates could also improvise the prediction performance and further validate the utility of these methods in clinical practice. Overall, our analysis is a step forward towards the enhancements of radiomicsterm·dom based clinical predictions.
