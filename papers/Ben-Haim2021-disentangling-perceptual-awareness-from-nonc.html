phenomenondouble dissociation of visual awareness in nonhuman primates
claim-typeexistence
transferfrom human cognitive science / experimental consciousness research on humans: They take the cross-over double-dissociation marker of nonconscious processing, developed and validated only in verbal human subjects, and transplant it into nonhuman-primate comparative research as a nonverbal test for awareness — “Critically, by importing one of the strictest indicators of genuine nonconscious processing in human studies”
transferfrom human cognitive science: the cross-over double dissociation of visual awareness, rebuilt as a nonverbal saccade task so that it can be run on a species that cannot be asked what it saw — “Much work in human cognitive science has shown that under certain circumstances, people display opposite behavioral signatures when they are consciously aware of stimuli (i.e., when they are exposed to clearly visible stimuli—supraliminal stimuli) versus when they are not aware (i.e., when they are exposed to stimuli that are flashed for a few milliseconds on the screen—subliminal stimuli).”
mechanismSubliminal cues capture gaze toward the cued (wrong) side and, without awareness, the subject cannot disengage before target onset, so the eyes are still at the wrong location when the target appears and the response is slowed — “subjects still failed to move their gaze to the target location ahead of time and maintained their gaze at the cue location during the final 100 ms prior to the target appearance”
mechanismConscious access to the cue lets the subject deploy a deliberate strategy that redirects gaze to the opposite location before the target appears, converting the same cue from interference into facilitation — “suggesting again that a conscious strategy was necessary in order for participants to redirect their gaze to the relevant location.”
mechanismBecause the two conditions push performance in opposite directions, the cross-over pattern itself is what separates aware from unaware processing in a nonverbal subject — “Since these opposite patterns of performance are predicted to emerge only if animals are aware in one condition (presenting functional facilitation) but unaware in the other (presenting persistent interference), they allow us to successfully disentangle the two types of processing in nonhuman species.”
mechanisma subliminal cue cannot be disengaged from, so it drags attention the wrong way; a supraliminal one can be reasoned about, so it helps - the two pull in opposite directions — “Researchers have argued that this phenomenon stems from the difficulty of disregarding a stimulus that is nonconsciously processed ( 33 , 34 ).”
mechanismthe design's force comes from predicting the REVERSE of what salience alone would predict, so a confound cannot produce the result — “our predictions for implicating visual awareness and nonconscious processing are predicted in the reverse direction of what would typically be expected when inhibiting salient versus nonsalient incongruent stimuli”
mechanismonly two processing modes can produce two opposite signatures, so the crossing itself is the inference - not either half of it — “Because this approach predicts that completely opposite signatures of performance would emerge only if there were both conscious and nonconscious processing modes, this framework can be used to reliably disentangle the two levels of processing in a nonhuman animal.”
relation — “All these important single dissociations of awareness exploration convincingly show that animals display properties similar to those seen in conscious or nonconscious processes.”
relation — “some advocate that consciousness may require complex processes like language, which is unique to adult humans ( 3 ) or a human-like theory of mind ( 4 )”
relation — “To examine nonconscious influence in monkeys, we adopted a spatial-cueing paradigm ( 37 – 40 ).”
replicationreplicated
limitationAwareness in monkeys could not be probed verbally, so the inference rests on the behavioural pattern alone. — “While of course we could not linguistically probe awareness in monkeys, monkeys displayed similar incongruent responses that required awareness in humans and critically also showed similar opposite performance with subliminal cues.”
limitationNo subjective reports were collected from the monkeys; the subliminal thresholds were imported from the human procedure. — “While we have not specifically collected the monkeys’ subjective reports, we employed the exact same subliminal thresholds as in humans.”
limitationThe nonconscious effect may arise from low-level attentional orienting or residual visual traces rather than from a richer nonconscious process. — “Whereas the nonconscious effect demonstrated here may possibly be generated by low-level nonconscious orienting of attention or from the subliminal cues’ leftover visual traces, it should be noted that it critically influenced free behavioral choices without participants awareness in both humans and monkeys.”
limitationThe inference rests on a behavioural signature, not on any access to experience - the explanatory gap the paper cites is not closed by it. — “For many, the “explanatory gap” in evidence that is needed to unambiguously infer animal consciousness is considered “as wide as ever” ( 32 ).”
limitationAwareness of the subliminal cue is established by self-report and a forced-choice test, both of which are null results rather than proof of absence. — “Participants’ performance did not differ from chance at the individual binomial level or at the group level”
limitationThe whole approach assumes the human dissociation transfers to another species; before this paper there was no evidence it does. — “To date, we have no evidence for such a double dissociation with opposite predictions of awareness in other species.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingSubliminal incongruent cues slowed human target detection relative to baseline even though participants reported and tested as unaware of them, establishing nonconscious processing. — “The observation that subliminal cues significantly hindered the detection of the targets relative to baseline, even though participants were unaware of the cues or their locations, demonstrates that these cues were processed nonconsciously.”
findingRhesus monkeys showed the identical subliminal slowdown relative to the nonpredictive-cue baseline in the spatial-cueing task. — “Critically, however, when the monkeys were tested in the subliminal condition, they were significantly slower to detect the target location compared to the control baseline condition with two nonpredictive subliminal cues”
findingIn the forced-guessing task monkeys performed below chance under subliminal cueing and never learned the rule, mirroring the human pattern. — “Just as we observed in human participants, monkeys failed to ignore the subliminal cues and showed no signs of learning”
findingHuman supraliminal facilitation depended on consciously grasping the cue's predictive rule; participants who did not report that insight showed no speed-up over baseline. — “In contrast, participants in the same supraliminal condition, who reported that they were not aware of the supraliminal cue’s predictive value, were not facilitated (mean = 324 ms) relative to baseline (mean = 312 ms)”
findingMerely telling participants that flashing cues exist flipped their performance, showing that awareness of the cue and not its low salience is what drives the effect. — “This suggests that cue awareness rather than cue saliency (which remained constantly difficult to see, here at 33 ms) is the critical factor in performance on our studies.”
findingWithin the same participants and the same 17 ms cue, trials rated as unseen produced below-chance performance while trials rated as seen produced above-chance performance. — “Remarkably, participants’ performance was still significantly below chance when they reported no awareness of where the cue was presented (0 rating)”
findingThe headline result is that a nonhuman primate reproduces the human double-dissociation signature, which the authors read as evidence for two distinct processing modes in monkeys. — “We found that one nonhuman species—the rhesus monkey—exhibits a double-dissociation performance remarkably similar to that observed in humans.”
findingHumans are much faster with a visible incongruent cue than with an invisible one - the conscious condition helps. — “participants were considerably faster to identify the target location following supraliminal incongruent cues than subliminal incongruent cues of which they reported not being aware [ F (1,3585) = 315.66, P < 0.0001, Fig. 1 B ]”
findingThe same cue presented invisibly does the opposite: participants who saw nothing were nevertheless slowed relative to baseline. — “all of the participants performing in the subliminal condition, regardless of block order, reported that they did not see any cues § yet showed a significant slowdown in detecting the target location compared to the baseline with two nonpredictive subliminal cues [ F (1,2989) = 13.36, P < 0.0001, Fig. 1 C , Left ]”
findingRhesus monkeys show the same two opposite signatures, which is the paper's basis for two processing modes in a nonhuman species. — “In a series of seven experiments, we first established these signatures in humans using both known and newly developed nonverbal double-dissociation tasks and then identified similar signatures in rhesus monkeys ( Macaca mulatta).”
findingEarlier work established only single dissociations, which show animals have properties resembling conscious or nonconscious processing without separating the two. — “All these important single dissociations of awareness exploration convincingly show that animals display properties similar to those seen in conscious or nonconscious processes.”
