phenomenontask-unrelated thought (mind-wandering)
claim-typeexistence
mechanismDeliberate off-task thought raises alpha activity, which inhibits irrelevant sensory input in a top-down manner and thereby protects the internally generated train of thought. — “We found that when participants were engaged in deliberate off-task thought, alpha activity was increased, a pattern that is often observed when individuals are deliberately engaged in internal tasks 71,110,118, and may reflect patterns of top down inhibition of irrelevant sensory input.”
mechanismSpontaneous off-task thought arises because top-down maintenance of the task set fails, which shows up as jointly reduced P2 (higher-order perceptual processing) and N2 (conflict monitoring) responses. — “The combination of reduced P2 and N2 linked with spontaneous off-task thought suggests that this aspect of off-task experience may be associated with failure in the top down maintenance of the appropriate task set.”
mechanismIntention determines whether executive control is being used to organise the internal train of thought or is intermittently failing: deliberate off-task states reflect motivated maintenance of a task set, spontaneous ones reflect lapses in maintaining it. — “In particular, based on our data we may expect that deliberate off-task thought is linked to the motivated maintenance of a task set, while more spontaneous off-task episodes are linked to intermittent failures to maintain task set in attention.”
relation — “These results support prior studies that show reduced processing of stimuli in this time range for both online and offline measures of task unrelated thinking26,36,38,39.”
relation — “Consistent with component process accounts of ongoing thought these data suggest that periods of off-task thought with different experiential features depend on a different balance of neural processes.”
relation — “This interpretation seems to be consistent with prior studies that suggest that spontaneous off-task thought is primarily linked to lack of external alertness and lower executive control117.”
differs indTUT → sTUT — WHICH INSTRUMENT IS ASKED. Alpha power and cortical phase-locking separate them; the P3 does not, being attenuated off-task regardless of intention. The distinction the paper's title asserts holds on two measures and fails on the one the literature relies on most — “Event-related potentials, such as the P3, were attenuated during off-task states, regardless of whether they were intentional or not. In contrast, increased alpha power and cortical phase-locking were linked to deliberate off-task thoughts, while reductions of evoked sensory response were prevalent in spontaneous off-task episodes”
replicationuntested
limitationThe four-trial pre-probe window is an a priori choice, since the true onset of an attentional shift is unknown. — “Although the temporal dynamics and the exact onset of attentional shifts remain unknown and difficult to assess31, we chose a window of four SART trials prior to a thought probe as reflecting the reported attentional state.”
limitationThe self-caught probe condition structurally lacks on-task reports, so the two condition pairs are not symmetric. — “Due to the nature of the task, participants were only instructed to trigger a SC probe when they caught themselves off-task and as a result the SC condition lacked on-task reports.”
limitationTrial-count exclusions cut the on-task versus off-task comparison to 15 participants. — “For subsequent analyses participants that did not have at least 8 trials (amounting to a total of 32 epochs) for both conditions of a condition pair were not considered which reduced the number of participants for the PC_OT/MW pair to 15.”
limitationThe most established marker in this literature does not distinguish the two states the paper is about: it is attenuated off-task whether the thought was intentional or not. — “Event-related potentials, such as the P3, were attenuated during off-task states, regardless of whether they were intentional or not.”
limitationThe artefact-rejection threshold is a choice, and the authors state which way they wanted it to err. — “threshold for rejection was set at a conservative 95% probability to favor preservation of neural activity”
limitationChannels were excluded on a stated susceptibility argument rather than on measured artefact. — “channels in close proximity to the face were excluded on the grounds of a higher susceptibility to artifacts”
limitationThe paper's central distinction is instrument-relative and the abstract says so in consecutive sentences: the P3 treats deliberate and spontaneous off-task thought as one state, alpha power and phase-locking treat them as two. — “Event-related potentials, such as the P3, were attenuated during off-task states, regardless of whether they were intentional or not. In contrast, increased alpha power and cortical phase-locking were linked to deliberate off-task thoughts, while reductions of evoked sensory response were prevalent in spontaneous off-task episodes”

findingProbe-caught off-task periods showed reduced P1 and P2 amplitude over the right parieto-occipital site PO4 and reduced amplitude over parieto-central sites in the P3 window (268-388 ms). — “To sum up, the PC_OT/MW condition pair revealed a right parieto-central attenuation as observed by a reduction in P1 and P2 amplitude over PO4 and reduced amplitude over parieto-central sites for the P3 time-interval (268 - 388 msec; see Figure 2).”
findingAlpha power in the trials preceding a report was significantly higher before deliberate off-task thought (1.54 dB) than before spontaneous off-task thought (0.58 dB). — “Follow-up t-tests determined alpha activity (Figure 5, C and supplemental Figure 2, right panel) to be significantly higher during dTUT pre-trials (M:1.54 dB SD: 1.44) when compared to sTUT (M:.58 dB SD: 1.2) [t(17)=2.25 p=.038].”
findingInter-trial phase coherence was significantly greater before deliberate than before spontaneous off-task thought (.52 vs .45 across all averaged channels). — “Lastly, two-tailed t-tests were found to be significant for ITPC values for all averaged channels [t(17)=2.8 p=.012] with increased coherence for dTUT (M: .52; SD: .1) relative to sTUT (M: .45; SD: .1)”
findingThe P2 component was significantly smaller before spontaneous than before deliberate off-task thought at both PO3 and PO4. — “Post-hoc t-tests showed significantly decreased amplitude for the sTUT condition for the P2 component on both PO3 (dTUT M:5.01µV SD: 4.41; MW M: 3.64µV SD: 3.01)[t(17)=2.4 p=.028] and PO4 (dTUT M: 4.44µV SD: 3.73; MW M: 3.56µV SD: 2.62)[t(17)=2.61 p=.018].”
findingFronto-central delta/theta power at FCz was significantly lower relative to baseline before off-task reports (-2.69 dB) than before on-task reports (-1.89 dB). — “Delta/theta power (thereafter referred to as FMT) measured at FCz [t(14)=2.45 p=.028], showed a significant decrease for the TUT condition (M:-2.69 dB SD: 1.24) relative to OT (M:-1.89 dB SD: .95) (Figure 3, right panel).”
findingThe stimulus-evoked alpha-beta event-related desynchronization differed by attentional state, reaching -2.27 dB before off-task reports versus -1.13 dB before on-task reports. — “T-tests unveiled a significantly decreased alpha-beta ERD (Figure 3, B) for MW pre-trials (M:-2.27 dB SD: 1.55) in comparison with OT pre-trials (M:-1.13 dB SD: 1.26) [t(14)=2.27 p=.039].”
findingInter-trial phase coherence did not distinguish on-task from off-task states in any of the three electrode sets, so the phase-coherence effect was specific to the deliberate/spontaneous contrast. — “T-tests for the ITPC values for all three sets of electrodes tested (all averaged nonboundary channels, FCz and PO3/4) failed to return significant differences for the PC_OT/MW condition pair.”
findingTonic (non-evoked) alpha power showed no significant on-task versus off-task difference at any electrode selection. — “The difference in tonic alpha power did not reach statistical significance for any of the tested channel constellations.”
findingOff-task thought attenuated evoked potentials whether or not it was intentional, while alpha power and phase-locking rose specifically for deliberate off-task thought and evoked sensory responses dropped specifically for spontaneous off-task thought. — “In contrast, increased alpha power and cortical phase-locking were linked to deliberate off-task thoughts, while reductions of evoked sensory response were prevalent in spontaneous off-task episodes.”
