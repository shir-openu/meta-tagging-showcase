phenomenontask-unrelated thought (mind-wandering)— “The present study used EEG to seek support for these component-process accounts by examining the neural correlates of deliberate and spontaneous task unrelated thought.”
claim-typeexistence— “In contrast, increased alpha power and cortical phase-locking were linked to deliberate off-task thoughts, while reductions of evoked sensory response were prevalent in spontaneous off-task episodes.”
method — evidence— “Electroencephalography (EEG) was used to record ongoing brain activity during this period.”
mechanismDeliberate off-task thought raises alpha activity, which inhibits irrelevant sensory input in a top-down manner and thereby protects the internally generated train of thought.— “We found that when participants were engaged in deliberate off-task thought, alpha activity was increased, a pattern that is often observed when individuals are deliberately engaged in internal tasks 71,110,118, and may reflect patterns of top down inhibition of irrelevant sensory input.”
mechanismSpontaneous off-task thought arises because top-down maintenance of the task set fails, which shows up as jointly reduced P2 (higher-order perceptual processing) and N2 (conflict monitoring) responses.— “The combination of reduced P2 and N2 linked with spontaneous off-task thought suggests that this aspect of off-task experience may be associated with failure in the top down maintenance of the appropriate task set.”
mechanismIntention determines whether executive control is being used to organise the internal train of thought or is intermittently failing: deliberate off-task states reflect motivated maintenance of a task set, spontaneous ones reflect lapses in maintaining it.— “In particular, based on our data we may expect that deliberate off-task thought is linked to the motivated maintenance of a task set, while more spontaneous off-task episodes are linked to intermittent failures to maintain task set in attention.”
replicates prior ERP studies of reduced stimulus processing during task-unrelated thinking (refs 26, 36, 38, 39)— “These results support prior studies that show reduced processing of stimuli in this time range for both online and offline measures of task unrelated thinking26,36,38,39.”
builds-on component process accounts of ongoing thought— “Consistent with component process accounts of ongoing thought these data suggest that periods of off-task thought with different experiential features depend on a different balance of neural processes.”
builds-on prior work linking spontaneous off-task thought to low external alertness and low executive control (ref 117)— “This interpretation seems to be consistent with prior studies that suggest that spontaneous off-task thought is primarily linked to lack of external alertness and lower executive control117.”
sensetask-unrelated thought - DELIBERATE (dTUT): the off-task state engaged in intentionally, one of two kinds the study separates— “Our study set out to explore the common and distinct neurocognitive processes that underlie patterns of intentional and unintentional off-task states, deliberate (dTUT) and spontaneous task-unrelated thought (sTUT) respectively.”
sensetask-unrelated thought - AS THE P3 SEES IT: not two kinds at all, since the component is attenuated off-task regardless of intention— “Event-related potentials, such as the P3, were attenuated during off-task states, regardless of whether they were intentional or not.”

senseoff-task thought - framed by the field it comes from as 'a family of states with overlapping experiential neural and cognitive features' rather than as a category with members— “Recent views from both psychology 24 and neuroscience25 have argued that off-task experiences can be understood as a family of states with overlapping experiential neural and cognitive features”

differs indTUT —distinct-from→ sTUT — WHICH INSTRUMENT IS ASKED. Alpha power and cortical phase-locking separate them; the P3 does not, being attenuated off-task regardless of intention. The distinction the paper's title asserts holds on two measures and fails on the one the literature relies on most— “Event-related potentials, such as the P3, were attenuated during off-task states, regardless of whether they were intentional or not. In contrast, increased alpha power and cortical phase-locking were linked to deliberate off-task thoughts, while reductions of evoked sensory response were prevalent in spontaneous off-task episodes”
notedTUT —distinct-from→ sTUT — WHETHER THESE ARE TWO KINDS OF THOUGHT OR ONE DEPENDS ENTIRELY ON WHICH MEASURE IS ASKED, and the paper's abstract puts both answers in consecutive sentences. The P3 - the literature's most established marker, and the record already carries a limitation saying so - is attenuated off-task 'REGARDLESS OF WHETHER THEY WERE INTENTIONAL OR NOT', so on that instrument the two states are ONE. Alpha power and cortical phase-locking separate them. A category claim that is true of one instrument and false of another, in one dataset.— “Event-related potentials, such as the P3, were attenuated during off-task states, regardless of whether they were intentional or not.
