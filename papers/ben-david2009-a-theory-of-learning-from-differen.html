mechanisma distance defined by the classifier you were going to use the L1 divergence takes a supremum over all measurable subsets and is rejected as unestimable and over-strict; the H-divergence takes it over the hypothesis class. So the distance between two FIXED distributions is a function of H - a richer class sees the domains as further apart, and the bound loosens because the model got more expressive rather than because the data moved— “Bounding the error in terms of the L1 divergence between distributions has two disadvantages. First, it cannot be accurately estimated from finite samples of arbitrary distributions (Batu et al. 2000; Kifer et al. 2004) and therefore has limited usefulness in practice. Second, for our purposes the L1 divergence is an overly strict measure…
mechanismmeasure the difference by trying to detect it label every unlabeled source instance 0 and every unlabeled target instance 1, train a classifier to tell them apart, and read the divergence off its error. If the domains are indistinguishable to H the divergence is zero. The measurement IS the adversary, which is why the whole domain-adversarial literature follows from this paragraph— “Our basic plan of attack will be as follows: Label each unlabeled source instance with 0 and unlabeled target instance as 1. Then train a classifier to discriminate between source and target instances. The H-divergence is immediately computable from the error.”
mechanismthe third term is the assumption target error is bounded by source error plus divergence plus lambda. The first two come from unlabeled data. Lambda is the error of the best joint hypothesis and needs labelled target data to evaluate - the thing the setting is defined by not having. THE BOUND IS USABLE EXACTLY WHEN ITS UNCHECKABLE TERM IS SMALL— “When the combined error of the ideal joint hypothesis is large, then there is no classifier that performs well on both the source and target domains, so we cannot hope to find a good target hypothesis by training only on the source domain.”
mechanismthe half of the difference that is assumed away two domains can differ in their labeling functions as well as in their input distributions. One clause - `since we expect the labeling function difference to be small in practice` - removes the first from the theory. Everything downstream measures distributional shift and inherits the assumption without restating it— “Since we expect the labeling function difference to be small in practice, we focus here on measuring distribution divergence, and especially on how to estimate it with finite samples of unlabeled data from DS and DT .”
mechanisman intractable quantity approximated by a surrogate computing the H-divergence needs the minimum-error hypothesis for the source-versus-target problem, which is intractable for most classes. What is used instead is the error of a hypothesis minimising a convex upper bound - so the reported divergence is an approximation of an approximation, and the paper says so— “Of course, minimizing error for most reasonable hypothesis classes is a computationally intractable problem. Nonetheless, as we shall see in Sect. 7, the error of hypotheses trained to minimize convex upper bounds on error are useful in approximating the H-divergence.”
