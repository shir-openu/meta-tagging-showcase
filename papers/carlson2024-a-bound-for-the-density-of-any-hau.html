phenomenondensity bounds for Hausdorff spaces— “The density of a topological space X, denoted by d(X), is the least cardinality of a dense subset of X.”
claim-typeexistence— “We generalize this result to the class of all Hausdorff spaces by introducing the nonquasiregularity degree nq(X), which is countable when X is quasiregular, and showing d(X) ≤ πχ(X)c(X)nq(X) for any Hausdorff space X.”
method — evidence— “We prove now the central result in this paper.”
mechanismThe nonquasiregularity degree simultaneously generalizes quasiregularity and the closed pseudocharacter, so a single inequality in nq(X) specialises to both Šapirovskiĭ's regular-space bound and Sun's Hausdorff bound.— “This shows that the nonquasiregular degree generalizes both quasiregularity in spaces for which nq(X) is defined and the closed pseudocharacter in Hausdorff spaces.”
extends Šapirovskiĭ's density bound d(X) ≤ πχ(X)c(X) for regular spaces— “We generalize this result to the class of all Hausdorff spaces by introducing the nonquasiregularity degree nq(X), which is countable when X is quasiregular, and showing d(X) ≤ πχ(X)c(X)nq(X) for any Hausdorff space X.”
extends Sun's bound πχ(X)c(X)ψc(X) for the cardinality of a Hausdorff space— “This improves Sun’s theorem and shows that the degree to which a space is nonquasiregular plays an important role in the cardinality of a Hausdorff space.”
replicates Šapirovskiĭ's and Sun's theorems (re-derived from one argument)— “This results in a unified proof of ˇSapirovski˘ı’s and Sun’s theorems, gives an improved bound for the cardinality of a Hausdorff space, and answers Question 3.3 in [2].”
sensenonregularity - AS A DEGREE RATHER THAN A DICHOTOMY: instead of a space being regular or not, the paper introduces a cardinal measuring HOW nonquasiregular it is, and the classical bound then holds for every space with that degree as a factor— “we introduce the nonquasiregular degree nq(X) (Definition 2.2) which gives a “measure” of how nonquasiregular a space is.”
sensequasiregular - THE HYPOTHESIS WEAKENED ONCE BEFORE IT WAS MEASURED: the classical result was stated for regular spaces, then observed to need only quasiregularity and not even Hausdorff, and only afterwards replaced by a degree
sensethe degree - SMALL FOR THE SPACES THAT MATTER: nq(X) is countable when the space is quasiregular and bounded by the closed pseudocharacter when it is Hausdorff, which is what stops the new factor from making the bound vacuous
