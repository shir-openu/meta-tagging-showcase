phenomenondensity bounds for Hausdorff spaces
claim-typeexistence
mechanismThe nonquasiregularity degree simultaneously generalizes quasiregularity and the closed pseudocharacter, so a single inequality in nq(X) specialises to both Šapirovskiĭ's regular-space bound and Sun's Hausdorff bound. — “This shows that the nonquasiregular degree generalizes both quasiregularity in spaces for which nq(X) is defined and the closed pseudocharacter in Hausdorff spaces.”
relation — “We generalize this result to the class of all Hausdorff spaces by introducing the nonquasiregularity degree nq(X), which is countable when X is quasiregular, and showing d(X) ≤ πχ(X)c(X)nq(X) for any Hausdorff space X.”
relation — “This improves Sun’s theorem and shows that the degree to which a space is nonquasiregular plays an important role in the cardinality of a Hausdorff space.”
relation — “This results in a unified proof of ˇSapirovski˘ı’s and Sun’s theorems, gives an improved bound for the cardinality of a Hausdorff space, and answers Question 3.3 in [2].”
replicationuntested
limitationThe new cardinal function nq(X) is not defined for every topological space, so the main bound does not apply universally. — “However, there are spaces for which nq(X) is not defined.”
limitationA compact T1 space is exhibited for which the invariant fails to exist. — “Example 3.4 gives an example of compact, T1 space for which nq(X) is not defined.”
limitationThe nq(X) factor cannot simply be dropped: examples are given where the plain bound fails. — “We give two examples of spaces X where nq(X) is required in our density bound; that is, d(X) > πχ(X)c(X).”
limitationThe two classical bounds this paper unifies had complicated proofs that were quite different from one another, which is the gap the paper exists to close. — “again using a complicated closing-off argument, but quite different than the proof of ˇSapirovski˘ı’s density bound”
limitationThe class of spaces the theorem covers is named after the invariant being defined on them, so membership is settled by whether the quantity exists rather than by a topological property. — “Definition 2.3. A space X is an nq-space if nq(X) is defined”

findingThe density of any space for which the nonquasiregularity degree is defined — in particular every Hausdorff space and every quasiregular space — is at most πχ(X) times c(X) times nq(X). — “Our main result is that d(X) ≤ πχ(X)c(X)nq(X) for any space X for which nq(X) is defined, including Hausdorff spaces and quasiregular spaces.”
findingIn a Hausdorff space the new cardinal invariant stays small, being bounded above by the closed pseudocharacter. — “Importantly, if X is Hausdorff then nq(X) is “small” in the sense that nq(X) ≤ ψc(X).”
findingThe single bound yields one common proof of Šapirovskiĭ's density bound for regular spaces and of Sun's cardinality bound πχ(X)c(X)ψc(X) for Hausdorff spaces, which previously had unrelated proofs. — “This results in a unified proof of both ˇSapirovski˘ı’s density bound for regular spaces and Sun’s bound πχ(X)c(X)ψc(X) for the cardinality of a Hausdorff space X.”
findingIt follows that the cardinality of any Hausdorff space is at most πχ(X)c(X)nq(X)wψc(X), which improves Sun's bound because wψc(X) never exceeds ψc(X) in Hausdorff spaces. — “A corollary is that |X| ≤ πχ(X)c(X)nq(X)wψc (X) for any Hausdorff space X, where wψc(X) was introduced in [2] and satisfies wψc(X) ≤ ψc(X) if X is Hausdorff.”
findingThe number of regular open sets of an nq-space is likewise bounded by πχ(X)c(X)nq(X). — “Let X be an nq-space. Then |RO(X)| ≤ πχ(X)c(X)nq(X).”
findingIn a Hausdorff space the nonquasiregularity degree is bounded above by the Lindelöf number. — “It is also shown that if X is Hausdorff then nq(X) ≤ L(X).”
findingHence Lindelöf Hausdorff spaces form a new class, beyond the quasiregular ones, satisfying Šapirovskiĭ's inequality d(X) ≤ πχ(X)c(X). — “If X is Lindel¨of and Hausdorff then d(X) ≤ πχ(X)c(X).”
findingTwo spaces are exhibited, one of them Urysohn, whose density strictly exceeds πχ(X)c(X), showing the extra factor nq(X) cannot be dropped from the bound. — “We give two examples of spaces X where nq(X) is required in our density bound; that is, d(X) > πχ(X)c(X). One of these examples is even Urysohn.”
findingThe nonquasiregularity degree is not defined for every space: the cofinite topology on an infinite set is compact and T1 yet has no nq(X). — “Example 3.4 gives an example of compact, T1 space for which nq(X) is not defined.”
quantitynq(X) = ℵ0 — For a quasiregular space the nonquasiregularity degree takes its least possible value, the first infinite cardinal — i.e. quasiregular spaces are exactly the 'countably nonquasiregular' ones. — “In the latter case we have nq(X) = ℵ0.”
quantityχ(X) = 2κ and d(X) > cf (22κ ) > 2κ — In van Douwen's example the character equals 2^κ while the density strictly exceeds 2^κ, so density outruns πχ(X)c(X) and the nq factor is unavoidable. — “It can be seen that X is Hausdorff, χ(X) = 2κ and d(X) > cf (22κ ) > 2κ.”
