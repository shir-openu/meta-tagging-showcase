mechanismdesign alternatives are chosen by asking two stated questions about UNDERSTANDABILITY - how hard is each to explain, and how easily can a reader completely grasp it and its implications - and the authors state plainly that this analysis is highly subjective — “There were numerous points in the design of Raft where we had to choose among alternative approaches. In these situations we evaluated the alternatives based on understandability: how hard is it to explain each alternative (for example, how complex is its state space, and does it have subtle implications?), and how easy will it be for a reader to completely understand the approach and its implications? We recognize that there is a high degree of subjectivity in such analysis; nonetheless, we used two techniques that are generally applicable.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
mechanismthe first technique is problem decomposition: the problem is divided into pieces that can be solved, explained AND understood relatively independently, and the paper names the four it used — “The first technique is the well-known approach of problem decomposition: wherever possible, we divided problems into separate pieces that could be solved, explained, and understood relatively independently. For example, in Raft we separated leader election, log replication, safety, and membership changes.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
mechanismthe second technique reduces the STATE SPACE rather than the code - fewer states to consider, more coherence, nondeterminism removed where possible - and it is made concrete by two rules about what logs are not allowed to do — “Our second approach was to simplify the state space by reducing the number of states to consider, making the system more coherent and eliminating nondeterminism where possible. Specifically, logs are not allowed to have holes, and Raft limits the ways in which logs can become inconsistent with each other.”
mechanismNONDETERMINISM IS DELIBERATELY ADDED WHERE IT SHRINKS THE STATE SPACE: randomised approaches handle all possible choices in one fashion - choose any, it does not matter - so randomisation is used to simplify leader election even though the general policy was to eliminate it — “Although in most cases we tried to eliminate nondeterminism, there are some situations where nondeterminism actually improves understandability. In particular, randomized approaches introduce nondeterminism, but they tend to reduce the state space by handling all possible choices in a similar fashion (“choose any; it doesn’t matter”). We used randomization to simplify the Raft leader election algorithm.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
mechanismthe comparison is a within-subject crossover: every participant learns both algorithms and takes both quizzes, with the ORDER reversed for about half of them, so that individual differences and the experience gained from the first half are both accounted for — “Each student watched one video, took the corresponding quiz, watched the second video, and took the second quiz. About half of the participants did the Paxos portion first and the other half did the Raft portion first in order to account for both individual differences in performance and experience gained from the first portion of the study.”
mechanismthe two ways the experiment FAVOURED THE COMPETITOR are stated by the authors rather than left to a reader: some participants had prior experience with the rival algorithm, and the rival's teaching video was longer — “We tried to make the comparison between Paxos and Raft as fair as possible. The experiment favored Paxos in two ways: 15 of the 43 participants reported having some prior experience with Paxos, and the Paxos video is 14% longer than the Raft video.”
mechanismthe regression model's estimate of the quiz effect is reported as LARGER than the raw score difference, and the gap between the two is explained by prior experience with the rival algorithm rather than set aside — “The model predicts that the choice of quiz produces a 12.5-point difference in favor of Raft. This is significantly higher than the observed difference of 4.9 points, because many of the actual students had prior Paxos experience, which helped Paxos considerably, whereas it helped Raft slightly less.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
mechanismthe participants' own preference is collected AND THEN DISCOUNTED by the authors, who name both reasons it may be unreliable: self-report against measured scores, and the participants' possible knowledge of the hypothesis being tested — “An overwhelming majority of participants reported Raft would be easier to implement and explain (33 of 41 for each question). However, these self-reported feelings may be less reliable than participants’ quiz scores, and participants may have been biased by knowledge of our hypothesis that Raft is easier to understand.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
mechanismthe correctness argument is anchored to a formal specification that is itself the subject of the proof, and the paper states exactly how far the mechanisation reaches - one property mechanically proven, resting on invariants that have not been mechanically checked — “We have developed a formal specification and a proof of safety for the consensus mechanism described in Section 5. The formal specification [31] makes the information summarized in Figure 2 completely precise using the TLA+ specification language [17]. It is about 400 lines long and serves as the subject of the proof. It is also useful on its own for anyone implementing Raft. We have mechanically proven the Log Completeness Property using the TLA proof system [7]. However, this proof relies on invariants that have not been mechanically checked” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
