phenomenonautomatic social behavior priming— “We propose that social behavior is often triggered automatically on the mere presence of relevant situational features; this behavior is unmediated by conscious perceptual or judgmental processes.”
claim-typeexistence— “Our central message is that social behavior can be triggered automatically by features of the environment.”
method — evidence— “The three experiments we report in this article were designed to provide a definitive test of this hypothesis.”
mechanismautomatic behavior priming (perception-behavior link): activating a trait or stereotype concept directly and unconsciously increases the tendency to act in line with it— “participants for whom an elderly stereotype was primed walked more slowly down the hallway when leaving the experiment than did control participants, consistent with the content of that stereotype”
extends priming research on social perception— “We argue here that such passive, automatic effects of priming need not be limited to social perception.”
extends Berkowitz and LePage (1967) and Carver et al. (1983)— “These studies showed that the intentional behavior could be affected in intensity or duration by the aggression priming manipulation (the presence of guns or prior exposure to synonyms of aggression), but they did not show the behavior to be produced automatically, in the absence of that explicitly given intention.”
builds-on Carver et al. (1983) behavioral schema model— “Thus, our results are in harmony with those of Carver et al. (1983), who advanced a "behavioral schema" model as an explanation for modeling effects.”
replicates Devine (1989, Experiment 2)— “This finding corresponds to that of Devine (1989, Experiment 2), who found that the automatic effect of the African American stereotype on social perception did not vary as a function of level of consciously expressed prejudice, as measured by the Modern Racism Scale.”
sensePRIMING DEFINED AS AN ORDINARY FEATURE OF BEING SOMEWHERE: `Priming refers to the INCIDENTAL ACTIVATION of knowledge structures, such as trait concepts and stereotypes, BY THE CURRENT SITUATIONAL CONTEXT.` **Not a laboratory operation - a description of what situations do to people continuously**, of which the experiment is a controlled instance. That is the same move srull-wyer1979 makes when it says accessibility rises with the number of activations `IN THE PAST`, and it is what licenses generalising from a word-scramble task to walking down a corridor— “Priming refers to the incidental activation of knowledge structures, such as trait concepts and stereotypes, by the current situational context”
senseTHE DESIGN LOGIC IN ONE SENTENCE: `The same priming techniques that have been shown in prior research to influence IMPRESSION FORMATION produce similar effects when THE DEPENDENT MEASURE IS SWITCHED to social behavior.` **Only the dependent measure changed.** The manipulation is held constant against the 1977-79 accessibility work and the outcome is swapped from a judgement to an action - which makes this a claim about the SCOPE of an established effect rather than a new effect. **And it means the replication debate about this paper is unavoidably also about higgins1977 and srull-wyer1979**, both of which are in this corpus— “The same priming techniques that have been shown in prior research to influence impression formation produce similar effects when the dependent measure is switched to social behavior”
senseTHE EFFECT TAKES THE CONTENT OF WHAT WAS PRIMED, WHICH IS WHAT RULES OUT AROUSAL OR MOOD: rudeness produced interrupting `more quickly and frequently`, the elderly stereotype produced walking `more slowly down the hallway ... CONSISTENT WITH THE CONTENT OF THAT STEREOTYPE`, and the third prime produced hostility. **Three primes, three different behaviours, each matching its own prime** - a general activation account predicts one direction for all three and gets none of them— “In Experiment 2, participants for whom an elderly stereotype was primed walked more slowly down the hallway when leaving the experiment than did control participants, consistent with the content of that stereotype”
senseAND TWO THEORETICAL PERSPECTIVES ARE OFFERED WITH NEITHER CHOSEN. First, situationally linked behaviour: `a person has behavioral responses (e.g., assertiveness, anger, patience) that are ASSOCIATIVELY LINKED TO PARTICULAR SITUATIONS.` Then: `another theoretical perspective CONSISTENT WITH the present findings is that of an AUTOMATIC PERCEPTION-BEHAVIOR LINK.` **Both are said to fit and the paper stops there.** dijksterhuis1998, tagged one record earlier, takes the second and builds on it - so the corpus holds the fork and the branch that was taken— “One explanation for the present results is in terms of situationally linked behavior; that is, that a person has behavioral responses (e.g., assertiveness, anger, patience) that are associatively linked to particular situations”
controlthe priming condition was assigned by a SECOND EXPERIMENTER who had no contact with the participant — held — rules out that the experimenter who ran the session knew the condition and shaped the behaviour being measured - the classic experimenter-expectancy route to a priming effect— “Which of the three versions (rude, polite, or neutral priming) the participant received had been randomly determined by another experimenter who did not have any contact with the participant, with the constraint that approximately equal numbers of participants received each of the forms.”
controlparticipants were partially debriefed and questioned about whether they thought the scrambled-sentence task had influenced them — held — rules out that participants worked out the manipulation and complied with what they took to be the hypothesis— “After the participant had completed this task, he or she was partially debriefed and questioned concerning how they thought the first, scrambled-sentence test might have influenced them in the rest of the experiment.”

notetrait construct —causes→ behavior — **`CONSISTENT WITH IT` AND `SUBSEQUENT UNRELATED CONTEXT` ARE BOTH IN THE QUOTE AND BOTH ARE LOAD-BEARING.** The behaviour matches the CONTENT of what was primed, which excludes a general arousal account; and the second context is UNRELATED, which is what makes it a carry-over rather than a demand effect. limitationThe paper describes itself as a first demonstration whose boundary conditions are not yet known.— “As a first demonstration of automatic social behavior, the limits and parameters of these effects remain to be established.”
limitationThe Experiment 1 timing measure suffered from a severe ceiling effect because most participants never interrupted.— “Fully 21 of the 34 participants did not interrupt at all in the 10 min available to them, so that the time variable suffered from a severe ceiling effect.”
limitationThe authors concede the strength of behavioural versus judgemental priming effects cannot really be compared across their situations.— “Although ultimately one cannot really compare the strength of priming effects on behavioral versus judgmental measures, because there are too many differences between the experimental situations to allow for a meaningful comparison, we can point to some differences that are likely to produce greater or lesser priming effects.”
