findingOf three thinning methods tried with one participant, increasing delays extinguished the alternative behavior and the fixed-interval schedule drove it to undesirably high rates, while only the multiple schedule kept it at moderate, stable levels as the extinction component lengthened. — “Results showed that (a) increasing delays resulted in extinction of the alternative behavior, (b) the FI schedule produced undesirably high rates of the alternative behavior, and (c) the multiple schedule resulted in moderate and stable levels of the alternative behavior as the duration of the extinction component was increased.”
findingFor the two participants compared on signalled versus unsignalled component alternation, the signalling stimuli of the multiple schedule made schedule thinning easier than the unsignalled mixed schedule. — “Results obtained for these 2 participants indicated that the use of discriminative stimuli in the multiple schedule facilitated reinforcement schedule thinning.”
findingAt the end of treatment problem behavior stayed at or near zero while the alternative communicative response was still maintained and discriminated under a schedule of four minutes extinction followed by one minute of reinforcement. — “Upon completion of treatment, problem behavior remained low (or at zero), whereas alternative behavior was maintained as well as differentiated during a multiple-schedule arrangement consisting of a 4-min extinction period followed by a 1-min reinforcement period.”
findingAll three participants, whose problem behavior was maintained by positive reinforcement, were successfully treated with functional communication training when the alternative response was reinforced on a fixed-ratio 1 schedule. — “Three participants whose problem behaviors were maintained by positive reinforcement were treated successfully with FCT in which reinforcement for alternative behavior was initially delivered on fixed-ratio (FR) 1 schedules.”
