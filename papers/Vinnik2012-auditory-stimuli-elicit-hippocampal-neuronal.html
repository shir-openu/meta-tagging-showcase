phenomenonhippocampal neuronal responses to sounds during sleep— “In the current experiment we characterized responses of hippocampal neurons to behaviorally relevant sounds which were played during sleep and to awake passively listening rats.”
claim-typeexistence— “In this study we show that a significant subset of hippocampal neurons responds to sounds presented passively to a rat during both quiet wakefulness and during sleep.”
method — evidence— “To investigate how hippocampal neurons code behaviorally salient stimuli, we recorded from neurons in the CA1 region of hippocampus in rats while they learned to associate the presence of sound with water reward.”
object of study — evidence— “To investigate how hippocampal neurons code behaviorally salient stimuli, we recorded from neurons in the CA1 region of hippocampus in rats while they learned to associate the presence of sound with water reward.”
research question — evidence— “In the current experiment we characterized responses of hippocampal neurons to behaviorally relevant sounds which were played during sleep and to awake passively listening rats. In the behavioral task, the sounds cued the upcoming release of a reward. Outside the task, the sounds were unrelated to the animal's behavior.”
contribution — evidence— “Based on the current findings and the results of previous experiments we described the existence of two types of hippocampal neuronal responses to sounds: sound-onset responses with very short latency and longer-lasting sound-specific responses that are likely to be present when the animal is actively engaged in the task.”
transferfrom auditory psychophysics and speech research: artificial vowels with formant structure, imported as a more naturalistic stimulus class than pure tones— “We chose artificial vowels as a more “naturalistic” class of stimuli than pure tones.”
mechanismThirst plus placement in a completely novel dim environment raises the animal's attention to the sounds, which is why hippocampal neurons responded here although they did not in the earlier over-trained, satiated condition— “In the present study the animals were thirsty during passive presentation and were placed in a completely novel dim environment, which could have facilitated attention to the auditory stimuli.”
mechanismShort-latency hippocampal responses are suppressed once the animal comes to expect the stimulus, so over-training or satiation abolishes them— “These responses are not present in the over-trained and/or satiated animals suggesting that they might disappear when the animal expects the stimuli.”
mechanismSound-evoked CA1 responses are driven anatomically by perforant path terminals from entorhinal cortex, or polysynaptically through the Schaffer collaterals— “The evoked responses in the CA1 region may be generated by perforant path terminals, originating in the entorhinal cortex or polisynaptically via the Schaffer collaterals.”
mechanismPersistent sustained firing, of the kind described in entorhinal cortex, holds the stimulus trace after stimulus offset and thereby produces the long-lasting responses— “This memory trace could be stored by sustained activity, previously described in entorhinal cortex (Egorov et al., 2002 ), or by some other cellular or network mechanisms in higher order associative cortices or hippocampus itself.”
mechanismthe lottery design holds position and motivational state constant between sound and no-sound trials, since both begin with the same nosepoke— “Trials with and without sound presentation were compared, assuring that the animal was in exactly the same position and motivational state in both types of trials.”
mechanismthe 50% schedule was introduced specifically to raise reward salience and make the animal attend to the sounds— “On the fifth day of the training, the reinforcement schedule was changed to increase the salience of the reward and, especially, to make the rat attend to novel sounds associated with reward.”
mechanismlong randomised inter-stimulus intervals of 25-30 s were used to reduce adaptation to repetition— “The long inter-stimulus interval was intended to reduce the animal's adaptation to stimulus repetitions”
mechanismresponses are tested at four a-priori bin sizes and a neuron counts as responsive if any bin survives FDR correction— “To take into account the variability of the responses (e.g., fast transient or sustained responses), we used four a-priori defined bin sizes (50 ms, 500 ms, 1 and 2 s).”
mechanismonly sleep trials that produced no visible behavioural response were analysed— “Only the trials where sounds did not cause any visible behavioral response from the sleeping animal were analyzed further.”

findingForty-one percent of the neurons recorded during the task (24 of 58) fired differently at the right versus the left nosepoke, confirming strong spatial selectivity in this paradigm.
findingEleven of 58 task-recorded cells (19%) responded within the first 300 ms after sound onset, and 14 (24%) responded at reward delivery.
findingSound-responsive and reward-responsive populations were completely non-overlapping: no single cell responded to both events.
findingDuring passive listening in a novel box outside the task, 3 of 25 recorded hippocampal neurons were sound-sensitive, two increasing and one decreasing their firing rate.
findingDuring sleep, 8 of 33 recorded neurons (24%) showed significant sound-evoked responses, five with decreased and three with increased firing rate.
findingResponse latencies during sleep fell between 50 and 150 ms after sound onset.
findingLong-lasting sustained responses occurred only during sleep, and sleep responses were on average roughly twice as long as responses during passive wakefulness.
findingAlmost no neuron distinguished which sound was played: only one cell showed sound-identity-dependent responses across the two vowels and the novel noise.
findingRats waited about 5.09 s at the nosepoke on unrewarded trials, and this waiting time shrank across the session, indicating the sound had become a reward cue and its absence a signal to switch ports.
