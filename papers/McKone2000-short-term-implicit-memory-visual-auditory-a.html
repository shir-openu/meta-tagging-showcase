phenomenonshort-term repetition priming— “Short-tenn implicit memory was examined for mixed auditory (A) and visual (V) stimuli.”
claim-typemechanism— “These modality differences suggest that short-tenn priming has a perceptual locus, with the phonological lexicon maintaining stimuli active longer than the ortho graphic lexicon and treating pseudowords as potential words.”
method — evidence— “In lexical decision, words and nonwords were repeated at lags of 0, 1, 3, and 6 intervening trials, in four prime target combinations (yV, VA, AV, AA).”
mechanismbecause continuous speech is ambiguous and many apparent nonwords later resolve into words, the phonological lexicon must keep pseudoword-status items active as long as word-status items, producing slow auditory decay for both— “The phonologicallexi con, therefore, has need to hold items assigned pseudo word status active for as long as those assigned word sta tus: In continuous speech, many "nonwords" will later turn out to be real words.”
mechanismbecause the written signal is far less ambiguous, the orthographic lexicon can discard a nonword as soon as the next item arrives, producing the fast visual nonword decay— “The orthographic lexicon can afford, therefore, to rapidly discard a non word when the next item is presented.”
mechanismeach stimulus is obligatorily recoded into the other perceptual form, and the secondary trace left by that recoding supports the weak cross-modal short-term priming— “The results sug gest that each stimulus undergoes obligatory recoding into the alternative perceptual form (i.e., orthographic to phonological and, more interestingly, vice versa), and that a secondary trace of this recoding can contribute to short-term priming.”
replicates McKone (1995)— “First, the results of McKone (1995) were replicated when both presentations were visual, although the list used here contained mixed auditory and visual stimuli.”
extends Kirsner and Smith (1974)— “Our experiment was similar to that ofKirsner and Smith, but specifically targeted the region in which the lag X lexicality interaction is found with visual presentation.”
contradicts Kirsner and Smith (1974)— “Few studies have used auditory targets, but we note that our small ef fect appears to conflict with Kirsner and Smith's (1974) large cross-modal reduction in long-term priming in lex ical decision (83 vs. - 14 msec, for words at lags 15 and 63).”
senseshort-term implicit memory - a name chosen by ANALOGY OF DURATION with explicit short-term memory, not by a claim about mechanism— “We refer to this effect as short-term implicit memory, where the label short-term was chosen to reflect its similarity in duration to that of standard ex plicit short-term or "working" memory”
senseshort-term implicit memory - and the commitment the name would carry is DECLINED in the next sentence: separate stores are explicitly not assumed— “We do not assume that different stores sub serve short- and long-term priming. Our preferred theo retical interpretation is of two mechanisms operating within a common system”
sensepriming - operationalised as the difference from the mean of ALL unprimed trials of matching lexicality and modality, after the first-versus-second-presentation definition was rejected— “Instead, we followed the procedure of Kirsner and Smith (1974) in choosing the baseline for priming to be the mean of all unprimed trials of the appropriate lexicality and modal ity.”
senseimplicit memory - automatic retrieval of the target's initial presentation, defined by CONTRAST with the deliberate conscious retrieval required for explicit memory— “where implicit memory refers to auto matic retrieval of the target's initial presentation and is contrasted with the deliberate conscious retrieval re quired for explicit memory”
controllexical decision times below 300 or above 1,700 msec excluded, along with incorrect responses - 1.3 per cent of reaction times — held — rules out anticipations and lapses of attention dominating a mean, with the exclusion rate reported so a reader can see how much was removed— “Lexical decision times were excluded if they fell below 300 or above 1,700 msec (1.3% ofRTs), or ifan incorrect response was given to either presentation of the item (6.2% ofRTs).”
