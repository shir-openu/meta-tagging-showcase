content-tags & facets: phenomenon · claim-type · cross-domain-transfer · mechanism · claim-relation · replication-status · limitation — grounded in the full text
phenomenonmasked cross-modal repetition priming in word recognition
claim-typemechanism
mechanismA visual prime activates whole-word orthographic representations, which pass activation on to the corresponding whole-word phonological representations, so cross-modal transfer travels the slower lexical route and shows up only in the N400. — “Activation engendered by a visual prime word reaches whole-word orthographic representations (giving rise to within-modality priming) and is sent on to corresponding whole-word phonological representations.”
mechanismLengthening prime exposure lets activation reach whole-word phonological units sooner and accumulate to a higher level, which shifts the cross-modal N400 effect earlier and makes it bigger. — “suggests that the longer prime duration allowed activation to reach whole-word phonological representations earlier and therefore to build up to higher levels than at the shorter prime duration”
mechanismAn inhibitory reset of modality-specific representations, triggered when prime and target are perceived as separate events, reduces within-modality priming at longer prime durations. — “One plausible mechanism for such inhibitory effects is that the word recognition system needs to be reset in order to correctly process new upcoming words.”
relation — “Experiment 1 successfully replicated the behavioral pattern of within and cross-modality masked repetition priming obtained in prior studies using the lexical decision task and similar stimulus presentation conditions (i.e., 50 ms visual primes, Grainger et al., 2003 ).”
relation — “In line with the results of Holcomb and Grainger (in press) , in Experiment 1 we also found evidence for visual-visual repetition effects starting as early as 200–300 ms post-target onset that resulted in a clear negative-going peak at 250 ms (see Figure 4 ).”
relation — “However, contrary to this prediction we failed to find any evidence for standard priming effects (i.e., reduced negativity in the related condition) in early ERP components (i.e., the N250) with auditory targets at the longer prime duration of Experiment 3.”
differs inwithin-modality priming → cross-modal priming — BOTH THE KIND OF DIFFERENCE AND ITS SIZE - the two words are the paper's own. Qualitatively: within-modality priming moves the early N250 and cross-modal priming does not, so they engage different processing stages rather than the same stage to different degrees. Quantitatively: at 50 ms prime exposures the cross-modal N400 effect is much the weaker of the two, though at 67 ms it is not — “the cross-modal priming effects were both qualitatively”
differs inN250 → N400 — WHAT MOVES THEM. Within-modality priming moves the N250; cross-modality priming does not; both move the N400 equally. That is differential sensitivity to an experimental variable, which the authors name as the most compelling evidence for component independence - stronger than the two morphological arguments (two peaks with an intervening positivity, different scalp distributions) that precede it — “the N250 and N400 are at least partially distinct and emanate from non-identical neural sources”
replicationreplicated
limitationThe post-experiment mask-effectiveness test probably overstates how visible the primes were during the ERP experiment itself. — “Note, however, that as in Experiment 1, the mask-effectiveness test is likely to have over estimated the visibility of primes during the ERP experiment as it focused attention on detecting the masked items themselves ( Kahan, 2000 ).”
limitationThe larger, earlier cross-modal effect at 67 ms may reflect removal of an early reversed priming effect rather than a genuinely larger N400; the authors cannot yet decide between these. — “One admittedly speculative possibility for the different size and time-course of priming effects across experiments is that the reversal effect in the first two experiments blocked or obscured the earlier phase of the N400 seen in Experiment 3 and that the resulting larger and somewhat earlier effect in Experiment 3 is more a reflection of the removal of the reversal than the addition of a larger/earlier N400.”
limitationThat alternative explanation is untested in the present data and awaits further experiments. — “Testing of this hypothesis will have to await further experimentation.”
limitationCross-study comparison of effect magnitude is difficult; the auditory priming effect obtained here is small and late relative to a previous cross-modal study. — “Although direct between-study comparisons are difficult to make, it is worth noting that the current 1.2 microvolt auditory priming effect at Cz between 500 and 750 ms is relatively late and somewhat small compared to at least one previous cross-modal priming study.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingBehaviourally, masked visual primes speeded lexical decisions to both visual and auditory targets by roughly equal amounts, with no modality-by-repetition interaction. — “Simple effects ANOVAs revealed that the repetition effect was reliable for visual words (39ms effect, F(1,23) = 20.176, p < 0.001), and auditory words (40ms effect, F(1,23) = 14.957, p < 0.001), but no interaction between target modality and repetition (F(1,23) = 0.004, p < 0.947).”
findingIn the ERPs, within-modality repetition effects appeared early and spanned the N250 and N400, while cross-modal effects only emerged on the trailing edge of the N400 after the visual effect had ended. — “While the visual ERP repetition effects started early (200–300 ms) and continued through the traditional N400 epoch (300–500 ms), a comparable effect in the auditory modality was not significant until the offset phase of the typical N400 (550–750 ms), a time period by which the visual effect had completely dissipated.”
findingRaising the prime duration from 50 ms to 67 ms made the cross-modal N400 priming effect both earlier and substantially larger. — “Our between-experiment analyses showed an earlier and much larger modulation of N400 amplitude in cross-modal repetition priming in Experiment 3 compared to Experiment 2.”
findingThe model's prediction that a longer prime would produce cross-modal effects in pre-N400 components was not supported: no N250 priming appeared for auditory targets even at 67 ms. — “However, contrary to this prediction we failed to find any evidence for standard priming effects (i.e., reduced negativity in the related condition) in early ERP components (i.e., the N250) with auditory targets at the longer prime duration of Experiment 3.”
findingWithin-modality (visual-visual) priming became smaller, not larger, as prime exposure increased from 50 to 67 ms. — “Within-modality repetition priming actually diminished with increasing prime exposure across Experiments 2 and 3.”
findingAt the short prime duration a small reversed (inverse) priming effect appeared in the cross-modal condition in the 200-300 ms window. — “One unpredicted result in Experiment 1 was a small inverse priming effect (repeated more negative than unrelated) in the cross-modal condition in the 200–300 ms measurement window over the right hemisphere.”
quantity39ms / 40ms — size of the behavioural repetition priming effect for visual and auditory word targets in Experiment 1 — “Simple effects ANOVAs revealed that the repetition effect was reliable for visual words (39ms effect, F(1,23) = 20.176, p < 0.001), and auditory words (40ms effect, F(1,23) = 14.957, p < 0.001), but no interaction between target modality and repetition (F(1,23) = 0.004, p < 0.947).”

quantity64% — proportion of masked primes correctly reported in the post-experiment visibility test with the longer 67 ms prime of Experiment 3 — “In the post-ERP mask-effectiveness procedure participants correctly reported an average of 64% of primes.”
