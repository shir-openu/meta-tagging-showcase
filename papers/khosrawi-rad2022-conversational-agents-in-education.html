phenomenonpedagogical conversational agents in education
claim-typecritique
mechanismResearch projects design pedagogical CAs for short-run demonstration rather than sustained use, so the agents are not maintained past the prototype and fail soon after deployment. — “Since Janssen et al. (2021) proved that most CAs nevertheless fail within a short period, we reckon that this might particularly go back to the rare long-term orientation of the research projects outlined above.”
mechanismDesigners draw on only isolated fragments of the psychological/learning knowledge base, so the resulting agents realise only part of their potential. — “articles that are based upon psychological concepts and theories, however, usually only consider isolated aspects (e.g., CAs to promote improved multimedia learning), while a more diverse consideration of the knowledge base was hard to find. For this reason, we assume that existing CAs do not yet realise their full potential.”
relation — “To contribute to future research, we conducted an SLR based on Webster and Watson (2002), Page et al. (2021), and Schoormann et al. (2021).”
relation — “Supplementary, we added the moderator function, because Gubareva and Lopes (2020) focused on academic student support and we covered a broader scope, also including the instructors’ perspective (e.g., Mikic-Fonte et al., 2019; Sakr et al., 2021).”
relation — “Similar to Strohmann (2021) and Diederich et al. (2022), we found that scenarios of the longterm orientation of CAs have so far been rarely considered, which is reflected in the lack of long-term studies (Diederich et al. 2022).”
relation — “However, contrary to what cross-domain literature reviews reveal, there is a difference in the quality of existing studies: While for CAs in general, a plethora of empirical studies with validated constructs exist (Diederich et al., 2022), those are underrepresented in the educational domain - many publications lack evaluations with a solid sample size.”

replicationuntested
limitationSelection and analysis rested on the authors' subjective decisions, mitigated but not removed by peer-reviewed screening and two coding waves. — “In spite of inescapable limitations regarding subjectively driven decisions during the SLR, we purposefully undertook measures to reduce these through a peer-reviewed process in screening, two successive coding waves in an interdisciplinary research team, after querying seven scientific databases.”
limitationRelevant publications may have been missed during screening despite the breadth of databases and search terms. — “Second, despite our extensive review of 252 publications, it is still possible that relevant publications might have been overlooked during the screening process, even though we included a variety of databases and search terms.”
limitationThe review captures only a fast-moving field's current state; later searches may give different results. — “Ultimately, as the field is evolving quickly, we can only provide the current state of the field and future searches on related topics might yield different results.”
limitationThe overview of the field excludes every publication not in English or German, so the reported state of the art is bounded by the reviewers' languages. — “The following exclusion criteria were applied: missing educational context, no relation to CAs, another language than English or German”
limitationThe review declares its own selection subjective, and states that this is normal for the method rather than particular to this team. — “First, as is common for SLRs, the selection and analysis process is based on subjective decisions of the authors”
limitationThe claim about the field's maturity is an inference from how often software names recur, not a measurement of the systems. — “although a large proportion of these software names are only referenced once, and have mostly not yet been further developed in subsequent publications. Thus, we assume that many pedagogical CAs are still in a prototypical stage”
limitationThe review's own object is defined by publication facts as much as by content: papers in any language other than English or German are excluded, as is everything published before 2016. A reader takes the resulting picture as the state of the field, and the record already carries that reading as a sense. — “The following exclusion criteria were applied: missing educational context, no relation to CAs, another language than English or German, duplicates, language learning or health focus, as well as publications before 2016”

findingOnly five of the 252 coded articles reported long-term studies of conversational agents in education. — “Only five studies out of 252 articles reported on long-term studies”
findingPedagogical CAs act most often as tutors (31%), then as motivators and organizers (21% each), mentors (18%) and moderators (9%). — “The distribution of the roles is as follows: 31% tutor, 21% motivator, 21% organizer, 18% mentor, 9% moderator.”
findingAcademic students are the target group in 78% of the studies, with school students, teachers, professionals and others each accounting for only about 5-6%. — “The target groups addressed by the CAs in the analysed sample are: 78% academic students (e.g., Mikic-Fonte et al., 2019; Sakr et al., 2021), 6% high school students or equivalent”
findingThe word "chatbot" appears in 167 of the 252 articles (85%) while "virtual companion(ship)" appears in only 2 articles, showing the companion framing is nearly absent from the education literature. — “While the term "chatbot" was used in 167 publications and thus a majority of the 252 articles (85%) with a total of 5661 entries, the term "virtual companion(ship)" appeared in only 2 articles (< 1%) with 28 overall mentions”
finding136 of the reviewed publications cite psychological or learning theory, and 80% of the cited theories come from educational research rather than from psychology or HCI. — “In 136 of the reviewed publications, we identified theoretical reflections referencing psychological or learning pedagogical theories on the topic of CAs in the educational setting. The majority of mentioned theories (80%) stem from educational research.”
findingGoogle Dialogflow (37%) and IBM Watson Assistant (30%) are the two building platforms most often used to implement pedagogical CAs. — “we analysed which platforms were mentioned most frequently: Google Dialogflow (37%) (e.g., Gonda and Chu, 2019; El Hefny et al., 2021) and IBM Watson Assistant (30%) (e.g., Ralston et al., 2019; Oliveira et al., 2019).”
findingMost of the reviewed studies evaluate their agents on small samples, so their results may not be representative. — “Furthermore, the majority of the studies have a small sample size, so the representativeness of the results must be further examined.”
findingExactly one of the 252 papers offers prescriptive design knowledge in the form of a nascent design theory, and it is confined to argumentation skills. — “Only one paper presents a (nascent) design theory as prescriptive design knowledge (Gregor, 2002; Gregor and Hevner, 2013), focusing on the advancement of argumentation skills as a specific application context”
findingOnly 5% of the articles address professional development outside academia, leaving lifelong and vocational learning largely unstudied. — “Only 5% of the articles cover professional development in a non-academic context”
findingA large share of the reviewed authors design their conversational agent without grounding it in any scientific theory. — “We found that a large number of authors do not base the design of their CA on scientific theories.”
