phenomenonnegative visuospatial priming— “NP is the normal reduction of performance when identifying target stimuli that appears where non-target stimuli appeared previously.”
claim-typemethod— “In summary, we describe a novel visuospatial priming task that detects negative priming in rats.”
method — evidence— “Performance was challenged by shortening prime duration and by administering amphetamine.”
transferfrom classical experimental psychology of arousal and performance: the Yerkes-Dodson inverted-U law, imported to explain why the same amphetamine dose helps low-NP and harms high-NP rats— “This “rate-dependent” effect of AMPH recalls the widely observed pattern described in the Yerkes-Dodson law, which states that performance, especially in difficult tasks, initially increases with physiological or mental arousal, but when levels of arousal become too high, performance is degraded”
mechanismAn inverted-U relation between dopamine transmission and inhibitory processing: amphetamine pushes low-NP animals toward and high-NP animals past an optimum, so the same drug raises or abolishes priming depending on baseline state— “The finding that AMPH improves NP in animals with normally low levels of NP, while reducing NP in animals with high levels of NP, suggests that an optimal level of dopamine transmission might produce maximal amount of negative visuospatial priming, and that levels either above or below that optimal point result in reduced or no negative…
mechanismBrief primes are routed through preconscious, automatic processing, which is the route that generates negative priming; longer primes recruit more controlled processing and weaken it— “Conceivably, shorter duration primes may favor processing by preconscious/automatic mechanisms, which may be required for the negative priming effect.”
mechanismA species difference in speed-accuracy strategy - rats protect response speed and sacrifice accuracy - channels the priming cost into accuracy rather than latency— “The tendency of rats to maintain speed of responding at the cost of accuracy may therefore explain why the NP effect in our task primarily impacted response accuracy rather than response latencies.”
builds-on the human visuospatial priming task of Tipper (1985) and Swerdlow et al. (1996)— “In this study, we aimed to describe the characteristics and heuristic values of a novel rat negative visuospatial priming paradigm ( Figure 1 ), based on a well-established human visuospatial priming task ( Swerdlow et al., 1996 ; Tipper, 1985 ).”
senseNEGATIVE PRIMING - a priming effect defined as a LOSS, and defined as the NORMAL one: `NP is the normal reduction of performance when identifying target stimuli that appears where non-target stimuli appeared previously.` Second record tonight whose priming effect harms performance (after Keane2015's costs), and the first where the harm is the healthy result— “NP is the normal reduction of performance when identifying target stimuli that appears where non-target stimuli appeared previously. TS subjects exhibit diminished NP”
senseLESS PRIMING IS THE DEFICIT - the direction that inverts everything. `TS subjects exhibit DIMINISHED NP`, so a smaller effect is the pathological finding and a larger one is intact function. Any summary that treats priming magnitude as one quantity with a good direction gets this record backwards, and the corpus contains many records where more priming is the notable result— “NP is the normal reduction of performance when identifying target stimuli that appears where non-target stimuli appeared previously. TS subjects exhibit diminished NP”
sensePRIMING AS A PROGNOSTIC INDEX - `their NP levels PREDICT THEIR RESPONSE TO BEHAVIORAL THERAPY`. Not a phenomenon under study, not an instrument for probing representations, but a clinical measurement used to forecast treatment outcome. A fourth job for the word in this corpus, alongside phenomenon, instrument and paradigm— “Individuals with Tourette syndrome (TS) exhibit deficits in inhibitory information processing which may reflect impaired neural mechanisms underlying symptoms and can be detected using a negative priming (NP) task. NP is the normal reduction of performance when identifying target stimuli that appears where non-target stimuli appeared…
senseTHE EFFECT ONLY APPEARS UNDER CHALLENGE, which is a fact about the animal model rather than about the animals: `during the SHORT PRIME CHALLENGE, rats exhibited lower accuracy in NP vs. baseline trials`. A 0.25 s prime was needed to make the phenomenon measurable at all, and the same duration was then carried into the drug study. A translational model whose sensitivity had to be engineered into the timing— “During the short prime challenge, rats exhibited lower accuracy in NP vs. baseline trials, indicative of inhibitory information processing. Modulation by amphetamine administration indicates that this drug had rate-dependent effects”
controltarget and non-target locations varied pseudorandomly, equal counts per location per session, with location unpredictable — held — rules out a location bias and an anticipation strategy - the same design as the 2015 sister paper, and the same two-part justification— “The location of the target and non-target stimuli varied pseudorandomly between trials, so that each location contained the stimuli an equal number of times per session, but their location was not predictable on any given trial.”
notenegative priming —operationalised-as→ inhibitory information processing — The measurement claim the whole paper rests on: a deficit in inhibitory processing `can be detected using a negative priming task`, so the loss of performance is read as the mechanism working. Direction matters - the effect is the INDEX and the inhibitory process is what it indexes, not the other way round.— “Individuals with Tourette syndrome (TS) exhibit deficits in inhibitory information processing which may reflect impaired neural mechanisms underlying symptoms and can be detected using a negative priming (NP) task. NP is the normal reduction of performance when identifying target stimuli that appears where non-target stimuli appeared…
