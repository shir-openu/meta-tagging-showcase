claim-typemechanism
mechanismHeterozygote superiority: because ST/CH heterozygotes are fitter than either homozygote, selection drives the population towards an intermediate equilibrium of chromosome frequencies rather than fixation. — “It is very probable that neither homozygotes for ST (ST/ST) nor for CH (CH/CH), but the heterozygotes ST/CH are the most favored genetic constitution. If so, the changes in the populations will proceed toward equilibrium values at which the relative proportions of the three genotypes will give the maximal degree of fitness of the population.”
mechanismThe inversions themselves are adaptively neutral, but they lock up different gene complexes that adapt their carriers to different seasonal environments, so seasonal change in the environment shifts their frequencies. — “The gene arrangements may be in themselves adaptively neutral (that is, free from position effects), but they contain different gene complexes which make their carriers adapted to different seasonal environments {DoBZHANSKY 1943).”
mechanismFrequency-dependent selection through niche filling: a rare genotype occupies only the niches it is best adapted to and is favoured, but when abundant it must spill into niches it fits poorly and is selected against. — “Each genotype may be favored by selection when rare and unable to occupy fully the ecological niches to which it is best adapted, but selected against when so abundant that it must in part occupy ecological niches to which it is less well adapted than other genotypes.”
relation — “Population cages used in the present experiments (fig. I) are a modification of the L'HERITIER and TEISSIER model.”
relation — “An alternative explanation has been suggested by HovANITZ (1944) namely, that the populations of Pinon and Andreas are swamped in summer by migration from the flourishing populations higher on the mountain, which resemble Keen in chromosome frequencies.”
replicationuntested
limitationAbsence of significant change at the low temperature may reflect too few sampling intervals rather than genuinely constant frequencies. — “This does not imply that there were no changes but merely that any changes were too small to be detected with confidence in the small number of intervals in these sets of experiments.”
limitationThe data cannot discriminate among the possible selective models (constant selective values, sex-differential selection, frequency-dependent values). — “Unfortunately the data at hand do not yield a unique interpretation.”
limitationThe spring reversal seen in nature was not reproduced and its causes remain unknown. — “It appears that there are important factors yet to be discovered.”
limitationA counting assumption is made, stated to be false in some strains, and the direction of the resulting error is given: the tabulated numbers are minima. — “it was assumed that every strain con tains one and only one kind of third chromosome with each gene arrangement found in that strain. Since more than a single kind were doubtless present in some of the strains”
limitationThe baseline the experiment is compared against is inferred rather than observed, and the authors offer that as one of two explanations for the unexpected control differences. — “It is possible that these differences were due merely to sampling errors. On the other hand, it should be kept in mind that the composition of the initial population is inferred from that of its parents”

findingIn population cages held at 25°C the frequency of the Standard gene arrangement rose highly significantly at the expense of the other arrangements, especially Chiricahua, whereas at 16.5°C mean frequencies did not change significantly. — “At 25°, with initial frequencies equal or with Chiricahua more frequent than Standard, there was a highly significant increase in frequency of Standard (where present) at the expense of either of the other types but especially of Chiricahua. On the other hand, there were no significant changes in mean frequency at 16.5°C.”
findingThe rate of frequency change per generation regressed negatively on frequency, meaning each chromosome type gained when it was rare and lost when it became common. — “The rate of change at 25° showed a strong negative regression on frequency in all cases, and a probably significant regression of this sort was observed in one case (Arrowhead) at 16.5°. The data further indicate that all chromosome types are favored when sufficiently rare and opposed when sufficiently common.”
findingFor cages containing only Standard and Chiricahua at 25°C, relative selective values of 0.70 for ST/ST, 1.00 for ST/CH and 0.30 for CH/CH reproduce the observed changes and put equilibrium at 70 percent Standard. — “The results in the experiments at 25°)n which only Standard and Chiricahua were present can be adequately explained by postulating the relative selective values .7o: 1.oo: .30 for ST/ST, ST/CH and CH/CH, respectively.
