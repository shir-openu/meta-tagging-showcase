mechanismbest frequencies are laid out as partially overlapping bandpass filters spaced one third of an octave apart, so near threshold the filter centre frequencies win out over the overlap regions and the measured distribution comes out saw-toothed — “Thus, near threshold, frequencies at the center of the filters are favored over those at the overlapping regions, and it results in the multi-modal, saw-toothed pattern of frequency distribution.”
mechanismthe discrete physiological frequency steps arise because the map is built from anatomically discrete fibrodendritic laminae, each lamina acting as one frequency-band module — “Thus, our anatomical and physiological data suggest that the tonotopic map in the CNIC in the rat is composed of discrete physiological laminae that correspond to the anatomical fibrodendritic laminae of the CNIC.”
mechanism — “At the IC level, best frequencies may be organized as partially overlapping bandpass filters with a center-to-center separation of one-third octave. findingSampling best frequency every 25-50 micrometres revealed a stair-step rather than smooth progression in every penetration through the central nucleus of the inferior colliculus. — “However, when the BF was recorded every 25–50 μm ( Fig. 2 ), we found a distinct stair-step pattern in every penetration ( n = 16 animals, 45 penetrations).”
findingBest frequency stayed within 0.1 octaves for about 151 micrometres of electrode travel and then jumped by about a third of an octave. — “The BF remained nearly constant (changing <0.1 octaves) for an average of 151 ± 41 μm (mean ± SD) and then jumped 0.34 ± 0.11 octaves ( n = 460 steps).”
findingBest frequencies of 604 independently recorded single units were not uniformly distributed but clustered into 16 peaks spaced about a third of an octave apart. — “When the BFs of all 604 units in this sample were pooled and displayed as a histogram with 0.1 octave bins, we observed a multimodal distribution with 16 peaks occurring at 0.32 ± 0.11 octave intervals ( Fig. 6 A ).”
findingStep thickness and step size were statistically indistinguishable between low-frequency and high-frequency regions, so laminae are similar throughout the central nucleus. — “When we compared low-frequency versus high-frequency regions, neither the thickness of the constant pattern nor the change in BF at the steps were significantly different (Mann–Whitney tests, p > 0.05).”
findingThe thinnest anatomically labelled axonal laminae, once corrected for histological shrinkage, are the same size as the physiological frequency steps, tying the stair-step map to the fibrodendritic laminae. — “After an adjustment for 10–12% shrinkage during histology, these measurements match the 140–150 μm mean step size, resulting in a stair-step jump in BF during multiunit mapping.”
findingReanalysis of 198 published single units from rat primary auditory cortex showed the same multipeaked best-frequency distribution at about 0.31 octave spacing, suggesting the discontinuity persists from midbrain to cortex. — “The cortical units showed a similar multipeaked distribution ( Fig. 9 B ), with the peaks occurring at 0.31 ± 0.10 octave intervals.”
findingTaken together the multiunit and single-unit data contradict the standard assumption that the inferior colliculus contains a continuous low-to-high frequency gradient. — “Together, both types of data argue against the general view that the tonotopic organization in the CNIC forms a continuous frequency gradient.”

If the auditory filters in the rat are similar, the midbrain filters may be approximately double those at the periphery.”
