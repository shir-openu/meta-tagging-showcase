phenomenonspike sorting of high-channel-count extracellular recordings
claim-typemethod
mechanismReplacing the full matrix convolution by a rank-3 low-rank template decomposition cuts the number of filtering operations by the ratio of channel count to template rank, which is what makes near-realtime sorting of hundreds of channels possible — “However, the low-rank decomposition of templates allows us to reduce the number of operations by a factor of Nchan/Nrank, where Nchan is the number of channels (typically > 100) and Nrank is the rank of the decomposed template (typically 3).”
mechanismOver-split clusters arise because the fitting algorithm splits a high-variance cluster across several templates in order to reduce total reconstruction error, which is why the resulting cluster pairs form one continuous density and can be merged automatically — “Instead, these clusters arise as a consequence of the algorithm partitioning clusters with large variance into multiple templates, so as to better explain their total variance.”
relation — “This approach is related to a previous method 6, but that method requires a generic convex optimization that is slow for recordings with large numbers of channels.”
relation — “As we demonstrate with constructed ground-truth datasets, our system is more accurate than a current widely-used method 18.”
relation — “We compared Kilosort and Klustakwik on 32 and 128 channel recordings, using a technique known as “hybrid ground truth” 18 (Fig. 3a-c).”
replicationuntested
limitationManual operator curation is still needed despite the automatic merging heuristic, mainly because of electrode drift and other non-stationarities. — “Although we have defined a heuristic which eliminates the need for many manual merges, operator curation is still required, primarily due to non-stationarities in the recordings such as electrode drift.”
limitationThe optimisation can settle in non-optimal local minima, which the authors only ameliorate by annealing several parameters. — “Like most clustering algorithms, this model is prone to non-optimal local minima.”
limitationThe speed comparison against the established framework was not run at the largest channel count, so no 384-channel baseline exists. — “These were significant improvements over an established framework (masked KlustaKwik, 18), which needed 480 minutes to sort the 32 channel recording, and 15,000 minutes (10 days) to sort the 128 channel recording, while running on a CPU cluster (we did not attempt to run KlustaKwik on 384 channel recordings).”
limitationThe ground-truth spikes are the curated output of one of the two algorithms being compared. — “To create a hybrid ground truth data set, we first selected all the clusters from a recording that had been previously analyzed with KlustaKwik, and curated by a human expert”
limitationThe accuracy benchmark was made by cropping a larger one. — “We detail here only the 32 channel benchmarks, which we created by cropping the full 128 channel hybrid benchmark (Fig. 3a-c), which we were able to run with many ground truth neurons”
limitationScores are also reported after a best-possible merging step that no user could perform. — “Same as (def) after greedy best possible merges. j, Number of merges required to reach best score”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingKilosort represents the raw voltage as a sum of template waveforms triggered at spike times, which allows spatiotemporally overlapping spikes to be identified and resolved rather than missed. — “Kilosort models the recorded voltage as a sum of template waveforms triggered on the spike times, allowing overlapping spikes to be identified and resolved.”
findingKilosort sorted 32-, 128- and 384-channel recordings in 10, 29 and 140 minutes on one GPU workstation, while KlustaKwik needed 480 minutes for the 32-channel and 15,000 minutes for the 128-channel recording on a CPU cluster. — “These were significant improvements over an established framework (masked KlustaKwik, 18), which needed 480 minutes to sort the 32 channel recording, and 15,000 minutes (10 days) to sort the 128 channel recording, while running on a CPU cluster”
findingOn hybrid ground-truth data Kilosort yielded well-isolated units (score above 0.9) for 53% of ground-truth neurons versus 35% for KlustaKwik. — “Both Kilosort and KlustaKwik performed well (Fig. 3d), with Kilosort producing significantly more neurons with well-isolated clusters (53% vs 35% units with scores above 0.9).”
findingAfter greedy best-possible operator merges Kilosort still led, 69% of units above score 0.9 against 60% for KlustaKwik. — “Final best results as well as the required number of matches are shown in Figure 3g-j (Kilosort vs KlustaKwik 69% vs 60% units with scores above 0.9).”
findingKilosort's accuracy advantage came from missing fewer spikes rather than from fewer false positives, which the authors attribute to its resolution of overlapping spikes. — “The relative performance improvement of Kilosort is clearly driven by fewer misses (Fig 3h), which are likely due to its ability to detect overlapping spikes.”
findingFitting each spike with its own three-component spatiotemporal SVD ("private PCs") left about five times less residual waveform variance than one common per-channel PCA basis. — “This approach of tailoring “private PCs” to each spike allows us to fit the spikes with ∼5 times less residual variance than the standard approach of applying a single PCA approximation per channel, to all neurons on that channel”
findingKilosort's run time grows linearly with the number of recorded neurons rather than with channel count, so denser electrodes cost no extra processing time. — “The time taken to run Kilosort scales linearly with the number of recorded neurons, rather than the number of channels, due to the low-dimensional parametrization of template waveforms.”
quantity10, 29 and 140 minutes — Kilosort run times for 32-, 128- and 384-channel recordings on a single GTX 980Ti workstation — “The average run times for 32, 128 and 384 channel recordings were 10, 29 and 140 minutes, on a single GPU-equipped workstation (GTX 980Ti).”
quantity480 minutes / 15,000 minutes (10 days) — KlustaKwik run times for the 32-channel and 128-channel recordings, the comparison baseline — “These were significant improvements over an established framework (masked KlustaKwik, 18), which needed 480 minutes to sort the 32 channel recording, and 15,000 minutes (10 days) to sort the 128 channel recording, while running on a CPU cluster”
