phenomenonresponse priming by luminance flankers
claim-typemechanism
mechanismThe feedforward sweeps triggered in turn by flankers and targets still carry only local contrast information, so motor activation is driven by raw local contrast while conscious brightness is settled later by recurrent interactions. — “this view suggests that the feedforward sweeps successively triggered by the flankers and targets are still dominated by local contrast signals.”
mechanismConscious lightness and brightness are computed later, by recurrent processing that trails the initial feedforward wave. — “The conscious perception of lightness and brightness will be determined later in processing, probably as the result of recurrent interactions trailing behind the initial processing wave”
mechanismResponse priming accesses a stimulus representation that precedes lightness constancy, i.e. one that has not yet discounted illumination. — “We adopt the less radical view that response priming reflects a stimulus representation that is earlier and less elaborate than the one underlying conscious vision and, consequently, deficient in lightness constancy.”
mechanismPlacing the high-luminance flanker on the lighted segment turns it into a local decrement (and the low-luminance flanker on the shadowed segment into a local increment), which is what inverts the priming sign. — “In the attenuation condition, however, the high-luminance flanker was a local decrement because it appeared on an even more luminant background”
relation — “Our conclusions are in agreement with a study by Moore and Brown (2001), in which visual search for highluminance and low-luminance targets was performed under conditions in which parts of the display seemed to be watched through a dark transparent filter.”
relation — “This conclusion might seem to conflict with some physiological studies showing that subjective lightness is signaled early in the processing hierarchy, such that the responses of many cells in the primary visual cortex (V1) and even some in the lateral geniculate nucleus are modulated by a simultaneous contrast illusion (MacEvoy & Par-rr adiso, 2001; Rossi & Paradiso, 1999; Rossi, Rittenhouse, & Paradiso, 1996).”
relation — “Breitmeyer, Ro, and Singhal (2004; Breitmeyer, Ö(cid:120)(cid:129) men, & Chen, 2004) have proposed that response priming is based on raw physical stimulus characteristics, such as the distribution of wavelength information, instead of perceived color. We adopt the less radical view that response priming reflects a stimulus representation that is earlier and less elaborate than the one underlying conscious vision and, consequently, deficient in lightness constancy”
relation — “In the framework of the rapid-chase model of response priming (Schmidt et al., 2006; Schmidt & Schmidt, 2009), this view suggests that the feedforward sweeps successively triggered by the flankers and targets are still dominated by local contrast signals.”
effect directionlocal luminance contrast → response priming — increases — “it seems to depend exclusively on the local luminance contrast”
replicationuntested
limitationExperiments 1 and 2 leave open the alternative that participants judged targets by local contrast sign rather than brightness (addressed only by Experiment 3). — “However, a possibility remains that the participants never responded to the brightness of the targets in the first place.”
limitationThe dissociation licenses only the weaker conclusion that some aspects of lightness constancy are missing from response activation, not that constancy is wholly absent. — “If priming effects are in systematic disagreement with the flankers’ perceived brightness values, this suggests that at least some aspects of lightness constancy are absent from the response activation process.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingWhen the illusion made the two flankers look similar in brightness, the priming effect reversed sign: consistent flankers slowed responses and inconsistent flankers speeded them. — “But when the illumination attenuates perceived flanker differences, priming effects unexpectedly are reversed: Responses become slower with consistent flankers and faster with inconsistent flankers.”
findingThe same flanker could look brighter than its partner while priming the response assigned to the darker stimulus. — “In other words, in the attenuation condition, the more luminant flanker looks brighter than the other flanker but primes as if it were darker.”
findingBrightness matching never reversed: the physically more luminant flanker was always judged the brighter one in every illusion condition. — “As in the previous experiments, the matching task revealed that the physically more luminant flanker was always judged brighter than the less luminant one in all the illusion conditions.”
findingVarying the light-to-shadow ratio showed that priming flipped sign in exactly the conditions where each flanker's contrast against its own background flipped from increment to decrement. — “Indeed, the experiment clearly shows that priming effects reverse in exactly those conditions in which the flankers’ local contrast to the background reverses, indicating that it is, indeed, local flanker-to-background contrast that drove the priming effect.”
findingThe reversal survived when targets were made all-decrement or all-increment pairs, so participants could not have been responding to target contrast sign alone. — “Despite these changes, Experiment 3 replicated the finding that priming effects are strongly positive in enhancement conditions but are reversed in attenuation conditions, in which the flankers switch their”
findingOverall, response priming tracked neither the flankers' physical luminance nor their perceived brightness, but only their local luminance contrast against the immediately surrounding background. — “Response priming by luminance flankers depends neither on the actual physical luminances of the flankers nor on their subjective brightness, as assessed in a psychophysical matching task; instead, it seems to depend exclusively on the local luminance contrast of the flankers to their immediate background.”
findingThe priming-perception dissociation was obtained with all stimuli fully visible, without masking or brief presentation. — “Note that this dissociation makes no use of visual masking or brief stimulus presentations of any kind: It is obtained even though the presented stimuli are all in plain view”
quantityEight — participants in Experiment 1 — “Eight students from the University of Giessen (19–24 years of age, all female, 1 left-handed, all with normal or corrected-to-normal vision) participated for payment or course credit after giving informed consent.”
quantity1.0, 34.2, and 11.1 cd/m2 — luminances of the shadowed, lighted and neutrally illuminated segments of the folded plane in Experiment 1 — “the shadowed, lighted, and neutrally illuminated (frontoparallel) segments had luminances of 1.0, 34.2, and 11.1 cd/m2, respectively.”
quantity17, 33, 50, 67, or 83 msec — the flanker-target SOAs used in Experiment 1 — “Following a flanker– target SOA of 17, 33, 50, 67, or 83 msec, the two targets appeared to the left and right of the fixation point on the midsegment 1,500 msec after the onset of the plane.”
quantity0.19% — share of Experiment 1 trials discarded as RT outliers — “RTs shorter than 100 msec or longer than 800 msec were discarded as outliers (0.19% of the data).”
quantityseven steps (light–shadow contrast between 1.6 and 336.3) — range over which the light-to-shadow luminance ratio was varied in Experiment 2 — “Stimulus conditions were also identical, except that the luminances of the shadowed and lighted segments were varied in seven steps (13.7– 43.9 cd/m2 for lighted segments, 0.1–8.8 cd/m2 for shadowed segments; light–shadow contrast between 1.6 and 336.3”
quantityabout 30 msec — how much faster responses were to mixed target pairs than to all-decrement or all-increment pairs in Experiment 3 — “Overall, RTs were about 30 msec shorter for mixed targets than for decrement or increment targets”
variableflanker-target consistency (independent) — “On consistent trials, each flanker had the same luminance as its adjacent target, whereas on inconsistent trials, flanker luminances were spatially switched with respect to target luminances.”
