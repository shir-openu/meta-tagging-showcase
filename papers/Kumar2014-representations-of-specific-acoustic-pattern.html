findingOf six regions of interest, only the hippocampus and planum temporale allowed a classifier to tell apart which of the three learnt tone-cloud exemplars was being heard; HG, entorhinal/perirhinal cortex and parahippocampal cortex did not.
findingSubjects detected repetition better for all three repeatedly presented reference tone clouds than for freshly generated repeated tone clouds, showing that the specific patterns had been learnt.
findingStandard univariate analysis found no difference in mean activity between the three learnt patterns anywhere in the brain, so the effect existed only in the multi-voxel activity pattern.
findingWhen the 60 trial-unique repeated tone clouds were split into three arbitrary classes, classification fell to chance in every region, confirming that decoding depended on stable repeated exemplars rather than on stimulus class labels.
findingMost participants did not notice that any sound had recurred across trials, indicating the learning was implicit.
findingThe authors claim this is the first demonstration that the hippocampus encodes noise-like acoustic patterns that carry no semantic content.
