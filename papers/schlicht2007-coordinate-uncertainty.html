phenomenoncoordinate transformation uncertainty in reach-to-grasp
claim-typemechanism
mechanismTHE UNCERTAINTY IS MANIPULATED WITHOUT MOVING THE TARGET: the object stays in one place and the participant's EYE POSITION is varied instead, which changes the transformation between eye and head and so adds uncertainty to the remapped location while leaving the object alone — “To address this question, we varied eye position in the head, while participants reached to grasp a spatially fixed object, both when the object was in view and when it was occluded. Varying eye position changes CTU between eye and head, producing additional uncertainty in remapped information away from forward view.”
mechanismTHE LEVER IS A KNOWN PROPERTY OF THE SYSTEM RATHER THAN AN INTERVENTION: the authors exploit the fact that error in encoding eye position already varies with saccade magnitude and with eccentricity from forward view, so simply choosing where to look sets the uncertainty — “To manipulate CTU between the eyes and head, we took advantage of the fact that error in eye position encoding varies both with saccade magnitude and the eye’s eccentricity away from forward view.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
mechanismTHE PREDICTION IS BUILT AS A CONDITIONAL WITH BOTH ANTECEDENTS NAMED: if the brain stores targets in eye-centred coordinates AND compensates for the effects of the uncertainty, then the grip aperture must vary with eye position EVEN WHEN THE TARGET CANNOT BE SEEN — “Therefore if the brain stores targets in eye-centered coordinates and compensates for the effects of CTU, it predicts that MGA should also vary as a function of eye position, even when the target is occluded.”
mechanismthe target is deliberately NEVER MOVED across the workspace, and the reason is stated: so that any change in reaching behaviour must come from eye position and viewing eccentricity rather than from the kinematic demands of reaching somewhere else — “The cylinder was not moved across the workspace to assure that any changes in reaching behavior resulted from changes in eye position/viewing eccentricity and not kinematic demands.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
mechanismand its position was CHOSEN to maximise the very quantity under study - placed so that the widest possible range of eye positions away from the target was available, giving the greatest range of uncertainty — “Moreover, the cylinder’s location was selected such that there was the maximal range of eye positions away from the target (0 – 80°) to allow for the greatest range of uncertainty.”
mechanismONE FIXATION POINT IS DROPPED PURELY TO KEEP THE TWO CONDITIONS COMPARABLE: the zero-degree fixation is excluded because the occluder would block both the target and that fixation letter, so the visual and occluded groups see the same set of fixations — “The fixation point corresponding to 0° target eccentricity (I) was excluded to keep similar fixations for both the visual and target occluded participants (because the occluder blocks both the target and the 0° fixation letter).” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
mechanismTWO DESIGNS ARE RUN WITH THE SAME NUMBER OF REPETITIONS PER FIXATION, and the trade-off between them is stated rather than left implicit: the full range captures the functional form of the response profile, the partial range buys statistical power in less time — “Note that the partial and full range conditions both have the same number of repetitions (147) per fixation point. However, the full range condition allowed us to capture the full functional form of the MGA profile, whereas the partial range condition allowed us to gain statistical power over a shorter period of time.”
mechanismvisual information is removed at DIFFERENT MOMENTS DURING the reach using liquid crystal shutter glasses, while separate occluders remove information about the hand and the target — “Occluders were used in the visual and target occluded conditions to remove any information about the hand and target. Moreover, liquid crystal shutter glasses (Milgram 1987) were used to remove visual information at different moments during the reach”
mechanismthe read-out is justified by prior work before it is used: maximum grip aperture is known to scale with object size and to INCREASE when visual information is degraded — “Previous work shows that maximum grip aperture scales with object size (Paulignan et al. 1991, 1997) and also increases when visual information is degraded” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
mechanismthe experimental logic is stated as an if-then over that read-out: if grip aperture changes with eye position when the target is OCCLUDED, that is taken to suggest the brain uses knowledge of the transformation uncertainty when planning a reach from a stored location — “We tested whether reaching behavior similarly adjusts for changes in target location uncertainty introduced by varying CTU. Specifically, participants reached to the remembered locations of occluded targets while eye position was varied. The experimental logic is that if we find that maximum grip aperture changes with eye position when the target is occluded, it suggests the brain uses knowledge of CTU for reach planning that results from remapping stored target location information between eyeand headcentered coordinates.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
relation — “Sober and Sabes (2005) showed that people use knowledge about CTU when integrating current sensory information.”
relation — “These results extend previous neural (Batista et al. 1999; Pouget et al. 2002) and psychophysical evidence (from pointing) (Crawford et al. 2004; Henriques et al. 1998; Lewald and Ehrenstein 2000; Mergner et al. 2001) for eyecentered target storage by showing that eye-centered storage persists in a task for which there is no visual information specifying target location.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
relation — “To show the effect of CTU on pointing behavior, we modeled data from a previously published experiment (Lewald and Ehrenstein 2000).”
replicationuntested
limitationAn attentional-load account of the occluded-condition effect cannot be excluded by the data, only argued against. — “Although we attribute the change in grasping behavior in the occluded condition to target uncertainty, it may be that holding eye position away from forward view creates an attentional load that affects grasping.”
limitationThe results are consistent with, but do not establish exclusive, eye-centred storage; multiple simultaneous reference frames remain possible. — “Note that our results do not preclude the possibility that target information is stored in multiple coordinate frames (Avillac 2005).”
limitationThe visual fixation marks used in the task may themselves have induced the eye-centred storage strategy the study infers. — “Another possibility is that visual fixation marks promoted an eyecentered storage strategy.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
limitationThe model assumes large memory uncertainty and the data could not adjudicate a limited-memory alternative. — “In addition, we could not reject a limited memory model on the basis of cross-trial analyses—any influences of fixations from previous trials were too small to be reliably measured.”
limitationThe paper's own alternative explanation is that the apparatus induced the strategy the study infers: the visual fixation marks may have 'promoted an eye-centered storage strategy', being usable to maintain a representation of the body's configuration with respect to the apparatus. It is raised as a possibility and not settled, so the inference from behaviour to storage frame is conditional on the fixation marks not having supplied the frame. — “Another possibility is that visual fixation marks promoted an eyecentered storage strategy. In particular, fixation marks may have been used to maintain an accurate representation of the body’s configuration with respect to the apparatus.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
