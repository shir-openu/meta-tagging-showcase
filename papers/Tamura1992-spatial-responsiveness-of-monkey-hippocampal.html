phenomenonspatial responsiveness of monkey hippocampal neurons— “To investigate involvement of the hippocampal formation in spatial information processing, activity of neurons in the hippocampal formation of the conscious monkey was recorded during presentation of various visual and auditory stimuli from several directions around the monkey.”
claim-typeexistence— “Of 1,047 neurons recorded, 106 (10.1%) responded to some stimuli from one or more directions.”
method — evidence— “Briefly, single-unit activity was recorded from the HF and PH.”
mechanismspatial information is converted from a primary retinotopic representation into egocentric or allocentric representations somewhere between the sensory association cortices and the hippocampal formation / parahippocampal cortices— “Therefore, some computation or conversion of information from the primary step of spatial representation (such as retinotopic space) to a more complicated spatial representation (such as egocentric or allocentric space) may be made between the sensory association cortices and the H F and PH, or within the H F and PH.”
mechanismprolonged attention directed to one region of space during training and recording recruits more neurons that encode stimulus relations in that region— “If learning or memory should be related to neuronal activity, it might be possible that the increased time of attention to that area caused the difference seen in the number of neurons, that is, more neurons represent relations to various combinations of stimuli that were seen in the right anterior.”
builds-on O'Keefe and Conway, 1978 (rat hippocampus)— “This means that, consistent with a previous study of the rat hippocampus (O'Keefe and Conway, 1978), some neurons in the HF of the monkey may be involved in the representation of relations between stimuli through both vision and audition.”
replicates Creutzfeldt, 1990; Vidyasagar et al., 1991 (hippocampal neurons activated by human actions)— “This report is very similar to the present results in that selective neurons tended to respond to human action.”
extends Feigenbaum and Rolls (1991)— “Responsiveness of the neurons that was changed by movement of the apparatus may represent relations between the test stimulus and background stimuli fixed on the apparatus, and these might correspond to neurons with responses in the frame-of-reference of allocentric coordinates reported by Feigenbaum and Rolls (1991).”
senseegocentric, here - A LABEL FROM ONE ROTATION, AND WIDER THAN BODY-CENTRED: neurons are so designated because their response directions followed the rotation of the monkey, and the relations meant are to background stimuli on the monkey OR ON THE RESTRAINING DEVICE that rotated with it, so apparatus-fixed and body-fixed responses fall under one name— “We designate these neurons as “egocentric,” because the directions of their responses followed the rotation of the monkey, and these responses were considered to represent relations between the test stimulus and background stimuli on the monkey or on the restraining device that rotated with the monkey”
senseallocentric, here - THE COMPLEMENT OF THE SAME OPERATION: neurons whose response directions did not follow the rotation but remained constant relative to the environment; both categories are produced by a single manipulation, and 'were considered to represent' marks where the operation ends and the interpretation begins— “We designated these neurons as “allocentric,” because the directions of their responses did not follow the rotation of the monkey but remained constant relative to the environment”
sensethe third group - THE LARGEST, AND UNNAMED: 12 of the 32 rotated neurons had their responses reversibly extinguished, against 11 egocentric and 9 allocentric, so the plurality of the sample falls outside the dichotomy that the key words carry— “Thirty-two of 106 neurons were also tested by rotating the monkey. The directionally differentiating responsiveness of 1 1 neurons followed the monkey (egocentric neurons), that of 9 remained in place in the environment (allocentric neurons), and responses of 12 were reversibly extinguished when the monkey was rotated”
controlthe left and right hemispheres were compared for the anterior-direction preference — held — rules out that the directional preference is a hemispheric asymmetry rather than a spatial response - no significant left-right difference was found— “The tendency of neurons to respond to visual stimuli presented from the anterior direction cannot be explained by leftright difference of hemispheres since there was no significant difference in the incidence of recording rate in the left and right hemispheres.”
differs inegocentric —contrasted-with→ allocentric — whether the response direction followed the rotated monkey or stayed fixed to the environment— “Thirty-two of 106 neurons were also tested by rotating the monkey. The directionally differentiating responsiveness of 1 1 neurons followed the monkey (egocentric neurons), that of 9 remained in place in the environment (allocentric neurons), and responses of 12 were reversibly extinguished when the monkey was rotated”— “followed the monkey (egocentric neurons), that of 9 remained in place in the environment (allocentric neurons)”
for which uselimitation — the subset of neurons whose responses did not track the apparatus: IT IS DIFFICULT TO INTERPRET RESPONSIVENESS OF NEURONS THAT CHANGE IRRESPECTIVE OF MOVEMENT OF THE APPARATUS— “It is difficult to interpret responsiveness of neurons that change irrespective of movement of the apparatus.”
for which uselimitation — the spatial interpretation of the responses: OTHER FACTORS MIGHT HAVE INFLUENCED THE RESPONSIVENESS, SUCH AS SUPPRESSION BY PARTICULAR STIMULI THAT COULD NOT BE SEEN IN THE USUAL POSITION— “However, other factors might have influenced the responsiveness, such as suppression by particular stimuli that could not be seen in the usual position.”
for which uselimitation — the visual-versus-auditory asymmetry: THE REASONS FOR THIS DISCRIMINATION ARE NOT ENTIRELY CLEAR, and the offered account is a plausibility argument about what is useful to the monkey— “The reasons for this discrimination are not entirely clear, but it seems reasonable that vision should depend on stimuli presented within the visual field, and audition might be more valuable to the monkey when stimuli cannot be seen.”
for which uselimitation — any claim about where the coordinate conversion happens: FURTHER STUDIES ARE NECESSARY TO INVESTIGATE SUCH COMPUTATION OR CONVERSION— “Further studies are necessary to investigate such computation or conversion.”
for which uselimitation — any use of the egocentric/allocentric dichotomy from this paper: of the 32 neurons tested by rotation, 11 followed the monkey and 9 remained fixed to the environment, while 12 - the largest group - were reversibly extinguished and received no label— “Thirty-two of 106 neurons were also tested by rotating the monkey. The directionally differentiating responsiveness of 1 1 neurons followed the monkey (egocentric neurons), that of 9 remained in place in the environment (allocentric neurons), and responses of 12 were reversibly extinguished when the monkey was rotated”
for which uselimitation — any reading of these egocentric neurons as body-centred: the designation covers responses considered to represent relations to background stimuli on the monkey OR on the restraining device that rotated with it— “We designate these neurons as “egocentric,” because the directions of their responses followed the rotation of the monkey, and these responses were considered to represent relations between the test stimulus and background stimuli on the monkey or on the restraining device that rotated with the monkey”
replicationuntested
limitationResponses that changed independently of the apparatus movement resist interpretation.— “It is difficult to interpret responsiveness of neurons that change irrespective of movement of the apparatus.”
limitationAlternative explanations, such as suppression by stimuli not visible in the usual position, cannot be ruled out for the neurons whose responsiveness was extinguished.— “However, other factors might have influenced the responsiveness, such as suppression by particular stimuli that could not be seen in the usual position.”
limitationThe directional bias of visual versus auditory responsiveness is not fully explained and may be an artefact of the training and recording set-up.
limitationWhere the conversion from retinotopic to egocentric or allocentric representation occurs is not resolved by these data.
limitationThe largest of the three response groups is left unnamed and absent from the paper's key words.
limitationThe egocentric category is defined to include stimuli fixed to the restraining apparatus, not only to the body.
