phenomenonspatial responsiveness of monkey hippocampal neurons
claim-typeexistence
mechanismspatial information is converted from a primary retinotopic representation into egocentric or allocentric representations somewhere between the sensory association cortices and the hippocampal formation / parahippocampal cortices — “Therefore, some computation or conversion of information from the primary step of spatial representation (such as retinotopic space) to a more complicated spatial representation (such as egocentric or allocentric space) may be made between the sensory association cortices and the H F and PH, or within the H F and PH.”
mechanismprolonged attention directed to one region of space during training and recording recruits more neurons that encode stimulus relations in that region — “If learning or memory should be related to neuronal activity, it might be possible that the increased time of attention to that area caused the difference seen in the number of neurons, that is, more neurons represent relations to various combinations of stimuli that were seen in the right anterior.”
relation — “This means that, consistent with a previous study of the rat hippocampus (O'Keefe and Conway, 1978), some neurons in the HF of the monkey may be involved in the representation of relations between stimuli through both vision and audition.”
relation — “This report is very similar to the present results in that selective neurons tended to respond to human action.”
relation — “Responsiveness of the neurons that was changed by movement of the apparatus may represent relations between the test stimulus and background stimuli fixed on the apparatus, and these might correspond to neurons with responses in the frame-of-reference of allocentric coordinates reported by Feigenbaum and Rolls (1991).”
differs inegocentric → allocentric — whether the response direction followed the rotated monkey or stayed fixed to the environment — “followed the monkey (egocentric neurons), that of 9 remained in place in the environment (allocentric neurons)”
replicationuntested
limitationResponses that changed independently of the apparatus movement resist interpretation. — “It is difficult to interpret responsiveness of neurons that change irrespective of movement of the apparatus.”
limitationAlternative explanations, such as suppression by stimuli not visible in the usual position, cannot be ruled out for the neurons whose responsiveness was extinguished. — “However, other factors might have influenced the responsiveness, such as suppression by particular stimuli that could not be seen in the usual position.”
limitationThe directional bias of visual versus auditory responsiveness is not fully explained and may be an artefact of the training and recording set-up. — “The reasons for this discrimination are not entirely clear, but it seems reasonable that vision should depend on stimuli presented within the visual field, and audition might be more valuable to the monkey when stimuli cannot be seen.”
limitationWhere the conversion from retinotopic to egocentric or allocentric representation occurs is not resolved by these data. — “Further studies are necessary to investigate such computation or conversion.”
limitationThe largest of the three response groups is left unnamed and absent from the paper's key words. — “Thirty-two of 106 neurons were also tested by rotating the monkey. The directionally differentiating responsiveness of 1 1 neurons followed the monkey (egocentric neurons), that of 9 remained in place in the environment (allocentric neurons), and responses of 12 were reversibly extinguished when the monkey was rotated”
limitationThe egocentric category is defined to include stimuli fixed to the restraining apparatus, not only to the body. — “We designate these neurons as “egocentric,” because the directions of their responses followed the rotation of the monkey, and these responses were considered to represent relations between the test stimulus and background stimuli on the monkey or on the restraining device that rotated with the monkey”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingOnly about one in ten hippocampal-formation neurons recorded (106 of 1,047) responded preferentially to stimuli coming from one or more particular directions. — “Of 1,047 neurons recorded, 106 (10.1%) responded to some stimuli from one or more directions.”
findingOf the 106 direction-sensitive neurons, 49 responded to vision only, 35 to audition only, and 22 to both modalities. — “Of these 106 neurons with directionally differentiating responsiveness, 49 responded to visual stimulation, 35 responded to auditory stimulation, and 22 responded to both visual and auditory stimulation.”
findingVisually driven neurons preferred stimuli from the right anterior direction whereas auditory ones preferred stimuli from behind the monkey. — “The vision-responsive neurons tended to respond to stimulation presented from the right anterior direction, and the sound-responsiveness neurons tended to respond more to stimulation presented from the posterior.”
findingMoving the experimental apparatus, which the monkey could see, altered the responses of 36 of the 59 neurons tested that way. — “Changing the location of the experimental apparatus modified the responses of 36 of 59 neurons.”
findingFor 24 of the affected neurons the preferred direction stayed the same but the response in that direction was strongly weakened or reversibly abolished. — “For the other 24, the preferred direction did not change, but responses in the preferred direction were very weakened, or the directionally differentiating responses were reversibly extinguished.”
findingRotating the monkey showed that 11 neurons carried their preferred direction with the animal, making them egocentric. — “When the monkey was rotated, the directionally differentiating responsiveness of 11 neurons followed the monkey.”
findingNine neurons kept their preferred direction anchored to the room rather than to the animal, making them allocentric. — “The directionally differentiating responsiveness of nine neurons remained in place in the environment and did not follow the monkey.”
findingTwelve further neurons lost their directional selectivity altogether while the monkey was rotated, and regained it afterwards. — “The directionally differentiating responsiveness of 12 other neurons was reversibly extinguished when the monkey was rotated.”
findingResponse size depended on exactly where the visible apparatus stood, not merely on whether it was visible at all. — “The response magnitude also depended on the precise location of the apparatus when the monkey could see it.”
findingStimulus-selective auditory neurons responded to complex experimenter-produced sounds - footsteps, a chair moving, a door opening, a data recorder being switched, board tapping and paper crumpling - rather than to pure tones. — “All of these neurons exhibited strongly selective responses to complex sounds made by the experimenter: seven responded to a step, four to the sound of a chair moving, two to the sound of a door opening, two to a data recorder being switched, two to board tapping, one to the sound of paper crumpling.”
findingDirection-sensitive neurons were found along the whole rostrocaudal axis of the hippocampal formation but were concentrated caudally. — “These neurons were located throughout the rostrocaudal extent of the HF, but tended to be located caudally (chi-square test, fY.01).”
findingEye position and eye movement did not account for the visual responses of most of these neurons. — “Judging from EOGs, television monitoring, and inspection of experimenter, there was no clear correlation between visual responses of most neurons and initial eye position or eye movement.”
findingIn the 22 bimodal neurons the visual and auditory preferred directions coincided in only half of the cells (11 of 22). — “Of the 22 visual plus auditory neurons with directionally differentiating responsiveness, the visual and auditory responses of 11 overlapped (4, left anterior; 2, anterior; 5, right anterior) and responses of 11 did not overlap direction.”
findingThe subicular complex held a higher share of neurons whose directional responsiveness changed when the apparatus moved (8 of 10) than the dentate gyrus, CA3 or CA1, which were split roughly evenly. — “In the subicular complex, the number of neurons that changed their directionally differentiating responsiveness when the location of the experimental apparatus was changed exceeded the number of stable neurons that did not change (8 of 10 changed, 2 of 10 stable), but there were no clear differences between the numbers of these neurons in other areas (dentate gyrus: 7 of 13 changed, 6 of 13 stable; CA3: 8 of 16 changed, 8 of 16 stable; CAI: 10 of 17 changed, 7 of 17 stable; EC: 2 of 2 changed; PH: 1 of 1 changed).”

definitiondirectionally differentiating responsiveness — “We use the term directionally differentiating since the neurons responded differently when stimuli were presented from different positions (or directions) around the monkey.”
definitionbackground stimuli — “Background stimuli is a general term meaning stimuli that could provide spatial reference for the test stimulus.”
definitionegocentric neurons — “We designate these neurons as “egocentric,” because the directions of their responses followed the rotation of the monkey, and these responses were considered to represent relations between the test stimulus and background stimuli on the monkey or on the restraining device that rotated with the monkey.”
definitionallocentric neurons — “We designated these neurons as “allocentric,” because the directions of their responses did not follow the rotation of the monkey but remained constant relative to the environment, and these responses were considered to represent relations between test stimulus and background stimuli fixed in the environment.”
