phenomenongradual accumulation of evidence in perceptual decision-making

mechanismWhen evidence is weak the accumulator drifts only slowly away from the decision boundary, so it takes longer to establish which side it is on, which is why difficult decisions are slow. — “In this model, when the evidence is strong, the accumulator’s value quickly diverges away from the decision boundary, and it rapidly become easy to say on which side of the decision boundary it is.”
mechanismFOF acts downstream of accumulation, categorizing the accumulator's value into a choice, which is why its activity is required near the go cue rather than throughout the stimulus. — “the FOF’s main role in the task lies in categorizing the value of the accumulator, an operation that occurs after the graded evidence accumulation itself”
mechanismDiscrepant PPC inactivation results across rodent visual tasks may arise because the inactivation spreads beyond PPC into neighbouring small secondary visual areas that supply sensory input. — “the observed effects from inactivations targeting rodent PPC could perhaps be due to inactivation spillover into one of the many small V2 areas”
mechanism — “Critically, when averaged across easy trials, firing rates ramped slowly; but when averaged across difficult trials, firing rates ramped much more sharply--precisely as expected of the evidence accumulator ([ 25 – 27 ]; Fig. 1b ).”
mechanism — “Thus what is best read out from FOF firing is not the graded value of the accumulator, but rather which categorical decision report the subject should make if the trial ended at that point in time.”

replicationcontested
limitationThe review restricts itself to two-alternative decisions and excludes human work. — “We limit ourselves to two-alternative decisions (for multi-alternative decisions, see [ 20 ]), and will not address work in humans (see [ 21 ]).”
limitationWhether rats and primates in fact use the same circuits and algorithms for accumulation is inferred from similar ramping responses and is not established. — “Whether this is indeed the case remains an open question, of course.”
limitationBecause the anatomical border of rodent PPC with adjacent small V2 areas is still uncertain, inactivation effects attributed to PPC may reflect spillover into V2. — “With the precise definition of rodent PPC and the location of its border with these V2 areas still highly uncertain and a matter of active research [ 68 – 70 ], the observed effects from inactivations targeting rodent PPC could perhaps be due to inactivation spillover into one of the many small V2 areas.”
limitationThe authors report that their own earlier serial model of the two regions was wrong — “As we describe below, this serial model turned out to be wrong.”
limitationNeither of the two candidate regions satisfies the criteria the authors themselves propose — “As described above, neither the FOF nor the PPC satisfy all three criteria.”
limitationA negative inactivation result cannot rule out a redundant circuit in which no single region is required — “While the PPC could, perhaps, play a role in gradual accumulation as part of a much larger redundant circuit in the Poisson Clicks task, it is notable that out of 6 different rat regions probed so far (medial prefrontal cortex and superior colliculus, (Hanks, Yartsev, et al. unpublished data); anterior dorsal striatum and auditory striatum (Yartsev, Hanks, et al., unpublished data); and FOF and PPC), the only region for which silencing has no impact on task behavior is the PPC.”
limitationThe region name itself is unsettled, so results labelled PPC may not concern the same tissue across studies — “Further clarity on the role of the PPC will greatly benefit from better definitions and knowledge about the topography of brain regions near the location currently referred to as “rodent PPC.””
limitationSeveral of the supporting results are unpublished data from the authors' own laboratory — “Current unpublished data (Yartsev, Hanks, et al.) suggests that while some subcortical regions may be like the FOF in playing an important role in the task, but not in gradual accumulation per se, at least one other subcortical region appears to satisfy all three criteria, and may thus become the first identified node in the causal circuit for evidence accumulation.”
limitationThe causal evidence in primates rests on three microstimulation studies, all with the confound the authors name — “To date, causal perturbation studies of these areas with the primate RDM task have been limited, with only three existing published studies, all using only unilateral electrical microstimulation.”

findingInactivating rat posterior parietal cortex left performance on the accumulation task essentially unaffected, despite PPC encoding the graded accumulator value. — “In contrast to expectations, however, inactivation of the PPC produced entirely negligible effects on performance of the Poisson Clicks task”
findingThe authors conclude that PPC is the least likely of the regions examined to be a genuine node of the accumulation circuit. — “Consequently, we consider the PPC to have the lowest likelihood of being an important center of the causally relevant circuit for gradual evidence accumulation.”
findingThe serial account in which PPC computes the accumulator and hands it to FOF was falsified by the perturbation results. — “As we describe below, this serial model turned out to be wrong.”
findingFrontal orienting fields carry a categorical, step-like read-out of which side of the decision boundary the accumulator is on, rather than its graded value. — “Thus what is best read out from FOF firing is not the graded value of the accumulator, but rather which categorical decision report the subject should make if the trial ended at that point in time.”
findingUnpublished data suggest at least one subcortical region meets all three criteria and could be the first identified node of the causal accumulation circuit. — “Current unpublished data (Yartsev, Hanks, et al.) suggests that while some subcortical regions may be like the FOF in playing an important role in the task, but not in gradual accumulation per se, at least one other subcortical region appears to satisfy all three criteria, and may thus become the first identified node in the causal circuit for evidence accumulation.”
findingThe authors propose a nomenclature reform, restricting the label 'rodent PPC' to tissue whose physiology and connectivity match primate PPC. — “We advocate reserving the name “posterior parietal cortex (PPC)” in rodents to regions displaying physiological and connectivity profiles similar to those of the primate PPC”
findingparietal cortex encodes the graded accumulator value and yet silencing it leaves task performance essentially unchanged — “In contrast to expectations, however, inactivation of the PPC produced entirely negligible effects on performance of the Poisson Clicks task [ 53 ].”
findingthe same inactivations did affect interspersed free-choice trials, so the manipulation itself worked — “This was the case even while the same inactivations had a large effect on interspersed control “free choice” trials [ 53 ], and even though PPC inactivations have a substantial effect on a different auditory task involving parametric working memory [ 54 ].”
findingof six rat regions silenced so far, parietal cortex is the only one with no effect on the task — “the only region for which silencing has no impact on task behavior is the PPC”
findingtuning curves distinguish two regions whose trial-averaged responses look alike: graded in parietal, step-like in frontal — “But while tuning curves in PPC were found to be smooth functions of the value of the accumulator --indicating that the graded value of the accumulator could be read out from PPC firing, as also found in the primate PPC [ 51 ]-- the tuning curves in the FOF were much more step-like, with one cluster of firing rates for accumulator values to one side of the decision boundary, and a different cluster of firing rates for values on the other side of the decision boundary (see Fig. 2e ).”
findingthe mapping from accumulator value to firing rate stays fixed across the trial even as the value changes — “Thus, although the value of the accumulator changes during the course of a trial, the mapping from accumulator value to firing rate does not.”
