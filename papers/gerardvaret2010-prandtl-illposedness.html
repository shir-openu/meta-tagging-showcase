phenomenonill-posedness of the Prandtl boundary layer equation
claim-typeexistence
mechanismTHE EXPECTED ANSWER IS STATED BEFORE IT IS OVERTURNED: on the strength of the inviscid result the authors say it seems reasonable to bet on well-posedness, and then show the equation is linearly ill-posed in that very setting — “On the basis of the inviscid result, it seems reasonable to bet for well-posedness of the Prandtl equation (1.3) in Sobolev type spaces. The aim of this paper is to show that it is actually linearly ill-posed in this framework.”
mechanismthe destabilisation is attributed to TWO ingredients together - viscosity, and critical points in the base velocity profile - and the authors immediately state what that conjunction buys them: the result does not contradict the positive results for the inviscid case or for monotonic data — “As we shall see later on, the reason for illposedness is a strong destabilization mechanism due to two ingredients: viscosity, and critical points in the base velocity profile. In particular, it does not contradict the positive results obtained in the inviscid case and for monotonic data.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]

findingThe Cauchy problem for the Prandtl boundary-layer equation is LINEARLY ILL-POSED in Sobolev-type spaces (though well-posed for analytic or monotone data), proven by constructing unstable quasimodes at high tangential frequencies around solutions with non-degenerate critical points. — “We prove here that it is linearly ill-posed in Sobolev type spaces. The key of the analysis is the construction, at high tangential frequencies, of unstable quasimodes for the linearization around solutions with non-degenerate critical points.”
findingStrikingly, the strong instability is caused by VISCOSITY — consistent with well-posedness of the inviscid version of the equation. — “the strong instability is due to vicosity, which is coherent with well-posedness results obtained for the inviscid version of the equation”
