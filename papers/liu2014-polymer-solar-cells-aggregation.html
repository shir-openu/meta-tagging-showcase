Abstract Although the field of polymer solar cellterm has seen much progress in device performance in the past few years, several limitations are holding back its further development. For instance, current high-efficiency (>9.0%) cells are restricted to material combinations that are based on limited donor polymers and only one specific fullereneterm·key acceptor. The uncovered aggregationterm·key and design rules yield three high-efficiency (>10%) donor polymers and will allow further synthetic advances and matching of both the polymer and fullereneterm·key materials, potentially leading to significantly improved performance and increased design flexibility.

Conventional inorganic solar cells can achieve high efficiencies but are produced through complicated, costly processes. The desirability of lower costs is driving the development of several third-generation solar technologies. Among these, polymer solar cellterm (PSCterm) 1 2 3 4 5 6 technology is an excellent example of low-cost production because PSCsterm can be produced using extremely high-throughput roll-to-roll printing methods similar to those used to print newspapers 7 . PSCsterm also offer several other advantages: vacuum processing and high-temperature sintering are not needed, and no toxic materials are used in the end product. Most importantly, a tandem cell architecture 6 8 9 10 can be easily implemented with PSCsterm and has proven to improve PSCterm efficiency by ~40–50% (refs 6 , 8 ). As PSCsterm are two-component, donor–acceptor material systems, it is generally important to control the morphologyterm·key of the donor:acceptor blends and to find an optimal materials combination with excellent optical and electronic properties. In the last few years, record-efficiency PSCsterm were achieved with only three donor polymers (which all belong to a specific polymer family based on fluorinated thieno[3,4- b ]thiophene, for example, PTB7) that are, furthermore, constrained to be used with a specific fullereneterm·key, PC 71 BM, to achieve their best performance 11 12 13 . In general, the morphologies 14 15 16 and thus performance of state-of-the-art donor polymers (for example, PTB7 (refs 11 , 17 ) and PBDT-DTNT 18 ) are sensitive to the choice of fullereneterm·key and replacing PC 71 BM with another C 60 -based or non-PCBM fullereneterm·key decreases PSCterm efficiency to 6-7% (refs 11 , 18 , 19 , 20 ) The dominant role of PC 71 BM places serious constraints on PSCterm material development, because the properties of the polymers must be precisely matched with fixed targets set by PC 71 BM. As tandem PSCsterm require two sets of perfectly matching polymer/fullereneterm·key materials, the constraint on their development is compounded. It has thus been pointed out that it is crucial to have the flexibility of being able to use different fullerenesterm·key and more generally to remove material constraints to achieve tandem PSCsterm with 15–20% efficiency envisioned by Brebac and colleagues 9 10 21 . The development of polymer:fullereneterm·key material systems that are morphologically insensitive to fullereneterm·key choice will remove these material constraints, and greatly accelerate material development for single-junction and tandem PSCsterm 10 22 .

Another important fundamental issue for the PSCterm field is how to control the morphologyterm·key of polymer:fullereneterm·key blends to achieve the best PSCterm performance. There is likely more than one near-optimum PSCterm morphologyterm·key. The famous PTB7 family donor polymers enabled one type of the near-optimum PSCterm morphologyterm·key, as high external quantum efficiencies (EQEs~80%) have been reported for PTB7-based cells 11 . However, the PTB7-based PSCterm materials and devices have certain limitations. Besides the sensitivity of the choice of fullerenesterm·key, another important limitation for PTB7 family polymersterm is that they cannot perform well when relatively thick active layers (~300 nm) are used in the PSCterm device. Thick-filmterm·key PSCsterm are important for the industrial application of PSCsterm, and thick films should also further increase the absorption strength of the solar cell and thus cell efficiency. The reason why PTB7 does not perform well in thick-filmterm·key PSCsterm is partially owing to the relatively low hole transport ability (space charge limited currentterm·mth (SCLCterm·mth) mobility ~6 × 10 −4 cm 2 V −1 s −1 ; ref. 17 ) related to the low crystallinityterm·key of the PTB7 polymer. There has been also evidence that high purity of the polymer domain may be an important factor to achieve efficient thick-filmterm·key PSCsterm 14 23 24 . The PTB7-based materials systems are characterized by relatively impure polymer domains 25 , which could be a reason why these polymers do not perform well in thick-filmterm·key PSCsterm. Clearly, there is a need for new materials systems that explore a different ‘near-optimum’ PSCterm morphologyterm·key in order to achieve thick-filmterm·key PSCsterm that have comparable or higher efficiencies than state-of-the-art PTB7 materials systems.

In the following, we report the achievement of high-performance (efficiencies up to 10.8% and fill factors (FFs) up to 77%) thick-filmterm·key PSCsterm based on three different donor polymers and 10 polymer:fullereneterm·key combinations, all of which exhibit efficiencies higher than the previous state of the art. In contrast to state-of-the-art PTB7-based materials systems, the high PSCterm performances in this report are achieved via the formation of an ‘optimum PSCterm morphologyterm·key’ that contains highly crystalline, sufficiently pure, yet reasonably small polymer domains. The high polymer crystallinityterm·key and thus excellent hole transport ability, combined with sufficiently pure polymer domains, are the main reasons why the PSCsterm exhibit high FFs and efficiency even when the active layer is 300 nm thick. Importantly, this ipso facto near-perfect morphologyterm·key is controlled by the temperature-dependent aggregation behaviourterm·dom of the donor polymers during casting and is insensitive to the choice of fullerenesterm·key. Taking advantage of the robust polymer:fullereneterm·key morphologyterm·key enabled by the three donor polymers, many non-traditional fullerenesterm·key are also used. Traditional PCBMs, the most dominant fullerenesterm·key in PSCsterm, are out-performed by several other non-traditional fullerenesterm·key, clearly indicating the benefits of exploring different fullerenesterm·key and the robust morphologyterm·key formation. Comparative studies on several structurally similar polymers reveal that the 2-octyldodecyl (2OD) alkyl chainsterm·key sitting on quaterthiophene is the key structural feature that causes the polymers’ highly temperature-dependent aggregation behaviourterm·dom that allows for the processing of the polymer solutions at elevated temperature, and, more importantly, controlled aggregationterm·key and strong crystallization of the polymer during the film cooling and drying process. The branching position and size of the branched alkyl chainsterm·key are critically important in enabling an optimal aggregationterm·key behaviour. With our approach, PSCterm production is no longer constrained by the use of a single fullereneterm·key or by a very thin active layer. Our aggregationterm·key and morphologyterm·key control approach and polymer design rules can be applied to multiple polymer:fullereneterm·key materials systems and will allow the PSCterm community to explore many more polymers and fullereneterm·key materials and to optimize their combinations (energy offsets, bandgap and so on) under a well-controlled morphological landscape, which would greatly accelerate the materials and process development towards improved PSCsterm.

The thick cell exhibits 10–20% higher EQE values, and the effective absorption bandwidth of a thick PSCterm can be increased as the result of a ~20 nm red-shift of the ‘leading, low energy edge’ of a PSCterm’s EQE spectrum. Polymer crystallinityterm·key and hole mobilityterm·key Grazing incident wide-angle X-ray diffractionterm·mth (GIWAXSterm·mth) 26 reveals the molecular packing and orientational texture of pure PffBT4T-2ODterm·key and PffBT4T-2ODterm·key:fullereneterm·key blend films. These morphological data show that PffBT4T-2ODterm·key can form a polymer:fullereneterm·key morphologyterm·key containing highly crystalline and sufficiently pure yet reasonably small polymer domains. Note that PTB7-type polymers have been the best donor polymer in PSCsterm for the past few years. By its very nature of high performance in thin films, PTB7 can form a ‘near-optimum’ PSCterm morphologyterm·key characterized by relatively low molecular ordering, relatively low hole mobilities and impure polymer domains 25 . PffBT4T-2ODterm·key exhibits high molecular ordering (‘crystallinityterm·key’), high hole mobilities and purer polymer domains, which appears to be a different ipso facto ‘near-optimum’ PSCterm morphologyterm·key. Although PTB7 enabled great thin-film PSCterm performance, PffBT4T-2ODterm·key offers high performance even in thick-filmterm·key PSCsterm owing to the high mobility of the highly ordered and sufficiently pure polymer domains it forms.

Morphologyterm·key control via temperature-dependent aggregationterm·key We attribute PffBT4T-2ODterm·key’s excellent performance and robust morphologyterm·key to its significant temperature-dependent aggregation behaviourterm·dom that can be exploited during device fabrication. The UV-Vis absorption spectra exhibit a marked red-shift when a low concentration PffBT4T-2ODterm·key solution in 1,2-dichlorobenzene (DCB) is lowered from 85 to 25 °C ( Fig. 1d ). At elevated temperature, PffBT4T-2ODterm·key is well dissolved and disaggregated. At progressively lower temperatures, a strong 0-0 transition peak at ~700 nm emerges with significant strength at 25 °C, indicating strong aggregationterm·key of the polymer chains in solution at that temperature. Note that the absorption spectrum of the 25 °C solution of PffBT4T-2ODterm·key is almost identical to that of the optimized PffBT4T-2ODterm·key solid film ( Fig. 1d ), which is observed to be highly crystalline by GIWAXSterm·mth. Consequently, devices are always cast from warm solutions (60–80 °C) of PffBT4T-2ODterm·key, which then aggregates during the cooling and film-forming process.

To understand details of PffBT4T-2ODterm·key’s aggregationterm·key behaviour during the film-forming process, the critical π – π molecular ordering ((010) coherence lengthterm·dom and intensity of the (010) peak) is determined with X-ray diffraction (XRD) for a series of PffBT4T-2ODterm·key films spun at different rates. As shown in Fig. 2d and Supplementary Table 2 , the π – π orderingterm·dom decreases markedly with increasing spin ratesterm·mth. As PffBT4T-2ODterm·key exhibits a strong yet progressively evolving aggregationterm·key property, the extent of PffBT4T-2ODterm·key’s aggregationterm·key depends upon temperature and concentration changes, the film drying time and the kinetics of aggregationterm·key. During a slow spin process (for example, 700 r.p.m.), it takes a relatively long time for the solution and film to dry, during which the temperature of the substrate and the wet film also decreases significantly. When using an ultra-fast rate (for example, 5,000 r.p.m.), however, the solvent evaporates more quickly, which results in kinetically quenched, poorly ordered films, whereas slow spin ratesterm·mth provides PffBT4T-2ODterm·key sufficient time to aggregate and to form crystalline polymer domains with large coherence lengthsterm·dom. Importantly, studies for PffBT4T-2ODterm·key pure films and PffBT4T-2ODterm·key blend films with two different fullerenesterm·key yield similar trends ( Supplementary Table 2 and Supplementary Fig. 4 ), demonstrating that the aggregationterm·key of PffBT4T-2ODterm·key is insensitive to the presence of fullerenesterm·key. Not surprisingly, high substrate temperatures were found to have a similar effect to fast spin ratesterm·mth. PffBT4T-2ODterm·key:fullereneterm·key films prepared with fast spin ratesterm·mth/high substrate temperatures show a decrease in the 0-0 transition peaks and a pronounced shift in the 0-0 transition energy in their UV-Vis absorption spectra, indicative of significant disorder ( Fig. 2e ). The corresponding hole-only and PSCterm devices fabricated using high spin ratesterm·mth and high substrate temperature also exhibit markedly decreased hole mobilities (3.1 × 10 −3 cm 2 V −1 s −1 ; Supplementary Table 3 ) and PSCterm efficiencies (3.6%; Supplementary Table 4 ). These morphological, spectroscopic and electric data demonstrate that PffBT4T-2ODterm·key’s morphologyterm·key is mainly controlled by the progress of its aggregationterm·key during the film-casting process until the film is dry, which locks-in the length scale of the morphologyterm·key. PffBT4T-2ODterm·key’s strong yet well-controllable aggregationterm·key property allows for convenient optimization of processing conditions that led to a near-ideal polymer:fullereneterm·key morphologyterm·key that is insensitive to the choices of fullereneterm·key. This approach of controlling the extent of polymer aggregationterm·key during a warm solution casting process is different from the common processing protocol of PTB7 family polymersterm that are typically processed at room temperature.

Discussion The key structural feature of PffBT4T-2ODterm·key that enables its pronounced, yet gradual temperature-dependent aggregationterm·key ( Fig. 1d ) is the second-position branched alkyl chainsterm·key (2OD) on a quaterthiophene (4T-2OD). To elucidate this aspect, we contrast PffBT4T-2ODterm·key with two structurally very similar polymers. These two polymers have the same backbone but their alkyl chainsterm·key are branched at the first or third side-chain carbon atom (for ease of comparison, these two polymers are named as PffBT4T-1ON and PffBT4T-3OT; Fig. 4a ). In contrast to PffBT4T-2ODterm·key, PffBT4T-1ON is disaggregated at 85 °C, and more importantly, also disaggregated at 25 °C ( Fig. 4b ). As a result, PffBT4T-1ON cannot aggregate easily during the film-forming process, leading to films with poor crystallinityterm·key (GIWAXSterm·mth pattern shown in Fig. 4c ) and thus PSCterm devices of only ~0.6% efficiency. PffBT4T-3OT exhibits the other extreme, showing excessive aggregationterm·key at both 25 and 85 °C. During our attempt to process PffBT4T-3OT, the PffBT4T-3OT solution quickly becomes a gel ( Fig. 4d ) even before the start of spin casting. These comparisons indicate that PffBT4T-1ON’s alkyl chainsterm·key cause too much steric hindrance, which results in poor aggregationterm·key and crystallinityterm·key. PffBT4T-3OT’s alkyl chainsterm·key provide too little steric hindrance that makes aggregationterm·key of PffBT4T-3OT too strong even at 85 °C and makes it difficult to process. PffBT4T-2ODterm·key’s second-position branched alkyl chainsterm·key offer an optimal tradeoff that allows for controllable aggregationterm·key of PffBT4T-2ODterm·key during the film-forming process. More discussions on the impact of alkyl chainterm·key branching positions are provided in Supplementary Note 1 .

Several structurally similar donor polymers containing quaterthiophene substituted with second-position branched alkyl chainsterm·key were reported in the literature 34 35 36 37 , including a recent report in which a polymer with longer alkyl chainsterm·key, FBT-Th 4 (1,4) (named as PffBT4T-2DT for simplicity in this paper, structure shown in Fig. 4a ), achieved 7.64% efficiency 34 . The difference of PffBT4T-2ODterm·key and PffBT4T-2DT devices can be understood from the following results. R-SoXSterm·mth ( Fig. 4e ) and prior AFMterm·mth studies 34 show that the average domain size of PffBT4T-2DT:fullereneterm·key films is too large (~100 nm). The R-SoXSterm·mth data furthermore show that the average purity of the polymer/polymer-rich domains in an PffBT4T-2DT:fullereneterm·key film is ~87% of that in PffBT4T-2ODterm·key:fullereneterm·key film. Lower purity of average polymer/polymer-rich domains can result in significant recombination for thick-filmterm·key PSCterm devices and thus lower performance 14 23 24 . Regarding molecular ordering, the two-dimensional GIWAXSterm·mth mapping of PffBT4T-2DT:PC 71 BM films 34 shows that PffBT4T-2DT:fullereneterm·key films exhibit weak laminar packing peaks, which are significantly weaker than those of PffBT4T-2ODterm·key:fullereneterm·key films. The smaller degree of laminar packing of PffBT4T-2DT is consistent with the lower average purity of PffBT4T-2DT polymer domains compared with that of PffBT4T-2ODterm·key’s, as more fullereneterm·key is expelled by the crystalline polymer domains of PffBT4T-2ODterm·key. Lastly, the absorption coefficient of PffBT4T-2DT is lower than that of PffBT4T-2ODterm·key owing to longer alkyl chainsterm·key that does not contribute to light absorption ( Supplementary Fig. 5 ). These studies show that the branching position and the size of the alkyl chainsterm·key are critically important in obtaining the optimal aggregationterm·key properties of PffBT4T-2ODterm·key. Insufficient aggregationterm·key (for example, PffBT4T-1ON) and unnecessarily long alkyl chainsterm·dom (for example, PffBT4T-2DT) resulted in low crystallinityterm·key and/or impure polymer domains. Excessive aggregationterm·key (for example, PffBT4T-3OT) makes the processing and aggregationterm·key difficult to control. Similar to recent observations 32 36 38 , the molecular weight of PffBT4T-2ODterm·key has a significant impact on its aggregationterm·key property and performance. Lower molecular weight ( M n =16.6 kDa, M w =29.5 kDa) batches of PffBT4T-2ODterm·key exhibit weaker aggregationterm·key and thus lower efficiency (7.7%) than the high molecular weight ( M n =47.5 kDa, M w =93.7 kDa) PffBT4T-2ODterm·key batches ( Supplementary Table 5 and Supplementary Fig. 6 ). The impacts of polymer molecular weight on PSCterm performances are discussed in details in the Supplementary Note 2 .

Following the rationale described above, we synthesized two other polymers (poly[(2,1,3-benzothiadiazol-4,7-diyl)- alt -(4′,3′′-difluoro-3,3′′′-di(2-octyldodecyl)-2,2′;5′,2′′;5′′,2′′′-quaterthiophen-5,5′′′-diyl)] (PBTff4T-2OD) and poly[(naphtho[1,2- c :5,6- c ′]bis[1,2,5]thiadiazol-5,10-diyl)- alt -(3,3′′′-di(2-octyldodecyl)-2,2′;5′,2′′;5′′,2′′′-quaterthiophen-5,5′′′-diyl)] (PNT4T-2OD); Fig. 1a ) with significantly different polymer backbones but with similar arrangements of 2OD alkyl chainsterm·dom. Both PBTff4T-2OD and PNT4T-2OD exhibit significant temperature-dependent aggregation behaviourterm·dom that leads to processing and morphologyterm·key control and thus efficiency (including >10% for thick-filmterm·key PSCsterm; Table 1 , Supplementary Table 1 and Supplementary Fig. 7 ) comparable to those achieved by PffBT4T-2OD-based PSCsterm. R-SoXSterm·mth and AFMterm·mth studies confirmed that the polymer domain size of these two new polymers are similar to that of PffBT4T-2ODterm·key (30–40 nm). XRD characterization of PBTff4T-2OD:fullereneterm·key and PNT4T-2OD:fullereneterm·key films also showed strong (010) π – π stacking peaks that are similar to those observed for PffBT4T-2ODterm·key. Note that PNT4T-2OD also significantly outperforms its analogue polymer with 2-decyltetradecyl (2DT) alkyl chainsterm·key 35 , providing another example that supports the critical importance of the size of the alkyl chainsterm·key. The synthesis, characterization and device performance of PBTff4T-2OD and PNT4T-2OD are described in detail in the Supplementary Information ( Supplementary Fig. 7 , Table 1 and Supplementary Table 1 ).

Although second-position branched alkyl chainsterm·key are a well-known structural motif and have been previously used on quaterthiophene-based polymers, previous work did not utilize a polymer with the most suitable alkyl chainsterm·key nor were warm-casting methods used that optimally harnessed aggregationterm·key. They thus failed to reveal the connections between chemical structure, polymer aggregationterm·key during warm processing, morphologyterm·key formation, polymer crystallinityterm·key and consequently PSCterm performance. Our study uncovered a new approach of aggregationterm·key and morphologyterm·key control enabled by a structural feature (2OD alkyl chainterm·dom) that is seemingly simple and commonly known, yet has surprisingly profound impact on PSCterm performances. The wide ranging applicability of our morphologyterm·key control approach is supported by the three polymers and over 10 polymer:fullereneterm·key combinations that all yielded similar blend morphologyterm·key and high-efficiency thick-filmterm·key PSCsterm. Furthermore, the aggregationterm·key behaviour as observed by UV-Vis might serve as a useful screening tool to identify materials that yield good devices when cast from warm solutions.

Note that the chemical structures of the three donor polymers presented in the paper are distinctively different from previous state-of-the-art PTB7 family of polymers 12 13 17 . The PTB7 family polymersterm consist of an electron deficient fluorinated thieno[3,4- b ]thiophene unit and a benzodithiophene unit with alkoxy, alkylthienyl or alkylthiothienyl substitution groups. The three polymers in this paper consist of an electron deficient unit (either difluorobenzothiadiazole or benzothiadiazole or naphthobisthiadiazole) combined with a quaterthiophene unit with two 2OD alkyl chainsterm·dom sitting on the first and fourth thiophenes. The difference in the chemical structures caused different aggregationterm·key properties, based on which different processing protocols are used. While PTB7 family polymersterm do not exhibit a strong temperature-dependent aggregationterm·key property and are often processed from room temperature solutions, the 4T-2OD based polymersterm are processed from warm solutions to utilize their temperature-dependent aggregationterm·key property so that the morphologyterm·key and extent of molecular ordering can be explicitly controlled during casting.

To summarize, we report that exquisite control of aggregationterm·key results in high-performance thick-filmterm·key PSCsterm for three different donor polymers and 10 polymer:fullereneterm·key combinations, all of which yielded efficiencies higher than the previous state of the art (9.5%). The common structural feature of the three donor polymers, the 2OD alkyl chainsterm·dom on quaterthiophene, causes a temperature-dependent aggregation behaviourterm·dom that allows for the processing of the polymer solutions at moderately elevated temperature, and more importantly, controlled aggregationterm·key and strong crystallization of the polymer during the film cooling and drying process. This results in a near-ideal polymer:fullereneterm·key morphologyterm·key (containing highly crystalline, preferentially orientated, yet small polymer domains) that is controlled by polymer aggregationterm·key during casting and thus insensitive to the choice of fullerenesterm·key. The branching position and size of the branched alkyl chainsterm·key are critically important in enabling a well-controllable aggregationterm·key behaviour. Unnecessarily long alkyl chainsterm·dom (for example, 2DT) cause several detrimental effects including weaker laminar stacking, poorer absorption properties and less pure polymer domainsterm·dom. Our structural design rationales and aggregationterm·key and morphologyterm·key control approach offer a new route to achieve high-performance thick-filmterm·key PSCsterm that cannot be obtained from previous state-of-the-art material systems. Given that the field and record performance in the last few years has been mostly dominated by a single system (PTB7 family with PC 71 BM), the 10 material systems and three polymers based on a single and simple design feature presented here point to a plethora of possible materials combinations that should further improve the performance. Our approach will allow the PSCterm community to explore many more polymers and fullereneterm·key materials and to optimize their combinations (energy offsets, bandgap and so on) under a well-controlled morphological landscape that would greatly accelerate the materials and process development towards improved PSCsterm.
