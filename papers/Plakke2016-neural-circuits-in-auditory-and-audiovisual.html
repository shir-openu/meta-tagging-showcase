The boom vocalization changes the meaning of the subsequent alarm call, and the Diana monkeys recognize this context and do not send out their own alarm calls.”

findingCooling the ventrolateral prefrontal cortex significantly impaired detection of both auditory and visual mismatches when monkeys had to hold a face-voice pair in memory. — “Inactivation of VLPFC, by cooling, resulted in a significant impairment on both auditory mismatch and visual mismatch trials, indicating that VLPFC is necessary for audiovisual working memory ( Figure 5 ).”
findingWhen only the face had to be remembered, VLPFC cooling left performance intact. — “In Experiment 2, when subjects were required to remember only the face information alone, VLPFC inactivation did not affect performance in detecting the mismatching face component ( Plakke et al., 2015 ), Figure 6 .”
findingWhen only the vocalization had to be remembered, VLPFC cooling significantly reduced accuracy, even at a delay of only about a second. — “In contrast, in Experiment 3, when subjects were required to remember only the vocalization, VLPFC inactivation by cooling resulted in a significant decrease in performance accuracy ( Plakke et al., 2015 ), Figure 6 .”
findingAcross the three cooling experiments VLPFC proved necessary for auditory and audiovisual working memory but dispensable for visual working memory. — “These three experiments demonstrate that VLPFC is essential for audiovisual and auditory working memory but not for visual working memory.”
findingThe inactivation results place VLPFC as a necessary endpoint of the auditory ventral stream for auditory cognition rather than merely a correlated node. — “The inactivation experiments in Plakke et al., (2015) provides direct evidence that VLPFC is an essential region for auditory working memory and suggests VLPFC is a major end point of the auditory ventral stream, necessary for auditory cognition.”
findingVisually responsive prefrontal neurons are distributed across both dorsal and ventral prefrontal cortex, whereas auditory-responsive cells are confined to VLPFC. — “On the other hand, there are unimodal visual responsive neurons, found across a wide region of both dorsal and ventral prefrontal cortex; these cells are not confined to VLPFC as auditory responsive cells are.”
findingAnterior auditory belt regions project to the frontal pole and to ventrolateral prefrontal areas 12vl, 12 orbital and 45, giving VLPFC direct access to non-spatial acoustic information. — “Anterior auditory belt and parts of ML show strong projections to the frontal pole, the ventrolateral prefrontal cortex areas 12 vl, 12 orbital and 45 (i.e. inferior convexity), and the rostral principle sulcus ( Romanski et al., 1999b ).”
findingCaudal auditory cortex, which codes sound location, targets a different frontal set: the caudal principal sulcus, frontal eye fields and dorsal premotor cortex. — “In contrast, caudal auditory cortical regions, which respond to sound location, project to the caudal principal sulcus, frontal eye fields and dorsal premotor cortex.”
findingMemory for sounds is weaker than memory for images: auditory memory decays faster and is more easily disrupted by interference than visual memory. — “Comparisons of auditory and visual memory curves highlight the notion that auditory memory is more susceptible to interference and less robust compared to visual memory ( Wright 2007 ).”
findingWhat degrades auditory memory is chiefly distraction during the retention interval rather than the sheer duration of the delay. — “This suggests that during auditory memory, distraction occurring during the delay, may be more important than the length of the delay itself”
findingcooling VLPFC impaired both auditory and visual mismatch detection when both components had to be held — “Inactivation of VLPFC, by cooling, resulted in a significant impairment on both auditory mismatch and visual mismatch trials, indicating that VLPFC is necessary for audiovisual working memory ( Figure 5 ).”
findingwith only the face to remember, cooling VLPFC did nothing; with only the vocalization, it impaired performance — “In Experiment 2, when subjects were required to remember only the face information alone, VLPFC inactivation did not affect performance in detecting the mismatching face component ( Plakke et al., 2015 ), Figure 6 . In contrast, in Experiment 3, when subjects were required to remember only the vocalization, VLPFC inactivation by cooling resulted in a significant decrease in performance accuracy ( Plakke et al., 2015 ), Figure 6 .”
