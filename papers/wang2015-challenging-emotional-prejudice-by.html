transferfrom cultural psychology: Markus and Kitayama's independent/interdependent self-construal distinction, imported as the cognitive variable that might explain a cultural difference in neural empathy— “According to Markus and Kitayama (2010) , an independent schema of self organizes behavior primarily in reference to an individual’s own thoughts and feelings and results in a weakened sense of in-group/out-group relationship so that people can move between in-group and out-group relatively easily.”
mechanismself-construal sets where the psychological boundary falls: interdependence draws the line between in-group and out-group, independence draws it between self and all others and so weakens the in-group/out-group split that produces biased empathy— “Our findings are consistent with a social-cognitive model of the link between self-construals and intergroup relationship ( Markus and Kitayama, 2010 ) that suggests that interdependent self-construals induce a strong boundary between in-group and out-group whereas independent self-construals define a strong boundary between the self and any…
mechanismindependent priming shifts cultural identity toward Western cultures, so Caucasian models are recategorised as in-group and their suffering draws a stronger neural response— “One possible account is that independent self-construal priming shifted our participants’ cultural identity toward Western cultures and Caucasian models were thus treated as in-group members, which in turn enhanced neural responses to their suffering.”
mechanismindependent priming weakens race-based group affiliation between the Chinese participants and the Chinese models, dissolving the basis of the in-group advantage— “Alternatively, priming independent self-construals may weaken race-based group affiliation between Chinese participants and Chinese models shown in video clips.”
mechanismempathic concern generates altruistic motivation, so a bias in empathy toward in-group members produces parochial altruism in behaviour— “Because empathic concern produces altruistic motivation ( Batson, 2011 ), in-group bias in empathy may provide a mechanism of behavioral parochial altruism.”
mechanisman interdependent self organises behaviour by reference to others in one's relationships, which sharpens the in-group/out-group line— “In contrast, an interdependent schema of self organizes behavior immediately in reference to the thoughts and feelings of others with whom a person is in relationship and leads to significant ingroup–outgroup distinction.”
mechanisman independent self organises behaviour by reference to one's own thoughts, which weakens the group boundary and lets a person cross it easily— “According to Markus and Kitayama (2010) , an independent schema of self organizes behavior primarily in reference to an individual’s own thoughts and feelings and results in a weakened sense of in-group/out-group relationship so that people can move between in-group and out-group relatively easily.”
mechanismthe route from bias to behaviour runs through empathy: empathic concern produces altruistic motivation, so a biased empathy yields a parochial altruism— “Because empathic concern produces altruistic motivation ( Batson, 2011 ), in-group bias in empathy may provide a mechanism of behavioral parochial altruism.”
