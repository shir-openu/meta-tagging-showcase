limitationThe link between the observed activity and actual long-term memory consequences of WM performance cannot be established. — “Therefore, we do not know for sure how LTM is actually affected by the WM performance for specific items, i.e., it is unknown which items are actually maintained in WM.”
limitationThe proposed link to persistent activity recorded in vitro is acknowledged as speculative. — “It should be noted, though, that this analogy between electrophysiological data in vitro and the neural patterns during WM maintenance must remain speculative at the current stage.”
limitationPosition-specific subsequent-memory analyses were statistically underpowered. — “Possibly, this lack of an effect is related to the reduced statistical power for position-specific analyses of subsequent memory effects.”

findingRecall probability was highest for words at the start of the list, reproducing the primacy effect in both samples. — “As expected, the proportion of successfully encoded items was higher at the beginning of the list than at later list positions.”
findingEarly-list words that were later forgotten were accompanied by hippocampal deactivation signatures in both fMRI and intracranial EEG. — “Items at the beginning of the list which were subsequently forgotten were associated with negative blood oxygen level dependent responses and positive direct current slopes, corresponding to hippocampal deactivations, suggesting that they were not processed in hippocampus-dependent WM.”
findingNo such deactivation appeared for later list items or for items that were successfully remembered. — “These deactivations were absent for items later in the list and for subsequently remembered items.”
findingIn the intracranial data the linear decline of DC slope across list positions was significant only for subsequently forgotten items. — “We thus performed separate one-way ANOVAs of subsequently remembered and forgotten items and found a significant linear effect of position on the DC slopes of subsequently forgotten items ( F (1,7) = 8.130; p < 0.05), but not of subsequently remembered items ( F (1,7) = 1.170; p > 0.3).”
findingA second, independent general linear model reproduced the effect: left-hippocampal negative BOLD for forgotten items at position one was absent at position seven. — “In the same anatomically defined region of interest in the left hippocampus used in the first general linear model, we observed a negative BOLD response for subsequently forgotten items presented at the first position, which was absent for items presented at the seventh position ( Fig. 6 A ) ( F (1,18) = 4.746; p < 0.05).”
findingThe position effect was left-lateralised: the right hippocampus showed a main effect of subsequent memory but no position effect. — “For the right hippocampus, a three-way ANOVA did not reveal an effect of, or interaction with, the factor position (all p > 0.3), but a significant main effect of memory ( F (1,18) = 8.898; p < 0.01).”
findingOutside the hippocampus, the anterior cingulate cortex was increasingly activated across early list positions irrespective of later memory. — “We found that for both subsequently remembered and subsequently forgotten items, activity was most pronounced in the anterior cingulate cortex (remembered: MNI −3/39/15; forgotten: MNI −3/36/15), a region commonly related to conflict processing.”
findingSuccessful encoding at the start of a list came in runs: encoding the first word raised the probability of encoding the second. — “While overall probability for LTM encoding at the second list position was 0.35, it increased to 0.51 if only trials where the first word was already successfully encoded were taken into account (conditional probability of encoding the second item given that the first item was encoded).”
findingThe authors conclude that failing to engage hippocampus-dependent working memory early in a list causes the later encoding failure. — “However, failure to recruit hippocampus-dependent WM processes at early list positions leads to failure of LTM formation.”
findinghippocampal deactivation at the first list position occurred for items that were later forgotten — “For subsequently forgotten items, we observed a deactivation during presentation of the first item which was reduced during presentation of items presented later in the list”
findingno such deactivation appeared for items that were later remembered — “For subsequently remembered items, this deactivation was not apparent ( Fig. 4 C ), again in line with our iEEG results.”
findingDC slopes were positive early in the list for subsequently forgotten items and flattened later — “We thus performed separate one-way ANOVAs of subsequently remembered and forgotten items and found a significant linear effect of position on the DC slopes of subsequently forgotten items ( F (1,7) = 8.130; p < 0.05), but not of subsequently remembered items ( F (1,7) = 1.170; p > 0.3). For subsequently forgotten items, DC potentials had a positive slope at early list positions, which was reduced later in the list.”
findingonly the first position differed significantly from zero — “To test whether the positive slopes observed at early list positions actually corresponded to deviations from zero, we calculated two-tailed t tests between the slopes at each list position and zero for later forgotten items. This analysis revealed a significant difference at the first position ( t (7) = 3.026; p < 0.05), but not at any other position.”
findingencoding the first item raised the conditional probability of encoding the second, in both groups — “For the iEEG subjects, encoding success at the second position was indeed slightly higher if the first item was successfully encoded: While overall probability for LTM encoding at the second list position was 0.35, it increased to 0.51 if only trials where the first word was already successfully encoded were taken into account (conditional probability of encoding the second item given that the first item was encoded).”
findingpatients showed both primacy and recency effects — “At the end of the list, there was a significant difference in recognition of the 11th and fifth item and between the 12th and fifth, sixth, seventh, and eighth item (recency effect).”
findingthe healthy fMRI group showed primacy but no recency at all — “Post hoc comparisons showed that recognition performance of the first three items was superior to recognition performance of items at all other positions (primacy effect). findingposition effects on the whole-brain level centred on anterior cingulate cortex for both remembered and forgotten items — “These effects did not survive correction for multiple comparisons, but did have a large extension of 60 voxels for the serial position contrast of subsequently forgotten words and even 204 voxels for the serial position contrast of subsequently remembered items. findinga second general linear model comparing the first and seventh positions reproduced the result — “The results are very similar to the results from the previous general linear model (where only the first three positions were taken into account): In the same anatomically defined region of interest in the left hippocampus used in the first general linear model, we observed a negative BOLD response for subsequently forgotten items presented at the first position, which was absent for items presented at the seventh position ( Fig. 6 A ) ( F (1,18) = 4.746; p < 0.05).
