mechanismInterchangeability salva veritate is too weak in an extensional language and question-begging in a language rich enough to contain 'necessarily'— “In an extensional language, therefore, interchangeability salva veritate is no assurance of cognitive synonymy of the desired type.”
mechanismAppeal to semantical rules fails because 'semantical rule' is itself undefined except by where the phrase is printed— “Semantical rules are distinguishable, apparently, only by the fact of appearing on a page under the heading 'Semantical Rules'; and this heading is itself then meaningless.”
mechanismBecause statements meet experience only collectively, any statement can be preserved by revising others, so no statement is analytic in the sense of being immune— “Any statement can be held true come what may, if we make drastic enough adjustments elsewhere in the system.”
mechanismThe two dogmas support each other: only if confirmation attaches to statements one at a time does a vacuously confirmed statement make sense— “as long as it is taken to be significant in general to speak of the confirmation and infirmation of a statement, it seems significant to speak also of a limiting kind of statement which is vacuously confirmed, ipso facto , come what may; and such a statement is analytic.”
contradicts the analytic-synthetic distinction (first dogma of empiricism)— “That there is such a distinction to be drawn at all is an unempirical dogma of empiricists, a metaphysical article of faith.”

contradicts Carnap, "Empiricism, Semantics, and Ontology" (1950)— “Carnap has recognized 12a that he is able to preserve a double standard for ontological questions and scientific hypotheses only by assuming an absolute distinction between the analytic and the synthetic; and I need not say again that this is a distinction which I reject.”
extends the pragmatism of Carnap, Lewis, and others— “Carnap, Lewis, and others take a pragmatic stand on the question of choosing between language forms, scientific frameworks; but their pragmatism leaves off at the imagined boundary between the analytic and the synthetic.”
critiques Carnap's explanation of analyticity by state-descriptions and by semantical rules— “The criterion in terms of state-descriptions is a reconstruction at best of logical truth.”
critiques Kant's formulation of the analytic statement— “This formulation has two shortcomings: it limits itself to statements of subject-predicate form, and it appeals to a notion of containment which is left at a metaphorical level.”

extends Carnap's own treatment of the physical world in the Aufbau— “My countersuggestion, issuing essentially from Carnap's doctrine of the physical world in the Aufbau , is that our statements about the external world face the tribunal of sense experience not individually but only as a corporate body.”
supports White's essay on the analytic and the synthetic as an untenable dualism— “White's excellent essay "The Analytic and the Synthetic: An Untenable Dualism," in John Dewey: Philosopher of Science and Freedom (New York, 1950), says much of what needed to be said on the topic; but in the present paper I touch on some further aspects of the problem.”
senseanalytic - first class, logical truth: true and staying true under every reinterpretation of everything except the logical particles— “If we suppose a prior inventory of logical particles, comprising 'no,' 'un-' 'if,' 'then,' 'and,' etc., then in general a logical truth is a statement which is true and remains true under all reinterpretations of its components other than the logical particles.”
senseanalytic - second class, synonymy-dependent: reducible to a logical truth only by putting synonyms for synonyms, and this is the class the paper says cannot be explained— “The characteristic of such a statement is that it can be turned into a logical truth by putting synonyms for synonyms; thus (2) can be turned into (1) by putting 'unmarried man' for its synonym 'bachelor.'”
senseanalytic - Kant's sense: the subject already conceptually contains the predicate, a formulation the paper faults for assuming subject-predicate form— “Kant conceived of an analytic statement as one that attributes to its subject no more than is already conceptually contained in the subject.”
senseanalytic - Carnap's sense: true under every state-description, which the paper argues reconstructs only logical truth— “A statement is then explained as analytic when it comes out true under every state-description.”
senseanalytic - Leibniz's sense: true in all possible worlds, i.e. incapable of being false— “Leibniz spoke of the truths of reason as true in all possible worlds. Picturesqueness aside, this is to say that the truths of reason are those which could not possibly be false.”
senseanalytic - the self-contradiction sense: a statement whose denial is self-contradictory, rejected as no clearer than what it explains— “In the same vein we hear analytic statements defined as statements whose denials are self-contradictory.”
sensesynonymy - cognitive: the only sense the argument needs, explicitly not identity of psychological association or poetic quality— “Now let us be clear that we are not concerned here with synonymy in the sense of complete identity in psychological associations or poetic quality; indeed no two expressions are synonymous in such a sense. We are concerned only with what may be called cognitive synonymy .”
sensedefinition - lexicographic report: records a synonymy already implicit in usage, so it cannot be the ground of that synonymy— “Definitions reporting selected instances of synonymy come then as reports upon usage.”
sensedefinition - explication: improves on the definiendum instead of reporting a synonymy, yet still rests on prior synonymies— “In explication the purpose is not merely to paraphrase the definiendum into an outright synonym, but actually to improve upon the definiendum by refining or supplementing its meaning.”
sensedefinition - conventional abbreviation: the sole case where synonymy is genuinely created rather than reported— “Here we have a really transparent case of synonymy created by definition; would that all species of synonymy were as intelligible.”
sensereductionism - radical: every meaningful statement translatable into a statement about immediate experience— “The most naive view of the relationship is that it is one of direct report. This is radical reductionism . Every meaningful statement is held to be translatable into a statement (true or false) about immediate experience.”
sensereductionism - attenuated: the surviving supposition that a statement in isolation can be confirmed at all, which the paper says is the form that still does damage— “The dogma of reductionism survives in the supposition that each statement, taken in isolation from its fellows, can admit of confirmation or infirmation at all.”
sensemeaning - as against reference: what essence becomes once it is detached from the object and attached to the word— “Meaning is what essence becomes when it is divorced from the object of reference and wedded to the word.”
senseeconomy - two antagonistic kinds in a formal system: brevity of expression versus poverty of primitive grammar and vocabulary— “In logical and mathematical systems either of two mutually antagonistic types of economy may be striven for, and each has its peculiar practical utility.”
