mechanismAND THE CONTROL CONDITION IS REPORTED AS PARTLY FAILING ITS OWN THEORY: the same group **OCCASIONALLY RESPONDED TO EXPLICIT RACIAL APPEALS EVEN THOUGH THESE APPEALS WERE RECOGNIZED AS RACIALLY INSENSITIVE**. On the dog-whistle account an explicit appeal should be neutralised by the egalitarian norm the moment it is recognised, so a recognised appeal that still moves opinion is a result against the mechanism, reported by its own testers— “We also find evidence that the same group occasionally responded to explicit racial appeals even though these appeals were recognized as racially insensitive”
contradicts racial priming theory (the dog-whistle hypothesis as previously formulated)— “However, among participants as whole, we found no evidence that the implicit appeal increases the strength of participants’ racial prejudice on their evaluation of welfare programs, contrary to what racial priming theory would predict.”
extends the dog-whistle hypothesis— “These findings extend the dog-whistle hypothesis by demonstrating that ideology can condition whites’ responses to racial appeals.”
builds-on Hutchings et al. (2010)— “This explanation is consistent with previous research suggesting that implicit racial appeals are more effective than explicit appeals only among social groups that endorse norms of egalitarianism (Hutchings et al. 2010).”

findingIn Study 1, white participants who read Reagan's implicit racial appeal opposing welfare reported significantly less support for welfare than those who read a race-neutral control message.— “Participants assigned to the implicit racial appeal condition reported significantly less support for welfare than those assigned to the no racial appeal condition (p = .02).”
findingThe welfare dog-whistle effect was concentrated among self-identified liberals high in racial resentment, who became less supportive of welfare after the implicit appeal.— “We find a negative three-way interaction between liberalism, racial resentment, and the implicit racial appeal condition (p = .04) such that liberals high in racial resentment were less supportive of welfare after reading the implicit racial appeal compared to a message with no racial appeal.”
findingStudy 2 replicated the ideology-conditioned pattern in a different policy domain: the implicit appeal strengthened the link between prior racial attitudes and gun-control support among liberals only.— “Looking first at the effect of the implicit appeal, results suggest that implicit racial appeals increased the effect of prior racial attitudes on support for gun control among liberals.”
findingIn Study 2 the implicit appeal also harnessed explicit racial prejudice, making prejudiced liberals more supportive of gun control than in the control condition.— “In addition, we found a similar positive, three-way interaction between liberalism…
findingContrary to racial priming theory, explicit racial appeals were not less effective than implicit ones at increasing the influence of racial attitudes on policy views.
findingAmong political moderates the implicit pro-gun-control appeal backfired, lowering gun-control support among the most racially prejudiced rather than raising it.
findingIn 2016 ANES data, white liberals high in racial resentment were the group most likely to have switched from Obama in 2012 to Trump in 2016.
