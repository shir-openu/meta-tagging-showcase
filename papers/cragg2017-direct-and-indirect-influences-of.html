phenomenonexecutive function contributions to mathematics achievement— “In this study we investigated the extent to which executive function skills contribute to these three components of mathematical knowledge, whether this mediates the relationship between executive functions and overall mathematics achievement, and if these relationships change with age.”
claim-typemechanism— “Domain-specific skills partially mediated the relationship between executive functions and mathematics achievement: Inhibitory control within the numerical domain was associated with factual knowledge and procedural skill, which in turn was associated with mathematical achievement.”
method — evidence— “Two hundred and ninety-three participants aged between 8 and 25 years completed a large battery of mathematics and executive function tests.”
mechanismAchievement tests additionally require locating the mathematical problem inside a verbal or visual description and building a problem representation, and working memory maintains and manipulates that representation - which is why a direct working-memory path survives beyond the component skills— “It is likely that working memory plays a key role in these processes in terms of maintaining and manipulating these problem representations in mind.”
mechanismWorking memory activates and retrieves number facts from long-term memory while inhibition suppresses the neighbouring but incorrect answers that are co-activated during retrieval— “This is consistent with theories that propose that working memory is required to activate and retrieve mathematical facts stored in long-term memory, and also that inhibitory processes are needed to suppress co-activated but incorrect answers.”
mechanismConceptual information is stored in a verbal code whereas arithmetic facts also carry a visuospatial component from how sums are presented and encoded, so only verbal working memory supports conceptual retrieval while both domains support fact retrieval— “could be because conceptual information is stored in a verbal code, whereas mathematical facts perhaps also contain a visuospatial component”
builds-on Geary (2004); Geary & Hoard (2005) hierarchical framework of mathematics— “The findings support a modified version of a hierarchical framework for mathematics (Geary, 2004; Geary & Hoard, 2005) in which domain-general executive function skills, in particular working memory, support domain-specific mathematical processes, which in turn underpin overall mathematics achievement.”
extends previous hierarchical multi-component models of mathematics— “We extended previous models by demonstrating that working memory also directly contributes to mathematical achievement (Fig. 3).”
contradicts Cowan and Powell (2014); Fuchs et al. (2005)— “We found that executive functions explained a similar amount of variance in both factual knowledge (12%) and procedural skill (15%), which is inconsistent with the findings of Cowan and Powell (2014) and Fuchs et al. (2005) who found that domain-general factors accounted for more variance in procedural skill than factual knowledge.”
contradicts Andersson (2010); Clark et al. (2010)— “This conflicts with findings from other studies that have examined the extent to which performance on a cognitive flexibility task predicts performance on a test of procedural skill (Andersson, 2010; Clark et al., 2010).”
replicates Hecht et al. (2003); Jordan et al. (2013)— “Together the executive function measures predicted more variance in factual knowledge and procedural skill than conceptual understanding, consistent with the findings of Hecht et al., (2003) and Jordan et al. (2013).”

findingThe domain-specific skills of factual knowledge, procedural skill and conceptual understanding only partially mediated the executive-function/achievement link, with numerical inhibitory control acting on achievement through factual knowledge and procedural skill.— “Domain-specific skills partially mediated the relationship between executive functions and mathematics achievement: Inhibitory control within the numerical domain was associated with factual knowledge and procedural skill, which in turn was associated with mathematical achievement.”
findingExecutive function measures explained far more variance in overall mathematics achievement (34%) than in any single component skill, and least of all in conceptual understanding (5%).— “As shown in Table 3 the executive function measures alone explained 34% of the variance in mathematical achievement, 12% of the variance in factual knowledge, 15% of the variance in procedural skill and 5% of the variance in conceptual understanding.”
findingVerbal working memory uniquely predicted all four outcomes - factual knowledge, procedural skill, conceptual understanding and mathematics achievement.— “Verbal working memory was a unique independent predictor of factual knowledge, procedural skill and conceptual understanding as well as mathematics achievement.”
findingSet shifting and non-numerical (animal-size Stroop) inhibition predicted nothing once working memory was in the model, whereas numerical inhibition uniquely predicted factual knowledge and procedural skill.— “Shifting and non-numerical inhibition did not independently predict any of the outcome variables, while numerical inhibition was a unique independent predictor of factual knowledge and procedural skill.”
findingContrary to the prediction that executive demands would fall as procedures automatise, the strength of the executive-function/mathematics relationships did not change between 8 years and adulthood.— “Contrary to our predictions we found that the relationships between executive functions and all components of mathematics were the same from 8 years of age through to adulthood.”
findingBeyond the indirect routes through the component skills, working memory retained a large direct path to mathematics achievement.— “There was also a substantial direct effect of visuospatial working memory on mathematics achievement (b = 0.25, 95% CI: 0.15–0.34).”
findingVerbal working memory influenced achievement indirectly through all three component skills, with the procedural-skill route the largest of the three indirect paths.— “There were small but significant indirect effects of verbal working memory on mathematics achievement through all three component arithmetic skills; factual knowledge (b = 0.020, 95% CI: 0.005–0.051), procedural skill (b = 0.045, 95% CI: 0.014–0.089) and conceptual understanding (b = 0.015, 95% CI: 0.002–0.041).”
findingThe predicted association of inhibition and shifting with overall mathematics achievement failed to appear when working memory was included in the same model.— “The predicted relationship between inhibition and shifting and overall mathematics achievement was not found”
