transferfrom rodent hippocampal electrophysiology: the claim that navigational machinery was conserved and repurposed, used to license reading a spatial-memory literature as a memory literature — “The hippocampal and entorhinal machinery used in spatial navigation in rodents may have been preserved in humans but put to a more elaborate and abstract use”

differs inrelation — THE AXIS IS WHETHER REPLAY HAS EVER BEEN TIED TO RECALL. 'However, THE RELATION OF SUCH REPLAY IN RODENTS TO RECALL OF PAST NAVIGATION EVENTS HAS BEEN MERELY CONJECTURAL.' The rodent replay literature is not disputed; the LINK to recall is called conjecture - and this record's [0] gives the reason it had to be, since free recall requires a declaration an animal cannot make. The limitation and its cause, in two rows of one paper. Q-511. — “the relation of such replay in rodents to recall of past navigation events has been merely conjectural”
evidential statusrelation — the paper's own words: indirectly measure — “Several neuroimaging studies show that brain activity present during the learning of information, as indirectly measured by the BOLD signal, is reinstated during cued or free recall ( 21 – 24 ), although spatiotemporal limits of fMRI restrict the possible conclusions.”
differs infree recall → recognition memory — THE AXIS IS WHETHER THE EVENT IS OBSERVABLE TO THE EXPERIMENTER. 'Whereas THE NEURONAL BASIS OF RECOGNITION MEMORY CAN BE PROBED EXPERIMENTALLY in human and nonhuman primates, THE STUDY OF FREE RECALL REQUIRES THAT THE MIND DECLARE THE OCCURRENCE OF A RECALLED MEMORY (an event INTRINSIC TO THE ORGANISM AND INVISIBLE TO AN OBSERVER).' The two are not distinguished by any property of memory - they are distinguished by WHETHER THE EXPERIMENTER CAN SEE THE EVENT HAPPEN, so the division is one of experimental access and it explains why one literature is larger than the other. Q-475. — “the neuronal basis of recognition memory can be probed experimentally in human and nonhuman primates, the study of free recall requires that the mind declare the occurrence of a recalled memory (an event intrinsic to the organism and invisible to an observer”

findingA subset of human medial temporal lobe neurons fired selectively and in a sustained way throughout and after specific video episodes, for durations up to 12 seconds. — “A subset of these neurons exhibited selective firing, which often persisted throughout and following specific episodes for as long as 12 seconds.”
findingThe same hippocampal and entorhinal neurons reactivated before the patient verbally reported freely recalling the corresponding episode. — “Verbal reports of memories of these specific episodes at the time of free recall were preceded by selective reactivation of the same hippocampal and entorhinal cortex neurons.”
findingThe reactivation effect held at the population level in hippocampus and entorhinal cortex but was absent in medial frontal units, which were selective during viewing only. — “This recurrence during free recall of the same selective neuronal responses present during viewing was found in the population of hippocampal and entorhinal units but not in medial frontal units”
findingIn an individual recall trial, firing rose above baseline roughly 1.5 seconds before the verbal report and peaked shortly before speech onset, preceding rather than following the report. — “The unit’s firing rate rose to more than 3 SD above baseline ( 15 ) about 1500 ms before onset of the verbal report of recall, peaked about 100 ms before verbal report onset, but returned to baseline only after 10 s or more”
findingEpisode-specific reactivation was regionally graded: striking for hippocampal and entorhinal cells with sustained responses, weak in amygdala, and absent in parahippocampal gyrus. — “This episode-specific reactivation phenomenon was weak in amygdala and absent from parahippocampal gyrus”
findingOver half of all recorded units responded to at least one clip, with no difference in the proportion of responsive units across the sampled regions. — “Overall, the majority of recorded neurons, 475 units (54.9%), showed a significant response to one or more of the clips, i.e., consistently increased firing rate in at least one 500-ms segment of clip presentation”
findingThe results are unlikely to be an epilepsy artefact because responses were sparse and only a minority of units came from seizure foci, whose exclusion changed nothing. — “Furthermore, only 27% of the units were recorded from within the epileptogenic seizure foci.”
finding475 of 857 recorded units (54.9%) responded to at least one clip — “the majority of recorded neurons, 475 units (54.9%), showed a significant response to one or more of the clips, i.e., consistently increased firing rate in at least one 500-ms segment of clip presentation”
findingsustained responses were almost entirely an MTL phenomenon: 44 of the 46 such cells were in MTL, only 2 in medial frontal lobe — “Of these 46 cells, 44 were in MTL and only 2 in medial frontal lobe [ P < 0.03, χ 2 ( 1 ) = 5.2]”
findingresponses lasted as long as 12 s and were typically ended only by the onset of the next clip — “Responses observed were as long as 12 s and were usually attenuated only by the onset of the next clip.”
findingacross the population, firing rose above baseline in the 3 s before the verbal report and stayed elevated for 2 s after it — “the averaged firing rate of the neurons increased significantly above baseline in the 3 s before onset of verbal report of recall and remained significantly above baseline in the ensuing 2 s”
findingthe effect was present in hippocampal and entorhinal units and absent in medial frontal units — “This recurrence during free recall of the same selective neuronal responses present during viewing was found in the population of hippocampal and entorhinal units but not in medial frontal units”
findingreactivation was strongest in cells whose viewing response had been sustained rather than transient — “but was particularly striking for hippocampal and entorhinal neurons with sustained responses”
findingwithin MTL the effect was graded by structure: weak in amygdala, absent in parahippocampal gyrus, most prominent in entorhinal cortex — “This episode-specific reactivation phenomenon was weak in amygdala and absent from parahippocampal gyrus ( fig. S11 ), but was particularly striking for hippocampal and entorhinal neurons with sustained responses”
findingthe proportion of responsive units did not differ across the sampled regions, so region selectivity appears at recall rather than in raw responsiveness — “There were no differences in proportion of responsive units among the various regions sampled in this study [ P > 0.05, χ 2 ( 5 ) = 7.6]”
findingremoving the 27% of units recorded inside the seizure focus did not change the result — “Furthermore, only 27% of the units were recorded from within the epileptogenic seizure foci. No significant difference was detected when these units were excluded from the analysis”
findingcells stayed at baseline during recall of clips that had not driven them at viewing, so the recall response is clip-specific rather than a general recall-state elevation — “These neurons remained at baseline firing during recall of clips that did not elicit significant responses during viewing (gray).”
findingthe population firing was already slightly elevated before clip onset, which the authors read as anticipation — “Note small elevation before clip onset, probably attributable to anticipatory effect.”
findingpatients recalled a mean of 83.2% of the clips they had been shown — “Patients spontaneously recalled a mean of 83.2% (±5% SEM) of the clips presented.”
