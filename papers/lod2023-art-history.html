phenomenonlinked open data modelling in art history
claim-typecritique
transferfrom semantic web and information science: Linked Data, reference ontologies and authority files, applied to art-historical research and found to fit only part of it — “In Digital Humanities (DH) research this process and its specific structure has to be represented on a data level.”
mechanisman ontology can never close: no cultural artefact is finally contextualisable, and the vocabularies are uneven across subtopics — “Complex ontologies will never be completely exhaustive since no cultural artifact or experience could be fully and finally contextualized and vocabularies are not equally accurate and detailed for every subtopic.”
mechanismthe target moves: meanings shift over the life of the project doing the modelling — “In addition, contexts and meanings shift over time, even during the timeframe of a research project.”
mechanismthe cost of manual tagging is what forces institutions to publish shallow metadata, which then constrains what anyone can ask of the data — “This leads institutions to publish only a subset of the available information, exposing minimal metadata (Doerr et al. 2010), which might prevent researchers from using the data and constrains them to conventional research methods.”
mechanismdeep modelling is not durable either - the British Museum modelled its whole collection in CIDOC CRM and then could not keep it accessible — “The British Museum, on the other hand, structured its entire collection using CIDOC CRM, but was unable to preserve its database in a consistent and accessible manner (Lincoln, 2015).”
relation — “According to Galloway "there are some things that are unrepresentable. And the computer is our guide into that realm" (2011: 91).”

relation — “The potential of these applications is obvious: LOD allows data to be reused and redistributed, revealing relationships with other data on the Web (Berners-Lee 2006).”

findingThe central question of the paper is a boundary question: what becomes structured data and what stays prose. — “A recurring question is: What should be modeled as linked open data (LOD) and what should be entered as free-form text?”
findingStandards buy accuracy and unambiguity, but research also needs room for observations that are not yet settled. — “Despite the proven utility of data standards, vocabularies, and authority files providing for accuracy, context, and unambiguity, there is also a need for an open space to formulate and present tentative observations.”
findingModelling a collection early forces a trade-off between standardisation and semantic accuracy. — “Semantic data modeling of a collection during an early stage of the research prompts a trade-off to be made between data standardization and semantic accuracy.”
findingEven major museums publishing LOD share only shallow models of their objects. — “it appears that these museums only share relatively shallow data models for their artworks on the web”
findingEven institutions with deep semantic models publish shallow ones in practice - and one that did model deeply lost the result. — “The British Museum, on the other hand, structured its entire collection using CIDOC CRM, but was unable to preserve its database in a consistent and accessible manner (Lincoln, 2015).”
findingManual tagging is precise but does not scale, and automatic tagging fails on inconsistent data - so neither route reaches existing collections. — “Manual tagging can be performed by domain experts in a very precise manner, but it cannot be performed manually to large volumes of already existing content due to the lack of resources (Simou et al. 2017) or may not be conducted automatically due to inconsistencies and/or the lack of standardization in the datasets.”
findingThe paper's boundary: quantitative relations belong in triples, reflexive and contextual material belongs in visual interfaces. — “Other, more elaborated, reflexive, or contextualized information may require different forms of representations like visual interfaces.”
findingFree text is treated as the starting point for semantic contextualisation rather than as a residue to be tidied away. — “Instead of hiding the full text in unstructured note fields, we approach this as a starting point for the semantic contextualization of the research process.”
