phenomenonlegislative voting defection
claim-typemechanism
mechanismCentralized candidate selection gives the national leadership a credible power to punish, so the MEP anticipates the cost and complies with national instructions — “Following the previous discussion, we can assume that the more centralized the system of candidate selection in the hands of the national party leadership, the more likely it is that the national party will be able to punish that MEP, regardless of the type of electoral system or the size of the MEP's electoral district.”
mechanismThe MEP weighs the cost of defecting from each principal and votes for whichever side leaves her better off, an expected-utility calculation — “Accordingly, each MEP makes a vote decision based on the overall expected utility of her action.”
mechanismWhere individual candidate votes decide electoral fortunes, reelection-seeking legislators cultivate a personal following independent of the party, which loosens party discipline — “In systems in which votes cast for individual candidates—as opposed to votes cast for a party list—significantly influence each candidate's electoral fortunes, legislators seeking reelection have strategic incentives to cultivate personal support among the electorate.”
relation — “This builds on the model in Matthew Gabel and Simon Hix,”

limitationStatistical significance is inflated by the very large number of vote-decision observations. — “The effect of most of the independent variables is highly significant, primarily because of the large number of observations.”
limitationPolicy conflict between an MEP's two principals is not observed directly but approximated by survey-based distance measures. — “These are proxy measures of policy conflict between the two party principals.”
limitationThe party-group coefficients do not generalise to MEPs in the largest national delegations because of how the model is specified. — “However, because of the structure of the model, these results for the two main European party groups apply only to those MEPs who are not members of the five largest national delegations in these party groups.”
limitationOne of the two policy-conflict predictors does not yield a clear result for national-party defection. — “The effect of the EU-integration policy distance between a national party and its European party group, however, is less clear.”
limitationA formal category is collapsed into a different one on the basis of how voters behave, not on the rule that defines it: semiopen-list systems are treated as closed-list because voters in practice choose the preordered list. — “In most countries employing these systems, however, the overwhelming majority of voters choose to vote for the preordered list rather than for individual politicians. Accordingly, votes for individual candidates rarely determine which candidates are elected. Therefore, because parties control who gets elected, most semiopen-list PR systems are, in practice, very similar to closed-list PR systems.”
limitationThe key explanatory variable is measured by proxy, and the reason given is endogeneity rather than validity: the observed quantity would be caused by the thing being explained. — “These are proxy measures of policy conflict between the two party principals. This, however, is preferable to using observed conflict in each vote because conflict in a particular vote is endogenous to the MEPs' vote decisions.”
limitationOne side of the model is assumed non-strategic, and the assumption is adopted because the quantity involved is endogenous. — “because the size of internal conflict is endogenous to the party group majority's position on an issue, I assume that the party group does not act strategically when issuing voting instructions—the majority simply takes a position, which is then communicated to the backbenchers.”
limitationThe arguments the article sets out to adjudicate are reported as never having been tested. — “However, these arguments have remained at a normative level and have not been developed theoretically or tested empirically.”
limitationThe merged electoral-system class is a modelling decision resting on a behavioural generalisation - that parties control who gets elected, so voter preferences for individuals rarely matter - and every result for that class inherits it. — “Accordingly, votes for individual candidates rarely determine which candidates are elected. Therefore, because parties control who gets elected, most semiopen-list PR systems are, in practice, very similar to closed-list PR systems”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingMEPs elected under institutions that weaken their national party's grip — a candidate-centered electoral system, decentralized candidate selection, and large district magnitude — are less likely to vote against their European party group. — “The less control a national party can exert over its MEPs as a result of the electoral institutions—if there is a candidate-centered electoral system, decentralized candidate selection, and a large district magnitude— the less likely it is that an MEP from this party will vote against the majority position of her European party group.”
findingThe same three institutions push in the opposite direction for the other principal: candidate-centered voting rules, decentralized candidate selection, or a large district each make an MEP more likely to vote against her own national party. — “In all models, if an MEP is elected under candidate-centered voting rules, with decentralized candidate-selection rules, or in a district with a large magnitude, that MEP is more likely to vote against her national party.”
findingFor defection from the national party the two institutions do not add up independently: only the combination of a candidate-centered system with decentralized selection raises defection, since either a party-centered system or centralized selection alone is enough to suppress it. — “An MEP is more likely to vote against her national party only when she is elected under a candidate-centered system with decentralized candidate-selection rules.”
findingLonger service in the European Parliament lowers defection from the European party group but raises defection from the national party. — “Finally, as expected, the longer an MEP has been in the European Parliament, the less likely it is that she will vote against her European party group, but the more likely it is that she will vote against her national party;”
findingDomestic constitutional design — semipresidentialism in France and federalism in Germany — does not carry over into MEP voting behaviour, since MEPs from both countries defect less than average from their national parties. — “Interestingly, the national party results suggest that the design of legislative-executive relations and the structure of territorial government at the domestic level does not affect individual legislative behavior in the European Parliament.”
findingDefection is rare overall: MEPs almost always voted with the majorities of both their European party group and their national party. — “As predicted, most of the time, MEPs voted the same way as the majorities of both their European party group and national party did.”
findingNational parties enforce their voting instructions best where the rules give them strong control, namely closed-list PR, small districts, or centralized candidate selection; where the rules are open-list PR, large districts, or decentralized selection, MEPs side with their European party group instead. — “The results reveal that national parties are more able to enforce their wishes on their MEPs in systems containing electoral institutions that provide these principals with strong controls—specifically, in systems with closed-list proportional representation, small districts, or centralized candidate selection.”
quantity1,031 — roll-call votes held in the European Parliament between July 1999 and June 2000, cast by 626 sitting MEPs, producing 645,406 individual vote decisions — “1,031 rollcall votes took place between July 1999 and June 2000. In these votes, with 626 MEPs serving, there were 645,406 separate vote decisions.”
quantity396,167 — vote decisions left in the analysis after exclusions, made by 526 MEPs from fifty-five national parties in fourteen member states — “Once these decisions were removed from analytical consideration, 396,167 vote decisions remained, by 526 MEPs (representing 82 percent of all MEPs serving during that period) from fifty-five national parties and fourteen member states”
quantitymore than 30 percent — reduction in the probability of voting against one's European party group for an MEP elected under the full set of institutions granting independence from the national party — “An MEP chosen via institutions that afford her a high level of independence from her national party leadership is more than 30 percent less likely to vote against her European party group than an MEP elected under the alternative set of institutions.”
quantityapproximately 20 percent — reduction in the probability of voting against one's national party for an MEP elected under the full set of institutions giving the national leadership control — “Conversely, an MEP chosen via institutions that provide a high level of control to her national party leadership is approximately 20 percent less likely to vote against her national party than an MEP elected under the alternative set of institutions.”
quantity16 percent / 15 percent — EPP members are that much less likely, and PES members that much more likely, than the average MEP to vote against their European party group — “Controlling for the membership of the main national party delegations in the groups, members of the European People's Party are approximately 16 percent less likely to vote against their European party group than the average MEP, while members of the Party of European Socialists are 15 percent more likely to vote against their European party group.”
quantity33 percent (± 2 percent) — extra likelihood that British Conservative MEPs vote against their European party group relative to the average MEP (Model 3); in Model 8 they are 19 percent (± 7 percent) more likely to defect from their national party — “In Model 3, for example, British Conservative MEPs are 33 percent (± 2 percent) more likely to vote against their European party group than the average MEP, and in Model 8 they are 19 percent (± 7 percent) more likely to defect from their national party.”
quantity7,504 — double defections in which an MEP voted yes or no directly against both principals — under 2 percent of all analysed vote decisions — “However, only 7,504 of these double defections (comprising less than 2 percent of all MEP vote decisions during this period) represented cases in which MEPs expressed a directly opposing view from their party principals”

definitionclosed-list proportional representation (PR) system — “At one end of the spectrum, closed-list proportional representation (PR) systems represent the most party-centered settings. In these systems, parties present lists of candidates, and voters cannot influence the order of the candidates on them.”
definitionsemiopen- or ordered-list PR system — “Some countries use semiopen- or ordered-list PR systems, in which parties propose preordered lists and voters can either vote for the party list as a whole or for an individual politician.”
definitionfully open-list PR system — “In fully open-list PR systems, candidates on each party's list are presented in no strategic order—for example, the candidates can be listed alphabetically—and voters must pick an individual candidate.”
definitionsingle-transferable-vote (STV) system — “Similarly, in STV systems, voters exercise ordinal preferences for the candidates in multimember constituencies.”
definitionelectoral institutions (the three that shape politician-party relations) — “Three main electoral institutions shape the relationship between politicians and parties: the electoral system; district magnitude; and candidateselection rules.”
definitionan MEP's two principals — “We then assume that each MEP has two principals: her European parliamentary group and her national party.”
definitionvoting defection (operational definition) — “if the majority of a party group voted yes, an MEP is considered to have defected from this group in a particular vote if she registered either a no or an abstain in the vote.”
definitionthe European Parliament as a laboratory — “The European Parliament is a good laboratory for isolating the impact of electoral institutions on individual legislative behavior.”
