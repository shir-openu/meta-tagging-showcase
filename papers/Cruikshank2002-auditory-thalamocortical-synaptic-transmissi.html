phenomenonauditory thalamocortical synaptic transmission— “Despite a large number of anatomical and physiological studies, very little is known about the cellular and synaptic mechanisms by which thalamic inputs are transmitted to, and processed in, ACx.”
claim-typemethod— “To facilitate an understanding of auditory thalamocortical mechanisms, we have developed a mouse brain-slice preparation with a functional connection between the ventral division of the medial geniculate (MGv) and the primary auditory cortex (ACx).”
method — evidence— “Recordings took place in an interface chamber (Haas model, Med Systems) maintained at 34°C, with a liberal flow of warmed humidified gas (95% O2-5% CO2) and ACSF passing over the slices.”
mechanismThalamic glutamatergic axons excite cortical inhibitory interneurons, which then synapse on the recorded cell, producing the disynaptic fast IPSP— “Instead, it is likely that excitatory thalamic projections drove cortical inhibitory interneurons to fire and those interneurons synapsed on the recorded cells, producing the IPSPs (discussed in Castro-Alamancos and Connors 1997; Douglas and Martin 1991; Porter et al. 2001).”
mechanismAntidromic activation of layer 5 corticotectal cells whose axons cross the shell stimulation site, feeding back through their intracortical collaterals to generate the layer 1 sink— “Thus it is possible that antidromic activation of these cells could contribute to the synaptic CSD sinks observed in the shell slice, via contacts made by their axon collaterals.”
builds-on Agmon and Connors (1991) somatosensory thalamocortical slice preparation— “To address similar problems in the somatosensory system, Agmon and Connors (1991) developed a slice preparation that maintained connections from the ventrobasal complex to barrel cortex.”
extends Metherate and Cruikshank (1999), the authors' own initial feasibility study in rat and mouse slices— “In initial studies using slices from rat and mouse, we demonstrated the likely feasibility of such a preparation by showing that stimulation within the thalamus or thalamocortical pathway could elicit cortical responses (Metherate and Cruikshank 1999).”
builds-on Barth and MacDonald (1996) finding that nonlemniscal stimulation evokes cortical gamma band activity— “These observations are broadly consistent with the finding by Barth and colleagues that stimuli presumed to activate nonlemniscal pathways evoke cortical gamma band activity whereas lemniscal stimulation does not (Barth and MacDonald 1996).”
sensean IPSP amplitude, across papers - NOT A COMPARABLE QUANTITY: whether an IPSP can be observed at all, and how large it is, depends heavily on methodology and criteria, so the same named measurement in two studies is two measurements and the authors state the comparison is difficult— “However, it is difficult to compare IPSPs across studies because the degree to which they can be observed, and their amplitudes, depends heavily on methodology and criteria”
sensecolumnar (on-beam) stimulation - NOT A SELECTIVE THALAMOCORTICAL PROBE: it is used to activate thalamocortical inputs, and the authors state it may not be a reliable means of doing so selectively, because MG and on-beam stimuli do not activate the same sets of synapses— “Fourth, we observed that these MG-evoked profiles, and the intracellular responses in layer 4, differ from those produced by conventional columnar stimulation, indicating that columnar stimulation may not be a reliable means of selectively activating thalamocortical inputs”

sensethe early IPSPs - CORTICAL IN ROUTE, INFERRED FROM A BLOCKER RATHER THAN FROM THE RESPONSE: a direct inhibitory thalamic projection is ruled out because an AMPA/KA antagonist blocked them, and the remaining account is that excitatory thalamic projections drove cortical interneurons which then synapsed on the recorded cells; the paper states this as likely, not as shown— “if the IPSPs had been the result of direct inhibitory projections from the thalamus, then antagonism of AMPA/KA receptors could not have blocked them. Instead, it is likely that excitatory thalamic projections drove cortical inhibitory interneurons to fire and those interneurons synapsed on the recorded cells, producing the IPSPs”
controlon-beam and MG-evoked IPSPs compared DIRECTLY WITHIN THE SAME SLICES rather than across the preparation — held — rules out preparation-dependent differences - slice health, cutting angle, anything that varies between preparations and would otherwise be read as a difference between the two pathways— “To control for these potential preparation-dependent effects, we directly compared on-beamand MG-evoked IPSPs within the same slices.”

findingA mouse brain slice can be cut in a near-horizontal plane that preserves a functionally intact thalamocortical pathway from the ventral medial geniculate to auditory cortex.— “The major finding of this study is that an in vitro preparation can be obtained from the mouse brain that contains functionally intact thalamocortical connections from the MGv to the ACx.”
findingAuditory thalamocortical transmission is carried by both AMPA/kainate and NMDA glutamate receptors.— “A second major finding is that auditory thalamocortical transmission involves both AMPA and NMDA types of glutamate receptors.”
findingEvery primary slice analysed showed a dominant current sink in cortical layers 3-4 after medial geniculate stimulation.— “Thus Fig. 3B shows that all 22 slices had a dominant sink in layers 3– 4.”
findingResponses evoked by thalamic stimulation differ from those evoked by conventional columnar (white matter/layer 6) stimulation, so columnar stimulation is not a reliable proxy for thalamocortical activation.— “Fourth, we observed that these MG-evoked profiles, and the intracellular responses in layer 4, differ from those produced by conventional columnar stimulation, indicating that columnar stimulation may not be a reliable means of selectively activating thalamocortical inputs.”
findingA second, more ventral 'shell' slice was introduced in which subcortical stimulation selectively activates synapses in superficial layer 1 of temporal cortex.— “Finally, a second in vitro preparation (the shell slice) is presented in which subcortical stimulation appears to selectively activate synapses in superficial layer 1 of temporal cortex.”
findingControl tracing from the inferior colliculus confirmed that layer 5 corticotectal axons pass through the shell stimulation site, providing an alternative route for the layer 1 response.— “These data confirm the hypothesis that axons belonging to layer 5 cortico-tectal cells project through the shell stimulation region on their way to the inferior colliculus.”
