Szemerédi's theorem is about 'any subset of THE INTEGERS of positive density'; the primes are not such a subset. The transference principle is about 'any subset of a sufficiently PSEUDORANDOM SET (or measure) of POSITIVE RELATIVE DENSITY'. Same word, two ambient sets, and the whole proof lives in the gap between them — “any subset of the integers of positive density contains progressions of arbitrary length”

limitationThe proof yields only a weak lower bound on the count of k-term progressions, far from the conjectured Hardy-Littlewood asymptotic. — “we do not come close to establishing this conjecture here, obtaining instead a lower bound (γ(k) + o(1))N 2/ logk N for some very small γ(k) > 0”
limitationThe methods give no progress on the Hardy-Littlewood prime tuples conjecture, which is why a pseudorandom majorant is used instead of the von Mangoldt function itself. — “Unfortunately, verifying the linear forms condition and the correlation condition for the von Mangoldt function (or minor variants thereof) is strictly harder than proving that the primes contain long arithmetic progressions; indeed, this task is comparable in difficulty to the notorious Hardy-Littlewood prime tuples conjecture, for which our methods here yield no progress.”
limitationThe argument neither requires nor delivers progress on the prime tuple conjecture. — “However we emphasise that our argument does not give (or require) any progress on this very difficult prime tuple conjecture, as we allow K to be nonzero.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
limitationA restriction-type property used in the k=3 case does not follow from pseudorandomness, and its generalisation to higher k is unclear. — “Such a property does not seem to follow simply from the pseudorandomness of ν, and generalisation to U k−1, k > 3, seems very difficult (it is not even clear what the form of such a generalisation would be).”
limitationThe paper marks the size of its own assumption in a bracket, with an exclamation mark: 'Thus, in this paper, we must assume Szemerédi's theorem. However with this one (rather large!) caveat our paper is self-contained.' The result is a conditional one - long progressions in the primes GIVEN Szemerédi - and the conditionality is carried by a parenthesis rather than by the statement of the theorem. — “In one sentence, our argument can be described instead as a transference principle which allows us to deduce Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 from Szemer´edi’s theorem, regardless of what bound we know for N0(δ, k); in fact we prove a more general statement in Theorem 3.5 below. findingGreen & Tao prove the primes contain arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions, via three ingredients: Szemeredi theorem (positive-density integer subsets contain arbitrarily long progressions), a new TRANSFERENCE PRINCIPLE extending it to positive-relative-density subsets of pseudorandom sets, and a Goldston-Yildirim result placing the primes in a pseudorandom set of almost primes. — “We prove that there are arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions of primes. There are three major ingredients. The first is Szemer ́edi’s theorem... The second, which is the main new ingredient of this paper, is a certain trans- ference principle. This allows us to deduce from Szemer ́edi’s theorem that any subset of a sufficiently pseudorandom set (or measure) of positive relative density contains progressions of arbitrary length.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
