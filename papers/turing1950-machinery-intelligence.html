claim-typemethod
transferfrom philosophy (of mind): takes the philosophical question can machines think? and replaces it with an operational, empirical test (the imitation game) — “I shall replace the question by another, which is closely related to it and is expressed in relatively unambiguous words”
mechanismDEFINITION BY ORDINARY USAGE IS REJECTED BY FOLLOWING IT TO ITS CONSEQUENCE: framing the definitions to reflect normal use is called DANGEROUS, because if the meaning of the words is to be found by examining how they are commonly used then both the meaning AND THE ANSWER must be sought in a statistical survey such as a GALLUP POLL - which is ABSURD. The method is refuted by where it leads, not by argument about meaning — “This should begin with definitions of the meaning of the terms "machine" and "think." The definitions might be framed so as to reflect so far as possible the normal use of the words, but this attitude is dangerous, If the meaning of the words "machine" and "think" are to be found by examining how they are commonly used it is difficult to escape the conclusion that the meaning and the answer to the question, "Can machines think?" is to be sought in a statistical survey such as a Gallup poll. But this is absurd.”
mechanismTHE QUESTION IS REPLACED RATHER THAN ANSWERED, AND THE REPLACEMENT IS CHOSEN FOR BEING UNAMBIGUOUS: instead of attempting a definition the question is swapped for another, described as CLOSELY RELATED TO IT and expressed IN RELATIVELY UNAMBIGUOUS WORDS. Closeness is asserted; what is claimed for the new question is its clarity, not its identity with the old one — “Instead of attempting such a definition I shall replace the question by another, which is closely related to it and is expressed in relatively unambiguous words.”
mechanismTHE SUBSTITUTION IS DECLARED AT THE MOMENT IT HAPPENS: after the game is specified, the text states flatly that THESE QUESTIONS REPLACE OUR ORIGINAL - so the swap is a recorded act rather than a drift, and a reader can see exactly which question is being answered from that point on — “We now ask the question, "What will happen when a machine takes the part of A in this game?" Will the interrogator decide wrongly as often when the game is played like this as he does when the game is played between a man and a woman? These questions replace our original, "Can machines think?"”
mechanismTHE WORTH OF THE NEW QUESTION IS ASKED, AND THE REGRESS IS DELIBERATELY CUT SHORT: as well as asking what the answer is, one may ask whether the new question IS A WORTHY ONE TO INVESTIGATE - and that second question is investigated WITHOUT FURTHER ADO, THEREBY CUTTING SHORT AN INFINITE REGRESS. The stopping rule is stated rather than hidden — “As well as asking, "What is the answer to this new form of the question," one may ask, "Is this new question a worthy one to investigate?" This latter question we investigate without further ado, thereby cutting short an infinite regress.”
mechanismTHE TEST'S DESIGN IS JUSTIFIED BY WHAT IT EXCLUDES: the new problem is said to draw A FAIRLY SHARP LINE between the physical and intellectual capacities of a man, and the physical separation of the rooms is the operationalisation of that line - so a condition of the apparatus IS the conceptual distinction — “The new problem has the advantage of drawing a fairly sharp line between the physical and the intellectual capacities of a man. No engineer or chemist claims to be able to produce a material which is indistinguishable from the human skin. It is possible that at some time this might be done, but even supposing this invention available we should feel there was little point in trying to make a "thinking machine" more human by dressing it up in such artificial flesh. The form in which we have set the problem reflects this fact in the condition which prevents the interrogator from seeing or touching the other competitors”
mechanismIRRELEVANT CAPACITIES ARE EXCLUDED BY THE FORM OF THE TEST RATHER THAN BY A RULE: the machine is not penalised for failing to shine in beauty competitions nor the man for losing a race against an aeroplane, because THE CONDITIONS OF OUR GAME MAKE THESE DISABILITIES IRRELEVANT - and the witnesses may BRAG as they please, since the interrogator cannot demand practical demonstrations — “We do not wish to penalise the machine for its inability to shine in beauty competitions, nor to penalise a man for losing in a race against an aeroplane. The conditions of our game make these disabilities irrelevant. The "witnesses" can brag, if they consider it advisable, as much as they please about their charms, strength or heroism, but the interrogator cannot demand practical demonstrations.”
mechanismA STRONG OBJECTION IS CONCEDED AS STRONG AND THEN NEUTRALISED BY SUFFICIENCY RATHER THAN ANSWERED: machines might do something that OUGHT TO BE DESCRIBED AS THINKING but is very different from what a man does - and the reply is that IF NEVERTHELESS a machine can play the game satisfactorily, WE NEED NOT BE TROUBLED BY THIS OBJECTION. A sufficient condition is offered where a definition was wanted — “May not machines carry out something which ought to be described as thinking but which is very different from what a man does? This objection is a very strong one, but at least we can say that if, nevertheless, a machine can be constructed to play the imitation game satisfactorily, we need not be troubled by this objection.”
mechanismTHREE REQUIREMENTS FOR THE DEFINITION ARE LISTED AND THE DIFFICULTY OF MEETING THEM IS ADMITTED: every engineering technique permitted; machines whose operation their own constructors cannot satisfactorily describe still allowed; and men born in the usual manner excluded. The author states outright that IT IS DIFFICULT TO FRAME THE DEFINITIONS so as to satisfy all three — “It is natural that we should wish to permit every kind of engineering technique to be used in our machines. We also wish to allow the possibility than an engineer or team of engineers may construct a machine which works, but whose manner of operation cannot be satisfactorily described by its constructors because they have applied a method which is largely experimental. Finally, we wish to exclude from the machines men born in the usual manner. It is difficult to frame the definitions so as to satisfy these three conditions.”
mechanismA CANDIDATE DEFINITIONAL CLAUSE IS TESTED AGAINST A COUNTEREXAMPLE AND DROPPED: requiring the team of engineers to be all of one sex is proposed and then defeated by the possibility of rearing an individual from a single skin cell - a feat DESERVING OF THE VERY HIGHEST PRAISE which we would still not be inclined to regard as CONSTRUCTING A THINKING MACHINE — “One might for instance insist that the team of engineers should be all of one sex, but this would not really be satisfactory, for it is probably possible to rear a complete individual from a single cell of the skin (say) of a man. To do so would be a feat of biological technique deserving of the very highest praise, but we would not be inclined to regard it as a case of "constructing a thinking machine."”
mechanismTHE TERM IS THEN NARROWED BY WHAT THE FIELD CURRENTLY FINDS INTERESTING, NOT BY PRINCIPLE: the requirement that every technique be permitted is abandoned, and the stated reason is that PRESENT INTEREST HAS BEEN AROUSED BY a particular kind of machine - so only digital computers are permitted to play. The scope of the word is set by the salience of a technology — “This prompts us to abandon the requirement that every kind of technique should be permitted. We are the more ready to do so in view of the fact that the present interest in "thinking machines" has been aroused by a particular kind of machine, usually called an "electronic computer" or "digital computer." Following this suggestion we only permit digital computers to take part in our game.”
mechanismTHE DEFINITION IS DECLARED CONTINGENT ON ITS OWN OUTCOME: the identification of machines with digital computers, LIKE OUR CRITERION FOR THINKING, WILL ONLY BE UNSATISFACTORY IF it turns out that digital computers cannot give a good showing in the game. Both the definition and the criterion are staked on the result they were introduced to adjudicate — “It may also be said that this identification of machines with digital computers, like our criterion for "thinking," will only be unsatisfactory if (contrary to my belief), it turns out that digital computers are unable to give a good showing in the game.”
mechanismTHE OBVIOUS EXPERIMENT IS DECLINED BECAUSE THE QUESTION IS ABOUT IMAGINABLE MACHINES: asked why not simply run the game with existing computers and compile statistics, the answer is that we are asking neither about ALL digital computers nor about those PRESENTLY AVAILABLE, but WHETHER THERE ARE IMAGINABLE COMPUTERS WHICH WOULD DO WELL — “The short answer is that we are not asking whether all digital computers would do well in the game nor whether the computers at present available would do well, but whether there are imaginable computers which would do well.”
mechanismCIRCULARITY IS NAMED AND SIDESTEPPED BY DOWNGRADING A DEFINITION TO AN OUTLINE: using the explanation as a definition WOULD PUT US IN DANGER OF CIRCULARITY OF ARGUMENT, so what is given instead is an OUTLINE of the means by which the effect is achieved. The author declines to define and says why — “If we use the above explanation as a definition we shall be in danger of circularity of argument. We avoid this by giving an outline. of the means by which the desired effect is achieved.”
mechanismTHE SUBSTITUTION IS ACKNOWLEDGED AS CONTESTABLE AND THE ORIGINAL IS KEPT ALIVE FOR THAT REASON: the original form of the problem CANNOT ALTOGETHER BE ABANDONED, because OPINIONS WILL DIFFER AS TO THE APPROPRIATENESS OF THE SUBSTITUTION and we must at least listen to what is said on that. The swap is treated as a claim that can be disputed, not as a definition that settles it — “We cannot altogether abandon the original form of the problem, for opinions will differ as to the appropriateness of the substitution and we must at least listen to what has to be said in this connexion.”
mechanismTHE ORIGINAL QUESTION IS DECLARED TOO MEANINGLESS TO DESERVE DISCUSSION - AND THE PREDICTION THAT FOLLOWS IS ABOUT LANGUAGE, NOT ABOUT MACHINES: at the end of the century THE USE OF WORDS AND GENERAL EDUCATED OPINION WILL HAVE ALTERED SO MUCH that one will be able to speak of machines thinking WITHOUT EXPECTING TO BE CONTRADICTED. The question is not answered; it is forecast to dissolve as the usage moves — “The original question, "Can machines think?" I believe to be too meaningless to deserve discussion. Nevertheless I believe that at the end of the century the use of words and general educated opinion will have altered so much that one will be able to speak of machines thinking without expecting to be contradicted.”
mechanismCONJECTURE IS DEFENDED AS A LEGITIMATE PRODUCT PROVIDED IT IS LABELLED AS SUCH: the popular view that scientists proceed inexorably from well-established fact to well-established fact is called QUITE MISTAKEN, and PROVIDED IT IS MADE CLEAR WHICH ARE PROVED FACTS AND WHICH ARE CONJECTURES, NO HARM CAN RESULT - because conjectures suggest useful lines of research — “The popular view that scientists proceed inexorably from well-established fact to well-established fact, never being influenced by any improved conjecture, is quite mistaken. Provided it is made clear which are proved facts and which are conjectures, no harm can result. Conjectures are of great importance since they suggest useful lines of research.”
