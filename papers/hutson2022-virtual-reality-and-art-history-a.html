findingRoughly three quarters of the surveyed students said the VR assignment helped them learn, and only three of the twenty-three disagreed.— “74% of students felt that VR assisted in their learning, while only 3 (12%) of the 23 participants disagreed with its usefulness.”
findingThree quarters of students judged the VR experience more useful for learning the assigned topic than reading about it.— “75% of the participants found that it was more useful to learn about the assigned topic through a VR experience than by reading about it.”
findingEvery faculty respondent judged classroom use of VR to be moderately to extremely useful for their own field.— “The crucial insight this survey provided is that all respondents stated that using VR technology in their classroom would be moderately to extremely useful.”
findingThe bottleneck reported by faculty was content, not hardware: all but one found it neutral to extremely difficult to locate applications relevant to their discipline.— “The difficulty, however, is that aside from one participant, all faculty members either reported that finding relevant applications for their field was neutral, or somewhat or extremely difficult.”
findingSeven of the eight faculty rated the Oculus Quest 2 headset extremely easy to use, indicating a minimal start-up learning curve for instructors.— “Of the 8 faculty respondents, 7 indicated that using the Oculus Quest 2 headset was extremely easy.”
findingThe preliminary survey showed students are aware of VR but inexperienced with it, already own the phones or tablets needed to run it, want it in their education for career reasons, yet will not spend their own money on a headset.— “The results of the initial survey confirm that students: are familiar with the technology, though have not experienced it; have the necessary hardware to adapt to using without significant investment; want to see it in their education and understand the importance for their future professions, but are generally unwilling to invest their own…
findingStudent enthusiasm was bounded: despite reporting learning benefits, only half wanted VR experiences carried over into their other courses.— “At the same time, only half of the polled group stated that they would like to see such experiences integrated into other coursework.”
findingGetting started was not a barrier for students, with 83% rating launching the assigned application neutral or easy.— “83% reported that launching the technology or getting started into the assigned application was either neutral or easy.”
