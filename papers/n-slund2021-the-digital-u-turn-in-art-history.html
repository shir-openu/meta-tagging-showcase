mechanismDAH and mainstream art history rest on incompatible theoretical foundations, and that incompatibility - not merely missing technical skill - is what keeps art historians from taking up computational methods.— “Based on our analysis, we argue that DAH methods have not been embraced by art historians more generally because of fundamental differences in the theoretical underpinnings of DAH versus the broader field of art history.”
mechanismCiting the Renaissance-focused founders of the discipline borrows their legitimacy, which is how a self-declared new field secures a place inside existing disciplinary boundaries.— “One simple explanation for this is that, as the nexus of art historical tradition, the Renaissance and its scholars confer legitimizing power, tying DAH back to some of the earliest roots of the academic discipline of art history and therefore asserting its place within disciplinary boundaries.”
mechanismThe image-analysis tools were built for law enforcement, warfare and science rather than for art history, so their inherited design pushes users toward superficial formal, iconographic and colour-based classification.— “Moreover, these tools favour analyses on a quite superficial level and are seldom adapted for a broader spectrum of art-historical research, since none of them were initially developed for this end, but are rather a spin-off of systems used for law enforcement, warfare and science.”
mechanismBecause art history's primary material is images and artefacts rather than text, computational treatment is harder for it than for text-based disciplines, which partly explains its slower uptake.— “One reason for this may be that the nature of our core material – images and artefacts rather than text – makes the use of computational methods more complex for art history compared to other fields.”
mechanismDigital art history is not resisted because it is new but because the theory it imports is old - formalist and iconographic methods the field spent sixty years moving away from— “Rather, they are closely tied to very particular pre-digital methods and theories of art history – very traditional ones, in fact.”
mechanismCiting early art historians as if they were contemporaries strips the theories of their historical context and hides the ideological commitments built into them— “By referencing art historians like Wölfflin, Warburg and Panofsky and treating them as contemporaries, scholars ignore the ways in which historical methods reflect particular historical contexts.”
mechanismThe available image tools shape the questions asked, because they were built for classification and pattern-matching in other domains entirely— “Moreover, these tools favour analyses on a quite superficial level and are seldom adapted for a broader spectrum of art-historical research, since none of them were initially developed for this end, but are rather a spin-off of systems used for law enforcement, warfare and science.”
mechanismIconographic analysis and nineteenth-century scientific practice share a historical root, so the tools and the revived theory reinforce each other— “Both ideologically and technically, iconographic analysis and its focus on formal details and classification have the same historical roots as nineteenth-century scientific practices.”
