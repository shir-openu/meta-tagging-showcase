phenomenoncross-modal semantic priming— “The present study compared the time courses of the cross-modal semantic priming effects elicited by naturalistic sounds and spoken words on visual picture processing.”
claim-typeexistence— “Naturalistic sounds consistently elicited a cross-modal semantic priming effect on visual sensitivity ( d' ) for pictures (higher d' in the congruent than in the incongruent condition) at the 350-ms rather than at the 1,000-ms stimulus onset asynchrony (SOA).”
method — evidence— “Two within-participants factors, prime type (naturalistic sound or spoken word) and congruency (congruent or incongruent), and one between-participants factor, SOA (350 or 1,000 ms), were manipulated.”
mechanismNaturalistic sounds reach meaning by a direct route while spoken words must go through lexical representations, so the sound-driven cross-modal priming arrives sooner— “Together, the results of the two experiments reported here demonstrate that naturalistic sounds elicit more rapid cross-modal priming than do spoken words, which is likely determined by their speed of semantic access stemming from the different processing routes.”
mechanismMeaning activated by a naturalistic sound decays quickly unless it is actively maintained, which explains why its priming effect vanishes at the longer interval— “Given that naturalistic sounds can access their meaning rapidly (within 350 ms in the current study), the short-lived priming effect suggests that the activated meaning would be forgotten rapidly as well, unless the information can be temporally maintained.”
replicates Chen and Spence (2011)— “We therefore replicated the results at the 350-ms SOA reported in Chen and Spence ( 2011 ).”
builds-on Chen & Spence (2011); Edmiston & Lupyan (2015); Lupyan & Thompson-Schill (2012)— “Given the different SOAs and methods, and given the different mechanisms proposed by previous research (Chen & Spence, 2011 ; Edmiston & Lupyan, 2015 ; Lupyan & Thompson-Schill, 2012 ), we wanted to carefully examine the time courses of cross-modal semantic priming effects elicited by naturalistic sounds and spoken words.”
sensepriming measured as SENSITIVITY (d'), not as speed - and the alternative explicitly refused. The claim is that the auditory cue helps the picture BREAK THROUGH INTO AWARENESS `rather than simply giving rise to some sort of criterion change`: the participant sees more, rather than becoming readier to say yes. A signal-detection design used for exactly what it is for, which is rare in this corpus— “the presentation of either a naturalistic sound or spoken word enhances the sensitivity ( d' ) of visual object detection (Chen & Spence, 2011 ; Lupyan & Ward, 2013 ). Such results suggest that the meaning of the auditory cue facilitates visual processing and boosts the breakthrough of the visual stimulus into awareness…
senseTIME COURSE AS THE INDIVIDUATING PROPERTY - two primes carrying the same meaning, producing the same effect on the same measure, told apart only by WHEN. Naturalistic sounds prime at 350 ms and not at 1,000; spoken words at 1,000 and not at 350. If the effect were named by what it does, these would be one thing; the paper's title insists they are two, and the difference is a latency— “The cross-modal semantic priming effects elicited by the presentation of naturalistic sounds versus spoken words can therefore be dissociated in terms of their differing time courses. Furthermore, naturalistic sounds consistently primed the visual pictures at the short SOA; in contrast, the priming effect elicited by spoken words was…
sensethe word effect is CONDITIONED BY THE ORDER OF TESTING, and the paper reports both halves: meet the 1,000-ms SOA first and priming carries over to 350 ms; meet the 350-ms SOA first - where nothing happens - and the 1,000-ms effect is eliminated too. **A priming effect that depends on what the participant was tested on beforehand** is partly a fact about the paradigm rather than about the mind, and a corpus of priming records should be able to find the papers that said so— “when the 350-ms SOA (where no priming effect was observed) was tested first, the priming effect at the 1,000-ms SOA was eliminated as well. Finally, higher sensitivity in the 350-ms than in the 1,000-ms SOA was observed in both experiments”
sense`via cross-modal associations` and `short lived` - the abstract's own characterisation of the sound effect, and both halves matter. The route is associative rather than lexical, and the duration is explicitly limited, which is what distinguishes this from the long-lasting perceptual priming other records in this corpus describe— “visual picture processing can be rapidly primed by naturalistic sounds via cross-modal associations, and this effect is short lived. In contrast, spoken words prime visual picture processing over a wider range of prime-target intervals, though this effect was conditioned by the prior context”
controlthe order of the naturalistic-sound and spoken-word blocks was counterbalanced across participants — held — rules out that a difference between the two stimulus types is an order or fatigue effect
differs innaturalistic sounds —distinct-from→ spoken words — THE ROUTE TO MEANING, AND THEREFORE THE LATENCY - naturalistic sounds access semantic information directly, spoken words must go via lexical representations first. One hop against two, which is the paper's whole explanation of the dissociated time courses

The paper argues for it rather than assuming it, and refuses the response-criterion reading explicitly; that refusal is the sense row, because the sentence carrying it does not name the effect.

no type for thisspoken words → their meanings — THE MEDIATION RELATION, AND NO TYPE IN THE VOCABULARY CAN EXPRESS IT. The paper's whole explanation of its dissociated time courses is a ROUTE: naturalistic sounds access semantic information DIRECTLY, whereas spoken words have to access their meanings VIA LEXICAL REPRESENTATIONS - one hop against two, which is why the sound primes at 350 ms and the word at 1,000. `causes` asserts production and would lose the intermediate stage entirely; `operationalised-as` says the authors CHOSE a measure, which is not what is claimed; `part-of` says lexical representations are a component of the meaning rather than a stage on the way to it. Splitting it into two `causes` edges (spoken words -> lexical representations, lexical representations -> meaning) would assert two direct productions where the paper asserts one indirect one, and would silently license the inference that the first can occur without the second. **A is reached from B THROUGH C is one of the commonest claims in the psychology half of this corpus and there is nowhere to put it.** Filed as; the same gap is why the Chen2018 record carries five concepts and only three edges.
limitationIn the within-participants design of Experiment 2 the priming effect carried over between sessions, which can mask the effect of SOA.

findingNaturalistic sounds boosted detection sensitivity for semantically congruent pictures at the short (350 ms) prime-target interval, whereas spoken words only did so at the long (1,000 ms) interval, when each participant saw only one interval.
findingThe priming effect of naturalistic sounds is fast but does not persist to the longer interval.
findingIn Experiment 2, testing order changed the spoken-word effect but left the naturalistic-sound effect intact.
findingExperiencing the effective 1,000-ms interval first made the spoken-word priming effect appear at 350 ms too, a carryover not seen in Experiment 1.
findingIndependently of congruency, detection sensitivity itself was higher at the short interval than at the long one in both experiments, which the authors attribute to attentional cuing by a temporally close sound.

Concepts
terms this paper carries, each with the sentence it was cut from — the layer that joins one paper to another. Terms with no quote yet are on CONCEPTS.html.
conceptcross-modal semantic priming — the effect: a heard prime raises visual SENSITIVITY for a semantically congruent picture. Cross-modal because prime and target are in different senses, semantic because congruency of meaning is what does the work
conceptnaturalistic sounds — one of the two prime types, and the fast one - a dog barking reaches meaning in one hop, so it primes early and briefly
conceptspoken words — the other, and the slow one: the same meaning arrives through a lexical representation first, which is the paper's explanation for why the effect appears later and lasts wider
conceptlexical representations — the extra hop that makes words slower than sounds here - the load-bearing term in the paper's whole account of the dissociation
conceptstimulus onset asynchrony — the independent variable that turns two similar effects into a dissociation: 350 ms against 1,000 ms between prime and target. The paper's title calls the result a difference in TIME COURSES, so this is the axis the finding lives on
conceptsensitivity — the dependent measure, and it is d' from signal detection theory rather than a reaction time - which lets the paper claim the prime changed what was SEEN and not merely what was reported
