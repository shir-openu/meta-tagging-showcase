mechanismNREM sleep imposes a graded, cumulative attenuation that builds up along the processing hierarchy rather than a single discrete gate at one anatomical station— “The robust correlation of NREM sleep attenuation with response latency suggests that NREM sleep may exert a gradual attenuation of cortical responses, rather than imposing a discrete “gate” at a specific brain region.”
mechanismBreakdown of cortical effective connectivity in NREM sleep means signals lose more strength at each additional synapse, so responses arriving later are attenuated more— “The fact that late responses exhibit stronger attenuation in NREM sleep is in line with the notion of breakdown of cortical effective connectivity during NREM sleep ( Massimini et al., 2005 ) that may lead to greater attenuation with increasing synaptic connections.”
mechanismthe disconnection is not at the sensory front end: auditory cortex responds nearly as strongly asleep as awake, so whatever gates perception acts further downstream— “In addition, several recent studies in the auditory system showed that responses in the primary and secondary auditory cortex (AC) are comparable across wakefulness and natural sleep”
mechanismperirhinal cortex is the candidate site because it is multimodal, downstream of several primary cortices, and carries intrinsic inhibition controlling flow to the hippocampus— “Moreover, it has been shown to contain intrinsic inhibitory mechanisms that affect information flow to the hippocampus ( de Curtis and Paré, 2004 ; Pelletier et al., 2004 ).”
mechanismthe operative variable is TIME not PLACE: whatever suppresses the response acts on late activity wherever it occurs, not on a particular region— “Beyond the separation according to cortical region, response latency in each neuronal cluster was correlated with the degree of NREM sleep attenuation, such that late (>40 ms) responses in all monitored regions diminished during NREM sleep.”
mechanismthe older explanation - thalamic gating - was already in trouble, because olfactory responses that bypass the thalamus are attenuated too— “However, this view was challenged by sleep attenuation of responses in the olfactory cortex that are not relayed through thalamus ( Murakami et al., 2005 ).”
replicates reports of preserved auditory cortical responses across vigilance states (Issa and Wang, 2008; Nir et al., 2015)— “In line with previous studies ( Issa and Wang, 2008 ; Nir et al., 2015 ), spiking responses in AC neurons exhibited similar profiles across vigilance states, whereas PRC neurons exhibited weaker responses in both NREM and REM sleep ( Fig. 4 ).”
extends Krom et al. (2018), the same early/late distinction observed in humans under light anesthesia— “In addition, a recent study comparing auditory responses at the neuronal level in humans across wakefulness and light anesthesia found a similar distinction between relatively preserved early responses in AC versus robust attenuation of late responses in association cortex ( Krom et al., 2018 ).”
contradicts Pereira et al. (2007), increased hippocampal responses to somatosensory microstimulation during sleep— “This discrepancy with the robust attenuation we find here in rat PRC may reflect differences in sensory modalities, or differences between natural stimuli used here versus ultra-short electrical stimulation.”
contradicts the "thalamic gating" model (Steriade and McCarley, 1990), which posits a discrete gate rather than graded attenuation— “The robust correlation of NREM sleep attenuation with response latency suggests that NREM sleep may exert a gradual attenuation of cortical responses, rather than imposing a discrete “gate” at a specific brain region.”
replicates prior findings that auditory cortex responses are comparable across wakefulness and sleep— “Neuronal responses in AC showed modest (∼10%) differences in response gain across vigilance states, replicating previous studies.”
contradicts the thalamic gating model of sleep-related sensory disconnection— “However, this view was challenged by sleep attenuation of responses in the olfactory cortex that are not relayed through thalamus ( Murakami et al., 2005 ).”
extends human imaging work showing attenuation downstream of sensory cortex during sleep— “However, investigation of sensory responses downstream from sensory cortical regions at the level of neuronal spiking activity is still missing.”
sensesleep - behavioural: the state in which an animal stops responding to what happens around it— “Reduced behavioral responses to external sensory stimuli are an essential definition of sleep ( Carskadon and Dement, 2011 ) and are ubiquitous across the animal kingdom ( Cirelli and Tononi, 2008 ).”
sensesleep - neural: a set of vigilance states, NREM and REM, that attenuate responses by different amounts— “By contrast, PRC neuronal responses were robustly attenuated by 47% and 36% during NREM sleep and REM sleep, respectively.”
sensedisconnection - not an absence of processing: discriminative sensory processing continues while perception does not follow— “During sleep, external stimuli rarely affect perception ( Nir and Tononi, 2010 ), but it is also clear that some discriminative sensory processing persists ( Andrillon et al., 2016 ).”
controlneurons with peak latencies in the 20-40 ms band DROPPED from the latency-based split, keeping only <20 ms and >40 ms — held — rules out ambiguous cases deciding the early/late contrast - a neuron near the boundary can fall either side on noise, and those are exactly the cases a threshold chosen after looking would assign favourably
controlthe first second after each auditory stimulus EXCLUDED before smoothing when detecting OFF periods — held — rules out stimulus-induced firing reductions being counted as spontaneous OFF periods - the sound itself suppresses firing, so an OFF-period detector run over that window would find the stimulus and report sleep
differs inthe "thalamic gating" model (Steriade and McCarley, 1990), which posits a discrete gate rather than graded attenuation — THE AXIS IS RESPONSE LATENCY. 'THE ROBUST CORRELATION OF NREM SLEEP ATTENUATION WITH RESPONSE LATENCY suggests that NREM sleep may exert A GRADUAL ATTENUATION of cortical responses, RATHER THAN IMPOSING A DISCRETE GATE at a specific brain region.' A gate predicts a step at one locus; the graded alternative predicts a monotone relationship with latency - so the two accounts differ on a measurable parameter rather than on a location.
