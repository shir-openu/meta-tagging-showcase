mechanismThe same link-strengthening process that produces priming is the process by which new faces are learned and stored representations of familiar faces are updated by new exemplars— “On this account, then, repetition priming taps the same pro cess that underlies the perceptual learning of faces and is the same mechanism by which new faces are learned and representations of familiar faces are modified through encounters with new exemplars.”
mechanismPriming reflects the ongoing operation of a system that develops and updates the representations used to identify faces, rather than retrieval of the priming episode— “For the moment, however,we maintain our current hypothesis that priming does reflect the operation of a system mediating the development and updating of the representations used to identify faces.”

extends Burton et al.'s (1990) interactive activation account— “However, the interactive activation account, as currently articulated, would not readily accommodate the effects of changes in environ mental context, and, to the extent that priming is, in gen eral, robust from the posters, this supports the model.”
sense**THE PRIME WAS NOT ADMINISTERED - IT WAS A POSTER IN A CORRIDOR.** Faces on subject recruitment advertisements primed laboratory face recognition, `even when the advertisements make no specific mention of experiments involving face recognition` and even when subjects `were not obliged to look at them`. **This is the only record in the corpus where the exposure happened outside the experiment**: dijksterhuis1998 doses by duration, Schmidt2009 by the prime-target gap, bargh1996 by a scrambled-sentence task, and every one of them is delivered by an experimenter to someone who has already consented. Here it happened to people who did not yet know they were in a study— “Repetition priming was found for both poster and lab faces, although the priming effect was smaller from the poster faces”
sense**AND THE DISSOCIATION IS IN THE DERIVATIVE, NOT THE LEVEL, WHICH IS A SHARPER CLAIM:** `there is LITTLE EVIDENCE that repeated or extended exposure to items influences REPETITION PRIMING AT ALL ..., WHEREAS it is clear that increase in exposure duration and/or frequency DOES AFFECT EXPLICIT MEMORY for the items.` **More exposure buys explicit memory and buys priming nothing.** Two systems that differ in whether they respond to MORE, rather than in whether they respond - which cannot be explained by one measure being less sensitive, the objection that sinks a difference in means— “there is little evidence that repeated or extended exposure to items influences repetition priming at all (see Roediger & McDermott's 1993 review), whereas it is clear that increase in exposure duration and/or frequency does affect explicit memory for the items”
sense**AND THE THEORETICAL GAP IS NAMED AND LEFT OPEN:** `As currently formulated, however, there is NO OBVIOUS WAY for structural accounts to explain contextual influences on repetition priming.` They say what their own result does to the account they favour, and stop. **Second record running to decline to close its own gap** - hesselmann2016 (Q-818) reports its surviving effect as `difficult to reconcile` with the obvious model. A corpus that only held resolved claims would hold neither, and the unresolved version is what a later paper actually picks up— “As currently formulated, however, there is no obvious way for structural accounts to explain contextual influences on repetition priming”
sense**AND THE CAREFUL CONCLUSION IS CAREFUL IN A SPECIFIC DIRECTION:** `What we can say, however, is that repetition priming of familiar faces is NOT TRIVIALLY BOUND to the superficial aspects of context that have been found to influence memory for pictures of faces.` **A negative claim about what the effect is NOT bound to**, rather than a positive claim about what carries it. `What we can say, however` is the authors marking the edge of their evidence inside the sentence that states it— “What we can say, however, is that repetition priming of familiar faces is not trivially bound to the superficial aspects of context that have been found to influence memory for pictures of faces”
controlthe test list mixed primed faces with unprimed famous faces, unfamiliar faces and six additional familiar filler items — held — rules out that the priming effect is recognition of fame or familiarity rather than the priming exposure— “The test list comprised a random sequence of the poster faces, the lab faces, and the unprimed faces, plus six additional familiar filler items.”

findingFaces first encountered on subject-recruitment posters outside the laboratory produced measurable repetition priming of laboratory familiarity decisions, even when the poster never mentioned face recognition.— “Here we show that gains in face recognition in the laboratory are found from faces initially seen in a rather different context on subject recruitment posters, even when the advertisements make no specific mention of experi ments involving face recognition.”
findingPoster priming was reliable across all four experiments but smaller in magnitude than priming from faces seen in the laboratory.— “1. Repetition priming can be demonstrated for faces shown on advertising posters (Experiments 1-4), although it was reduced in magnitude, as compared with that found for faces encountered in the lab (Experiments 2-4).”
findingPriming survived fully incidental exposure, where participants were never required to look at or name the poster faces.— “2. Repetition priming was found even when the faces were presented quite incidentally and subjects were not obliged to look at them (Experiments 2 and 3).”
findingChanging the pictured exemplar between prime and test reduced priming by about the same amount in the poster and laboratory conditions, so exemplar change and context change acted additively rather than interactively.— “3. Changing the pictorial exemplar between the prime and test phases affected priming similarly in lab and on the poster (Experiments 3 and 4). Thus, the effect ofreduced priming after a change ofview is additive with reductions observed after a change in the environmental context within which the faces were primed.”
findingChanging the background colour behind the face between prime and test had no detectable effect on the size of the priming effect.— “5. A change in the background color on which the faces were presented had no measurable effects on the amount of priming (Experiment 4).”
findingExplicit episodic memory for the poster faces was very poor: they neither interfered as distractors nor were recognised at anything like the rate of laboratory faces.— “6.
