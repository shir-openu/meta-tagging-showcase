mechanismIncidental extraction of the pattern generates an anticipation of the next disk's location, which speeds responses when the anticipation is correct and slows them when it is violated — “Because of the hypothesized pattern-driven anticipations of the disks’ locations, RTs to last disks in Pattern sets (where pattern-based anticipations are veridical) should be faster than RTs to the last disks in Broken Pattern sets (where pattern-based anticipations are systematically misleading), with Control sets falling somewhere in between.”
mechanismConscious, intentional search for patterns produces frequent failures on the pattern-less majority of sets, and those failures interfere with the implicit extraction process — “Given the literature succinctly described above, the conscious search and its prominent failures are likely to interfere with the relevant implicit processes, and to diminish successful pattern extraction.”
mechanismDiscovering a pattern itself imposes a brief processing cost - an implicit 'aha' - that partly cancels the speedup it produces — “One possibility that comes to mind is that maybe there is an inherent slowdown upon the discovery of a pattern, as if the system takes a few milliseconds to develop an implicit “aha” experience.”

limitationWhether pattern naming, anticipation and control are done by working memory alone or by working memory interacting with long-term memory is left unresolved. — “Whether the processes of “naming” the patterns, creating anticipations for future locations, and controlling cognition, are done solely by WM or, like many other WM paradigms ( Baddeley, 2003 ; Gathercole, Pickering, Hall, & Peaker, 2001 ), involve the interaction of WM and long term memory, is an empirical question that we sadly leave for future research.”
limitationThe comparison between excluded (aware) and included participants is heuristic rather than a formal test. — “We acknowledge that these analyses are heuristical in nature.”
limitationThe unequal group sizes prevented a direct statistical comparison of aware and unaware participants. — “Given these huge differences in numbers we cannot directly compare the effects using an ANOVA.”
limitationThe proposed implicit "aha" account of the asymmetry between speedup and slowdown is speculative. — “Admittedly, this idea is speculative.”
limitationthe subject pool was contaminated by the laboratory's own research programme — participants arrived suspecting a hidden manipulation — “Thus, Hassin’s lab (where this study was conducted) runs many non-conscious goal priming studies. Towards the end of the academic year, many participants come to the lab suspecting that there is more to our experiments than meets the eye.”
limitationthe excluded-participant analysis is conceded to be informal, compared by effect size because the group sizes forbid a test — “Given these huge differences in numbers we cannot directly compare the effects using an ANOVA. Hence, we use as heuristics the actual size of the effect in milliseconds and effect sizes.”
limitationthe predicted pattern is asymmetric in a way the hypothesis did not anticipate: the slowdown is reliable and the speedup mostly is not — “The differences between Broken Pattern and Control sets were significant in three studies (1, 2, & 3), and marginally significant in one (4). The difference between Pattern and control sets, however, was significant in one study (4) and insignificant in the rest.”
limitationthe explanation offered for that asymmetry is labelled speculation by the authors — “Admittedly, this idea is speculative. Regrettably, we leave the resolution of this issue to future research.”
limitationestablishing that anything is non-conscious is acknowledged as never fully achievable — “Concluding that a process or a phenomenon is non-conscious is always a very difficult challenge.”
limitationwhether long-term memory also contributes is left open, defended by parsimony rather than by data — “Whether the processes of “naming” the patterns, creating anticipations for future locations, and controlling cognition, are done solely by WM or, like many other WM paradigms ( Baddeley, 2003 ; Gathercole, Pickering, Hall, & Peaker, 2001 ), involve the interaction of WM and long term memory, is an empirical question that we sadly leave for future research.”
limitationblock-level data exist for only two of the five studies because the analysis was thought of late — “The idea to examine the effects of Patterns in the first block came pretty late in the project. Hence, we have information about blocks only in Experiments 1 and 4.”
limitationwhether implicit and explicit WM are one system in two modes or two systems is unresolved — “The putative differences between explicit and implicit WM, as well as other intriguing questions such as whether they belong to the same system or to two separate systems (cf. Keren & Schul, in press ), and what are their respective roles in higher order cognitive processes, are topics for future research.”

findingParticipants responded faster to the final disk of pattern-following sets than to pattern-breaking sets, with control sets in between, even though the patterns were irrelevant to their explicit task. — “A 3 (Pattern: Pattern, Broken Pattern, Control) × 2 (Block) within subjects’ ANOVA revealed that, as hypothesized, RTs in the Pattern condition ( M = 528 ms, SD = 48) were considerably shorter than those for the Broken pattern condition ( M = 565 ms, SD = 62) with RTs for control sets falling in between ( M = 532 ms, SD = 48), F (2, 30) = 5.55, p < .01, MSE = 2.41, partial η 2 = .27.”
findingThe pattern effect was already present in the very first block, which rules out gradual implicit learning as its source. — “Importantly, pattern extraction occurred on the first block, thus ruling out implicit learning as an alternative explanation of the results.”
findingJudges could detect no awareness of the patterns in participants' immediate reconstructions of the last set they had seen. — “Both judges concluded that none of the participants had been aware of the patterns.”
findingRecognition of the last pattern set was exactly at chance, corroborating that participants were unaware of the patterns. — “Out of the remaining 30 participants only 5, or 20%, correctly recognized the set.”
findingThe pattern effect appeared only when instructions kept the task incidental; telling participants to search for patterns abolished it. — “As hypothesized, the interaction consisted of a significant effect of Pattern in the Implicit condition, F (2, 32) = 10.75, p < .001, MSE = 1.12, partial η 2 = .40, and no effect of Pattern in the Explicit condition F = 1.1 (for means see Table 2 ).”
findingThe effect replicated conceptually with numeric (algebraic) rather than visuospatial patterns. — “As hypothesized, RTs for the fourth terms in the Pattern condition were significantly shorter than those in the Broken Pattern condition ( M = 547 ms, SD = 84 vs. M = 561 ms, SD = 82) t (25) = 2.35, p < .05.”
findingThe effect is asymmetric: the slowdown on broken-pattern sets is more reliable than the speedup on pattern sets. — “Coupled with the analyses described above this suggests to us that there are both speedup and slowdown, but the latter is stronger than the former.”
findingParticipants excluded for reporting awareness showed a smaller, not larger, pattern effect than the unaware participants. — “First, whereas the difference between broken pattern and pattern RTs was 31 ms in the Included condition, it was only 17 ms in the Excluded”

findingthe effect was already present in the first block, where each pattern had appeared only once, ruling out statistical learning — “And indeed, a repeated measures ANOVA on this block revealed a highly significant effect of Pattern, F (2, 30) = 7.17, p < .01, MSE = 1.55, partial η 2 = .32.”

findingrecognition of the last set was exactly at chance — 5 of 30, or 20%, in a five-alternative test — “Out of the remaining 30 participants only 5, or 20%, correctly recognized the set. This proportion is exactly that expected by chance.”
findingthe effect held even when only participants who failed the recognition test were analysed — “Although recognition rate is at chance level, we also conducted similar analyses only for those participants who could not recognize (or correctly guess) the last set that they have seen. These analyses yielded a similar pattern of results.”
findingtelling participants the patterns exist abolished the effect — a significant Instructions by Pattern interaction, with the effect present implicitly and absent explicitly — “As hypothesized, the interaction consisted of a significant effect of Pattern in the Implicit condition, F (2, 32) = 10.75, p < .001, MSE = 1.12, partial η 2 = .40, and no effect of Pattern in the Explicit condition F = 1.1 (for means see Table 2 ).”
findingthe effect replicated with algebraic rather than spatial patterns, in sets of one-digit numbers — “As hypothesized, RTs for the fourth terms in the Pattern condition were significantly shorter than those in the Broken Pattern condition ( M = 547 ms, SD = 84 vs. M = 561 ms, SD = 82) t (25) = 2.35, p < .05.”
