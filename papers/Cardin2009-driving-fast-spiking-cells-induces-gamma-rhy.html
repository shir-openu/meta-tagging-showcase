claim-typemechanism
transferfrom microbial/algal photobiology (a light-gated ion channel from the green alga Chlamydomonas reinhardtii): a microbial light-sensitive cation channel is imported as the tool for driving a specific mammalian cortical cell type in vivo — “we used the light-sensitive bacteriorhodopsin Chlamydomonas reinhardtii channelrhodopsin-2 (ChR2), a cation channel activated by ~470 nm blue light”
mechanismRhythmic FS-generated IPSPs gate sensory transmission: inhibition blocks input at its peak and releases it during decay, temporally sharpening cortical responses — “Our results indicate that the rhythmic, FS-induced IPSP restricts sensory transmission during its peak, and permits transmission after its decay, leading to a temporal sharpening of cortical sensory responses”
mechanismIncreased FS firing recruits a resonance in the surrounding cortical network whose preferred band is set by the circuit's synaptic and biophysical properties — “One possible explanation for these results is that increased FS firing recruits resonant gamma-range activity in the surrounding local network as a function of the synaptic and biophysical properties of the cortical circuit.”
mechanism — “Current theory predicts that gamma oscillations are generated by synchronous activity of fast-spiking inhibitory interneurons, with the resulting rhythmic inhibition producing neural ensemble synchrony by generating a narrow window for effective excitation.”
mechanism — “Whereas drive affected the amplitude of enhancement, LFP power was selectively amplified within the gamma range regardless of light intensity or spike probability ( Fig. 3h ), indicating that the gamma oscillations evoked by FS activity are a resonant circuit property.”
mechanism — “In addition, randomly patterned light stimulation of FS cells with frequencies evenly distributed across a broad range evoked a significant increase in LFP power specific to the gamma range ( n =7 sites in 4 animals; P <0.05; Supplementary Fig. 7 ), further indicating that FS-evoked gamma oscillations are an emergent property of the circuit and do not require exclusive drive in the gamma range.”
mechanism — “Furthermore, light-induced gamma oscillations were largely eliminated by blocking AMPA (α-amino-3-hydroxy-5-methyl-4-isoxazole propionic acid) and NMDA ( N -methyl- d -aspartate) receptors, despite high levels of evoked FS firing ( n =4 sites in 4 animals; P <0.01; Supplementary Fig. 9 ).”
mechanism — “Our results indicate that the rhythmic, FS-induced IPSP restricts sensory transmission during its peak, and permits transmission after its decay, leading to a temporal sharpening of cortical sensory responses ( Fig. 4g ).”
mechanism — “We targeted expression of ChR2 specifically to parvalbumin-positive fast-spiking (FS-PV + ) interneurons by injecting the adeno-associated viral vector double-floxed inverted open reading frame ChR2–mCherry (AAV DIO ChR2–mCherry ), with Cre-dependent expression of ChR2, into PV–Cre knock-in mice ( Fig. 1a , Supplementary Figs 1 and 2 and Methods ) 18 , 19 .”
relation — “Our data directly support the fast-spiking-gamma hypothesis and provide the first causal evidence that distinct network activity states can be induced in vivo by cell-type-specific activation.”

limitationThe effect was not obtained in layer 5, so the result is confined to the layers where activation was observed — “We did not observe light activation of layer 5 FS cells ( n =12 sites in 7 animals).”
limitationLabelling efficiency falls off with distance from the injection, so the manipulated population is not uniform across the field — “The labelling efficiency of AAV DIO ChR2–mCherry varied over distance from the injection site; close to the centre of the injection, >97% of the PV + interneurons expressed ChR2–mCherry.”
limitationThe excitatory comparison covers at most half the target population, against near-complete coverage on the inhibitory side — “At least 50% of the αCamKII + neurons in layer 2/3 expressed ChR2–mCherry (913 of 1,638 cells in a total area of 8.4×10 6 μm 3 ) close to the injection site, covering an anterioposterior distance of 1,560±154.9 μm (mean±s.d., n =3).”
limitationThe pyramidal-drive comparison at 40 Hz produced a small increase that did not reach significance, so the dissociation is asymmetric in strength — “In contrast, stimulation of RS cells at 8 Hz in the αCamKII–Cre mice caused a significant increase of LFP power at 8 Hz ( P <0.001), whereas RS stimulation at 40 Hz caused only a small, nonsignificant increase in 40-Hz LFP power ( Fig. 3f ).”
limitationThe phase-gating experiment rests on twenty cells in three animals — “To test this hypothesis directly, we stimulated FS cells at 40 Hz with light pulses to establish gamma oscillations, and recorded the responses of RS cells to a single vibrissa deflection presented at one of five phases relative to a single gamma cycle ( n =20 cells in 3 animals; Fig. 4a ).”

findingOptogenetically driving fast-spiking interneurons across a wide frequency range amplified oscillations selectively in the gamma band. — “Here we show that light-driven activation of fast-spiking interneurons at varied frequencies (8–200 Hz) selectively amplifies gamma oscillations.”
findingDriving FS cells within 20–80 Hz significantly increased local field potential power at those frequencies, whereas lower-frequency drive did not. — “We found that activation of FS cells in the 20–80 Hz range resulted in significant amplification of LFP power at those frequencies”
findingGamma-band amplification persisted regardless of stimulation intensity or evoked spike probability, indicating gamma is a resonant property of the local circuit rather than a copy of the drive. — “Whereas drive affected the amplitude of enhancement, LFP power was selectively amplified within the gamma range regardless of light intensity or spike probability”
findingA single 1-ms light pulse to FS cells produced large fast IPSPs in nearby regular-spiking neurons, confirming direct monosynaptic inhibition. — “We found that a 1-ms light pulse was sufficient to evoke large, fast IPSPs, confirming direct synaptic inhibition of RS cells by light-activated FS cells”
findingActivating FS cells suppressed whisker-evoked responses in neighbouring excitatory neurons. — “Consistent with IPSP induction, activation of FS cells blocked vibrissa-evoked responses in neighbouring RS cells”
findingThe phase of the gamma cycle at which a whisker deflection arrived determined the amplitude, latency and temporal precision of the resulting cortical response. — “The timing of vibrissa-induced RS action potentials relative to light-evoked inhibition and the gamma cycle had a significant impact on the amplitude, timing and precision of the sensory-evoked responses of RS cells”
findingLight-induced gamma depended on fast excitatory synaptic transmission, since blocking AMPA and NMDA receptors abolished it even while FS cells still fired strongly. — “Furthermore, light-induced gamma oscillations were largely eliminated by blocking AMPA (α-amino-3-hydroxy-5-methyl-4-isoxazole propionic acid) and NMDA ( N -methyl- d -aspartate) receptors, despite high levels of evoked FS firing”
findingA brief FS activation reset the phase of spontaneously occurring gamma oscillations, linking naturally occurring gamma to FS activity. — “We found that brief FS activation shifted the phase of both spontaneously occurring gamma oscillations ( n =26 trials, 4 animals; Kruskal–Wallis test with Dunn's post-test; P <0.01; Fig. 3i )”
findingdriving fast-spiking interneurons amplified the local field potential selectively in the 20-80 Hz band — “We found that activation of FS cells in the 20–80 Hz range resulted in significant amplification of LFP power at those frequencies ( n =14 sites in 6 animals; Fig. 3d ).”
findingdriving excitatory neurons amplified only the lower frequencies, giving a double dissociation by cell type — “In contrast, 8–24 Hz light activation of RS cells in αCamKII–Cre mice induced increased LFP power at these frequencies, but RS activation at higher frequencies did not affect LFP power ( n =13 sites in 5 mice; Fig. 3d and Supplementary Fig. 5 ).”
findingthe amplified band did not move with light intensity or spike probability, so it is set by the circuit — “Whereas drive affected the amplitude of enhancement, LFP power was selectively amplified within the gamma range regardless of light intensity or spike probability ( Fig. 3h ), indicating that the gamma oscillations evoked by FS activity are a resonant circuit property.”
findingsingle pulses shifted the phase of naturally occurring gamma, linking the induced rhythm to the spontaneous one — “We found that brief FS activation shifted the phase of both spontaneously occurring gamma oscillations ( n =26 trials, 4 animals; Kruskal–Wallis test with Dunn's post-test; P <0.01; Fig. 3i ) and those evoked by midbrain reticular formation stimulation ( n =18 trials, 2 animals; P <0.05; Supplementary Fig. 8 ).”
findingthe position of a whisker deflection within the gamma cycle changed the amplitude, latency and precision of the response — “The timing of vibrissa-induced RS action potentials relative to light-evoked inhibition and the gamma cycle had a significant impact on the amplitude, timing and precision of the sensory-evoked responses of RS cells ( Fig. 4b, c ).”
findingactivating the interneurons blocked whisker-evoked responses in neighbouring excitatory cells — “Consistent with IPSP induction, activation of FS cells blocked vibrissa-evoked responses in neighbouring RS cells ( Fig. 2d, e ; n =6 sites in 5 PV–Cre mice).”
