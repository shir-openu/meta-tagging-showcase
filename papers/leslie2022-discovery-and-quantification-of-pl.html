mechanismTHE INFERENCE THE NUMBER LICENSES IS A RATE COMPARISON, NOT A QUANTITY: finding particles in blood `indicates that ... THE RATE OF ELIMINATION via e.g. the biliary tract, kidney or transfer to and deposition in organs IS SLOWER THAN THE RATE OF ABSORPTION into the blood`. A standing concentration is evidence about two rates and about neither one alone— “This indicates that at least some of the plastic particles humans come in contact with can be bioavailable and that the rate of elimination via e.g. the biliary tract, kidney or transfer to and deposition in organs is slower than the rate of absorption into the blood.”
mechanismand the paper is careful about what the blood compartment is: it `REMAINS TO BE DETERMINED whether plastic particles are present in THE PLASMA or are CARRIED BY SPECIFIC CELL TYPES`. `In blood` is a location, and the mechanism of carriage is not in evidence— “It remains to be determined whether plastic particles are present in the plasma or are carried by specific cell types”
mechanismblood is the transport route: it irrigates the organs, so a particle that enters it can reach tissues, and its fate is renal or biliary elimination or deposition in liver, spleen or other organs— “Blood as a compartment makes up 6–7% of body weight in humans.”
mechanismcapillary diameter sets a physical ceiling on what can circulate, and anything at that size would itself affect microvascular flow— “Considering capillaries are typically only 5–8 µm in diameter, this forms a limit to particle sizes that can be expected in circulation in these microvessels, and any particles present would likely have an impact on microvascular fluid dynamics.”
mechanismparticles may be localised in immune cells, stuck to proteins, lipids, each other or the vessel wall - so a mass concentration can be real while duplicates disagree— “Some are likely to be localized in immune cells, while others may be adhered to proteins, lipid particles, other plastic particles or the vascular endothelium.”
extends Leslie and Depledge's and Vethaak and Legler's position that a human health risk assessment for plastic particles is not currently possible for want of exposure data— “A human health risk assessment (HRA) for plastic particle pollution is currently not possible due to lack of data on both toxicological hazard and human exposure”
