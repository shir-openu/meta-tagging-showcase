mechanismA TERM IS BORROWED PARTLY, FOR A PROCEDURE THE SOURCE USED DIFFERENTLY, AND THE INEXACT FIT IS STATED IN THE SAME SENTENCE: we use the term **CROSS-FITTING** to characterize our recommended procedure, **PARTLY BORROWING THE JARGON FROM** an earlier paper **WHICH EMPLOYED A SLIGHTLY DIFFERENT FORM** of sample-splitting. The word's provenance and its imprecision are both on the page — “We use the term “cross-fitting” to characterize our recommended procedure, partly borrowing the jargon from Fan et al. (2012) which employed a slightly different form of sample-splitting”
mechanismA TRADITIONAL ASSUMPTION IS SAID TO BREAK DOWN OUTSIDE ITS REGIME, AND THE REGIME IS NAMED: the paper departs from the classical setting BY ALLOWING the nuisance parameter to be SO HIGH-DIMENSIONAL that the traditional assumptions - **SUCH AS DONSKER PROPERTIES, THAT LIMIT COMPLEXITY OF THE PARAMETER SPACE** - **BREAK DOWN**. What fails is not a result but a condition, and the condition is named — “We depart from the classical setting by allowing for η0 to be so high-dimensional that the traditional assumptions, such as Donsker properties, that limit complexity of the parameter space for this object break down.”
mechanismTWO GOOD METHODS COMPOSED NAIVELY ARE SHOWN TO FAIL, AND THE WORD `NAIVELY` IS THE PAPER'S: both regularization bias and overfitting in estimating the nuisance parameter cause **A HEAVY BIAS** in estimators of the target that are obtained **BY NAIVELY PLUGGING** ML estimators into the estimating equations. Each piece is sound; the composition is not, and the paper names the fault in the composing rather than in either part — “However, both regularization bias and overfitting in estimating η0 cause a heavy bias in estimators of θ0 that are obtained by naively plugging ML estimators of η0 into estimating equations for θ0.”
mechanismTHE REMEDY IS PRESENTED AS TWO INGREDIENTS AND THE RESULT IS GIVEN TWO NAMES AT ONCE: the impact can be removed using **TWO SIMPLE, YET CRITICAL, INGREDIENTS** - orthogonal moments with REDUCED SENSITIVITY to nuisance parameters, and cross-fitting - and **WE CALL THE RESULTING SET OF METHODS DOUBLE OR DEBIASED ML**. One method, two adjectives, offered as alternatives rather than ranked — “can be removed by using two simple, yet critical, ingredients: (1) using Neyman-orthogonal moments/scores that have reduced sensitivity with respect to nuisance parameters to estimate θ0, and (2) making use of cross-fitting which provides an efficient form of data-splitting. We call the resulting set of methods double or debiased ML (DML)”
mechanismA PROPERTY IS NAMED AFTER THE PERSON WHO INTRODUCED IT, AND THE ATTRIBUTION IS GIVEN AS THE REASON FOR THE NAME: we refer to property (1.8) as **NEYMAN ORTHOGONALITY** and to the score as the Neyman orthogonal score function **DUE TO THE FUNDAMENTAL CONTRIBUTIONS** in Neyman (1959) and Neyman (1979), **WHERE THIS NOTION WAS INTRODUCED**. The name records a debt, not a definition - and the property it names is a numbered equation, so the term is anchored to a formula rather than a gloss — “We refer to property (1.8) as “Neyman orthogonality” and to ψ as the Neyman orthogonal score function due to the fundamental contributions in Neyman (1959) and Neyman (1979), where this notion was introduced.”
mechanismTHE FORMAL CONDITION IS GIVEN AN INTUITIVE GLOSS THAT STATES WHAT IT BUYS: **INTUITIVELY**, the orthogonality condition means the moment conditions used to identify the parameter are **LOCALLY INSENSITIVE** to the value of the nuisance parameter, **WHICH ALLOWS ONE TO PLUG IN NOISY ESTIMATES of these parameters WITHOUT STRONGLY VIOLATING the moment condition**. The gloss is not a restatement - it is the licence the condition grants — “Intuitively, the Neyman orthogonality condition means that the moment conditions used to identify θ0 are locally insensitive to the value of the nuisance parameter which allows one to plug-in noisy estimates of these parameters without strongly violating the moment condition.”
