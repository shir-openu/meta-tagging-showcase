senseA DOSE-RESPONSE ON PRIME DURATION, MEASURED ON BEHAVIOUR: `the MAGNITUDE of the behavioral effects simply MIRRORED THE MAGNITUDE OF THE PERCEPTUAL INPUT. In Experiments 2 and 3, participants who were primed for 9 MIN showed stronger behavioral effects than participants who were primed for 2 MIN.` **Nine minutes against two, read off general-knowledge performance.** srull-wyer1979 doses by FREQUENCY of activation and measures a judgement; this doses by DURATION and measures what someone does - and the two papers are 19 years and one dependent-variable class apart— “we demonstrated that the magnitude of the behavioral effects simply mirrored the magnitude of the perceptual input. In Experiments 2 and 3, participants who were primed for 9 min showed stronger behavioral effects than participants who were primed for 2 min. In other words, longer priming led to greater behavioral changes”
senseAND THE INFERENCE DRAWN FROM THE DOSE IS THE STEP WORTH RECORDING SEPARATELY: `These results underscore the fact that the process under consideration can be characterized as rather PASSIVE.` **Dose-dependence is taken as evidence of passivity** - if the effect size simply tracks the amount of input, then no decision, strategy or correction is intervening to modulate it. That is a real argument and not an obvious one, and it is a different kind of claim from the measurement it rests on: the dose-response was measured, the passivity is inferred from its linearity— “These results underscore the fact that the process under consideration can be characterized as rather passive (cf. Bargh, Chen, & Burrows, 1996). Also, these results parallel findings from experiments in which the relation between perception and judgment is investigated”
senseA CONCEPTUAL REPLICATION WITH EVERYTHING CHANGED AT ONCE: `the findings of Bargh, Chen, and Burrows (1996) were replicated using a DIFFERENT PRIMING PROCEDURE, DIFFERENT STEREOTYPES, AND A DIFFERENT DEPENDENT MEASURE.` **Three things varied simultaneously.** That buys generality and spends diagnosticity - a FAILURE would not have said which of the three was responsible, and a success cannot say which mattered. In a corpus that holds several papers on when priming effects replicate, the shape of a replication is worth recording as plainly as its outcome— “the findings of Bargh, Chen, and Burrows (1996) were replicated using a different priming procedure, different stereotypes, and a different dependent measure”
senseAND THE PAPER PLACES ITSELF IN THE ACCESSIBILITY LINEAGE EXPLICITLY: `these results parallel findings from experiments in which the relation between perception and JUDGMENT is investigated (e.g., Higgins et al., 1985; Srull & Wyer, 1979, 1980).` **Both of those are in this corpus** - higgins1977 and srull-wyer1979, tagged earlier tonight. The claimed step is from judgement to BEHAVIOUR while keeping the same mechanism, so the 1977-79 accessibility work is not merely cited but load-bearing: if accessibility does not drive judgement, this paper has no base to extend from— “these results parallel findings from experiments in which the relation between perception and judgment is investigated (e.g., Higgins et al., 1985; Srull & Wyer, 1979, 1980)”
