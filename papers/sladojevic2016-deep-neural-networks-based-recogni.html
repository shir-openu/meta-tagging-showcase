method — evidence— “All the images collected for the dataset were downloaded from the Internet, searched by disease and plant name on various sources in different languages, such as Latin, English, German, Serbian, and Hungarian.”
object of study — evidence— “The developed model is able to recognize 13 different types of plant diseases out of healthy leaves, with the ability to distinguish plant leaves from their surroundings.”
research question — evidence— “This paper is concerned with a new approach to the development of plant disease recognition model, based on leaf image classification, by the use of deep convolutional networks.”
contribution — evidence— “New plant disease image database was created, containing more than 3,000 original images taken from the available Internet sources and extended to more than 30,000 using appropriate transformations.”
mechanismAUGMENTATION SUBSTITUTES FOR DATA: more transformed copies increase the chance the network learns the distinguishing features, and the distortion itself is what reduces overfitting - so the transforms are doing two jobs, enlarging the set and regularising it— “The main purpose of applying augmentation is to increase the dataset and introduce slight distortion to the images which helps in reducing overfitting during the training stage.”
mechanismTHE TRANSFORMS WERE CHOSEN TO MATCH THE DEPLOYMENT: affine, perspective and rotation, because a leaf photographed in a natural environment varies in visual perspective. The augmentation encodes an assumption about how the model will be used, not a generic regulariser— “The process of augmentation was chosen to fit the needs; the leaves in a natural environment could vary in visual perspective.”
mechanismA BACKGROUND CLASS TEACHES THE NETWORK WHAT IS NOT A LEAF, taken from the Stanford background dataset - which is why the model can separate leaves from their surroundings at all, and why its best class score (98.21%) is background— “An extra class in the dataset with background images was beneficial to get more accurate classification. Thus, deep neural network could be trained to differentiate the leaves from the surrounding.”
mechanismAUGMENTATION MATTERED MORE THAN FINE-TUNING: fine-tuning moved overall accuracy from 95.8% to 96.3% and is called insignificant by the authors, while augmentation is credited with the result— “Fine-tuning has not shown significant changes in the overall accuracy, but augmentation process had greater influence to achieve respectable results.”
distinguishes-from Leafsnap, the existing commercial leaf-image system, which identifies tree SPECIES rather than diseases and was therefore not used as a comparison— “Presently, there is a commercial solution, Leafsnap \[57\], which uses visual recognition in order to identify tree species from their leaves’ images but as the network presented in this paper is classifying the plant diseases instead of types of plant, Leafsnap was not used for comparison of the achieved results.”
extends earlier non-deep-learning methods of detecting disease from leaf images, against which the authors say their results are better— “As the presented method has not been exploited, as far as we know, in the field of plant disease recognition, there was no comparison with related results, using the exact technique.”
sensethe 30,880 images - NOT 30,880 PLANTS. Just over 3,000 ORIGINAL images were collected and extended to more than 30,000 by affine, perspective and rotation transforms. Every accuracy figure in the paper rests on a dataset that is roughly 90% transformed copies, and the paper says so plainly— “New plant disease image database was created, containing more than 3,000 original images taken from the available Internet sources and extended to more than 30,000 using appropriate transformations.”
sensevalidation here is 2,589 ORIGINAL images against training on the full 30,880 - so the held-out set is untransformed while training is mostly augmented. THE PAPER DOES NOT STATE WHETHER AUGMENTED COPIES OF THE VALIDATION ORIGINALS WERE EXCLUDED FROM TRAINING, which is the question a reader of the 96.3% would want answered and it is an ABSENCE, not a stated flaw— “Different tests were performed with 2589 original images, when trained with 30880 images from database.”
sense`13 different types of plant diseases` - BOUNDED TO WHAT IS VISUALLY DIAGNOSABLE FROM A LEAF. The scope is set by what a photograph of a leaf can show, not by what matters agronomically, and the authors state that bound— “The developed model was able to detect leaf presence and distinguish between healthy leaves and 13 different diseases, which can be visually diagnosed.”
senseduplicate removal - BY METADATA ONLY: name, size and date, by script, then human assessment. Two copies of one photograph saved under different names and sizes would survive the automated pass, and the paper's guard against that is the expert review rather than image comparison— “In this stage, all duplicated images taken from different sources were removed by developed python script applying the comparing procedure. The script removed the duplicates by comparing the images’ metadata: name, size, and the date.”
controlduplicates were removed by script on image metadata (name, size, date) and the remaining images were then assessed by human experts over several iterations — partial— “After the automated removal, images were assessed by human experts in much iteration.” — rules out the same photograph appearing in both training and validation under different filenames - though only partly, since the automated pass compares file metadata rather than image content and the real guard is the expert review— “In this stage, all duplicated images taken from different sources were removed by developed python script applying the comparing procedure. The script removed the duplicates by comparing the images’ metadata: name, size, and the date.”
control10-fold cross validation, repeated after every thousand training iterations rather than once at the end — held— “For the accuracy test, 10-fold cross validation technique was used to evaluate a predictive model. The cross validation procedure was repeated after every thousand training iteration.” — rules out a single fortunate train/test split as the explanation of the accuracy - 10-fold cross validation, repeated every thousand iterations rather than once at the end— “For the accuracy test, 10-fold cross validation technique was used to evaluate a predictive model. The cross validation procedure was repeated after every thousand training iteration.”
controla background class from the Stanford background dataset was added so the network must learn what is not a leaf — held— “High accuracy of model’s prediction of background images allows good separation of plants leaves and the surroundings.” — rules out the network scoring well by keying on background rather than on the leaf - a background class forces it to learn what is not a leaf, and the 98.21% on that class is what makes the leaf/surroundings separation checkable
controlTHE NO-AUGMENTATION BASELINE WAS NOT RUN. The authors state that results from training on original images only `will not be explored`, so the contribution they attribute to augmentation has no control condition — absent-declared-unreported — rules out the augmentation itself - training on the original images alone is the comparison that would say how much the augmentation contributed, and the paper states it `will not be explored`
