transferfrom social / cognitive psychology (priming): using a subtle money-priming manipulation (a psychology method) to study its effects on economic and social behavior— “Reminders of money, relative to nonmoney reminders, led to reduced requests for help and reduced helpfulness toward others”
mechanismreminders of money shift people into a self-sufficient orientation - more independent, less helpful and less help-seeking— “money brings about a self-sufficient orientation in which people prefer to be free of dependency and dependents”
extends prior research on the effects of money— “The self-sufficiency hypothesis encapsulates findings from extant research on money.”
builds-on Lea and Webley (1)— “A recent theory by Lea and Webley (1), which characterizes money as both a tool and a drug, emphasizes that people value money for its instrumentality: Money enables people to achieve goals without aid from others.”

senseTHE DEFINITION AND THEN THE REFUSAL OF ITS CONNOTATION, IN CONSECUTIVE SENTENCES. Self-sufficiency is `an INSULATED STATE wherein people put forth effort to attain personal goals and prefer to be separate from others` - and then: `The term as we define it DOES NOT IMPLY A VALUE JUDGMENT and encompasses a mixture of desirable and undesirable qualities.` **That second sentence is the paper's whole argumentative move.** `Self-sufficiency` in ordinary English is a compliment; stripped of its valence it can carry `worked harder` and `helped less` at once, which is exactly what the nine experiments show— “The term as we define it does not imply a value judgment and encompasses a mixture of desirable and undesirable qualities, which may help explain the positive and negative consequences of money”
senseONE PROCESS FOR TWO OPPOSITE-VALENCE LITERATURES, STATED AS THE PAPER'S PROPOSAL: `Some scholars have pointed to its role as an incentive ... Others, however, have deplored money for undermining interpersonal harmony. WE PROPOSE THAT BOTH OUTCOMES EMERGE FROM THE SAME UNDERLYING PROCESS: Money makes people feel self-sufficient and behave accordingly.` Two bodies of work that had been read as contradicting each other are re-described as one effect seen from two sides - and the only thing that makes that possible is the value-neutral definition in the previous row— “We propose that both outcomes emerge from the same underlying process: Money makes people feel self-sufficient and behave accordingly”
senseTHE OBJECT IS AN IDEA AND THE PAPER SAYS SO BEFORE ANY EXPERIMENT: `In this Report, `money` refers to a distinct entity, a particular economic concept ... we use the term money to represent THE IDEA OF MONEY, NOT PROPERTY OR POSSESSIONS.` Nobody is given, shown or promised wealth. **A stipulative definition placed before the method**, which is rare in this corpus and is what makes the result a claim about concepts rather than about incentives - and it is precisely the move a critic must accept before the findings can even be stated— “In this Report, “money” refers to a distinct entity, a particular economic concept. Consistent with other scholarly uses of the term (1), we use the term money to represent the idea of money, not property or possessions”
senseAND PRIMING IS DEFINED BY MECHANISM AND LOCATION RATHER THAN BY EFFECT: it works `through the use of mental priming techniques, WHICH HEIGHTEN THE ACCESSIBILITY of the idea of money BUT AT A LEVEL BELOW PARTICIPANTS' CONSCIOUS AWARENESS. Thus, priming acts as a NONCONSCIOUS REMINDER of the concept of money.` Two commitments in one sentence - what the prime changes (accessibility) and where it changes it (below awareness) - and the second is the one a replication can fail on without touching the first— “Our research activates the concept of money through the use of mental priming techniques, which heighten the accessibility of the idea of money but at a level below participants’ conscious awareness. Thus, priming acts as a nonconscious reminder of the concept of money”
