phenomenoneffort-based prosocial decision-making— “However, how the brain encodes effort costs when actions benefit others is unknown.”
claim-typeexistence— “Strikingly, we identified a unique neural signature of effort in the anterior cingulate gyrus (ACCg) for prosocial acts, both when choosing to help others and when exerting force to benefit them.”
method — evidence— “During fMRI, participants completed a decision-making task where they chose in each trial whether to “work” and exert force (30%–70% of maximum grip strength) or “rest” (no effort) for rewards (2–10 credits).”
object of study — evidence— “However, how the brain encodes effort costs when actions benefit others is unknown.”
transferfrom economics / decision theory: discounting - the devaluation of a reward by the cost of obtaining it - applied to effort rather than to delay or probability, and used as the computational frame for prosocial choice— “This phenomenon, referred to as effort discounting, relies on computations in which rewards are devalued by exerting effort.”
mechanismThe ACCg represents the effort cost of helping in order to motivate the act and push the person past the barriers standing between another and a good outcome— “In particular, the ACCg may be crucial for motivating people to help others and overcome barriers to others receiving positive outcomes.”
mechanismResonating with another's emotional state strengthens how sharply the brain represents the cost of helping them, and that representation in turn sets how willing the person is to help— “Our findings demonstrate that representing how costly and effortful a prosocial behavior is may be linked to how strongly one represents the emotional states of others, which leads to variability in how willing people are to help others.”
mechanismEffort discounting devalues reward as required force rises, and the paper's model gives the discount a separate rate for self and for other— “This phenomenon, referred to as effort discounting, relies on computations in which rewards are devalued by exerting effort.”
mechanismThe discount rate is steeper for others, so the same reward loses more of its value when the beneficiary is someone else— “Thus, as the required effort increased, the subjective value of decisions decreased at a higher rate when making prosocial versus self-benefiting choices.”
mechanismThe ACCg carries the prosocial effort code at both stages of the act - deciding whether to help, and energising the force once committed— “Multivariate patterns in the ACCg specifically encoded representations of prosocial effort when making a choice, and univariate signals scaled with how much effort was required, with no such signals for self-benefiting efforts.”
mechanismRepresenting how costly a prosocial act is may be the missing link between feeling what others feel and actually helping them— “Our findings demonstrate that representing how costly and effortful a prosocial behavior is may be linked to how strongly one represents the emotional states of others, which leads to variability in how willing people are to help others.”
mechanismMultivariate RSA can detect structure that univariate averaging destroys, which is why the social specificity shows up here and not in earlier work— “RSA can be more sensitive than univariate analysis since it captures effects that are washed away by averaging across voxels 59 but that are crucial for understanding social specialization.”
mechanismIf the VTA codes value only for self-benefiting effort, then a simple warm-glow account - helping is rewarding to the helper - cannot be the whole story— “Such findings concord with the idea that prosocial and self-benefiting actions are distinct, may be linked to partially distinct motivational processes, and suggest that perhaps “warm glow” is not always the driver of prosocial acts.”
builds-on Lockwood and colleagues (reference 9), the prior behavioural prosocial effort task— “Lockwood and colleagues 9 required participants to make decisions about whether they would rather take a rest for small reward (1 credit) or exert physical effort (30%–70% of their max grip strength) to obtain higher rewards (2–10 credits).”
builds-on Marr's three levels of analysis as applied to social specificity (reference 12)— “Recently, we highlighted how using the framework of Marr’s three levels can be fruitful for examining if a process is socially, or self, specific, either in how it is implemented in the brain, or in its algorithmic processes.”
extends Lockwood et al. (reference 9), the behavioural effort task this study puts in the scanner— “Here, to address the question of whether prosocial efforts are processed distinctly from self-benefiting ones, participants completed a physical effort task 9 where self-benefiting and prosocial decisions were dissociated.”
supports monkey neurophysiology showing ACCg neurons signalling exclusively when another receives reward— “Neurophysiological recordings in monkeys indicate that the ACCg contains a higher proportion of neurons that signal exclusively when another, not oneself, receives rewards compared with other frontal regions.”
critiques the inference from economic-game studies that AI and dACC/dmPFC activity is specifically prosocial— “This finding raises the possibility that these areas may be less directly linked to prosocial behavior and more linked to domain-general decision processes.”
