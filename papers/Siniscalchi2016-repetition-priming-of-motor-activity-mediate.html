phenomenonrepetition priming of motor programs— “Repetition priming is characterized by increased performance as a behavior is repeated.”
claim-typemechanism— “In summary, taken together these results and previous work support the idea that repetition priming in the feeding network is mediated by the release of modulatory neurotransmitters from inputs to the CPG.”
method — evidence— “Conventional intracellular and extracellular recording techniques were used.”
mechanismPriming requires modulatory peptides released by extrinsic CPG inputs; stimulating a core CPG neuron does not release them, so no priming accumulates— “Rather, it is likely that the appropriate modulators are simply not released when only B63 is stimulated.”
mechanismFCAP/CP-2 released from CBI-2 raise the excitability of the ingestive interneuron B40, which is then recruited into later programs and imposes an ingestive B8 firing pattern— “The recruitment of B40 is likely to result from a peptide (i.e., FCAP/CP-2)-mediated excitability increase.”
mechanismPriming is stored as persistent modulator-induced changes in the pattern-generating interneurons B20 and B40 inside the feeding network rather than presynaptically in the input terminals— “However, taken together our data strongly suggest that effects of repetition priming are at least partially mediated by effects of modulators on pattern generating interneurons within the feeding network (i.e., B20 and B40).”
mechanismCarry-over between behaviours occurs only when the interneuron modified by the first behaviour is also part of the circuit recruited by the second, which is why switch costs are asymmetric— “This will happen if neurons that are modified during the first type of activity are part of the circuitry activated during the second type.”
builds-on the chemical coding model of repetition priming developed from the radula opener circuitry (Friedman et al. 2015; Friedman and Weiss 2010)— “Our results are therefore consistent with the chemical coding model developed as a result of previous experiments conducted on the radula opener circuitry ( Friedman et al. 2015 ; Friedman and Weiss 2010 ).”
replicates previous demonstrations that unprimed CBI-2- and EN-induced buccal motor programs have intermediate rather than ingestive or egestive characteristics (Dacks et al. 2012; Friedman et al. 2009; Proekt et al. 2004)— “We confirmed these results, demonstrating that 80% of the unprimed BMPs induced by CBI-2 were classified as intermediate, as were 75% of the unprimed BMPs induced by EN stimulation ( Fig. 2 ).”

One paper applies `priming` to a preparation with no subject at all - a buccal ganglion on a dish, where the criterion is that a motor pattern becomes better articulated on repetition.
