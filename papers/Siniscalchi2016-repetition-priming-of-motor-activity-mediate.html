phenomenonrepetition priming of motor programs
claim-typemechanism
mechanismPriming requires modulatory peptides released by extrinsic CPG inputs; stimulating a core CPG neuron does not release them, so no priming accumulates — “Rather, it is likely that the appropriate modulators are simply not released when only B63 is stimulated.”
mechanismFCAP/CP-2 released from CBI-2 raise the excitability of the ingestive interneuron B40, which is then recruited into later programs and imposes an ingestive B8 firing pattern — “The recruitment of B40 is likely to result from a peptide (i.e., FCAP/CP-2)-mediated excitability increase.”
mechanismPriming is stored as persistent modulator-induced changes in the pattern-generating interneurons B20 and B40 inside the feeding network rather than presynaptically in the input terminals — “However, taken together our data strongly suggest that effects of repetition priming are at least partially mediated by effects of modulators on pattern generating interneurons within the feeding network (i.e., B20 and B40).”
mechanismCarry-over between behaviours occurs only when the interneuron modified by the first behaviour is also part of the circuit recruited by the second, which is why switch costs are asymmetric — “This will happen if neurons that are modified during the first type of activity are part of the circuitry activated during the second type.”
relation — “Our results are therefore consistent with the chemical coding model developed as a result of previous experiments conducted on the radula opener circuitry ( Friedman et al. 2015 ; Friedman and Weiss 2010 ).”
relation — “We confirmed these results, demonstrating that 80% of the unprimed BMPs induced by CBI-2 were classified as intermediate, as were 75% of the unprimed BMPs induced by EN stimulation ( Fig. 2 ).”
effect directionB63 → repetition priming — no-change — “repetition priming does not occur”
effect directionCBI-2 → repetition priming — increases — “an important role in the induction of repetition priming”
effect directionCBI-2 → B40 — increases — “increases the excitability of B40”
replicationuntested
limitationSomatic recordings used to rule out indirect recruitment of the CPG inputs may not reflect activity at those inputs' terminals. — “However, somatic recordings do not necessarily reflect electrical activity in the terminals of CPG inputs (see, e.g., Nusbaum 1994 ).”
limitationAn additional contribution from modulation of the motor neuron B8 itself is not excluded. — “Thus our findings do not rule out an effect of input activation on the properties of B8 itself.”
Content tags
content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingRepeatedly triggering feeding motor programs from inside the central pattern generator, by stimulating interneuron B63, produced no repetition priming: B8 firing stayed low in both protraction and retraction across repetitions. — “When B63 was stimulated we found no significant improvement in response articulation ( Fig. 3 ).”
findingRepetition priming did occur when the same programs were driven through extrinsic inputs, the esophageal nerve or CBI-2, showing priming depends on which element triggers the program rather than on CPG activation per se. — “In contrast, repetition priming was observed when motor programs were induced by stimulating either the EN or CBI-2 ( Fig. 3 B ).”
findingUnprimed programs were mostly intermediate (neither ingestive nor egestive) whichever element triggered them: 80% for CBI-2, 75% for the esophageal nerve, and 94% for B63. — “We confirmed these results, demonstrating that 80% of the unprimed BMPs induced by CBI-2 were classified as intermediate, as were 75% of the unprimed BMPs induced by EN stimulation ( Fig. 2 ).”
findingPriming the ingestive input CBI-2 first, then switching to B63 to trigger programs, made the B63-triggered programs ingestive with a fivefold rise in retraction-phase B8 firing that later returned to baseline, showing the priming is stored postsynaptically in the core network. — “These data suggest that CBI-2-induced repetition priming modifies properties of the core network postsynaptic to CBI-2.”
findingPriming the egestive esophageal-nerve input first likewise made subsequent B63-triggered programs egestive, with a threefold rise in protraction-phase B8 firing that recovered within 15 minutes. — “These data suggest that repeated stimulation of the EN also induces repetition priming by modifying properties of the core feeding network.”
findingBath application of the CBI-2 peptides FCAP and CP-2 reproduced the ingestive signature, raising retraction-phase B8 firing to over 250% of baseline without significantly changing protraction firing. — “FCAP/CP-2 superfusion partially mimicked effects of CBI-2 stimulation, i.e., it had a significant effect on the retraction-phase firing frequency of B8 [1-way repeated-measures ANOVA: F (2,10) = 39.93, P < 0.0001], increasing it to >250% of baseline ( Fig. 6 , A and C ).”
findingBath application of the esophageal-nerve peptide SCPb reproduced the egestive signature, raising protraction-phase B8 firing to over 250% of baseline. — “In contrast, SCP b superfusion had a significant effect on the protraction-phase firing frequency of B8 [ Fig. 6 , B and D ; 1-way repeated-measures ANOVA: F (2,6) = 33.95, P = 0.0005], namely, it increased the frequency to >250% of baseline”
findingIngestive priming acted by making the ingestive pattern-generating interneuron B40 more excitable, so that it was recruited into subsequent B63-triggered programs with more spikes per current pulse, shorter spike latency and a depolarized resting potential. — “These data indicate that ingestive priming increases the excitability of the interneuron B40 and increases its firing frequency during B63-induced motor programs.”
findingEgestive priming instead recruited the egestive interneuron B20, whose protraction firing frequency more than doubled after esophageal-nerve stimulation. — “EN stimulation had a significant effect on firing frequency [ Fig. 9 , A and B 1 ; 1-way repeated-measures ANOVA: F (2,8) = 16.99, P = 0.0013]. It more than doubled, increasing to 5.1 ± 0.5 Hz [post hoc comparison; t (4) = 5.48, P = 0.0018].”
findingBecause B20 receives excitatory drive during both ingestive and egestive programs whereas B40 receives it only during ingestive ones, the authors explain the observed asymmetry of task switching: negative biasing when switching from egestive to ingestive but positive biasing in the opposite direction. — “We suggest that this at least partially accounts for the asymmetries in task switching that have been observed in the feeding network, i.e., negative biasing in the egestive-ingestive direction and positive biasing in the ingestive-egestive direction.”
quantity94% — Share of unprimed buccal motor programs triggered from inside the CPG (B63) that were classified as intermediate — “Similar results were obtained with B63 stimulation ( Fig. 2 ), namely, 94% of the induced BMPs were intermediate.”
quantity1.2 ± 0.4 Hz to 6.1 ± 0.8 Hz — Fivefold increase in B8 retraction-phase firing in B63-triggered programs caused by prior CBI-2 (ingestive) priming — “A post hoc comparison indicated that there was a fivefold increase in the B8 firing frequency during retraction, i.e., the firing frequency before CBI-2 stimulation was 1.2 ± 0.4 Hz and the frequency after CBI-2 stimulation was 6.1 ± 0.8 Hz [ t (9) = 7.8, P < 0.0001].”
quantity1.0 ± 0.2 Hz to 3.1 ± 0.5 Hz — Threefold increase in B8 protraction-phase firing in B63-triggered programs caused by prior esophageal-nerve (egestive) priming, recovering within 15 min — “A post hoc comparison indicated a threefold increase in firing frequency. It went from 1.0 ± 0.2 Hz to 3.1 ± 0.5 Hz [ t (7) = 6.35, P < 0.0001] and recovered within 15 min, falling to 1.1 ± 0.3 Hz [ t (7) = 0.34, P = 1].”
quantity9.8 ± 5.7% to 87.5 ± 4.2% — Fraction of the protraction phase during which B40 was active, before versus after CBI-2-induced priming — “Thus it increased from 9.8 ± 5.7% to 87.5 ± 4.2% [post hoc comparison; t (4) = 10.65, P < 0.001].”
quantity8.7 ± 0.9 to 19.2 ± 2.3 spikes — Spikes evoked in B40 by a fixed 3-s current pulse before and after three trains of CBI-2 stimulation, i.e. the excitability increase — “Before CBI-2 was stimulated, 8.7 ± 0.9 spikes were triggered by current injection into B40. After the last burst of CBI-2 activity, 19.2 ± 2.3 spikes were triggered [post hoc comparison; t (4) = 7.26, P = 0.0003].”
quantityr = 0.7665, P < 0.0001, n = 103 BMPs — Correlation between B40 firing and B8 retraction-phase activity pooled across time points and preparations — “Pooled analysis of all time points within and across preparations revealed a significant correlation between B40 firing and B8 retraction activity ( r = 0.7665, P < 0.0001, n = 103 BMPs).”
quantityr = 0.8296, P < 0.0001, n = 56 BMPs — Correlation between B20 firing and B8 firing during protraction — “Analysis of all 56 programs recorded revealed a significant correlation between B20 and B8 firing during protraction ( r = 0.8296, P < 0.0001, n = 56 BMPs).”
quantity1.0 ± 0.2 Hz vs 1.4 ± 0.2 Hz, P = 0.18 — B8 protraction and retraction firing in unprimed B63-triggered programs, statistically indistinguishable between phases — “In B63-induced BMPs, B8 fired at a mean rate of 1.0 ± 0.2 Hz during protraction and 1.4 ± 0.2 Hz during retraction, with no significant difference between the two phases of the motor program [paired t -test; t (17) = 1.38, P = 0.18].”

definitionrepetition priming — “Repetition priming is characterized by increased performance as a behavior is repeated.”
definitioningestion (ingestive program) — “During ingestion the radula is open during protraction and closed during retraction, which pulls food in ( Morton and Chiel 1993a , 1993b ).”
definitionegestion (egestive program) — “During egestion, the radula is closed during protraction and open during retraction ( Morton and Chiel 1993a , 1993b ); this pushes food out.”
definitioningestive motor program (operational classification criteria) — “More specifically, motor programs were classified as ingestive if they met the following three criteria: the protraction-phase B8 firing frequency was <3.5 Hz, the retraction-phase B8 firing frequency was >4.5 Hz, and the ratio obtained by dividing the B8 firing frequency during protraction by the B8 firing frequency during retraction was <0.65.”
definitionprotraction phase (in vitro definition) — “In vitro, protraction is defined as the phase of the motor program in which there is activity in the I2 nerve (since it contains axons of radula protraction motor neurons)”
definitionchemical coding model of repetition priming — “This model postulates that repetition priming is induced by modulators released from CPG inputs, with the nature of priming depending on the input activated and the type of modulator released.”
definitionnegative biasing — “Negative biasing is observed when motor programs are triggered by stimulating CBI-2 after EN-induced repetition priming.”
definitiontask switching — “Potentially this arrangement could have interesting consequences for task switching, i.e., the cessation of one type of motor activity and the initiation of a second type.”
