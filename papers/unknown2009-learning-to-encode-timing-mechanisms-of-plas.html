phenomenonauditory brainstem plasticity
claim-typeexistence
transferfrom visual perception / visual cortical processing: The Reverse Hierarchy Theory, first formulated to describe top-down influence of higher visual cortical areas on lower ones, is taken over to explain top-down modulation of auditory processing and extended below cortex to the brainstem. — “Because the top-down mechanism was originally proposed for the impact of higher-order visual cortical areas to lower-order cortical areas, the focus has been primarily on the intracortical feedback pathway.”
transferfrom electrosensory neurobiology of mormyrid electric fish: The adaptive-sensory-processor / 'negative image' account of anti-Hebbian STDP in the cerebellum-like electrosensory lobe is imported as a candidate functional model for the mammalian dorsal cochlear nucleus. — “The auditory system faces a similar task in that it must differentiate spectral changes in a sound introduced by the animal’s own movements from spectral changes introduced by the movement of an external sound source relative to the animal.”
transferfrom electrosensory neuroscience in mormyrid fish: anti-Hebbian STDP building a negative image that cancels the sensory consequences of the animal's own movements, imported as the candidate function of the dorsal cochlear nucleus — “In this way, anti-Hebbian STDP adjusts parallel fiber synaptic weights in order to sculpt a “negative image” of the neural response caused by the fish’s own movements.”
transferfrom visual perception theory: the Reverse Hierarchy Theory, built for higher-to-lower visual cortical areas and extended here to corticofugal control of the brainstem — “The RHT postulates that the performance of a perceptual task is first based on the highest available level of sensory representation. If the task cannot be accomplished at that level (because of poor sensory resolution), it proceeds down the representational hierarchy to obtain more detailed, lower-level cues that participate in generating the percept.”
transferfrom cerebellar physiology: the parallel-fiber and climbing-fiber arrangement, used both as the structural analogy for the DCN and as the model for how auditory nerve activity might induce plasticity — “Cerebellum-like structures act as adaptive sensory processors in which the signals conveyed by parallel fibers in the molecular layer predict the patterns of sensory input to the deep layers through a process of associative anti-Hebbian synaptic plasticity”
mechanismSynapse-specific plasticity simultaneously disinhibits fusiform cells and strengthens their excitatory input, driving the fusiform hyperactivity that underlies tinnitus — “Synapse-specific synaptic plasticity, by decreasing the activity of inhibitory interneurons (disinhibition) and simultaneously increasing excitatory input to fusiform cells, can lead to hyperactivity in fusiform cells, similar to the kind observed in animals with behavioral evidence of tinnitus.”
mechanismAnti-Hebbian STDP reweights parallel-fiber synapses so that inputs predicting incoming sensory activity are weakened, building a 'negative image' that cancels the sensory consequences of the animal's own movements — “In this way, anti-Hebbian STDP adjusts parallel fiber synaptic weights in order to sculpt a “negative image” of the neural response caused by the fish’s own movements.”
mechanismNitric oxide diffusing out of MNTB principal neurons suppresses postsynaptic Kv3 channels in neighbouring cells, providing intercellular gain control during loud sound — “Diffusion of NO from MNTB principal neurons provides modulatory control of excitability via direct suppression of postsynaptic Kv3 channels”

replicationuntested
limitationThe synaptic-plasticity evidence base comes overwhelmingly from brain slices, which differ substantially from the intact brain. — “Most studies of synaptic plasticity in the auditory brainstem have been performed in brain slices.”
limitationHow naturally occurring noise exposure would induce the plasticity proposed to underlie tinnitus is not established. — “While it is obvious that electrical stimulation of parallel fibers can induce synaptic plasticity that could change the balance of excitation and inhibition in fusiform cells, it is not entirely clear how stimulation caused by noise exposure could produce similar plasticity effects.”
limitationHow brainstem pathways adapt to ongoing sensory activity and contribute to perception of environmental sound remains largely unknown. — “This research has provided insight into how sounds are localized by vertebrates, but much less is known about how these pathways adapt to ongoing sensory activity and how they contribute to the perception and interpretation of environmental sounds, including speech, under normal and pathological conditions.”
limitationmost of the plasticity evidence comes from slices, which differ from the intact brain in ways that have already changed one conclusion — “Most studies of synaptic plasticity in the auditory brainstem have been performed in brain slices. This experimental approach has substantial differences from studying intact brains in vivo.”
limitationhow noise exposure would induce the plasticity proposed to cause tinnitus is not established — “While it is obvious that electrical stimulation of parallel fibers can induce synaptic plasticity that could change the balance of excitation and inhibition in fusiform cells, it is not entirely clear how stimulation caused by noise exposure could produce similar plasticity effects.”
limitationthe pathophysiology of tinnitus remains poorly understood despite its prevalence — “Despite the wide prevalence of tinnitus, the pathophysiology of the disorder is poorly understood.”
limitationwhether the fish-derived cancellation function applies to the dorsal cochlear nucleus is posed as an open question, not answered — “A key question is whether anti-Hebbian plasticity observed in the DCN can serve a similar function.”
limitationwhether brainstem plasticity is driven top-down, locally, or by both is unresolved — “Whether activity-dependent changes are initiated/modulated in a top-down fashion, as predicted by the RHT through the efferent, corticofugal system linking the cortex and the auditory brainstem; through local mechanisms of adaptation to the acoustic properties of the input ( Dean et al., 2005 ); or through an interaction of afferent and efferent mechanisms is a challenge for future research to resolve.”
limitationhow these pathways adapt and contribute to perception is much less understood than how they transmit timing — “This research has provided insight into how sounds are localized by vertebrates, but much less is known about how these pathways adapt to ongoing sensory activity and how they contribute to the perception and interpretation of environmental sounds, including speech, under normal and pathological conditions.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingThe auditory brainstem, traditionally regarded as hard-wired, is in fact dynamic and expresses experience-dependent plasticity. — “Contrary to traditional views that early stages of sensory processing are not plastic, new studies discussed in this article have established that the auditory brainstem is dynamic.”
findingIn the dorsal cochlear nucleus one and the same spike-pairing protocol strengthens parallel-fiber synapses onto fusiform cells (LTP, Hebbian) while weakening those onto cartwheel cells (LTD, anti-Hebbian), so the plasticity rule is cell-specific rather than nucleus-wide. — “The same protocol induces LTP in fusiform cells and LTD at cartwheel cells.”
findingLanguage experience reshapes brainstem coding: speakers of tonal languages show enhanced neural representation of pitch. — “Krishnan and colleagues were the first to demonstrate that language experience affects brainstem activity by showing that speakers of tonal languages have enhanced neural representation of pitch”
findingExperience-driven changes in the brainstem are selective to particular stimulus features rather than an across-the-board amplification of all responses. — “Modification of auditory brainstem processing by language and musical experience does not result in a stimulus-independent, generalized gain effect.”
findingAcoustic environment tunes intrinsic excitability: in quiet conditions PKC phosphorylation of Kv3.1b potassium channels lowers the potassium current so that neurons fire only at low frequencies. — “At low levels of sound intensity (quiet environment), phosphorylation of Kv3.1b by protein kinase C (PKC) reduces potassium current, thus allowing low-frequency firing.”
findingThe medial nucleus of the trapezoid body is not a faithful relay: under in-vivo-like spontaneous activity it fails to entrain reliably to prolonged high-frequency stimuli because of short-term plasticity, even though it still encodes stimulus onset precisely. — “This is an important finding suggesting that the MNTB is not a simple and faithful relay nucleus, and while it encodes precisely the onset of sound-like stimuli (as bursts of discharges), it may not reliably entrain to high-frequency stimuli for prolonged periods, due to short-term plasticity.”
findingThe authors argue that the Reverse Hierarchy Theory, formulated for cortex, extends to subcortical processing and may itself initiate or modulate brainstem plasticity. — “Therefore, it is our view that RHT may apply to subcortical sensory processing and that the application of its principles may initiate or modulate plasticity at the auditory brainstem.”
findingone induction protocol produces LTP in fusiform cells and LTD in cartwheel cells, so the sign of plasticity is set by cell type — “The same protocol induces LTP in fusiform cells and LTD at cartwheel cells.”
findingspeakers of tonal languages have enhanced neural representation of pitch in the brainstem — “Krishnan and colleagues were the first to demonstrate that language experience affects brainstem activity by showing that speakers of tonal languages have enhanced neural representation of pitch ( Krishnan et al., 2005”
findingmusical experience produces more robust brainstem encoding of speech, linguistic pitch patterns and vocal emotion — “Musical experience can also result in more robust brainstem encoding of speech sounds, linguistic pitch-patterns, and processing of vocal expressions of emotion ( Figure 1 , middle and bottom panels, Musacchia et al., 2007 ; Wong et al., 2007”
findingbrainstem encoding is disrupted in subsets of poor readers and children with autism — “Conversely, distinct aspects of brainstem encoding are disrupted in a subset of children with language impairment (e.g., poor readers, children with autism) ( Figure 1 , middle and bottom panels, Cunningham et al., 2001 ; Banai et al., 2009 ; Russo et al., 2008”
findingbrainstem responses are affected by attention and are larger to a speech syllable than to its time-reversed version — “Galbraith recognized the dynamic nature of the human brainstem response over a decade ago, finding that responses were affected by attention ( Galbraith et al., 1998 ) and were larger to a speech syllable than to its time-reversed version”
findingexperience does not produce a generalised gain change but alters specific stimulus features — “Modification of auditory brainstem processing by language and musical experience does not result in a stimulus-independent, generalized gain effect. Instead, distinctive aspects of stimulus processing (e.g., high-frequency phase locking, onset synchrony) are impaired or enhanced depending on the behavioral relevance and relative complexity of the stimulus, likely influencing how the sensory system responds”
findingbrainstem processing can also be changed by auditory training over weeks, not only by lifelong experience — “While language, musical training, and subcortical impairments represent lifelong experiences, brainstem processing can also be modified by shorter-term auditory training (e.g., over the course of weeks) ( Russo et al., 2005 ; Song et al., 2008 ; de Boer and Thornton, 2008”
findingadding in-vivo-like spontaneous activity to MNTB slices revealed synaptic failures at high frequency that earlier studies had not seen — “Introduction of high spontaneous, in-vivo-like activity in brain slices prepared from the medial nucleus of the trapezoid body (MNTB) revealed synaptic failures during high-frequency activity ( Hermann et al., 2007 ) not seen in previous studies.”
findingso the MNTB is not a simple faithful relay and may not entrain reliably to sustained high-frequency input — “This is an important finding suggesting that the MNTB is not a simple and faithful relay nucleus, and while it encodes precisely the onset of sound-like stimuli (as bursts of discharges), it may not reliably entrain to high-frequency stimuli for prolonged periods, due to short-term plasticity.”
findingphosphorylation state of Kv3.1b channels sets whether neurons fire at low or high frequency, tracking the acoustic environment — “At low levels of sound intensity (quiet environment), phosphorylation of Kv3.1b by protein kinase C (PKC) reduces potassium current, thus allowing low-frequency firing. Conversely, in response to high-frequency auditory or synaptic stimulation, channel dephosphorylation and increased Kv3.1b channel promote the ability of neurons to fire at high frequency ( Song et al., 2005”
findingnitric oxide from one MNTB neuron modulates the excitability of its neighbours, a candidate gain control for loud environments — “ivity in one MNTB neuron can modulate the excitability of adjacent neurons, suggesting that this modulation can serve as a homeostatic function for gain reduction during loud noise conditions.”
findingretrograde GABA signalling in the lateral superior olive adjusts the excitation-inhibition balance underlying sound localisation — “For example, recent studies in the lateral superior olive (LSO) indicate that retrograde GABA signaling adjusts sound localization by balancing excitation and inhibition in the brainstem”
findingcannabinoid receptors involved in DCN plasticity are downregulated in animals with behavioural evidence of tinnitus — “Synapse-specific synaptic plasticity, by decreasing the activity of inhibitory interneurons (disinhibition) and simultaneously increasing excitatory input to fusiform cells, can lead to hyperactivity in fusiform cells, similar to the kind observed in animals with behavioral evidence of tinnitus.”
findingcortical activation shapes the tuning of cochlear nucleus neurons, giving a route for top-down control — “Recent findings indicate that cortical activation shapes the tuning properties of neurons in the cochlear nucleus ( Luo et al., 2008 ) similar to intra-cortical, experience-dependent shaping of receptive fields observed in primary auditory cortex ( Schreiner and Winer, 2007 ; Fritz et al., 2007 ; Atiani et al., 2009”
