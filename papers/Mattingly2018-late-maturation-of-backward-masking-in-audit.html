transferfrom human psychoacoustics: the masked-versus-unmasked threshold comparison, reproduced in the neurometric so the two can be put side by side — “Our neurometric comparison used Noise-Alone-evoked firing and spontaneous firing as the backgrounds against which each threshold was determined to match perceptual measures. In psychophysical detection, listeners choose between alternatives of a masker with and without a tone, to yield a masked detection threshold. A separate choice between silence and a tone yields a Tone-Alone detection threshold. The two thresholds are then compared to yield a mask-induced threshold shift. Because the masker immediately follows the tone, a longer detection window such as the TrialWin will encompass the response to the masker, making the relative background level a critical element of detection above baseline. In contrast, only a very short analysis window will exclude the Noise-Alone-evoked firing background.”
transferfrom signal detection neurometrics: the encoding time window as an explicit parameter, and the requirement to choose one — “Neurometric analysis requires the choosing of an appropriate encoding time window, defined as the window containing the response patterns contributing to the signal to be detected ( Panzeri et al. 2010 ). For these neurometric analyses, we employed two windows to test different aspects of masking. First, a brief analysis window encompassed only the response to the tone without any response to the successive masker. This quantified backward masking of the tone response while excluding forward masking of the subsequent masker response by the tone, allowing a measure of cortical backward masking uncontaminated by a forward masked component. Second, a longer analysis window encompassed the response to both the tone and the subsequent masker. This was used because behaviorally, tone detection in the presence of a masker is measured in comparison to that of the masker alone, and because the behavioral reaction time for short signal detection occurs well after subsequent masker onset. Thus perception of backward masked signals could be influenced by neural responses to the masker, which are encompassed by the longer analysis window. The longer window may more accurately reflect the information used by the nervous system for masked signal detection.”

However, potential neural contributions to backward masking are unresolved. Because the signal to be detected occurs before the masker, masking cannot be fully explained by responses in the auditory periphery ( Zwicker and Fastl 2013 ). Backward masking occurs with both dichotic and monotic stimuli, also suggesting central contributions ( Elliott 1962 ). Recordings have shown forward but not backward masking in the auditory nerve ( Killian et al. 1994 ) and in cochlear nucleus neurons ( Watanabe and Simada 1971 ), but backward masking has been demonstrated in inferior colliculus ( Faure et al. 2003 ; Watanabe and Simada 1971 ). One study examined backward masking within a subset of recorded cortical cells that exhibited a very delayed response (>150 ms after signal onset; Brosch et al. 1998 ). However, despite the involvement of cortex in temporal information processing ( Green et al. 2017 ; Ohl et al. 1999 ; Rosen et al. 2010 ), typical ACx responses to signals have never been examined during backward masking.”

findinga shorter latency for inhibition than excitation lets the later masker suppress the earlier tone, and predicts the negative correlation observed — “Mechanistically, forward masking is considered to involve a combination of synaptic depression, afterhyperpolarization, and inhibition ( Brosch and Schreiner 1997 ; Wehr and Zador 2005 ). definitionbackward masking — “Perceptually, auditory backward masking manifests as higher thresholds for detection of a short signal followed by a masker than for the same signal in silence. Cortical masking was driven by a combination of suppressed responses to the signal and a reduced dynamic range available for signal detection in the presence of the masker. Both coding elements contributed to greater masked threshold shifts in juveniles compared with adults, but signal-evoked firing suppression was more pronounced in juveniles. Neural threshold shifts were a better match to human psychophysical threshold shifts when quantified with a longer temporal window that included the response to the delayed masker, suggesting that temporally selective listening may contribute to age-related differences in backward masking.”
definitionwindow — “Neurometric analysis requires the choosing of an appropriate encoding time window, defined as the window containing the response patterns contributing to the signal to be detected ( Panzeri et al. 2010 ). For these neurometric analyses, we employed two windows to test different aspects of masking. First, a brief analysis window encompassed only the response to the tone without any response to the successive masker. This quantified backward masking of the tone response while excluding forward masking of the subsequent masker response by the tone, allowing a measure of cortical backward masking uncontaminated by a forward masked component. Second, a longer analysis window encompassed the response to both the tone and the subsequent masker. This was used because behaviorally, tone detection in the presence of a masker is measured in comparison to that of the masker alone, and because the behavioral reaction time for short signal detection occurs well after subsequent masker onset. Thus perception of backward masked signals could be influenced by neural responses to the masker, which are encompassed by the longer analysis window. The longer window may more accurately reflect the information used by the nervous system for masked signal detection.”
definitionlower envelope — “A strength and limitation of the population neurometric used in the present study is that it pools information across all cells to make a decision for or against tone detection. In general, population coding assumes that all active neurons contribute to perception, whereas the lower envelope principle assumes that perception is driven by the most sensitive neurons and that less sensitive neurons will not interfere”
definitionToneWin — “We chose such a short window, the ToneWin, to encompass just the tone-evoked response. The ToneWin ended at the mean FSL of the noise-evoked response, quantifying backward masking of the tone response while minimizing forward masking of the subsequent masker response by the tone. With the use of this strict analysis window, our data revealed that backward masking was visible at the level of auditory cortex, that tone detection thresholds were higher in juvenile neurons, and that tone-evoked responses were more suppressed by a backward masker in juvenile neurons. Furthermore, the dynamic range difference, a cue available for masked signal detection, only correlated with backward masking in juveniles for this narrow window, suggesting a possible differential use of dynamic range in masked signal detection across development.”
definitionBF — “Neurons were isolated based on their reliable, short-latency onset responses to a 200-ms tone pip used as a search stimulus. Offset-responsive neurons were rarely encountered and were not used in these analyses. The best frequency (BF) of the unit was determined with an iso-intensity function at 60 dB SPL. For the remaining data collection, neurons were presented with short tone pips (10 ms; 2-ms cosine-ramped rise/fall) at the BF of the unit, and only units that responded reliably to these short-duration tones were recorded (firing above baseline on at least 50% of trials within 50 ms of tone onset). This is because, perceptually, the phenomena of backward masking is reliable only for short-duration tones.”
