phenomenonconscious access
claim-typemechanism
transferfrom theoretical physics: the concept of a stochastic phase transition, used to describe the sudden switch of the network into the ignited conscious state — “The transition to the ignited state can be described, in theoretical physics terms, as a stochastic phase transition – a sudden change in neuronal dynamics whose occurrence depends in part on stimulus characteristics and in part on spontaneous fluctuations in activity (Dehaene and Changeux, 2005; Dehaene et al., 2003b).”
transferfrom artificial intelligence: the blackboard architecture - a shared data structure updated by many specialised modules - taken over as the global workspace of consciousness — “However, capitalizing on the earlier concept of a blackboard 22 system in artificial intelligence (a common data structure shared and updated by many specialized modules), Baars (1989) proposed an homunculus-free psychological model where the current conscious content is represented within a distinct mental space called global workspace, with the capacity to broadcast this information to a set of other processors (figure 6).”
mechanismincoming activity that exceeds a dynamical threshold ignites a self-supporting reverberating cell assembly which sustains the conscious content and broadcasts it to distant sites — “Such a selfconnected system exhibits a dynamical threshold: if the incoming activity carries sufficient weight, it leads to the ignition of a self-supporting, reverberating, temporary, metastable, and distributed cell assembly that represents the current conscious contents and broadcasts it to virtually all distant sites.”
mechanismglobal broadcasting frees information from encapsulated circuits and makes it available to many processors, including the ones that formulate verbal reports, which is why conscious information is reportable — “Global broadcasting allows information to be more efficiently processed (because it it no longer confined to a subset of non-conscious circuits, but can be flexibly shared by many cortical processors) and to be verbally reported (because these processors include those involved in formulating verbal messages).”
mechanismduring ignition the workspace is mobilised as a whole, with the non-selected neurons actively inhibited, which blocks a second simultaneous ignition and produces the serial bottleneck — “Such a disruption occurs because during ignition, the GNW is mobilized as a whole, some GNW neurons being active while the rest is actively inhibited, thus preventing multiple simultaneous ignitions.”
mechanismspontaneous prestimulus fluctuations barely affect the fast feedforward stage but strongly bias the slow NMDA-based reverberating stage, so an identical stimulus ignites on some trials and not others, producing all-or-none perception — “In GNW simulations, prestimulus fluctuations in neural discharges only have a small effect on the early sensory stage, which largely reflects objective stimulus amplitude and duration, but they have a large influence on the second slower stage, which is characterized by NMDA-based reverberating integration and ultimately leads to a bimodal "all-ornone" distribution of activity”
mechanisma weak stimulus produces a feedforward wave that cannot recruit enough self-sustaining reverberation, so it dies out before the late global activation and stays subliminal — “For very brief or low-amplitude stimuli, a feedforward wave was seen in the initial thalamic and cortical stages of the simulation, but it died out without triggering the late global activation, because it was not able to gather sufficient selfsustaining reverberant activation (Dehaene and Changeux, 2005).”
mechanismbecause far more workspace neurons are laterally inhibited than activated, the summed scalp signal during ignition is net positive, which is why conscious access shows up as the positive P3b — “Thus, the model can explain why, during conscious access, the resulting event-related potential is dominated by a positive waveform, the P3b.”
relation — “Following Crick and Koch (Crick and Koch, 1990), we focussed solely here on the simpler and well-studied question of what neurophysiological mechanisms differentiate conscious access to some information from nonconscious processing of the same information.”
relation — “The main anatomical premise of the GNW model is that it consists of “a distributed set of cortical neurons characterized by their ability to receive from and send back to homologous neurons in other cortical areas horizontal projections through longrange excitatory axons mostly originating from the pyramidal cells of layers II and III” (Dehaene et al., 1998a) and more densely distributed in prefrontal and inferior parietal cortices.”
replicationuntested
limitationThe review deliberately covers only conscious access, not higher-order concepts of consciousness — “The present review was deliberately limited to conscious access.”
limitationHigher-order concepts such as metacognition and self-representation are excluded because the authors judge them insufficiently defined empirically — “Thus, our view is that these concepts, although essential, have not yet received a sufficient empirical and neurophysiological definition to figure in this review.”
limitationThe supporting simulations implement only a small fragment of the proposed architecture — “Although the full GNW architecture was not simulated, four areas were selected and hierarchically interconnected (figure 7).”
limitationLesion and TMS evidence does not establish that prefrontal contribution is causally necessary for conscious perception — “While suggestive, these observations do not quite suffice to establish that a frontal contribution is causally necessary for conscious perception.”
limitationThe field's strict operational criterion is stated to OVERESTIMATE the thing it measures: subjects perform above chance while denying they perceived anything. — “This strict objective criterion raises problems, however (Persaud et al., 2007; Schurger and Sher, 2008). First, it tends to overestimate conscious perception: there are many conditions in which subjects perform better than chance”
limitationWhich task counts as evidence is unresolved, because performance can be at chance on one task and not another. — “Second, performance can be at chance level for some tasks, but not others, raising the issue of which tasks count as evidence of conscious perception or merely of subliminal processing.”
limitationThe verdict rests on accepting a null hypothesis, and the review states the significance often depends on an analyst's choice of how many trials to run. — “Third, the approach requires accepting the null hypothesis of chance-level performance, yet performance never really falls down to zero, and whether it is significant or not often depends on arbitrary choices such as the number of trials dedicated to its measurement.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingAcross many paradigms, conscious access is marked by three convergent physiological signatures: late amplification of the relevant sensory activity, long-distance cortico-cortical synchronisation in the beta and gamma bands, and ignition of a large-scale prefronto-parietal network. — “In conclusion, human neuroimaging methods and electrophysiological recordings during conscious access, under a broad variety of paradigms, consistently reveal a late amplification of relevant sensory activity, long-distance cortico-cortical synchronisation at beta and gamma frequencies, and "ignition" of a large-scale prefronto-parietal network.”
findingSubliminal influence shrinks as processing depth increases, leaving only small effects at high-level cognitive and decision stages. — “First, subliminal priming quickly decreases with processing depth, such that only small influences are detectable at higher cognitive and decision levels (Dehaene, 2008; van Gaal et al., 2008).”
findingSubliminal priming decays with time and is typically undetectable beyond about 500 ms. — “Second, subliminal priming decreases with elapsed time, and therefore typically ceases to be detectable after 500 ms (Dupoux et al., 2008; Greenwald et al., 1996; Mattler, 2005).”
findingSubliminal stimuli do not produce durable or flexible changes in executive control. — “Third, subliminal stimuli typically fail to yield lasting and flexible modifications in executive control.”
findingThe first roughly 200 ms of visual processing is fully preserved on trials where the subject reports not seeing the stimulus, so early sensory activity does not distinguish conscious from non-conscious trials. — “Evidence from the attentional blink also confirms that the first 200 ms of initial visual processing can be fully preserved on trials in which subjects deny seeing a stimulus (Sergent et al., 2005; Vogel et al., 1998)”
findingThe most reliable electrophysiological correlate of visibility is the late, broadly distributed positive P3/P3b component at about 300-500 ms, distinct from the earlier focal anterior P3a which can occur non-consciously. — “In ERPs, the most consistent correlate of visibility appears to be a late (~300-500 ms) and broadly distributed positive component called P3 or sometimes P3b”
findingIn monkeys viewing binocular rivalry, the share of neurons whose firing follows the reported percept rises steeply from primary visual cortex to higher associative cortex, supporting the idea that conscious assemblies are denser in higher areas. — “The proportion of such cells increased from about 20% in V1/V2 to 40% in V4, MT or MST, to as high as 90% in IT and STS.”
findingConscious words modulated late activity at 68.8% of intracranial electrode sites versus 24.4% for non-conscious words, showing that conscious access has a brain-wide impact. — “Indeed, seen stimuli had a global impact on late evoked activity virtually anywhere in the cortex: 68.8% of electrode sites, although selected for clinical purposes, were modulated by the presence of conscious words (as opposed to 24.4% of sites for non-conscious words).”
findingTranscranial magnetic stimulation over parietal or prefrontal cortex, far from visual areas, can block conscious perception and even make a fixated stimulus subjectively disappear. — “In normal subjects, transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) over either parietal or prefrontal cortex can prevent conscious perception and even trigger a sudden subjective disappearance of visual stimulis during prolonged fixation (Kanai et al., 2008)”
findingPatients with focal prefrontal lesions needed stronger stimuli to see a masked target, in proportion to how far the lesion extended into left anterior prefrontal cortex, while their subliminal performance stayed normal. — “We recently made similar observations in patients with focal prefrontal lesions (Del Cul et al., 2009): their masking threshold was significantly elevated, in tight correlation with the degree of expansion of the lesions into left anterior prefrontal cortex, while subliminal performance on "not-seen" trials did not differ from normal.”
findingEarly-stage multiple sclerosis patients, whose white-matter damage disrupts long-distance connectivity, required a longer target-mask delay before a stimulus became conscious. — “As predicted, MS patients showed abnormal conscious perception of masked stimuli: they needed a longer target-mask delay before conscious access occurred.”
findingThe GNW model predicts and its simulations reproduce a strict seriality of conscious access, so only one content can be consciously processed at a time. — “A strict seriality of conscious access and processing is therefore predicted and has been simulated (Dehaene and Changeux, 2005; Dehaene et al., 2003b; Zylberberg et al., 2010).”
findingSubliminal no-go signals activate only a small set of specialised inhibitory processors, whereas conscious no-go signals additionally recruit anterior cingulate, inferior and middle frontal gyrus, dorsolateral prefrontal and inferior parietal cortex. — “Only conscious no-go signals triggered a broad and more anterior activation expanding into anterior cingulate, inferior and middle frontal gyrus, dorsolateral prefrontal cortex and inferior parietal cortex – a network fully compatible with the GNW model (see figure 1).”
findingSchizophrenic patients show impaired neural signatures of conscious access while their subliminal processing remains normal. — “Indeed, there is direct evidence for impaired neural signatures of conscious access, together with normal subliminal processing, in schizophrenic patients (Dehaene et al., 2003a; Del Cul et al., 2006; Luck et al., 2006).”
findingThe review's inventory of empirical markers of conscious access is: all-or-none prefronto-parietal ignition, all-or-none amplification of sensory activation, a late global P3b, late broad-band gamma power increase, enhanced long-distance beta-band phase synchronisation, and enhanced causal coupling between distant areas including a top-down component. — “In the present state of investigations, experimental measures of conscious access identified in this review include: (1) sudden, all-or-none ignition of prefronto-parietal networks; (2) concomitant all-or-none amplification of sensory activation; (3) a late global P3b wave in eventrelated potentials; (4) late amplification of broad-band power in the gamma range; (5) enhanced long-distance phase synchronization, particularly in the beta range; (6) enhanced causal relations between distant areas, including a significant top-down component.”

definitionconscious access — “In this review, we outline recent advances made in understanding the delimited issue of conscious access: how does an external or internal piece of information gain access to conscious processing, defined as a reportable subjective experience?”
definitionsubliminal stimulus — “A subliminal stimulus is one in which the bottom-up, stimulus-driven information is so reduced as to make it undetectable, even with focussed attention.”
definitionpreconscious stimulus — “A preconscious stimulus, by contrast, is one which is potentially visible (its energy and duration is such that it could be seen), but which, on a given trial, is not consciously perceived due to temporary distraction or inattention.”
definitionmasking — “Subliminal presentation is often achieved by masking, a method whereby the subjective visibility of a stimulus is reduced or eliminated by the presentation, in close spatial and temporal contiguity, of other stimuli acting as “masks”(Breitmeyer, 2006).”
definitionsubliminal priming — “Masked stimuli are frequently used to induce subliminal priming, the facilitation of the processing of a visible target by the prior presentation of an identical or related subliminal prime (for review, see Kouider and Dehaene, 2007).”
definitionattentional blink — “The attentional blink (AB) is a short-term variant of this effect where a brief distraction by a first stimulus T1 prevents the conscious perception of a second stimulus T2 briefly presented within a few hundreds of milliseconds of T1 (Raymond et al., 1992).”
definitionselection / selective attention — “Selection, also called selective attention, refers to the separation of relevant versus irrelevant information, isolation of an object or spatial location, based on its saliency or relevance to current goals, and amplification of its sensory attributes.”
definitionGlobal Neuronal Workspace — “Our proposal is that a subset of cortical pyramidal cells with long-range excitatory axons, particularly dense in prefrontal, cingulate, and parietal regions, together with the relevant thalamocortical loops, form a horizontal “neuronal workspace” interconnecting the multiple specialized, automatic and non-conscious processors (figure 6).”
definitionignition — “Such a selfconnected system exhibits a dynamical threshold: if the incoming activity carries sufficient weight, it leads to the ignition of a self-supporting, reverberating, temporary, metastable, and distributed cell assembly that represents the current conscious contents and broadcasts it to virtually all distant sites.”
definitionΦ (integrated information) — “A notable feature of the dynamic core hypothesis is the proposal of a quantitative mathematical measure of information integration called Φ, high values of which are achieved only through a hierarchical recurrent connectivity and would be necessary and sufficient to sustain conscious experience”
definitionmismatch negativity (MMN) — “mismatch negativities (MMN), i.e. electrophysiological responses that arise primarily from the temporal lobe in response to rare, deviant or otherwise unpredictable auditory stimuli”
