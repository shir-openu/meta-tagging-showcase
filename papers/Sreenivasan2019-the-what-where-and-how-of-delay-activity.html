mechanismStructured recurrent excitation with tuned excitation–inhibition balance lets network spiking settle into a self-sustaining attractor state that keeps the memory alive without input — “When the connectivity between neurons in the network is properly structured, and when the balance between excitation and inhibition is well-tuned, spiking dynamics settle into a stable attractor state in the absence of any input”
mechanismSlow NMDA-receptor excitation stabilises attractor networks so that persistent spiking can be maintained over seconds — “The relatively slow excitatory dynamics of NMDAR activation can provide a stabilizing influence on attractor networks, allowing these networks to sustain a representation through persistent spiking over several seconds”
mechanismEncoding-driven changes in synaptic weights turn the network into a matched filter, so the memory is stored silently and revealed when noisy input is fed through it — “shifts in synaptic weights during stimulus encoding cause the network to act as a matched filter”
mechanismFeedforward connections between similarly tuned cells relay brief bursts of spiking along a chain, storing information without any single cell firing continuously — “This connectivity enables the network to store information through brief periods of above-baseline spiking that are passed between cells”
mechanismLong-range input (from thalamus) is required for local cortical circuits to sustain delay activity at all — “local circuit mechanisms, which have long been implicated in delay activity, may be insufficient to sustain delay activity in the absence of long-range input”

findingStable firing across the delay is the exception rather than the rule: in one lateral prefrontal cortex study only 3% of delay-active neurons maintained a constant spike rate for the whole delay. — “one study of lPFC estimated that only 3% of delay-active neurons spike at a stable rate throughout the delay”
findingGAMMA BURSTS WERE ASSOCIATED WITH INCREASED INFORMATION OR MORE ITEMS STORED IN WM, WHEREAS BETA BURSTS SEEMED TO INHIBIT GAMMA BURSTS AND REDUCED THE INFORMATION CONTENT OF SPIKING ACTIVITY — “gamma bursts were associated with increased information or more items stored in WM, whereas beta bursts seemed to inhibit gamma bursts and reduced the information content of spiking activity”
findingCortical delay activity depends causally on long-range thalamic input: optogenetically silencing thalamic delay activity in mice disrupted frontal delay activity and impaired delayed-discrimination behaviour. — “Photoinhibition of thalamic delay activity disrupted delay activity in frontal regions”
findingThalamic delay activity is not a copy of the cortical signal — it is less content-selective than frontal delay activity yet causally sets frontal selectivity. — “thalamic delay activity was less content-selective than frontal delay activity and causally modulated the selectivity”
findingPOPULATION ANALYSES OF fMRI DATA HAVE REVEALED SUSTAINED REPRESENTATIONS OF WM CONTENT IN THE PATTERN OF ACTIVITY OF VOXELS DISTRIBUTED WITHIN PARIETAL AND SENSORY CORTICES, EVEN WHEN THE MEAN BOLD SIGNAL DOES NOT DIFFER FROM BASELINE ACROSS THE DELAY — “population analyses of fMRI data have revealed sustained representations of WM content in the pattern of activity of voxels distributed within parietal and sensory cortices, even when the mean BOLD signal does not differ from baseline across the delay”
findingWhich region shows delay activity depends on the animal's strategy: a prospective motor strategy raised prefrontal delay activity while a retrospective sensory strategy raised it in posterior cortex. — “a prospective motor-based strategy increased PFC delay activity, whereas a retrospective, sensory-based strategy increased delay activity in posterior regions”
findingA continuous-attractor prediction was confirmed in monkey lPFC: how far population delay spiking drifted predicted how large the behavioural error was. — “drifts in population delay spiking predicted the degree of error in behavioural responses”
findingBlocking prefrontal NMDA receptors abolishes delay spiking in non-human primates, confirming the modelled role of slow NMDAR excitation in sustaining persistent activity. — “blockade or antagonism of prefrontal NMDARs eliminated delay spiking in NHPs”
findingNo single biophysical model reproduces every property of delay activity, and most empirical results are equally compatible with several model classes. — “The story that emerges is that no single model can account for all features of delay activity and that many results can be explained by multiple classes of models”
