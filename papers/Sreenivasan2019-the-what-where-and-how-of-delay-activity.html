mechanismStructured recurrent excitation with tuned excitation–inhibition balance lets network spiking settle into a self-sustaining attractor state that keeps the memory alive without input— “When the connectivity between neurons in the network is properly structured, and when the balance between excitation and inhibition is well-tuned, spiking dynamics settle into a stable attractor state in the absence of any input”
mechanismSlow NMDA-receptor excitation stabilises attractor networks so that persistent spiking can be maintained over seconds— “The relatively slow excitatory dynamics of NMDAR activation can provide a stabilizing influence on attractor networks, allowing these networks to sustain a representation through persistent spiking over several seconds”
mechanismEncoding-driven changes in synaptic weights turn the network into a matched filter, so the memory is stored silently and revealed when noisy input is fed through it— “shifts in synaptic weights during stimulus encoding cause the network to act as a matched filter”
mechanismFeedforward connections between similarly tuned cells relay brief bursts of spiking along a chain, storing information without any single cell firing continuously— “This connectivity enables the network to store information through brief periods of above-baseline spiking that are passed between cells”
mechanismLong-range input (from thalamus) is required for local cortical circuits to sustain delay activity at all— “local circuit mechanisms, which have long been implicated in delay activity, may be insufficient to sustain delay activity in the absence of long-range input”
