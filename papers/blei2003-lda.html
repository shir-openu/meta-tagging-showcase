limitationThe bag-of-words (exchangeability) assumption can split words that belong to a single phrase across different topics. — “In particular, the bag-of-words assumption allows words that should be generated by the same topic (e.g., “William Randolph Hearst Foundation”) to be allocated to several different topics.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
limitationThe text-classification results are presented as preliminary and needing further support. — “Although these results need further substantiation, they suggest that the topic-based representation provided by LDA may be useful as a fast filtering algorithm for feature selection in text classification.”
limitationExact posterior inference in LDA cannot be done; only approximate inference is available. — “Exact inference is intractable for LDA, but any of a large suite of approximate inference algorithms can be used for inference and parameter estimation within the LDA framework.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
limitationThe authors disclaim any epistemological claim about the variables they call topics. — “We refer to the latent multinomial variables in the LDA model as topics, so as to exploit text-oriented intuitions, but we make no epistemological claims regarding these latent variables beyond their utility in representing probability distributions on sets of words”
limitationThe text vocabulary is stated to be an aid to intuition, and the model is not tied to text. — “We use the language of text collections throughout the paper, referring to entities such as “words,” “documents,” and “corpora.” This is useful in that it helps to guide intuition, particularly when we introduce latent variables which aim to capture abstract notions such as topics. It is important to note, however, that the LDA model is not necessarily tied to text”
limitationDocuments as well as words are assumed exchangeable, which the authors note is less often stated. — “All of these methods are based on the “bag-of-words” assumption—that the order of words in a document can be neglected. In the language of probability theory, this is an assumption of exchangeability for the words in a document (Aldous, 1985). Moreover, although less often stated formally, these methods also assume that documents are exchangeable; the specific ordering of the documents in a corpus can also be neglected”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingLDA is a generative probabilistic (three-level hierarchical Bayesian) model for text corpora: each document is a finite mixture over latent topics, each topic a distribution over words — giving an explicit topic-based representation of documents. — “latent Dirichlet allocation (LDA), a generative probabilistic model for collections of discrete data such as text corpora. LDA is a three-level hierarchical Bayesian model, in which each item of a collection is modeled as a finite mixture over an underlying set of topics.”
findingEfficient approximate inference via variational methods + EM; LDA outperforms mixture-of-unigrams and probabilistic LSI on document modeling, text classification, and collaborative filtering. — “We present efficient approximate inference techniques based on variational methods and an EM algorithm for empirical Bayes parameter estimation. We report results in document modeling, text classification, and collaborative filtering, comparing to a mixture of unigrams model and the probabilistic LSI model.”
