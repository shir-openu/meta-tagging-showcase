phenomenonrepetition priming between pictures and printed words
claim-typeexistence
mechanismRepetition priming arises because processing an item changes the system, and the change facilitates later identification in proportion to how many of the same processes are re-invoked at test. — “The firstof these assumptions is thatrepetition priming reflects changes in the system resulting from processing of an item, changes that facilitate subsequent identification to the extentthat the sameset of processes are invoked dur ing the studyand test presentations.”
mechanismPictures prime words because task demands lead subjects to treat the drawings as linguistic objects, engaging word-structure processes even without alphabetic input. — “If the task demands encourage people to invoke processes that reflect word structure, then that is what peo ple will do, even when no alphabetic information is present.”
mechanismVariability in relative priming is produced by sense (nuance) matching: priming is complete only when the same sense is accessed at study and test. — “The proposition is, in brief, that repetition priming is sense-specific, and that sense dominance is mode- and, to a lesser extent, modality-specific.”
relation — “This outcome is inconsistent with several accounts of the relationship between word and picture identification processes (e. g., Durso & Johnson, 1979; Morton, 1984).”
relation — “Given the dis crepancy between the results of the repetition priming ex periments reported here and those reported by Scar borough et al. (1979, Experiments 1 and 2), we designed the fifth experiment as a replication of their episodic ex periment.”
replicationcontested
limitationEqual relative-priming values do not entail that the same underlying processes are involved in each stimulus-class combination. — “It should be noted, of course, that the equivalence of two RP values does not logically demand the conclusion that the same processes are involved in each case.”
limitationThe strategy-insensitivity result rests on a single dissociation and inherits its inferential weakness. — “This result is, of course, subject to the inferential limita tions associated with single dissociation.”
limitationThe account cannot explain the observed variability in relative priming across experiments and experimenters. — “Perhaps the weakest link in our analysis involves the observed variability in RP.”
limitationThe estimated picture-word priming value may hold only when pictures are treated as linguistic objects; treated as pictures, priming may vanish. — “We accept the possibility, then, that when pictures are treated as pic tures, RP will be reduced or eliminated.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingPrior exposure to a line drawing does facilitate later identification of the corresponding printed word, contradicting the claim that picture-to-word repetition transfer is absent. — “The most important result from the series of experi ments reported here is that repetition priming is observed when a test word is preceded by a line drawing of the same concept.”
findingIntermodal (picture-word) priming is real but smaller in magnitude than intramodal (word-word) priming. — “Second, the magnitude of repetition priming under intermodal con ditions is less than that observed under intramodal condi tions, an outcome which is consistent with the modality data (Experiments 1 and 4).”
findingThe size of picture-to-word priming is unaffected by whether subjects encoded the pictures by naming or by referential size judgment, i.e. it is insensitive to study-phase strategy. — “Third, the magnitude of prim ing under intermodal conditions does not vary according to whether subjects study the initial pictures with size judg ment or naming (Experiment 2).”
findingWord frequency modulates only the intermodal component of priming; the extra facilitation attributable to intramodal repetition is frequency-independent. — “Fourth, when repe tition effects for low-frequency and high-frequency words are compared, when the prime is either a picture or a word, there is a frequency difference for intermodal prim ing, but the additional priming attributable to intramodal effects does not vary with frequency (Experiments 3 and 4).”
findingIn episodic recognition the ordering of conditions reverses, with picture-word better than word-word, the opposite of the perceptual identification result. — “Fifth, when episodic recognition is tested, the rela tionship between the intra- and intermodal conditions is reversed, and the picture-word treatment is superior in regard to speed and accuracy (Experiment 5).”
