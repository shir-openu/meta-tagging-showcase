phenomenonbackward masking of figure–ground segregation— “In a backward masking paradigm, a target stimulus is rapidly (<100 msec) followed by a second stimulus.”
claim-typemechanism— “As figure – ground modulations depend on feedback from extrastriate areas, these results suggest that masking selectively interrupts the recurrent interactions between V1 and higher visual areas.”
method — evidence— “Meanwhile, we recorded neural activity from neurons in V1 (Figure 2).”
mechanismThe mask replaces the target's information at the low level by the time descending signals arrive, so the recurrent V1-extrastriate loop that generates figure-ground modulation is cut off while the feedforward sweep is left intact.— “As figure – ground modulations depend on feedback from extrastriate areas, these results suggest that masking selectively interrupts the recurrent interactions between V1 and higher visual areas.”
mechanismPerception proceeds in stages: a feedforward sweep first analyses elementary features, then recurrent interactions between V1 and higher areas bind them into a coherent percept, and only when that recurrent activity spreads into executive areas can a conscious report be made.— “Early on, feedforward processing analyzes the scene in terms of its elementary features. Next, recurrent processing, involving widespread interactions between visual areas including V1 and higher areas, lays the foundations for a coherent percept of the”
mechanismFigure-ground modulation is necessary but not sufficient for report, because an independent decision stage is interposed between the V1 modulation and the animal's behavioural response.— “When there is no modulation, there is no perception. However, when there is modulation, this does not in itself guarantee that the animal ‘‘reports’’ to have seen the stimulus. A decision process sits between the modulation and the animals report, which is independent of the modulation.”
replicates Lamme, Supèr, Landman, Roelfsema, & Spekreijse (2000)— “This dual finding corroborates our earlier suggestion that within V1, different regimes of activity seem to coexist (Lamme, Supe`r, Landman, Roelfsema, & Spekreijse, 2000).”
replicates Supèr et al. (2001)— “This confirms our earlier report on the role of figure – ground activity in visual perception (Supe`r et al., 2001): There, we found that the presence or absence of modulation determines whether perception will occur.”
sensethe masking effect - SELECTIVE, AND BOTH HALVES ARE IN ONE SENTENCE: figure-ground signals are fully suppressed at exactly the intervals that make the target invisible, while the initial response transients are not affected, so 'masking reduces responses in V1' names one of two opposite results— “in a pattern backward masking paradigm. We show that the V1 figure – ground signals are selectively and fully suppressed at target – mask intervals that psychophysically result in the target being invisible. Initial response transients, signalling the features that make up the scene, are not affected”
sensethe conclusion - HELD GIVEN AN IMPORTED PREMISE: the measurement is a suppression in V1 and the conclusion is about recurrent interactions with higher areas, reached because figure-ground modulations are taken to depend on feedback from extrastriate areas, and offered as a suggestion— “As figure – ground modulations depend on feedback from extrastriate areas, these results suggest that masking selectively interrupts the recurrent interactions between V1 and higher visual areas”
