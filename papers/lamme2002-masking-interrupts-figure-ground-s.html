phenomenonbackward masking of figure–ground segregation
claim-typemechanism
mechanismThe mask replaces the target's information at the low level by the time descending signals arrive, so the recurrent V1-extrastriate loop that generates figure-ground modulation is cut off while the feedforward sweep is left intact. — “As figure – ground modulations depend on feedback from extrastriate areas, these results suggest that masking selectively interrupts the recurrent interactions between V1 and higher visual areas.”
mechanismPerception proceeds in stages: a feedforward sweep first analyses elementary features, then recurrent interactions between V1 and higher areas bind them into a coherent percept, and only when that recurrent activity spreads into executive areas can a conscious report be made. — “Early on, feedforward processing analyzes the scene in terms of its elementary features. Next, recurrent processing, involving widespread interactions between visual areas including V1 and higher areas, lays the foundations for a coherent percept of the”
mechanismFigure-ground modulation is necessary but not sufficient for report, because an independent decision stage is interposed between the V1 modulation and the animal's behavioural response. — “When there is no modulation, there is no perception. However, when there is modulation, this does not in itself guarantee that the animal ‘‘reports’’ to have seen the stimulus. A decision process sits between the modulation and the animals report, which is independent of the modulation.”
relation — “This dual finding corroborates our earlier suggestion that within V1, different regimes of activity seem to coexist (Lamme, Supe`r, Landman, Roelfsema, & Spekreijse, 2000).”
relation — “This confirms our earlier report on the role of figure – ground activity in visual perception (Supe`r et al., 2001): There, we found that the presence or absence of modulation determines whether perception will occur.”
effect directionbackward masking → figure – ground signals — eliminates — “selectively and fully suppressed”
holds whenbackward masking → figure – ground signals — at the target-mask intervals that make the target psychophysically invisible — “at target – mask intervals that psychophysically result in the target being invisible”
effect directionbackward masking → initial response transients — no-change — “are not affected”
replicationuntested
limitationOrientation selectivity at the multiunit recording sites was weak, limiting the sensitivity of the feature-signal measure. — “Orientation selectivity at these ‘‘multiunit’’ sites is not very strong.”
limitationThe texture orientation comparison used only two fixed orientations, which further weakened the measured selectivity. — “For the orientation of texture elements, the selectivity is even less pronounced, in part because there were only two orientations, fixed at 458 and 1358, to compare.”
limitationThe conclusion is one inferential step from the measurement, and the premise is stated. — “As figure – ground modulations depend on feedback from extrastriate areas, these results suggest that masking selectively interrupts the recurrent interactions between V1 and higher visual areas”
limitationThe effect is selective: one signal is fully suppressed and another is unaffected. — “in a pattern backward masking paradigm. We show that the V1 figure – ground signals are selectively and fully suppressed at target – mask intervals that psychophysically result in the target being invisible. Initial response transients, signalling the features that make up the scene, are not affected”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingBackward masking abolishes the figure-ground signal in V1 exactly at those target-mask intervals at which the monkey cannot see the figure. — “We show that the V1 figure – ground signals are selectively and fully suppressed at target – mask intervals that psychophysically result in the target being invisible.”
findingThe early transient V1 response that codes the local features of the scene survives the mask untouched. — “Initial response transients, signalling the features that make up the scene, are not affected.”
findingWith a texture pattern mask at short SOAs the monkeys performed at chance in locating the figure, confirming that the masks made the figure invisible. — “When the figure – ground stimuli were followed by the texture pattern masks, at short SOAs, the monkeys were at chance in detecting the position of the figures.”
findingWhen the same brief displays were followed by a blank screen instead of a mask, figure-ground modulation remained present even at the shortest SOAs, so the suppression is caused by the mask and not by the short presentation. — “We found that figure – ground signals are still present at very short SOAs (Figure 5 top).”
findingOrientation selectivity for the texture elements was essentially unaffected by backward masking at every SOA but the shortest. — “Orientation selectivity for texture elements (Figure 6 bottom) is hardly affected by backward masking (except at an SOA of 1 frame).”
findingOrientation information about the line segments remains available in V1 even at the SOAs at which the animals see nothing. — “Our results show that information about the orientation of the line segments is available in V1 at even the shortest SOAs (2 and 3 frames).”
findingThe neurometric curve of figure-ground modulation tracks the behavioural psychometric curve far better (error 0.033) than the neurometric curve of orientation selectivity does (error 0.138), which rules out a purely feedforward account of figure-ground segregation. — “we found a much worse correspondence (mean squared error = 0.138) than when the same psychometric curve is compared with the neurometric curve of figure – ground modulation (where the error is 0.033, Figure 4 bottom).”
findingTrial by trial, the amount of figure-ground modulation did not predict whether the monkey reported seeing the figure. — “We analyzed our data to determine whether there was a trial by trial relation between the monkeys report on the visibility of the figure (indicated by the eye movement) and the amount of figure – ground modulation (in the neural data). We did not find any significant effect (data not shown).”
findingDetection of the figure occurs only when the stimulus also drives recurrent processing between V1 and higher areas, expressed as figure-ground modulation of V1 activity. — “Only when the stimulus also engages recurrent processing between high and low cortical areas, which results in figure – ground modulation of V1 activity, the animal is able to detect it.”
quantity0.033 — mean squared difference between the figure-ground neurometric curve and the behavioural psychometric curve — “The mean squared difference between these two measures is 0.033.”
quantity0.138 — mean squared error between the orientation-selectivity neurometric curve and the same psychometric curve, i.e. the much poorer feedforward fit — “we found a much worse correspondence (mean squared error = 0.138) than when the same psychometric curve is compared with the neurometric curve of figure – ground modulation (where the error is 0.033, Figure 4 bottom).”
quantityp < .025 — significance threshold at which figure-ground modulation was present only at SOAs of 5-8 frames under pattern masking — “Significant modulation ( p < .025) is only found at SOAs of 5, 6, 7, and 8 frames (two-sided t test with the electrodes as a group, t statistics for the 1- to 8-frame conditions: 0.40, 0.13, 0.44, 2.23, 5.21, 4.96, 5.96, 3.17, respectively).”
quantity25 of these 32 electrodes — number of implanted V1 recording channels contributing data to this study — “For the present experiments, we recorded from 25 of these 32 electrodes in total, selected on the basis of RF position and the presence of contextual modulation.”
quantity700 (total 11,000) — trials collected per SOA/mask condition, and in total, for the neural analysis — “about 700 trials (total 11,000) of neural data were collected”
quantity1.94 — median orientation-selectivity ratio of the multiunit recording sites — “The median orientation selectivity ratio (average response level while a bar of optimal orientation moved over the RF divided by average response for least effective orientation) was 1.94 (mean = 2.19, range = 1.16 – 9.03).”
quantity14 – 110 msec — range of stimulus-onset asynchronies (1 to 8 monitor frames) between figure-ground display and mask — “These displays were shown for 1 to 8 monitor frames (14 – 110 msec) and were then followed by a texture pattern mask.”
quantity100 msec — latency at which V1 responses start to distinguish figure from background texture — “responses to the figure texture are stronger than to background texture, starting at a latency of 100 msec, indicating that at this latency, neurons signal figure – ground relationships.”
quantity0.18 — maximum figure-ground modulation, equated to 100% correct when building the neurometric curve — “we took the maximum amount of modulation (0.18) and equated this to 100% correct performance”

definitionbackward masking — “When a visual stimulus is rapidly followed by another one, the visibility of the first stimulus may be greatly reduced, depending on factors like relative timing (stimulus onset asynchrony [SOA]), similarity, and spatial position of the two stimuli (Enns & DiLollo, 2000; Breitmeyer, 1984). This phenomenon is known as backward masking.”
definitionrecurrent (reentrant) processing — “processing, information flows from low to high levels, and then back again to the low levels (and this may occur several times).”
