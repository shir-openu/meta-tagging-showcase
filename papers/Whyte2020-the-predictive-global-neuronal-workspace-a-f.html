phenomenonvisual consciousness (conscious access)
claim-typemechanism
mechanismConscious access happens because a temporally deep level of the hierarchy infers and integrates lower-level states over a slow enough timescale to contextualise them and drive extended report behaviour. — “The core notion underlying our model is that conscious access is a fundamentally inferential process that can only occur at a level of processing that is sufficiently temporally deep to integrate information from lower levels of the hierarchy and contextualise processing at these lower levels.”
mechanismLate ERPs arise because the ERP is the time derivative of the depolarisation variable, so a rapid change in high-level belief produces a large late deflection. — “ERPs and local field potentials are modelled as the time derivative (rate of change) of the depolarisation variable because the change in membrane potential over neuronal populations is what generates ERPs.”
mechanismRapid belief change increases the magnitude of the depolarisation derivative, which is what makes a late component appear. — “When confidence in a state changes rapidly, the derivative of the depolarisation variable increases in magnitude.”
mechanismExpectations act by raising second-level posterior confidence, which magnifies top-down feedback to sensory populations and pushes marginal trials past the ignition threshold. — “Consistent expectations increase second-level posterior confidence to a sufficiently large degree to shift otherwise unconscious trials over the threshold for “ignition” by magnifying the feedback to the first level”
relation — “Here we aim to make progress in overcoming these limitations by advancing an extension of the global neuronal workspace - the predictive global neuronal workspace (PGNW) - that unifies essential aspects of the GNW with the more recent (Bayesian) Active Inference approach to understanding brain function.”
relation — “Formalising ideas first introduced by Hohwy (2013), Whyte (2019), and Friston et al (2012), we will argue that conscious access or “ignition” is a fundamentally inferential process that depends upon a level of processing of sufficient temporal depth to contextualise and coordinate lower levels of processing.”
relation — “Although this is ultimately an empirical question, we regard the explicit representation of the absence of a stimulus in the perceptual state space to be a somewhat unrealistic idealisation.”
differs intask relevance → conscious access — which of the two the P3 tracks — “the P3 is related to task relevance and is not a necessary signature of conscious access”

limitationScope deliberately restricted to settings where visual consciousness is operationalised by report. — “To make the PGNW testable, we have deliberately limited the scope of the model to experimental settings where visual consciousness is operationalised via report.”
limitationReport paradigms themselves carry known methodological problems. — “However, we acknowledge that report paradigms come with methodological difficulties (Tsuchiya et al., 2015).”
limitationRestricting the model to vision perpetuates the visual bias of consciousness science. — “By the same token, in limiting the scope of the PGNW to the visual modality, we are aware that we are reifying the pervasive bias in consciousness science of primarily studying vision.”
limitationThe match to the four-way taxonomy is partly built in, since parameters were tuned to the published behavioural results. — “This should not be a surprise, as we finetuned the parameter values to capture behaviour reported in the empirical literature.”
limitationThe model cannot represent partial awareness, because information cannot skip hierarchical levels in it. — “This requires information to skip levels of the hierarchy, which is also not possible in the present model.”
limitationSimulated report frequencies are only directional, not quantitative, predictions. — “Finally, it must be highlighted that the behavioural results of the following simulations should be interpreted as directional hypotheses, as opposed to precise predictions about the percentage of seen vs unseen trials.”
limitationTwo predictions of the theory this paper extends are stated to have been falsified. — “Of greater concern is the fact that, as experimental paradigms have become more sophisticated, two predictions of the original theory have been falsified”
limitationThe falsifying result is qualified inline by whether its instrument is a fair proxy. — “Together these results demonstrate that, contrary to the original formulation of the GNW, information is processed and unconsciously maintained (at least briefly and insofar as multivariate decoding is a defensible proxy for information processing) by the same structures implicated in conscious processing”
limitationThe P3 is reported to track task relevance rather than conscious access. — “This result was recently replicated in a standard masking paradigm (Cohen, et al., 2020) showing unequivocally that the P3 is related to task relevance and is not a necessary signature of conscious access”
limitationA marker was abandoned without any accompanying theoretical revision, which the authors name as a degenerative pattern. — “In addition, the abandonment of the P3 as a signature of conscious access was not accompanied by any theoretical revisions and makes no additional behavioural or neurobiological predictions. This situates the GNW in a tenuous position, in which revision primarily explains away contrary results – a recognized characteristic of degenerative research programs”
limitationThe replacement model offered for the falsified one is stated to be too idealised for neurobiological predictions. — “However, the modified computational model of subjective report (King & Dehaene, 2014) is too idealised to make neurobiological predictions”
limitationWhere phenomenology sits in the model was chosen to avoid an empirically unfalsifiable consequence. — “i.e., the presence of a particular phenomenology would become empirically unfalsifiable). To avoid this uncomfortable conclusion, while also keeping sensory phenomenology at the correct timescale, we suggest that phenomenology in our model is most plausibly situated at the point during which (and based on the manner in which) lower-level representations update the content of higher-level states”
limitationA parameter that empirical work fits to data was fixed by assumption, and it affects the predicted ERPs. — “there is an optional parameter (mdp.erp) that encodes the assumed degree of decay or attenuation in posterior beliefs across timesteps (i.e., before each period of gradient descent following each new observation). In empirical work, this parameter needs to be fit to neuronal or behavioural responses. Here we do not include this parameter (i.e., we set it to a value of 1), which entails that posterior beliefs at one timepoint fully carry over as priors for the subsequent timepoint in a trial. This is analogous to predictive coding formulations and is most appropriate for our simulations due to the fact that multiple timepoints in our simulated trials are treated as single observations (i.e., there should be no attenuation or ‘resetting’ of priors within a single stimulus presentation). It is important to note this here, however, as predicted ERPs in simulations can be affected by different values of this parameter”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingWith one consistent parameter set the simulated agent reproduced the GNW four-way taxonomy behaviourally: weak signal plus absent attention gave chance forced-choice performance and almost no 'seen' reports. — “When signal strength was low and attention absent, the agent displayed chance levels of forced-choice performance (47%) and only reported having seen the square on 10% of trials.”
findingSimulated first-level ERPs, corresponding to early components such as P100 and N100, barely changed with visibility. — “Here we found that ERPs at the first level of the model – which correspond to early components such as the P100 and N100 – are relatively unaffected by changes in visibility.”
findingSimulated second-level ERPs, corresponding to the late P3, were strongly modulated by visibility, matching the empirical early-versus-late ERP dissociation. — “In contrast, ERPs at the second level – which correspond to late components, and specifically the P3 – appear to be strongly modulated by changes in visibility.”
findingThe model reproduced the Pitts inattentional-blindness dissociation: only in phase three, when the square was both seen and task-relevant, did a large late P3-like ERP appear. — “Finally, in phase three, when the model was attending to the internal stimulus, the square was reported on 100% of trials and there was a large and late ERP at the second level resembling the P3.”
findingThe model yields the testable prediction that a late ERP accompanies conscious access only when high-level confidence shifts quickly, so awareness without a P3 is possible. — “it leads to a straightforward prediction; visual consciousness will be accompanied by a late ERP whenever confidence in a particular state at a high level of the hierarchy changes rapidly.”
findingAdding prior expectation as a third factor, expectations consistent with the stimulus raised both forced-choice accuracy and the rate of 'seen' reports in every quadrant of the taxonomy. — “Across all taxonomy conditions, we found that consistent prior expectations increased both the accuracy of forced-choice behaviour and the percentage of trials where the model reported having seen the stimulus”
findingWith a strong attended stimulus, a consistent prior shrank the simulated P3 while an inconsistent prior enlarged it relative to neutral priors. — “the amplitude of ERPs at the second level of the model, corresponding to the P3, were dampened by a consistent prior and boosted by an inconsistent prior relative to neutral priors.”
findingThe model reconciles univariate ignition results with multivariate decoding of unseen stimuli, because above-baseline firing persists on unconscious trials and would be readable by a classifier even though mean rates are lower. — “although firing rates are higher for conscious compared to unconscious conditions, there is still a greater than baseline firing rate in unconscious conditions at both the firstand second-level within our simulations that could easily be exploited by a classifier.”

definitionglobal workspace theory (GWT) — “GWT was first proposed by Baars (1988) as a cognitive architecture that identifies consciousness with the global availability of information.”
definitionglobal neuronal workspace (GNW) model — “Dehaene and colleagues have advanced a global neuronal workspace (GNW) model, which identifies the global workspace with a large-scale network of excitatory pyramidal neurons with long-range axonal pathways connecting prefrontal and parietal cortices”
definitionignition — “The key working hypothesis of the GNW is that when a stimulus becomes conscious there will be a late, non-linear, all-ornothing “ignition” of prefrontal and parietal regions”
definitionaccess consciousness — “this paper is chiefly concerned with what Block (2005) terms “access consciousness” which is defined as the availability of information for verbal report, voluntary action, and executive processing.”
definitionpredictive global neuronal workspace (PGNW) — “Here we aim to make progress in overcoming these limitations by advancing an extension of the global neuronal workspace - the predictive global neuronal workspace (PGNW) - that unifies essential aspects of the GNW with the more recent (Bayesian) Active Inference approach to understanding brain function.”
definitionActive Inference — “Active Inference, a corollary of the free energy principle (FEP), is a first principles approach to modelling (approximately) Bayes optimal behaviour”
definitionpartially observable Markov decision process (POMDP) — “POMDPs model discrete transitions between latent variables and the observations they generate.”
