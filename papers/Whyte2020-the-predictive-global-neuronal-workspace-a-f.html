phenomenonvisual consciousness (conscious access)— “However, this paper is chiefly concerned with what Block (2005) terms “access consciousness” which is defined as the availability of information for verbal report, voluntary action, and executive processing.”

method — evidence— “We report simulations demonstrating the model’s ability to reproduce: 1) the electrophysiological and behaviour results observed in previous studies of inattentional blindness; and 2) the previously introduced four-way taxonomy predicted by the GNW, which describes the relationship between consciousness, attention, and sensory signal…
transferfrom the free energy principle / Active Inference: the Active Inference formalism used to cast global neuronal workspace dynamics as approximate Bayesian inference, yielding testable behavioural and neural predictions— “Active Inference, a corollary of the free energy principle (FEP), is a first principles approach to modelling (approximately) Bayes optimal behaviour (Friston; 2010; Friston et al, 2016, 2017a).”
mechanismConscious access happens because a temporally deep level of the hierarchy infers and integrates lower-level states over a slow enough timescale to contextualise them and drive extended report behaviour.— “The core notion underlying our model is that conscious access is a fundamentally inferential process that can only occur at a level of processing that is sufficiently temporally deep to integrate information from lower levels of the hierarchy and contextualise processing at these lower levels.”
mechanismLate ERPs arise because the ERP is the time derivative of the depolarisation variable, so a rapid change in high-level belief produces a large late deflection.— “ERPs and local field potentials are modelled as the time derivative (rate of change) of the depolarisation variable because the change in membrane potential over neuronal populations is what generates ERPs.”
mechanismRapid belief change increases the magnitude of the depolarisation derivative, which is what makes a late component appear.— “When confidence in a state changes rapidly, the derivative of the depolarisation variable increases in magnitude.”
mechanismExpectations act by raising second-level posterior confidence, which magnifies top-down feedback to sensory populations and pushes marginal trials past the ignition threshold.— “Consistent expectations increase second-level posterior confidence to a sufficiently large degree to shift otherwise unconscious trials over the threshold for “ignition” by magnifying the feedback to the first level”
extends the global neuronal workspace (GNW) model— “Here we aim to make progress in overcoming these limitations by advancing an extension of the global neuronal workspace - the predictive global neuronal workspace (PGNW) - that unifies essential aspects of the GNW with the more recent (Bayesian) Active Inference approach to understanding brain function.”
builds-on Hohwy (2013), Whyte (2019), and Friston et al (2012)— “Formalising ideas first introduced by Hohwy (2013), Whyte (2019), and Friston et al (2012), we will argue that conscious access or “ignition” is a fundamentally inferential process that depends upon a level of processing of sufficient temporal depth to contextualise and coordinate lower levels of processing.”

findingWith one consistent parameter set the simulated agent reproduced the GNW four-way taxonomy behaviourally: weak signal plus absent attention gave chance forced-choice performance and almost no 'seen' reports.— “When signal strength was low and attention absent, the agent displayed chance levels of forced-choice performance (47%) and only reported having seen the square on 10% of trials.”
findingSimulated first-level ERPs, corresponding to early components such as P100 and N100, barely changed with visibility.— “Here we found that ERPs at the first level of the model – which correspond to early components such as the P100 and N100 – are relatively unaffected by changes in visibility.”
findingSimulated second-level ERPs, corresponding to the late P3, were strongly modulated by visibility, matching the empirical early-versus-late ERP dissociation.— “In contrast, ERPs at the second level – which correspond to late components, and specifically the P3 – appear to be strongly modulated by changes in visibility.”
findingThe model reproduced the Pitts inattentional-blindness dissociation: only in phase three, when the square was both seen and task-relevant, did a large late P3-like ERP appear.— “Finally, in phase three, when the model was attending to the internal stimulus, the square was reported on 100% of trials and there was a large and late ERP at the second level resembling the P3.”
findingThe model yields the testable prediction that a late ERP accompanies conscious access only when high-level confidence shifts quickly, so awareness without a P3 is possible.— “it leads to a straightforward prediction; visual consciousness will be accompanied by a late ERP whenever confidence in a particular state at a high level of the hierarchy changes rapidly.”
findingAdding prior expectation as a third factor, expectations consistent with the stimulus raised both forced-choice accuracy and the rate of 'seen' reports in every quadrant of the taxonomy.— “Across all taxonomy conditions, we found that consistent prior expectations increased both the accuracy of forced-choice behaviour and the percentage of trials where the model reported having seen the stimulus”
findingWith a strong attended stimulus, a consistent prior shrank the simulated P3 while an inconsistent prior enlarged it relative to neutral priors.
findingThe model reconciles univariate ignition results with multivariate decoding of unseen stimuli, because above-baseline firing persists on unconscious trials and would be readable by a classifier even though mean rates are lower.
