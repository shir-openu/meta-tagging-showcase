While monolayer h-BNterm·dom has been synthesized on Pt and Cu foil using chemical vapour depositionterm·mth (CVDterm·mth), multilayer h-BNterm·dom is still absent. Here we use Fe foilterm·key and synthesize large-area multilayer h-BNterm·dom film by CVDterm·mth with a borazineterm·key precursor. In addition to metallic grapheneterm·key and semiconducting transition metal dichalcogenidesterm·dom, hexagonal boron nitrideterm·dom (h-BNterm·dom) is an insulating two-dimensional (2Dterm·dom) material, in which all these materials serve as primary components for soft electronics with high flexibility and transmittance 1 2 . Since all the atoms in 2Dterm·dom layer are exposed to the surface, the related physical and chemical properties are strongly influenced by adjacent materials and sometimes surface corrugation 3 4 . Therefore, a special care is required to deal with atomically thin-layered materials. h-BNterm·dom has several unique physical and chemical properties of its own, which has potential applications as a dry-lubricant 5 , passivation layer 6 and deep ultraviolet emitter 7 8 9 . The h-BNterm·dom layer has been recently demonstrated to be an ideal substrate for 2Dterm·dom materials due to its atomic flatness, large optical bandgap, superb mechanical strength, absence of dangling bonds and low dielectric screening 10 11 12 13 14 15 16 .

So far, tremendous efforts have been provided to large-area synthesis of 2Dterm·dom materials. For example, a meter-scale polycrystalline and centimetre-scale monocrystalline monolayer grapheneterm·key have been synthesized on copper foil 17 18 . A centimeter-scale monolayer transition metal dichalcogenideterm·dom (TMdCterm·dom) has been synthesized on an insulator or Au substrate 19 20 21 22 . Several methods including ultrahigh vacuum chemical vapour depositionterm·mth (CVDterm·mth) on various single-crystalline transition metals including Ni (111) 23 24 , Cu (111) 25 26 , Pt (111) 27 and Ag (111) 28 have been reported to achieve large-area monolayer h-BNterm·dom. More recently, more scalable synthesis techniques on polycrystalline Cu and Pt foils were used to obtain monolayer h-BNterm·dom by CVDterm·mth 11 29 30 31 . Monolayer h-BNterm·dom due to its ultrathin nature is very useful for a variety of applications, such as growth templates 32 , tunnelling barriers 33 34 and atomic membranes 35 . Yet, multilayer h-BNterm·dom is in general highly desired 3 36 37 38 for numerous real device applications, such as dielectric layers, atomically flat and dangling bond-free substrates or mechanical membranes.

For the synthesis of multilayer h-BNterm·dom, liquid–metal methodterm·mth 39 , solid-phase epitaxy growth 10 40 , direct growth in CVDterm·mth 13 and co-segregation method 41 have been reported. The liquid–metal methodterm·mth so far produces the highest crystallinity but the bulk crystals are produced, resulting in isolated small-size flakes of h-BNterm·dom after exfoliation 3 . Solid-phase epitaxial growthterm·mth involves the low-temperature deposition of precursors followed by a post-annealing process, which provides large-area h-BNterm·dom film with a controllable thickness; however, expensive single-crystalline substrates such as Ni (111) or Ru (0001) are required 10 40 . Other methods such as the direct growth with atmospheric pressure CVDterm·mth on copper foil 13 or the diffusion and co-segregation of boron and nitrogen atoms from a Fe–Ni alloy 41 leaves poor crystallinity of h-BNterm·dom, making it difficult to serve as a substrate for 2Dterm·dom materials. Therefore, improving the crystallinity of large-area h-BNterm·dom films on a cheap substrate is still required for electronic device applications.

Here we report large-area h-BNterm·dom films grown by CVDterm·mth using Fe foilterm·key. The thickness of the h-BNterm·dom (5–15 nm) is controlled by the cooling rateterm·dom, that is, the segregated boron and nitrogen atoms that are precipitated in Fe substrate at high temperature. X-ray diffraction measurements exclusively revealed the (0002), (0004) and (0006) peaks, indicating that the layers are well aligned perpendicular to the c axis. All these values are in good agreement with theoretical predictions, indicating the high crystalline quality of the grown multilayer h-BNterm·dom films. The reported grapheneterm·key mobility is the highest value among those of the previous reports with CVD-grown grapheneterm·key samples on CVD-grown h-BNterm·dom substrates.

A typical size of a single-crystalline grapheneterm·key domain is between 100 μm to a few hundred micrometres ( Supplementary Fig. 15 ). As a control reference, the same CVDterm·mth grapheneterm·key was also transferred onto a reference SiO 2 wafer. Multi-terminal devices were then fabricated from each sample to avoid any contact resistance artefacts in the measurements, as illustrated in Fig. 5a , showing boron (dark blue), nitrogen (light blue) and carbon (black). The right inset of Fig. 5a shows the optical image of an actual device. The typical channel width ( W ) and length ( L ) are 1.5 and 2 μm, respectively. Discussion Large-area and high-quality h-BNterm·dom films were obtained using CVDterm·mth on a Fe foilterm·key with a slow-cooling rate, which allows boron and nitrogen to diffuse out of the iron surface to form multilayer h-BNterm·dom. More detailed experimentsterm·mth with supporting theoretical calculations would help to elucidate the detailed growth mechanism in the future. The optical, mechanical and electrical properties of h-BNterm·dom film were characterized using several measurement techniques, indicating that the quality of h-BNterm·dom is almost similar to single-crystalline h-BNterm·dom. The CL spectra also proved that the h-BNterm·dom film can be applied for deep ultraviolet emitter application and for biological sensing applications. In addition, grapheneterm·key, MoS 2term·key and WSe 2term·key FETterm·dom devices on a h-BNterm·dom substrate were fabricated, resulting in carrier mobilities of grapheneterm·key, MoS 2term·key and WSe 2term·key to improve up to ∼24,000, 40 and 9 cm 2 V −1 s −1 , respectively, indicating the compatibility of our h-BNterm·dom substrate for 2Dterm·dom electronics. Furthermore, the effect of the h-BNterm·dom on the doping levels within both the grapheneterm·key and MoS 2term·key (or WSe 2term·key ) transistors suggests that the h-BNterm·dom can also be used for both substrate/work function engineering of 2Dterm·dom electronics, such as controlling the threshold voltages for complementary logic applications or improving the electrostatics/doping between ohmic interfaces. The large-area and high-quality h-BNterm·dom substrate in this work not only advances the high performance of 2Dterm·dom nanoelectronics for the future but also provides a new synthesis technique for potential multilayer 2Dterm·dom materials.

Methods Synthesis of multi- and monolayer materials Multilayer h-BNterm·dom was synthesized using low-pressure CVDterm·mth with a borazineterm·key precursor. The detailed CVDterm·mth set-up is described elsewhere 29 . Before synthesis of h-BNterm·dom, the Fe foilterm·key (Alfa Aesar) was annealed at 1,100 °C for 1 h to smoothen the Fe surface under a 10 s.c.c.m. hydrogen atmosphere. For h-BNterm·dom growth, borazineterm·key vapour and hydrogen with a rate of 0.1 and 10 s.c.c.m., respectively, were supplied at 1,100 °C for 30 min. After h-BNterm·dom growth, different cooling ratesterm·dom (slow: 5 °C min −1 , fast: 30 °C min −1 ) until 700 °C were applied. For grapheneterm·key growth, a two-step growth method was applied with low-pressure CVDterm·mth, which was described elsewhere 63 . Before grapheneterm·key growth, copper foil (Alfa Aesar) was annealed at 1,040 °C for 30 min under a 10 s.c.c.m. hydrogen atmosphere and then methane and hydrogen with a rate of 0.1 and 10 s.c.c.m., respectively, for 20 min was supplied to reduce the nucleation density, followed by increasing the methane flow rate to 2 s.c.c.m. while maintaining the same flow rate of hydrogen to form a continuous grapheneterm·key film. To grow monolayer MoS 2term·key and WSe 2term·key by CVDterm·mth, MoO 3 and S for MoS 2term·key and WO 3 and Se for WSe 2term·key were used as precursors. The growth of monolayers MoS 2term·key and WSe 2term·key was conducted at 750 °C for 10 min on a SiO 2 /Si substrate, respectively. The detailed methods were described elsewhere 19 20 .

Transfer of multi- and monolayer materials To transfer 2Dterm·dom materials onto a target substrate, the conventional poly(methyl methacrylate) (PMMA) transfer method was applied. h-BNterm·dom/Fe, grapheneterm·key/Cu and MoS 2term·key (or WSe 2term·key )/SiO 2 were spin coated with PMMA (C4, Micro Chem) at 2,500 r.p.m. for 1 min and then dried in the oven at 80 °C for 30 min. For a multilayer h-BNterm·dom film on Fe, the bubbling transfer method was applied 29 . The PMMA/h-BNterm·dom/Fe was immersed in an aqueous solution of 0.1 M NaOH and used as a cathode. PMMA/h-BNterm·dom was delaminated from the Fe foilterm·key by applying a voltage of 2 V. The PMMA/MoS 2term·key (or WSe 2term·key )/SiO 2 was immersed in 0.1 M KOH to detach the PMMA/MoS 2term·key (or WSe 2term·key ) film from the SiO 2 substrate. For grapheneterm·key, the conventional wet-etching transfer was used. Cu was etched by a copper etchant. After all PMMA/h-BNterm·dom, PMMA/grapheneterm·key and PMMA/MoS 2term·key (or WSe 2term·key ) films were rinsed with distilled water three times, the films were transferred onto the target substrate. The PMMA on 2Dterm·dom materials was removed by acetone and thermal annealing under forming gas at 450 °C. For a grapheneterm·key, MoS 2term·key or WSe 2term·key FETterm·dom device, the h-BNterm·dom film was first transferred, followed by transferring grapheneterm·key, MoS 2term·key or WSe 2term·key on the h-BNterm·dom substrate. For the cross-sectional TEMterm·mth measurements, the sample was prepared using a focus ion beam (SMI3050TB, SII).
