phenomenondiscrimination against protected attributes in supervised learning
claim-typemethod
mechanismA NAMED APPROACH IS SHOWN NOT TO DO WHAT ITS NAME PROMISES: `fairness through unawareness` - simply ignoring the protected attributes - is called INEFFECTIVE, because redundant encodings let the protected attribute be predicted from other features anyway. Removing the variable does not remove the information — “However, this idea of “fairness through unawareness” is ineffective due to the existence of redundant encodings, ways of predicting protected attributes from other features [PRT08].”
mechanismAN ESTABLISHED CRITERION IS SATISFIED BY EXACTLY THE BEHAVIOUR IT WAS MEANT TO FORBID: demographic parity PERMITS accepting qualified applicants in one group and UNQUALIFIED individuals in the other, so long as the acceptance percentages match - and the authors add that this can arise NATURALLY when one group has little or no training data. The criterion is not merely weak; it is passed by the discrimination it names — “Unfortunately, as was already argued by Dwork et al. [DHP+12], the notion is seriously flawed on two counts. First, it doesn’t ensure fairness. Indeed, the notion permits that we accept qualified applicants in the demographic A = 0, but unqualified individuals in A = 1, so long as the percentages of acceptance match. This behavior can arise naturally, when there is little or no training data available within A = 1.”
mechanismA CRITERION IS SHOWN TO FORBID THE GROUND TRUTH: demographic parity would not allow the ideal predictor that simply equals the true outcome - which, the authors say, CAN HARDLY BE CONSIDERED DISCRIMINATORY as it represents the actual outcome. The case arises whenever the target is correlated with the protected attribute — “Second, demographic parity often cripples the utility that we might hope to achieve. Just imagine the common scenario in which the target variable Y —whether an individual actually defaults or not—is correlated with A. Demographic parity would not allow the ideal predictor (cid:98)Y = Y , which can hardly be considered discriminatory as it represents the actual outcome.”
mechanismTHE NEW CRITERION IS ENGINEERED SO THE PERFECT PREDICTOR ALWAYS PASSES, AND SO THAT IT GETS EASIER AS ACCURACY RISES: the authors state that unlike demographic parity theirs always allows the perfectly accurate solution, and that the criterion is easier to achieve the more accurate the predictor is - explicitly ALIGNING fairness with the existing objective of supervised learning rather than trading against it — “Unlike demographic parity, our notion always allows for the perfectly accurate solution of (cid:98)Y = Y . More broadly, our criterion is easier to achieve the more accurate the predictor (cid:98)Y is, aligning fairness with the central goal in supervised learning of building more accurate predictors.”
mechanismTHE CRITERION PERMITS ONE ROUTE AND PROHIBITS ANOTHER BETWEEN THE SAME TWO VARIABLES: the prediction may depend on the protected attribute but ONLY THROUGH the target, so the definition ENCOURAGES features that predict the target directly and PROHIBITS abusing the protected attribute as a proxy for it. What the definition constrains is the PATH the dependence takes — “Unlike demographic parity, equalized odds allows (cid:98)Y to depend on A but only through the target variable Y . As such, the definition encourages the use of features that allow to directly predict Y , but prohibits abusing A as a proxy for Y .”
mechanismA PROPERTY IS DEFINED FOR PROPERTIES, NOT FOR PREDICTORS: `oblivious` is given a formal definition one level up - a PROPERTY of a predictor or score is oblivious if it depends only on the joint distribution of outcome, protected attribute and prediction. The definition's subject is other definitions — “A property of a predictor (cid:98)Y or score R is said to be oblivious if it only depends on the joint distribution of (Y , A, (cid:98)Y ) or (Y , A, R), respectively.”
mechanismTHE CRITERION IS RESTRICTED TO WHAT IS OBSERVABLE, AND THE RESTRICTION IS JUSTIFIED BY OBSERVABILITY RATHER THAN BY CORRECTNESS: in many cases only oblivious analysis is POSSIBLE, because the functional form of the score and the training data are not public - and going beyond it would require SUBJECTIVE INTERPRETATION or, as the paper prints it, `casual` assumptions about specific features (causal is meant), WHICH THE AUTHORS AIM TO AVOID. The definition is cut to fit what can be seen — “In many cases, only oblivious analysis is possible as the functional form of the score and underlying training data are not public. The only information about the score is the score itself, which can then be correlated with the target and protected attribute. Furthermore, even if the features or the functional form are available, going beyond oblivious analysis essentially requires subjective interpretation or casual assumptions about specific features, which we aim to avoid.”
mechanismA WEAKER CRITERION IS OBTAINED BY RESTRICTING THE STRONGER ONE TO ONE OUTCOME, AND WHICH OUTCOME IS AN INTERPRETIVE CHOICE: the authors say they OFTEN THINK OF one value as the ADVANTAGED outcome, then relax the requirement to hold only within that group, WITHOUT SPECIFYING ANY REQUIREMENT for those who will ultimately default - and name the result `equal opportunity`. The asymmetry that makes the weaker notion weaker is supplied by the reading of the outcome, not by the mathematics — “In the binary case, we often think of the outcome Y = 1 as the “advantaged” outcome, such as “not defaulting on a loan”, “admission to a college” or “receiving a promotion”. A possible relaxation of equalized odds is to require non-discrimination only within the “advantaged” outcome group. That is, to require that people who pay back their loan, have an equal opportunity of getting the loan in the first place (without specifying any requirement for those that will ultimately default). This leads to a relaxation of our notion that we call “equal opportunity”.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
mechanismTHE CRITERION IS PRESENTED IN TERMS OF WHERE IT PUTS THE COST: it is described as SHIFTING THE BURDEN OF UNCERTAINTY from the protected class to the decision maker, and thereby INCENTIVISING the collection of better features - ones depending more directly on the target than on the protected attribute - and of data allowing better prediction for all groups — “It can also be viewed as shifting the burden of uncertainty in classification from the protected class to the decision maker. In doing so, our notion helps to incentivize the collection of better features, that depend more directly on the target rather then the protected attribute, and of data that allows better prediction for all protected classes.”
mechanismTHE PAPER PROVES THE LIMIT OF ITS OWN ENTIRE FAMILY OF DEFINITIONS: a NON-IDENTIFIABILITY result shows that different dependency structures, WITH POSSIBLY DIFFERENT INTUITIVE NOTIONS OF FAIRNESS, cannot be separated by ANY oblivious notion or test - not merely by theirs. The contribution is a proof about what the class of measures cannot see — “We capture the inherent limitations of our approach, as well as any other oblivious approach, through a non-identifiability result showing that different dependency structures with possibly different intuitive notions of fairness cannot be separated based on any oblivious notion or test.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
mechanismTWO SITUATIONS THAT DIFFER IN EXACTLY THE WAY THE CONCEPT IS SUPPOSED TO TRACK ARE SHOWN TO BE INDISTINGUISHABLE: the authors construct two dependency structures which can have FUNDAMENTALLY DIFFERENT INTERPRETATIONS FROM THE POINT OF VIEW OF FAIRNESS and yet be indistinguishable from their joint distribution - so no oblivious test can resolve which applies — “We will argue that while these two scenarios can have fundamentally different interpretations from the point of view of fairness, they can be indistinguishable from their joint distribution. In particular, no oblivious test can resolve which of the two scenarios applies.”
mechanismACCESS TO THE FEATURES DOES NOT RESCUE IT - THE PAPER CLOSES ITS OWN LOOP: without ATTACHING MEANING to the features or making CAUSAL ASSUMPTIONS about them, no further insight into the two scores is gained. Subjective interpretation was excluded in order to make the criterion objective, and here the authors state that without it nothing more can be learned — “Without attaching meaning to the features or making causal assumptions about them, we do not gain any further insight on the two scores.”
mechanismA PROPERTY IS NAMED, GIVEN A USEFUL MEANING, AND THEN SHOWN NOT TO MEAN WHAT IT SOUNDS LIKE: having MATCHING conditional ROC curves does correspond to being deterministically correctable to non-discrimination - but the authors state plainly that it DOES NOT AT ALL mean the score is itself non-discriminatory, since it can be biased according to the protected attribute and need correcting — “But as our examples show, having matching ROC curves does not at all mean the score is itself non-discriminatory: it can be biased according to A, and a (deterministic) correction might be necessary in order to ensure equalized odds.”
mechanismA PROPERTY OFFERED AS A FAIRNESS MEASURE IS EQUIVALENT TO THE SCORE USING EVERYTHING THE PROTECTED ATTRIBUTE OFFERS: matching conditional frequencies means the score includes ALL POSSIBLE INFORMATION the protected attribute can provide about the target, so the score is IN A SENSE OPTIMALLY DEPENDENT on that attribute — “That is, the score R includes all possible information the protected attribute can provide on the target Y . Indeed having matching conditional frequencies means that the score is in a sense “optimally dependent” on the protected attribute A.”

limitationthe criterion is oblivious - it uses only joint statistics, not the interpretation of individual features — “it depends only on the joint statistics of the predictor, the target and the protected attribute, but not on interpretation of individual features”
limitationSatisfying the proposed oblivious criterion is not a proof of fairness (nor is violating it a proof of unfairness). — “Due to the limitations we described, satisfying our notion (or any other oblivious measure) should not be considered a conclusive proof of fairness.”
limitationThe criterion requires reliable observed outcome labels, which are not always available and may themselves be biased. — “That said, having access to reliable “labeled data” is not always possible.”
limitationThe near-optimality guarantee is relative to other constrained predictors only; the derived predictor need not be good in absolute terms. — “However, this does not mean that the predictor is necessarily good in an absolute sense.”
limitationNo test of this kind can tell two situations apart, and the authors prove it about their own class of test. — “we pause to consider to what extent “black box” oblivious tests like ours can identify discriminatory predictions. To shed light on this issue, we introduce two possible scenarios for the dependency structure of the score, the target and the protected attribute. We will argue that while these two scenarios can have fundamentally different interpretations from the point of view of fairness, they can be indistinguishable from their joint distribution. In particular, no oblivious test can resolve which of the two scenarios applies”
limitationAccess to the features does not rescue the test, without assumptions the authors decline to make. — “Without attaching meaning to the features or making causal assumptions about them, we do not gain any further insight on the two scores”
limitationMeeting the criterion can require deciding individual cases partly at random. — “This cannot be achieved with a single threshold for each group, but requires randomization”
limitationWho pays for the fairness is a property of the design, and the authors name the payer. — “The cost is thus born by the company using the classifier, which can decide to invest in better classification, rather than the classified group, which cannot”
