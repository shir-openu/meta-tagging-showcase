phenomenondisciplinary boundaries between the social sciences and the humanities
claim-typecritique
transferfrom philosophy (Wittgenstein's Tractatus logico-philosophicus): the metaphor of a net of a given mesh laid over a stained surface is taken over and developed into the paper's own model of how each discipline's theoretical production captures reality partially and differently — “Developing this metaphor, each intellectual construct whatever the origin would thus appear as a net whose mesh shape is different from another net or in other words, another discipline, another school of thought, another approach, etc.”
mechanisma new discipline seeking recognition models itself on the already-legitimate hard sciences and simultaneously demarcates itself from the humanities, so the split is produced by the pursuit of institutional legitimacy rather than by the objects studied — “sociology and social sciences in general tried to develop by being modelled on so-called hard sciences, and strove to attain the ideal efficiency and the already acquired social and institutional legitimacy of the latter; this effort represents a kind of entry test for new disciplines seeking recognition;”
mechanisminternal specialisation that is not accompanied by maintained exchange and dialogue turns into disjunction and research seal-off, which defeats the deeper knowledge of humans and society that the specialisation was meant to serve — “But articulating such processes such that they do not maintain necessary exchanges and dialogue often results in disjunctions and research seal-off dynamics compromising the social sciences initial project, that of a purported deeper knowledge of humans and society.”
relation — “In short, the French School of Sociology under Durkheim’s leadership in particular, made it an obligation to find for the science it wants to institute a specific object and method by asserting itself as an indefectible advocate of the explanatory method underpinned by "methodological naturalism" too often criticised (Simon 2008 : 347-348) and frankly too hastily too.”
relation — “So, as a "late comer among sciences" (Simon 2008 : 7), sociology certainly shares its area of study with other close disciplines trying however to appear as "a special viewpoint" (Tschannen 2004) on the foundations and characteristics of individuals’ lives as a group no matter the scale (whether micro-sociologic, mesosociologic, macro-sociologic).”
relation — “Also appearing in the study are this "hyper-complexity" (Morin 1994) and this frailty of human knowledge which call for more humility and openness especially toward the humanities, an attitude running contrary to the "facesaving pride" (Boudon 1984) that has prevailed in most recognised sociology and social sciences.”
differs inrelation — WHY THE DISCIPLINE NEEDED A METHOD AT ALL. The founding requirement is described as institutional rather than epistemic: the French School 'MADE IT AN OBLIGATION TO FIND FOR THE SCIENCE IT WANTS TO INSTITUTE A SPECIFIC OBJECT AND METHOD', which is a condition of BEING a science rather than a finding about its subject. The paper then defends the resulting methodological naturalism as 'TOO OFTEN CRITICISED ... AND FRANKLY TOO HASTILY TOO', so it disputes the criticism while accepting the account of the motive — “made it an obligation to find for the science it wants to institute a specific object and method”
differs inrelation — WHETHER THE DISTINCTION IS IN THE OBJECT OR IN THE STANCE. 'SOCIOLOGY CERTAINLY SHARES ITS AREA OF STUDY WITH OTHER CLOSE DISCIPLINES trying HOWEVER to appear as “A SPECIAL VIEWPOINT”' - the territory is conceded and the distinctiveness is relocated to a way of looking. A boundary that cannot be drawn around the subject matter is drawn around the perspective instead — “sociology certainly shares its area of study with other close disciplines”
differs inrelation — WHAT FOLLOWS FROM THE LIMITS OF KNOWLEDGE. 'HYPER-COMPLEXITY' and 'THIS FRAILTY OF HUMAN KNOWLEDGE' are cited as grounds for 'MORE HUMILITY AND OPENNESS ESPECIALLY TOWARD THE HUMANITIES, AN ATTITUDE RUNNING CONTRARY TO THE “FACESAVING PRIDE” ... THAT HAS PREVAILED in most recognised sociology and social sciences'. The same premise that usually licenses methodological retrenchment is used here to argue for opening the boundary — “face”
replicationuntested
limitationThe author limits the scope of the argument: it does not claim to abolish all disciplinary distinctions, only their essentialisation. — “This standpoint understandably does not purport to eliminate all distinctions but instead admits that disciplines would more or less retain some of their specificities; however, if designed in a non-irreducible way, these differences can feed and enrich research.”
limitationThe boundary the paper is about is explained by a bid for institutional standing rather than by any difference in what the two groups of disciplines study. — “the social sciences struggled to maintain that distinction in order to gain institutional and social recognition”
limitationThe proposed remedy is illustrated from the author's own department and teaching rather than from evidence gathered for the purpose. — “In dispensing many of our teachings, we immediately realised there was some relevance – in not blurring "disciplinary frontiers" but instead at least to put teachers of different profile in the same class and even sometimes in simultaneous pair teachings or the like”
limitationThe claim that scientific definitions are arbitrary reaches the reader through a textbook rather than from the source it is attributed to. — “who already integrated the arbitrariness which may be contained in scientific productions (definitions subject to variations, hypotheses, etc.) as recalled by Grawitz”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingThe distinction between the social sciences and the humanities was not epistemic but strategic: the social sciences maintained it from the nineteenth century onwards in order to win institutional and social recognition. — “Initiated in the ninteenth century, the social sciences struggled to maintain that distinction in order to gain institutional and social recognition.”
findingThe scientist and pragmatist impulse gave the social sciences a solid epistemological, theoretical and methodological base but simultaneously essentialised the concept of science and narrowed it. — “This rather scientist and pragmatist will provided the social sciences with a solid development, epistemological, theoretical and methodological base; but it was also a source of essentialising science concept concomitantly reducing it to an increasingly narrowing vision.”
findingNineteenth-century sociography - social surveys and statistical handbooks - supplied nascent sociology with its data-collection instruments: the questionnaire, the monograph and observation. — “Similarly, sociography or in other words the social surveys or statistical handbooks of the XIX century provided nascent sociology with data collecting methods (questionnaire, monograph and observation) refined over time thus adding to its stock of investigating tools.”
findingContemporary sociology is fragmented internally into multiple specialisations, formed either by reference to neighbouring disciplines (legal, economic sociology) or by delimited objects (urban, family, labour sociology). — “Thus, contemporary sociology seems to be strongly marked by partitioning and division into multiple specialisations internally.”
findingQualitative method was dominant in early German sociology and the Chicago School until the 1930s and then disappeared, pushed out precisely as the social sciences reasserted their claim to be scientific. — “Qualitative approach was very fashionable in the initial stages of German sociology and at the Chicago School until the 1930s then vanished from front stage at one point especially due to the fact that sociology and more generally the social sciences were reasserting their scientist claims.”
findingQualitative approach returned forcefully from the 1980s and now enriches research through a systematised and expanding set of tools. — “Qualitative approach made a forceful return since the 1980s and now especially offers considerable prospects for research enrichment”
findingThe German romantic-hermeneutic tradition reversed the demarcation, bringing the social sciences (as sciences of the mind) closer to the humanities rather than to the hard sciences. — “In the same wake, this analysis tried to demonstrate that on the contrary, in the context of German tradition strongly marked by romanticism and methodological dispute, this disconnect assumed different contours, as it brings the social sciences or "sciences of the mind" closer to the humanities.”
findingIn the American tradition, the Chicago School's promotion of qualitative method served to humanise the social sciences. — “Equally in the American tradition represented by the Chicago School, the promotion of qualitative method largely contributed to advantageously "humanising" the social sciences19.”
findingThe study of art exposes the symmetric limits of both camps: the social scientist's external gaze is too detached from the work of art, while the humanist's internal gaze does not distance itself enough from it. — “In short, this example simply illustrates the limitations of each of these approaches as crystallised between an internal vision said not to distance itself enough and an external one said to be too detached.”
findingDisciplinary dissociation did earn the separate sciences recognition, but its cost was an illusory and impoverishing wall between the social sciences and the humanities. — “Historically, these kinds of disciplinary dissociations have been important for different sciences to gain recognition albeit resulting in installing an illusory and pauperising wall between the social sciences and the humanities.”
findingThe paper's own position is that inter-disciplinary differences of theme, theory and method remain meaningful and even productive so long as they are not treated as essential. — “Lastly, emphasis is laid on the fact that in the end, the differences introduced between disciplines in terms of theme focus, theoretical and methodological options etc. may be meaningful and even interesting on condition that they not be essentialised.”
definitionthird voice/pathway (the territory the social sciences carved out) — “The social sciences thus worked at defining other territories or in a nutshell, a "third voice/ pathway" alongside those already pervaded by the so-called hard sciences and the humanities”
