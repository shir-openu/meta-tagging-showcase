Results Inkjet printingterm·key of unclonable quantum dotterm·key security labelsterm·key The fabrication process of unclonable security labelsterm·key by inkjet printingterm·key are illustrated in Fig. 1 . Three types of commercially available II–VI semiconductor core-shell quantum dotsterm·key (CdSe/CdS/CdZnS, ZnCdSe/CdZnS, and ZnCdS/CdZnS) that emitted red, green, and blue light were chosen as model inks owing to their high fluorescent quantum yield and outstanding stability (Fig. 1a ). These quantum dotsterm·key were synthesized using a well-established chemical route 26 , 27 , in which high boiling point oleic acid and 1-octadecen were used as a capping agent and solvent, respectively (see Methods for details). Typical low-magnification transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and high-resolution TEM micrographs present an extremely narrow particle size distribution and high crystallinity of the synthesized quantum dotsterm·key (Fig. 1a, b and Supplementary Fig. 1 ). The particle sizes of the red, green, and blue emission quantum dotsterm·key are 6.5 ± 2 nm, 10.5 ± 3 nm, and 11.0 ± 2.5 nm, respectively. Generally, for quantum dotsterm·key, the relationship between size and emissive wavelength accords with quantum confinement effect — that a small size corresponds to a larger band gap and emits short-wavelength fluorescence 28 . In this case, the emission wavelength of red, green and blue quantum dotsterm·key shown in Fig. 1a was determined by the composition rather than the size of quantum dotsterm·key (see Supplementary Fig. 1c ) 26 . The Fourier-transform infrared absorption peaks at 2923 and 2854 cm −1 , corresponding to C–C and C–H stretching modes of the -CH 2 - group, reveals the presence of oleic acid on the quantum dotterm·key surface (see Supplementary Fig. 2 ) 29 . The surface ligands are of importance for the preparation of highly dispersed and stable quantum dotterm·key inks as they can stabilize the quantum dotsterm·key in non-polar solvents for months at room temperature. In this work, n-octylcyclohexane with low evaporation rateterm·dom at ambient environment was adopted as model solvent for ink preparation. The low evaporation rateterm·dom of the jetted ink droplet leads to a slow movement of the three-phase contact lineterm·dom on the print substrate, which allows us to dynamically study the quantum dotterm·key deposition behavior. Photographs of twenty jetted single droplets show almost identical shape both before contacting a print substrate and at the early stage after the contact (see Supplementary Fig. 3 ). Representative dark-field and atomic force microscopy images revealed the presence of randomly distributed PMMAterm·dom nanoparticles with particle size in the range of a few nanometers to a few micrometers on the print substrates (see Supplementary Fig. 4 ). Following the surface modification of the print substrates, they were subjected to the inkjet printingterm·key to produce 2D graphic security labelsterm·key. The inkjet printingterm·key was conducted by continuously jetting the quantum dotterm·key ink droplets with size of ~130 μm on the print substrates (Fig. 1d ). The interval between two adjacent ink droplets is 200 μm. If each macroscopic security labelterm·key contains 1000 flower-liketerm·key points, we can achieve 1600 security labelsterm·key in 5 min (i.e., the drying time of each batch of security labelterm·key) with our single-nozzle printing machine. The PMMAterm·dom nanoparticles on the poorly wettable PMMAterm·dom film were acted as stochastic pinning pointsterm·dom at the three-phase contact linesterm·key of the ink droplets. With the evaporation of solvent, the three-phase contact lineterm·key continually slides and shrinks at the smooth PMMAterm·dom areas owing to the poor wettability of the surface but is captured by some pinning pointsterm·dom. Consequently, the pinning pointsterm·dom stretch and pin the contact line, distorting the fluid convex and forming irregular quantum dotterm·key ink pattern (Fig. 1e ). During the solvent evaporation process, the quantum dotterm·key concentration increases gradually. When the quantum dotterm·key concentration reached their saturation point, they started to deposit at the stochastic PMMAterm·dom pinning pointsterm·dom on the three-phase contact lineterm·key, forming a unique flower-liketerm·key dot pattern (Fig. 1f ). With the shrinking of droplet, the volume-smaller droplet is more liable to be tortured by pinning pointsterm·dom, thus splitting into several smaller sub-droplets (see Supplementary Fig. 5 ). Due to the small space between these sub-droplets and some stochastic external factors (such as airflow), they experienced merging and splitting irregularly before eventual drying (Fig. 1f ). Such a non-deterministic pattern formation process makes it impossible to reproduce the flower-liketerm·key dot patterns, which were used as PUFterm·def codes in our security labelsterm·key. The fabricated security labelsterm·key were then covered with a thin, optically transparent, sticky gel film to protect them being damaged during the real circulation (Fig. 1g ). However, if the print substrates were coated with a continuous, smooth, poorly wettable PMMAterm·dom layer, the three-phase contact lineterm·key of the droplets are incline to shrink and slide towards to the center, rather than pinned on substrates, bringing the quantum dotsterm·key to center of the droplet. In the absence of the stochastic PMMAterm·dom pinning pointsterm·dom, either a uniform coffee ring or central bump pattern was formed, which was determined by the wettability property of the print substrates. These deterministic pattern formation processes ensure the reproducibility of the patterns under the same conditions.

The miniaturized dark-field and fluorescence microscopes integrated with smartphones have been developed for single nanoparticle imaging 31 , 32 . Such small, affordable, portable microscopes were utilized by consumers to authenticate the inkjet-printed security labelsterm·key. The magnification-adjustable objective lens of the portable microscope used here is covered with a cylindrical metal shell that creates a small dark imaging environment by blocking the ambient light (Supplementary Fig. 7 ). The portable microscope is able to quickly (typically within one second), accurately and nondestructively readout the produced flower-liketerm·key dot patterns, which show different geometries as expected (Supplementary Fig. 7 ).

Unlimited colorful pattern design capability Ideal fluorescent security labelsterm·key require the ink materials having strong fluorescence intensity, which guarantees the high fluorescence brightness. The prepared quantum dotterm·key inks exhibit a narrow fluorescence emission peak with maximum intensity at 459 nm, 532 nm, and 631 nm, respectively, when excited at 375 nm (Fig. 2a ). The full-width at half-maximum (FWHM) of these blue, green and red emission quantum dotsterm·key is 20 nm, 24 nm, and 28 nm, respectively. They also bear a high fluorescence quantum yield of 75, 90, and 80%, respectively. The time-resolved fluorescence decays of the blue, green, and red emission quantum dotsterm·key show their lifetime of 27.5 ns, 17.0 ns, and 7.6 ns (Fig. 2b ), respectively, in consistent with consistent with previous reports 26 , 33 , 34 . According to the Commission Internationale de I’éclairage (CIE) 1931 color coordinate triangle, the quantum dotsterm·key achieved a wide color gamut of 124% of the National Television System Committee (NTSC) standard (see Supplementary Fig. 8 ), better than phosphor (color gamut: 85.6%) 35 – 37 , implying outstanding full-color fluorescence properties for security labelterm·key applications. A copy of the red, green, and blue fluorescence counterparts of the FZU letters shown in Fig. 3 fabricated under the same conditions also shows entirely different geometries of the corresponding pixels (see Supplementary Fig. 15 ). Synthesis of core-shell quantum dotsterm·key For a typical synthesis of CdSe/CdS/CdZnS red emission quantum dotsterm·key: 7 mmol of CdO, 10 mL of OA and 25 mL of ODE were mixed in a 250 mL round flask. The mixture was heated to 160 °C, degassed for 15 min, then filled with N 2 gas and further heated to 310 °C. Subsequently, Se precursor was injected swiftly into the flask. Then the reaction was cooled to 300 °C and remained at this temperature for 5 min. To grow the CdS shell, S source was injected dropwise to react with the remaining cadmium ions for 20 min. For the growth of CdZnS shell, keep the reaction temperature and dropwise injected the pre-prepared shelling materials, the shelling process last for ~30 mins. After the reaction, the temperature was naturally cooled down to room temperature. The synthesized quantum dotsterm·key were finally purified using toluene and ethanolfor several times and finally dispersed in n-octylcyclohexane. For ZnCdSe/CdZnS green emission quantum dotsterm·key: 35 mmol of ZnO, 25 mL of OA, and 20 mL of ODE were mixed in a 250 mL round flask. The mixture was heated to 160 °C, degassed for 15 min, then filled with N 2 gas and further heated to 300 g to get a clear solution. The solution was cooled to 200 °C, at which Se and Cd stock precursors were quickly injected into the flask sequentially. Then the temperature was elevated to 310 °C and remained at this temperature for 30 min to form alloyed CdZnSe core. For the growth of the CdZnS shell, S source and Cd source were dropwise injected into the flask repeatedly while keeping temperature at 270 °C. After the precursor injection, the temperature was kept unchanged for 30 min to 1 h, and then naturally cooled down to room temperature. The shell materials (S source and Cd source) were added afterwards drop-wisely, and the reaction was allowed to proceed from 30 min to 1 h for the shell growth.
