mechanismAI systems continuously re-tune problem difficulty to the individual student's measured performance, holding each learner at a level of challenge that is neither too easy nor too hard, which is the route by which personalisation is claimed to produce deeper learning — “For instance, adaptive learning platforms adjust the difficulty of problems based on the student’s performance, ensuring that they are neither overwhelmed nor under-stimulated.”
mechanismimmediate machine feedback lets a learner detect and repair an error while the attempt is still active, so the correct concept is reinforced instead of the mistake being consolidated — “Moreover, AI-driven systems can offer immediate feedback, allowing learners to correct their mistakes in real-time and reinforcing key concepts.”

differs inrelation — WHETHER THE TOOL BUILDS THE SKILL OR SUBSTITUTES FOR IT. 'HOWEVER, THERE ARE CONCERNS THAT OVER-RELIANCE ON AI TOOLS SUCH AS CHATGPT MIGHT UNDERMINE STUDENTS' ABILITY TO DEVELOP THEIR OWN PROBLEM-SOLVING SKILLS.' The same capability that makes the tool useful for a task is what removes the occasion to learn the task - and note that wang2024's students said this themselves, 22 of 48 unprompted, so it is reported here as a literature concern and there as a first-person one — “over-reliance on AI tools such as ChatGPT might undermine students”
differs inrelation — WHAT THE SAME SOURCE IS CITED FOR. Grájeda et al. supplies BOTH the list of effective-teaching components - which includes 'INCORPORATING TECHNOLOGY' - AND, two sentences later, the over-reliance concern. One citation carries the endorsement and the caution, and a reader following either citation alone gets half of what that source says — “incorporating technology”
differs inrelation — WHICH THREE THINGS ARE PUT IN ONE LIST. 'the integration of AI into education also raises CRITICAL QUESTIONS REGARDING ITS EFFECTIVENESS, ACCESSIBILITY AND ETHICAL IMPLICATIONS' - an empirical question, a distributional one and a normative one, joined by 'and' and answerable by entirely different kinds of evidence. The record's own limitation already notes access being named as a precondition for any claimed benefit — “critical questions regarding its effectiveness, accessibility and ethical implications”
replicationuntested
limitationThe subject is introduced by equating three different things - a field, a capability and a class of model - in the editorial's first sentence. — “Artificial intelligence (AI), also known as generative AI and large language models, has steadily emerged as a transformative force across various sectors of daily life”
limitationThe term the whole argument rests on is given only by what it exceeds, never by a criterion that would decide a case. — “Meaningful learning in mathematics goes beyond rote memorisation of formulas or the mechanical application of algorithms”
limitationThe editorial's own caveat is that the technology may displace the teacher rather than assist them. — “One concern is the potential for over-reliance on AI tools, which may lead to a diminished role for teachers (Harry & Sayudin, 2023). While AI can automate certain tasks, the human element of teaching – such as providing emotional support, fostering critical thinking and encouraging collaboration – remains irreplaceable (García-Martínez et al., 2023). Therefore, AI should be seen as a tool that complements, rather than replaces, the teacher’s role in facilitating meaningful learning”
limitationAccess to the technology is named as a precondition for any of the claimed benefits. — “Ensuring equitable access to AI technology is essential if it is to have a positive impact on meaningful learning in mathematics”
