phenomenonresponding under interval schedules of reinforcement— “Interval schedules of reinforcement maintained pigeons' key-pecking in six experiments.”
claim-typemechanism— “It was concluded that the rate of responding maintained by a given interval schedule depends not on the overall rate of reinforcement provided but rather on the summation of different local effects of reinforcement at different times within intervals.”
method — evidence— “In Exp. 1, the relative durations of a sequence of intervals from an arithmetic progression were held constant while the mean interval was varied.”
mechanismThe effect of a reinforcement spreads along the continuum of time since reinforcement, so responding at any moment is controlled by reinforcement probability smeared over a surrounding window rather than at that instant alone— “The basic premise in converting probabilities of reinforcement to local rates of reinforcement is that the effect of a given probability of reinforcement may spread over time and may depend on the closeness in time of other opportunities for reinforcement.”
mechanismThat temporal spread is interpreted as a gradient of temporal generalization around the moment of reinforcement— “The spread of the effect of reinforcement at one time since reinforcement to local rates of responding at other times could be interpreted in terms of a gradient of temporal generalization.”
mechanismOverall response rate is produced indirectly: reinforcement rate acts on local response rates at each time after reinforcement, and the overall rate is their weighted average— “Rather, a given rate of reinforcement produces a given overall rate of responding through its effects on local rates of responding at different times after reinforcement.”
mechanismTime since reinforcement acts as a discriminative stimulus dimension, so intervals reliably paired with nonreinforcement come to control low response rates just as other stimulus dimensions do— “Times since reinforcement that are consistently correlated with nonreinforcement may come to control low rates of responding in much the same way as do other stimulus properties (e.g., intensity, wavelength).”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingAcross seven variable-interval schedules the overall rate of pigeons' key-pecking rose monotonically with the overall rate of reinforcement but with diminishing returns, over a span from 8.4 to 300 reinforcements per hour.— “Rate of responding was a monotonically increasing, negatively accelerated function of rate of reinforcement over a range from 8.4 to 300 reinforcements per hour.”
findingWithin a single schedule, how fast the bird pecked at a given moment after reinforcement tracked the local rate of reinforcement at that moment, computed from the probability of reinforcement there and the nearness of reinforcement opportunities at other moments.— “In each schedule, the rate of responding at a particular time within an interval was shown to depend at least in part on the local rate of reinforcement at that time, derived from a measure of the probability of reinforcement at that time and the proximity of potential reinforcements at other times.”
findingThe paper's overall conclusion is that a schedule's overall response rate is not set by its overall reinforcement rate but is the sum of separate local reinforcement effects distributed across times within the interval.— “It was concluded that the rate of responding maintained by a given interval schedule depends not on the overall rate of reinforcement provided but rather on the summation of different local effects of reinforcement at different times within intervals.”
findingA schedule engineered to hold the probability of reinforcement constant across time since reinforcement flattened the within-interval response gradient, keeping the local pecking rate roughly constant instead of rising.— “When the probability of reinforcement was held constant, the local rate of responding remained roughly constant throughout the interval between reinforcements.”
findingAdding extra short intervals raised the probability of reinforcement at t sec and correspondingly raised the local pecking rate exactly at t sec, and adding four such intervals raised it more than adding two.— “Thus, the local rate of responding at t sec depended on the probability of reinforcement at that time.”
findingIncluding a 0-sec interval (first peck after reinforcement is reinforced) raised responding only in the first two or three time bins after reinforcement, not across the whole interval.— “The effect of the 0-sec interval was restricted primarily to the time shortly after reinforcement (first two or three points on each function).”
findingRemoving reinforcement at the end of the long interval (replacing it with timeout) destroyed the temporal pattern of responding when the early probability was only 0.05, showing that responding early in an interval is partly sustained by the later, high-probability reinforcement.— “Thus, the probability of 0.05, unlike the probability of 0.50, was less effective in maintaining responding when the higher probability of reinforcement at a later time was removed.”
findingAt matched overall reinforcement rates, arithmetic variable-interval schedules sustained higher overall response rates than fixed-interval schedules for all but one pigeon.— “For example, except for Pigeon 121 in Fig. 1, the overall rates of responding maintained by arithmetic VI schedules were consistently higher than the overall rates maintained by corresponding Fl schedules in Fig. 17.”
findingReinforcing a response immediately after a preceding reinforcement influenced later responding only briefly, so schedules with and without a 0-sec interval remain comparable at later times.— “Experiment 2 demonstrated that reinforcement of a response immediately after a preceding reinforcement affected responding over only a relatively short period of time since reinforcement.”
quantity8.4 to 300 reinforcements per hour — the span of overall reinforcement rates over which the response-rate function was measured in Experiment 1— “Rate of responding was a monotonically increasing, negatively accelerated function of rate of reinforcement over a range from 8.4 to 300 reinforcements per hour.”
quantitysix — number of pigeons whose key-pecking was studied in Experiment 1, maintained at 80% of free-feeding weight— “The key-pecking of each of six adult, male, White Carneaux pigeons, maintained at 80% of free-feeding body weights, had been reinforced on VI schedules for at least 50 hr before the present experiments.”
quantity0.10 + 0.02 — the reinforcement probability held nearly constant in the constant-probability schedule, over a span where the arithmetic schedule's probability rose from 0.07 to 0.33— “In the present constant-probability VI schedule, shown in the upper frame of Fig. 11, the probability of reinforcement remained equal to 0.10 + 0.02 over a range of time since reinforcement within which, in the arithmetic VI schedule, this probability increased almost five-fold, from 0.07 to 0.33.”
quantityabout 2 resp/min — the near-flat rise in local response rate across the constant-probability interval, about one-tenth of the rise seen in the arithmetic schedule— “The increase in rate was only about 2 resp/min over the time from 2t to 17t sec, or roughly one-tenth the increase over the same range of time in the arithmetic VI schedule.”
quantity63.7 and 63.2 resp/min ... 61.1 and 73.7 resp/min — overall response rates on the schedules without and with a 0-sec interval, for Pigeons 278 and 281 respectively— “The overall rates of responding maintained by the VI 123-sec and VI 108-sec schedules were 63.7 and 63.2 resp/min for Pigeon 278 and 61.1 and 73.7 resp/min for Pigeon 281.”
quantityabout 6% ... 50% to 90% — a tiny change in overall reinforcement rate produced a disproportionately large change in overall response rate, showing the distribution of intervals rather than the overall rate was responsible— “This is particularly the case for the probability of 0.05, for which a change in rft/hr of only about 6% (from 15 to 16 rft/hr) produced 50% to 90% increases in overall rates of responding.”
quantitya factor of almost 40 — the growth of local reinforcement rate from 10 to 140 sec after reinforcement in the arithmetic schedule, far larger than the accompanying growth in local response rate— “The local rate of reinforcement in the arithmetic VI schedule, for example, increases by a factor of almost 40 over the time from 10 to 140 sec after reinforcement, whereas the local rate of responding increases by a much smaller factor.”
quantityabout 12 resp/min — the rise in immediate post-reinforcement response rate that a single extra reinforced response would explain, against an observed increment roughly two and a half times larger— “One additional response at the beginning of each interval would have raised the local rate of responding immediately after reinforcement by about 12 resp/min and would have had no effect on subsequent local rates of responding.”

definitionoverall rate versus local rate— “(The former, a rate calculated over the total time in all the intervals of a schedule, will be referred to as an overall rate; the latter, a rate calculated over a period of time that is short relative to the average interval between reinforcements, will be referred to as a local rate.”
definitionreinforcements per opportunity (rft/op)— “(Reinforcements per opportunity is defined as the probability of reinforcement for the first response that occurs after a particular time since reinforcement.”
definitionopportunity for reinforcement— “An opportunity for reinforcement is defined as a point on the continuum of time since reinforcement at which the probability of reinforcement is greater than zero, or at which at least one interval ends.”
definitionlocal rate of reinforcement— “Local rates of reinforcement, illusttated in Fig. 6, take the separation of successive opportunities for reinforcement into account; they are calculated by dividing the number of reinforcements at a given opportunity by the period of time within which that opportunity is isolated.”
definitioninterval schedule— “An interval schedule arranges reinforcement for the first response that occurs after a specified time has elapsed since the occurrence of a preceding reinforcement or some other environmental event (Ferster and Skinner, 1957).”
definitionfixed-interval schedule as the limiting case of the variable-interval schedule— “The fixed-interval (FI) schedule is the limiting case of the VI schedule: the distribution of intervals is narrowed down to a single value.”
definitiongeometric VI schedule— “A geometric VI schedule consists of a sequence of intervals in which the duration of a given interval is equal to the duration of the next shorter interval multiplied by a constant”
definitionFibonacci VI schedule— “A Fibonacci VI schedule consists of a sequence of intervals in which the duration of a given interval is equal to the sum of the durations of the next two shorter intervals”
