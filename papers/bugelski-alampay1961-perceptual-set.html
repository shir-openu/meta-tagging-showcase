mechanismA PAPER DISOWNS THE TERM IN ITS OWN TITLE, AND SAYS WHICH TERM IT SHOULD HAVE BEEN: it appears appropriate to indicate that our findings **MIGHT HAVE MORE BEARING ON WHAT MIGHT BE TERMED “COGNITIVE” SETS RATHER THAN ON PERCEPTUAL SETS**. The article is titled “The Role of Frequency in Developing PERCEPTUAL Sets”, and the correction is made in the paper's own voice rather than left to a reader — “It appears appropriate to indicate tibat our findings might have more bearing on what might be termed "cognitive'' sets rather tiian on perceptual sets.”
mechanismAND THE DISOWNED WORD IS THEN RESERVED FOR A NARROWER CLASS, WITH AN EXAMPLE A READER CAN PICTURE: the term “perceptual” set **MIGHT BETTER BE RESERVED FOR** reference to those instances where a subject **IS LOOKING FOR OR EXPECTING TO SEE A SPECIFIC STIMULUS**, a friend's face in a crowd, for example. Having said the word does not fit their study, they say what it does fit - which is the harder half and the one usually skipped — “The term "perceptual" set might better be reserved for reference to those instances where a subject is looking for or expecting to see a specific stimulus, a friend's face in a crowd, for example”
mechanismTHE AMBIGUOUS STIMULUS IS DEFINED BY THE TWO CATEGORIES IT CAN FALL INTO, AND THE CATEGORIES ARE NAMED: it **CAN BE PERCEIVED AS BELONGING TO EITHER OF TWO COGNITIVE CATEGORIES OR CLASSES OR OBJECTS: ANIMALS AND HUMANS**. The figure's ambiguity is not a property of the drawing but of which of two named classes a viewer assigns it to — “It can be perceived as belonging to either of two cognitive categories or classes or objects: animals and humans.”
mechanismA GAP IN THE PRIOR LITERATURE IS STATED AS A MISSING QUANTITY RATHER THAN A WRONG RESULT: while Leeper did find that he could control his subjects' responses by manipulating past experiences, he **DID NOT ATTEMPT TO DEMONSTRATE ANY SYSTEMATIC OR QUANTITATIVE RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN AMOUNT OF EXPERIENCE AND RESPONSE**. The predecessor is credited with the effect and the paper claims the function relating it to dose — “While Leeper did find that he could control his subjects' responses by manipulating past experiences, he did not attempt to demonstrate any systematic or quantitative relationship between amount of experience and response” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
mechanismTHE INFERENCE FROM THE MEASUREMENT TO THE CONSTRUCT IS STATED AS A PRESUMPTION, IN THAT WORD: **THE PRESUMPTION HERE IS THAT** those with a strong set **WILL RESIST SUCH NEW SET INDUCTION MORE THAN** those with a weak set. What the experiment observes is resistance to a second induction; what it reports is strength of set, and the bridge between them is named a presumption by the authors — “The presumption here is that those with a strong set will resist such new set induction more than those with a weak set”
mechanismAND THE STRENGTH OF THE SET IS OPERATIONALISED BY BORROWING AN ASSUMPTION FROM A NAMED AUTHORITY: **ASSUMING, WITH ALLPORT,** that having different numbers of such experiences would result in different strengths of set, it was **ALSO DECIDED TO TEST FOR SUCH STRENGTH BY ATTEMPTING TO INDUCE THE ALTERNATE SET**. The premise is attributed rather than argued, and the test for the quantity is chosen after the premise is granted — “Assuming, with Allport, diat having different numbers of such experiences would result in different strengths of set, it was also decided to test for such strength by attempting to induce die alternate set”
mechanismTHE SCOPE OF THE WORD `PERCEPTION` IS LIMITED IN THE SAME BREATH AS THE METHOD IS DESCRIBED: although our experiment makes use of an ambiguous figure, **IT SHOULD BE CLEAR THAT WE ARE NOT SUGGESTING THAT THE STUDY IS DIRECTLY CONCERNED WITH PERCEPTION EXCEPT IN THE LIMITED SENSE OF PERCEPTION OF DIAGRAMMATIC PICTURES**. A disclaimer about what the central word covers, placed before the results rather than in a limitations section — “Although our experiment makes use of an ambiguous figure, it should be clear that we are not suggesting that die study is directly concerned with perception except in die limited sense of perception of diagrammatic pictures”
