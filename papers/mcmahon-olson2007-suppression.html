sense**THE CONCLUSION IS A NEGATIVE AND IT FITS IN ONE LINE:** `We conclude that repetition suppression ACCOMPANIES repetition priming but is UNLIKELY TO BE ITS CAUSE.` The neural correlate everyone reaches for is a passenger. **Carlesimo2004, merged into this corpus forty minutes ago, reads its rCBF decreases as `the reduced neural activity needed to reprocess recently seen words`** - suppression explaining priming, taken as read; and Desimone1996 calls suppression `an intrinsic property of visual cortical areas`. Three records, three positions on whether a reduced response is the mechanism or the companion, and none of them assembled on purpose— “We conclude that repetition suppression accompanies repetition priming but is unlikely to be its cause.”
sense**AND THE ARGUMENT IS TURNED FROM A STATISTICAL QUESTION INTO A MECHANICAL ONE, WHICH IS THE ONLY WAY TO RULE OUT A CO-OCCURRING SIGNAL:** `Whether repetition suppression indeed contributes to repetition priming must depend in part on HOW IT AFFECTS THE STIMULUS SELECTIVITY OF NEURONS.` Correlation is granted, not contested. The test is whether suppression has the PROPERTIES a cause would need - because a uniform reduction across all neurons degrades the representation, while priming requires it sharpened. **A correlate can only be dismissed by asking what it would have to be like**— “Whether repetition suppression indeed contributes to repetition priming must depend in part on how it affects the stimulus selectivity of neurons.”
sense**THE DEFINITION IS PURELY PHYSIOLOGICAL AND THAT IS WHY THE PAPER IS POSSIBLE:** `Repetition suppression is a physiological phenomenon, first demonstrated in recording studies of monkey inferotemporal cortex (IT), whereby the strength of the neuronal response to a visual stimulus DECLINES OVER SUCCESSIVE PRESENTATIONS.` No meaning, no memory, no behaviour in it. **A construct defined without reference to the thing it is supposed to explain can be tested against that thing**; one defined as `the neural basis of priming` could not be. Compare Carlesimo2004's repetition priming, defined by what the test INSTRUCTIONS do not mention - also an operational definition, and also load-bearing— “Repetition suppression is a physiological phenomenon, first demonstrated in recording studies of monkey inferotemporal cortex (IT), whereby the strength of the neuronal response to a visual stimulus declines over successive presentations.”
controlthe first twelve trials of every session were discarded — held — rules out that an early, transient, NON-STIMULUS-SPECIFIC drop in both reaction time and firing rate is counted as a priming effect— “Because there was a tendency toward a transient, nonstimulus-specific decrease in both RT and firing rate very early in a session, we excluded the first 12 trials of each session from subsequent analysis to restrict our focus to data collected under stationary conditions.”
controlresponses to stimuli classified as subthreshold by a signal-detection thresholding filter were excluded — held — rules out that suppression is measured on responses too weak to be distinguished from noise— “Third, we excluded responses to stimuli that were identified as subthreshold on the basis of the signaldetection thresholding filter (described in METHODS, Assessing priming and suppression effects).”
