phenomenonshort-term memory localisation in mouse neocortex
claim-typemechanism
mechanismPosterior parietal area RL, which projects both frontally to M2 and posteriorly to area P, routes the sensory information to one destination or the other according to the animal's behavioural state — “Therefore, they could route information to either frontal or posterior cortices depending on the mouse’s state.”
mechanismWhen information is held frontally in M2, posterior cortex is actively suppressed below baseline, which is why the two memory patterns are mutually exclusive — “The latter observation suggests that posterior areas may be inhibited when information is maintained in M2 (Allen et al., 2017).”
relation — “Thus, consistent with previous reports (Chen et al., 2016; Yang et al., 2015), sensory-related posterior areas S1BC, S2 and RL encode choice shortly after touch for both behavioral strategies.”
relation — “Discrimination between hit and CR trials occurred within a few hundred milliseconds, in line with previous studies (Chen et al., 2013, 2016).”
relation — “In passive trials, M2 as well as other frontal areas (including ALM; Guo et al., 2014; Li et al., 2015) were not activated and could not discriminate trial types (Figure 4), suggesting that they are less important for maintaining information for this type of behavior, at least before any preparatory movements.”
relation — “Maintenance of information in short-term memory is not necessarily tied to one cortical area but is flexibly localized according to behavioral strategy (Leavitt et al., 2017).”
replicationuntested
limitationWide-field signals are bulk population signals that may be confounded by non-neuronal fluorescence — “The disadvantage of the method is that the wide-field fluorescence signals represent bulk signals, summed over neuronal populations (as well as potentially confounded by non-neuronal fluorescence changes, which needs to be controlled for).”
limitationOnly layer 2/3 was studied; other layers may behave differently — “In addition, this study focused on layer 2/3 neurons, whereas other layers may contribute differently to task-related processes (Chandrasekaran et al., 2017; van Kerkoerle et al., 2014; Li et al., 2015).”
limitationThe identity of the posterior activation spot is uncertain and may include neighbouring areas — “Given the variability among animals, however, the observed activation spot may also encompass adjacent posterior areas such as lateromedial visual cortex or postrhinal cortices.”
limitationThe proposed what/where pathway interpretation is not directly tested here — “Nevertheless, additional experiments directly testing each pathway will be needed to substantiate these ideas.”
limitationThe measurement is a bulk signal, and the authors state they cannot exclude having missed intermingled subpopulations averaged out within it. — “wide-field fluorescence signals represent bulk signals, summed over neuronal populations (as well as potentially confounded by non-neuronal fluorescence changes, which needs to be controlled for). We therefore cannot exclude the possibility that we have missed intermingled sub- 28 populations of neurons that carry task-relevant information but are averaged out in the population signal.”
limitationThe grouping variable the entire result rests on is, in some sessions, assigned by a substitute measure whose accuracy is stated as 75%. — “For a small subset of sessions where the body camera gave low quality images or was missing, we predicted the mouse’s movement based on the z-axis drop in focus (using a 10% drop from baseline in 3 in-focus single cells as criterion; 75% accuracy in labeling trials by validating with move vector from the body camera”
limitationThe independent variable is changed by the manipulation. Strategy is treated as what determines where short-term memory sits, but optogenetic silencing of the site induces strategy switches that persist across days and appear in non-perturbation trials. The paper reports this as a result rather than as a threat, and it is the correct reading - but it means strategy and site are mutually determining, not one prior to the other, and the title states only one direction. — “Here, we find that some mice manage to maintain relatively high performance despite perturbation of one area (M2 or P) whereas other mice show significantly reduced performance. The former mice apparently flexibly switch to the alternative unperturbed strategy whereas the latter mice maintain their strategy. Strategy switches were consistent across days, present also in trials without perturbation and were clearly reflected in corresponding switches between M2- and P-dominated delay maps.”
limitationTwo thresholds on the same movement signal do different jobs: one splits trials into active and passive by movement during the sensation period, the other discards trials with movement in the first second of the delay as noisy. The grouping rule and the exclusion rule read the same measure, so which trials survive is not independent of which class they were assigned to. — “Dashed black line indicates threshold to construct the corresponding binary movement vectors (lower black traces). Classification into active (dark green) and passive (light green) trials is based on movement during the sensation period. Trials with the mouse moving during the first second in the delay period were defined as noisy and discarded (bottom trial).”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingWhich cortical area held the delay-period memory depended on the mouse's behavioural strategy: persistent activity sat medio-frontally (M2) on active trials but in a lateral posterior area on passive trials. — “During the delay, in contrast, persistent cortical activity was located medio-frontally in active trials but in a lateral posterior area in passive trials.”
findingThe posterior whisker areas encoded the animal's choice soon after touch under both strategies, so the strategy difference appeared only later, during the delay. — “Independent of strategy, whisker-related posterior areas encoded choice early after touch.”
findingOptogenetically silencing M2 or area P lowered task performance only in trials run with the strategy that area supports, and left the other strategy unaffected. — “In summary, perturbation of one area (M2 or P) decreased performance exclusively when the mouse used the associated strategy (Figure 7D; P < 0.05; signed-rank test).”
findingSilencing M2 and area P together dropped all four VGAT-ChR2 mice to performance indistinguishable from the non-expert threshold, which single-area silencing had not done. — “All four mice significantly reduced their performance upon ChR2 activation (P < 0.001; signed-rank test), down to levels not significantly different from the expert threshold (Figure 7F; P > 0.05; signed-rank test).”
findingSome mice escaped a perturbation by switching behavioural strategy rather than losing performance, one passive mouse turning active during the perturbation sessions. — “Notably, however, this mouse very efficiently switched its strategy from passive to active in the perturbation sessions (Figure 6Fii, iii; P < 0.001; Mann-Whitney U-test).”
findingThe two memory sites hold different content: the motor-sensory index was positive in M2 (activity grouped by the upcoming action) and negative in area P (activity grouped by the past texture). — “MSI was significantly positive in M2 and significantly negative in P (Figure 8D; P < 0.05; Wilcoxon signed-rank test), implying that each area encodes a different type of short-term memory”
findingOn passive trials a classifier restricted to frontal-cortex pixels could not tell hit from correct-rejection trials at all, showing short-term memory need not reside in frontal cortex. — “Interestingly, for passive trials, SVM accuracy was at chance level when we applied the SVM only to pixels in the frontal cortex (Figure 4F; P > 0.05; signed-rank test compared to 50%; Bonferroni corrected; n = 39 imaging sessions from 5 passive mice).”
findingThe more a mouse used the active strategy the better it discriminated textures, and passive mice needed roughly twice as many sessions to reach expert level. — “In addition, d’ performance positively correlated with activeness (Figure 1H; r = 0.6; P < 0.001)”
