mechanismThe inferior frontal gyrus retrieves loosely related semantic concepts, screens candidate ideas and evaluates their originality, which is how creative ideas get generated— “It has been shown that the IFG generates creative ideas through the retrieval of loosely related semantic concepts, the screening of creative ideas, and the evaluation of originality”
mechanismMathematical creativity arises from a division of labour in which the IFG integrates diverse concepts into new ideas while the SFG holds and manipulates that information through working memory and executive control— “The IFG facilitates the generation of innovative mathematical ideas through the integration of diverse concepts, while the SFG supports higher cognitive functions such as working memory and executive control, essential for complex problem-solving in mathematics.”
mechanismThe frontoparietal network supplies the goal orientation, working memory and cognitive control that both the domain-specific mathematical component and the general creative component require— “This network’s involvement in goal orientation, working memory, and cognitive control suggests its integral role in both domain-specific mathematical abilities and general creative performance.”
mechanismpooling coordinates rather than effects: ALE fits each study's activation points to a probability distribution over voxels and looks for spatial consistency across studies— “The basic principle of ALE meta-analysis of coordinate information is to fit the activation points in each experiment to a probability distribution, that is, the magnitude of the probability that the activation points fall on each voxel of the brain ( Eickhoff et al., 2009 ).”
mechanismthe shared regions are ones already associated with idea generation, working memory and executive control - so the overlap is read as shared cognitive machinery rather than coincidence— “The findings reveal significant activation in the left inferior frontal gyrus (IFG) and the left superior frontal gyrus (SFG) during both mathematical and creative tasks, emphasizing their roles in idea generation, working memory, and executive control.”
mechanismthe frontoparietal network's known functions - attention, working memory, goal orientation - are matched against what mathematical problem-solving demands— “This network’s involvement in attention, working memory, and goal orientation aligns with the demands of mathematical problem-solving.”
mechanismthe argument for imaging at all is that behavioural methods could not reach the question, so the neural evidence is offered as leverage for education research

findingMathematical tasks and creative tasks converge on two shared regions: the left inferior frontal gyrus and the left superior frontal gyrus.
findingAcross 44 mathematics studies the ALE meta-analysis found consistent activation in bilateral inferior parietal lobule, left superior parietal lobule, left superior frontal gyrus, right and left medial frontal gyrus, bilateral insula and left precentral gyrus.
findingAcross 56 creativity studies the consistent activations were left-lateralized: inferior frontal gyrus, middle frontal gyrus, superior frontal gyrus, middle occipital gyrus, middle temporal gyrus and inferior parietal lobule.
findingMathematics tasks exceeded creativity tasks specifically in the precuneus, claustrum and superior parietal lobule.
findingCreativity tasks exceeded mathematics tasks specifically in the inferior frontal gyrus, inferior parietal lobule and middle frontal gyrus.
findingMeta-analytic connectivity modelling of the overlap regions showed that the frontoparietal network is the network implicated in mathematical creativity.
findingLeft IFG and left SFG are active in both mathematical and creative tasks - the overlap the study was looking for.
