claim-typemethod
mechanismA WIDELY USED REGION NAME IS REPORTED TO HAVE NO COHERENT DEFINITION, AND THE COST IS NAMED: despite the popularity of the animal for studying the region there is LITTLE CONSENSUS ON A COHERENT ANATOMICAL DEFINITION, WHICH IS PROBLEMATIC BECAUSE IT COMPLICATES THE INTERPRETATION OF THE WEALTH OF NEW DATA. The missing definition is treated as a defect in the literature's ability to read its own results — “Despite the popularity of the mouse for studying parietal cortex, however, there is little consensus on a coherent anatomical definition of PPC in the mouse, which is problematic because it complicates the interpretation of the wealth of new data.”
mechanismTHE PARTS THAT ARE ILL-DEFINED ARE ENUMERATED RATHER THAN GESTURED AT: what remains ILL-DEFINED is named as the BOUNDARIES which distinguish it from neighbouring areas, its LAMINAR ORGANIZATION and its CHEMOARCHITECTURAL PROFILE - three specific things, each of which a definition would have to fix — “More detailed aspects of mouse PPC anatomy, including the boundaries which distinguish it from neighboring areas, its laminar organization and chemoarchitectural profile remain ill-defined.”
mechanismIN THE ABSENCE OF A DEFINITION, PRACTICE SUBSTITUTES TWO PROXIES, AND BOTH ARE NAMED: targeting has relied EITHER on functional mapping of the areas around the region OR on stereotactic coordinates followed by POST-HOC HISTOLOGICAL COMPARISON to one of SEVERAL REFERENCE ATLASES. Where the definition is missing, the coordinates and the atlas stand in for it — “Recent strategies for targeting PPC in mice have therefore relied either on functional mapping of extrastriate areas around PPC (Wang & Burkhalter, 2007), or on stereotactic coordinates followed by post-hoc histological comparison to one of several reference atlases”
mechanismAND THE PROXIES ARE CONSISTENT WITHIN A STUDY AND INCOMPARABLE ACROSS STUDIES, FOR A STATED REASON: these conventions MAY YIELD CONSISTENT RECORDING LOCATIONS WITHIN A STUDY, BUT HAMPER THE COMPARISON of the region ACROSS STUDIES, SINCE THEY ARE BASED ON DIFFERENT LABELING METHODOLOGIES AND UNRELATED NOMENCLATURES. Reliability inside a lab and incommensurability between labs, traced to the naming systems themselves — “These conventions may yield consistent recording locations within a study, but hamper the comparison of PPC across studies since they are based on different labeling methodologies and unrelated nomenclatures.”
mechanismTHE CONTRIBUTION IS EXPLICITLY A RECONCILIATION OF NOMENCLATURES, NOT A NEW FINDING: the paper states that it RECONCILED WIDELY USED BUT DISPARATE NOMENCLATURES referring to the region VERSUS the areas around it - two naming systems, each with its own literature, mapped onto one another — “Importantly, we reconciled widely used but disparate nomenclatures that refer to PPC (Paxinos & Franklin, 2012) versus the extrastriate areas around it”
mechanismTHE NEW DEFINITION IS ASSEMBLED FROM FOUR INDEPENDENT KINDS OF EVIDENCE, AND THE LIST IS THE DEFINITION: the combined approaches are said to provide A COHERENT DEFINITION that INCORPORATES laminar architecture, extrastriate projections, thalamic connections and cortico-cortical connections. Nothing here is a criterion on its own; the definition is the conjunction — “These combined approaches provide a coherent definition of mouse PPC that incorporates laminar architecture, extrastriate projections, thalamic, and cortico-cortical connections.”
mechanismTHE DEFINING CRITERIA ARE STAINS - THE DEFINITION IS OPERATIONALISED BY REAGENTS: the region is delineated using CYTOARCHITECTURAL AND LAMINAR CRITERIA obtained from Nissl-, parvalbumin- and type-2 muscarinic acetylcholine receptor-immunostained sections. What the term picks out is fixed by what three stains show — “We sought to resolve these discrepancies by first delineating mouse PPC using cytoarchitectural and laminar criteria obtained from Nissl-, parvalbumin (PV)-, and type-2 muscarinic acetylcholine receptor (M2AChR)-immunostained coronal sections.”
mechanismAND ONE STATED AIM IS THAT THE CRITERIA BE AVAILABLE TO EVERYONE: the study aims to provide A RESOURCE for identifying the region with anatomical criteria THAT ARE EVIDENT USING UBIQUITOUS STAINING METHODS, SUCH AS A NISSL STAIN. A definition is chosen partly for the commonness of the equipment needed to apply it — “The two major aims of this study were therefore (i) to provide a resource for identifying mouse PPC with anatomical criteria that are evident using ubiquitous staining methods, such as a Nissl stain, and (ii) to define where PPC falls in relation to extrastriate areas around V1.”
mechanismWHEN THE ANATOMY OFFERS NO LANDMARKS, FUNCTION IS BORROWED TO SERVE AS ONE: since the dorsal cortical surface in rodents LACKS GROSS ANATOMICAL LANDMARKS, the pattern of responses in the surrounding areas has provided INCREASINGLY-USED FUNCTIONAL LANDMARKS for locating higher visual and associative regions. A location is fixed by what a region DOES because there is nothing to see — “Since the dorsal cortical surface in rodents lacks gross anatomical landmarks, the pattern of responses in extrastriate areas have provided increasinglyused functional landmarks for locating higher visual (Garrett et al., 2014) and associative regions in the posterior cortex”
mechanismAND THE BORROWED METHOD CARRIES ITS OWN PRECONDITION, WHICH IS STATED: functional mapping of that kind is NOT FEASIBLE IN THE ABSENCE OF a broadly expressed calcium indicator or intrinsic optical imaging - so the substitute for the missing definition is itself unavailable to laboratories without those tools — “However, functional mapping of this kind is not feasible in the absence of a broadly expressed calcium indicator or intrinsic optical imaging, and the location of these areas relative to PPC had not been defined explicitly until now.”
mechanismTHE TWO NAMING SYSTEMS ARE FOUND TO OVERLAP PARTIALLY, NOT CLEANLY, AND THE PATTERN IS REPORTED PIECE BY PIECE: the anterior pole of the region DOES NOT OVERLAP with any of the neighbouring areas, the posterior sectors OVERLAP PARTLY with two of them, and its most lateral and posterior extent LARGELY OVERLAPPED with a third. Neither vocabulary's units nest inside the other's — “we established that the anterior pole of PPC does not overlap with any extrastriate areas, whereas the posterior sectors of PPC overlap partly with areas A and AM. The most lateral and posterior extents of PPC, area PtP (Paxinos & Franklin, 2012), largely overlapped with area RL.”
mechanismA NAME IS SHOWN TO ASSERT SOMETHING ITS OWN EVIDENCE COMPLICATES: these areas ARE REFERRED TO AS EXTRASTRIATE, IMPLYING A PRIMACY OF VISUAL PROCESSING - THOUGH they OVERLAP CONSIDERABLY with a region the paper characterises as cognitive. The label's morphology makes a claim about what the territory is for, and the overlap is the counter-evidence — “These areas are referred to as “extrastriate”, implying a primacy of visual processing (Wang & Burkhalter, 2007; Andermann et al., 2011; Marshel et al., 2011; Garrett et al., 2014), though they overlap considerably with PPC, which has cognitive”
relation — “A main goal of this study was to describe the location of extrastriate areas as described by Montero (1993) and Wang & Burkhalter (2007) in relation to the laminar, cyto- and chemoarchitectural features that distinguish PPC in the mouse.”
relation — “By characterizing the architectural boundaries of PPC and cross-referencing them with projections from V1 (Wang & Burkhalter, 2007), we established that the anterior pole of PPC does not overlap with any extrastriate areas, whereas the posterior sectors of PPC overlap partly with areas A and AM.”

limitationWhere associative, motor and sensory inputs are integrated synaptically inside PPC, and whether modality-specific topographies exist there, remain unmapped. — “As for mice, it remains to be mapped more fully where the inputs from associative, motor, and sensory areas are integrated synaptically within PPC, and whether graded topographies exists for different sensory modalities, as shown for visual and vibrissal afferents in area RL (Olcese et al., 2013).”
limitationThe mapping was done only against extrastriate visual projections; equivalent mapping against somatosensory, auditory or motor inputs was not performed. — “While the present study focused on characterizing PPC relative to extrastriate projections, comparable mapping could likely be performed in the context of somatosensory, auditory or motor inputs.”

findingMedial PPC is marked out from its neighbours by a drop in parvalbumin staining in both cell bodies and neuropil, against dense PV staining in adjacent retrosplenial and somatosensory cortex. — “A salient cellular marker for the appearance of mPPC is the decrease in PV staining in both cell bodies and the neuropil (Figure 1, middle column, row 2), which contrasts with the dense staining medially in layers II-V of RSA, and the darker staining in layers IV and V of S1 (Figure 1, rows 1 and 2).”
findingA sharp fall in M2 muscarinic acetylcholine receptor staining in superficial layers marks the border between agranular retrosplenial cortex and medial PPC. — “The border between RSA and mPPC is marked by a sharp decrease in M2AChR staining in the superficial parietal layers, which returns laterally in S1 (Figure 1, right column, row 2).”
findingBecause V2M closely resembles PPC in Nissl and staining terms, the appearance of primary visual cortex is the most reliable sign that a coronal section lies caudal to all PPC subdivisions. — “Here, the emergence of V1 is marked by strong M2AChR staining directly posterior to lPPC and PtP; again the appearance of V1 is the best indicator of being caudal to all PPC sub-fields in the coronal plane.”
findingThe anterior pole of PPC overlaps no extrastriate area, its posterior sectors overlap areas A and AM, and its most lateral-posterior part PtP overlaps largely with area RL - reconciling the Paxinos-Franklin parietal nomenclature with the Montero/Wang-Burkhalter extrastriate one. — “we established that the anterior pole of PPC does not overlap with any extrastriate areas, whereas the posterior sectors of PPC overlap partly with areas A and AM. The most lateral and posterior extents of PPC, area PtP (Paxinos & Franklin, 2012), largely overlapped with area RL.”
findingAt -1.91mm from bregma all labelled fibres of extrastriate area A fall inside lateral PPC, with no extrastriate label in medial PPC. — “As shown in Figure 3B, at -1.91mm posterior to bregma the entire complement of labeled fibers for area A is contained in lPPC, with no apparent extrastriate labeling in mPPC.”
findingAnterograde injections confined to PPC label the associative thalamic nuclei LP, Po and, more anteriorly, LD. — “As shown in Figures 4A and B, tracer injections contained wholly within the cytoarchitectonic boundaries of PPC produced robust anterograde labeling in LP and Po, and farther anterior sections contained strong projections confined to LD (Supplementary Figure 1).”
findingPPC thalamic output was specific to associative nuclei, sparing the adjacent primary relay nuclei DLG (visual) and VPM (somatosensory). — “In all cases, the thalamic projections were specific to associative nuclei with no staining in the immediately adjacent dorsal lateral geniculate nucleus (DLG), which receives projections from V1, nor in the ventral posterior medial nucleus (VPM), which receives projections from S1.”
findingParietal output to frontal cortex is coarsely topographic: medial PPC projects to cingulate cortex and lateral PPC to secondary motor cortex. — “The injections also produced strong labeling in cingulate (Cg) and secondary motor (M2) cortices, which appeared to follow a coarsely topographical distribution, with medial PPC projecting medially toward Cg, and lateral PPC projecting more laterally into M2 (Figure 5C-D, left).”
findingMonosynaptic rabies tracing showed PPC receives input from every cortical area to which it sends anterograde label, so all these cortical connections are reciprocal. — “To determine whether these cortical outputs of PPC were reciprocal, we performed monosynaptic circuit tracing with rabies virus in a parallel series of mice (n = 4, Figure 5A, right) (Wickersham et al., 2007), which yielded unequivocal evidence that PPC received monosynaptic inputs from each cortical area with anterograde labeling (Figure 5B-E, right).”
findingPPC's densest cortical projections go to extrastriate areas AM/V2M and AL/V2L, with AM/V2M showing a medial-to-lateral topography matching the injection sites and AL/V2L sometimes showing a mirrored order. — “The densest projections from PPC were to extrastriate areas AM/V2M and AL/V2L (Figure 6B, middle and bottom), with AM/V2M showing a medial-to-lateral topography in line with the location of tracer injections in PPC, and AL/V2L in some cases showing a mirrored ordering”
findingV1 projects most strongly to the lateromedial field immediately lateral to it and to the laterointermediate field beyond that. — “Similar to their findings, we report a particularly strong projection from V1 to the lateromedial (LM) field, located immediately lateral to V1, and to the laterointermediate (LI) field lateral to LM (Figure 2A).”
