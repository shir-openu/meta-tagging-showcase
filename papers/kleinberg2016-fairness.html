sensefairness - AS CALIBRATION WITHIN GROUPS (condition A): within each group, the people in a bin scored v really do belong to the positive class at rate v, i.e. the scores mean what they claim to mean when read separately in each group— “Calibration within groups requires that for each group t, and each bin b with associated score vb, the expected number of people from group t in b who belong to the positive class should be a vb fraction of the expected number of people from group t assigned to b”
sensefairness - AS BALANCE FOR THE NEGATIVE CLASS (condition B): people who do NOT have the property get the same average score whichever group they are in, so the errors are not systematically worse for one group's negative instances— “Balance for the negative class requires that the average score assigned to people of group 1 who belong to the negative class should be the same as the average score assigned to people of group 2 who belong to the negative class. In other words, the assignment of scores shouldn’t be systematically more inaccurate for negative instances in one…
sensefairness - AS BALANCE FOR THE POSITIVE CLASS (condition C): people who DO have the property get the same average score whichever group they are in - the condition the COMPAS critique turned on— “Balance for the positive class symmetrically requires that the average score assigned to people of group 1 who belong to the positive class should be the same as the average score assigned to people of group 2 who belong to the positive class”
sensethe impossibility - AS A FACT ABOUT BASE RATES, NOT ABOUT A DOMAIN: the authors state that the trade-off is not a fact about medicine but about risk estimates whenever base rates differ, which is the paper stating its own transfer condition— “The point is that this trade-off among (a), (b), and (c) is not a fact about medicine; it is simply a fact about risk estimates when the base rates differ between two groups”
