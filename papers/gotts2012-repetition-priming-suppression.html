phenomenonrepetition priming and repetition suppression— “Stimulus repetition in identification tasks leads to improved behavioral performance (“repetition priming”) but attenuated neural responses (“repetition suppression”) throughout task-engaged cortical regions.”
claim-typemechanism— “In this Discussion Paper, we review four different theoretical proposals that have the potential to link repetition suppression and priming, with a particular focus on a proposal that stimulus repetition affects improved efficiency through enhanced neural synchronization.”
method — evidence— “Below, we review four of the main theoretical proposals about what form these solutions might take (see also Grill-Spector et al., 2006).”
builds-on Gotts (2003) neocortical circuit model— “In a simplified neocortical circuit model that incorporated biologically proportionate numbers of excitatory and inhibitory cells and short-term plasticity mechanisms, Gotts (2003) showed that it was possible to simultaneously address short-lag repetition suppression and priming effects through enhanced synchronization.”
contradicts the Facilitation model (Henson, 2003; James & Gauthier, 2006; James et al., 2000)— “Having reviewed four basic proposals as to how repetition suppression might afford repetition priming, the only view that we consider definitively ruled out by current data is the facilitation model, at least in its existing form.”
contradicts Friston's Bayesian 'explaining away' / predictive coding account (Friston, 2005)— “Nevertheless, we do not believe that it is self-evident that the brain behaves in its details as a Bayesian neural network model, at least one that relies on anti-symmetrical coupling in the feed-forward versus feedback directions.”
replicates Gilbert et al. (2010)— “We found that stimulus repetition led to repetition priming in both patients and increases in low-frequency evoked power (1–15 Hz) for virtually all task-engaged electrodes (i.e., those that exhibited significant evoked responses), replicating the basic pattern of Gilbert et al. (2010).”
sense**THE PUZZLE, STATED AS A PUZZLE, WHICH IS WHY THIS RECORD IS WORTH HAVING:** `How is it that REDUCTIONS IN NEURAL ACTIVITY CAN MEDIATE BETTER BEHAVIORAL PERFORMANCE?` **The corpus now holds four positions on that question, all merged today and none of them citing another in this set:** Desimone1996 calls suppression `an intrinsic property of visual cortical areas`; Carlesimo2004 reads an rCBF decrease as `the reduced neural activity needed to reprocess recently seen words`; mcmahon-olson2007 concludes suppression `ACCOMPANIES repetition priming but is UNLIKELY TO BE ITS CAUSE`; and this paper says the puzzle is real and rate is the wrong variable— “However, the relationship between repetition priming and repetition suppression also presents a major puzzle: How is it that reductions in neural activity can mediate better behavioral performance”
sense**AND THE ANSWER IS A REPLY TO McMAHON AND OLSON RATHER THAN A CONTRADICTION OF THEM.** `as cells are firing at LOWER OVERALL RATES, they are firing MORE SYNCHRONOUSLY with one another, leading to more efficient neural processing` - and in simulation, `as the model's firing rates DECREASED with repetition, the synchronization of the spike times SIMULTANEOUSLY INCREASED.` **Both papers agree the rate drop is not the mechanism.** They differ on whether anything else in the same signal is: McMahon and Olson tested rate and selectivity and found neither sufficient; Gotts proposes a variable neither of them measured— “Importantly, as the model’s firing rates decreased with repetition, the synchronization of the spike times simultaneously increased.”
sense**IT NAMES THE GAP IN ITS OWN EVIDENCE, WHICH A PROPOSAL PAPER USUALLY DOES NOT:** `despite exciting recent work on the role of neural synchronization in cognitive processes such as attention and perception, similar studies in the domain of learning and memory - and priming, in particular - have been LACKING.` **The mechanism is offered together with the statement that the direct evidence for it has not been collected in this domain.** Compare meyer1971's `At present we do not have ways to test all the possible explanations of these effects` - forty-one years apart, the same willingness to write the limitation flat— “We argue that despite exciting recent work on the role of neural synchronization in cognitive processes such as attention and perception, similar studies in the domain of learning and memory ––and priming, in particular––have been lacking.”
sense`sharpening` - A NAMED ACCOUNT WHOSE NAME ENCODES ITS MECHANISM: `A second idea, OFTEN REFERRED TO AS “SHARPENING” (Desimone, 1996; Wiggs & Martin, 1998), holds that while neural activity is decreasing ON THE AVERAGE, the decreases are carried mainly by THE CELLS THAT ARE POORLY TUNED and/or weakly responsive ... with the “BEST” CELLS ... INSTEAD RETAINING THEIR ACTIVITY LEVELS.` THE WORD IS THE HYPOTHESIS: a population response gets NARROWER, not smaller, and the mean decrease that `repetition suppression` names is a side effect of that narrowing. Two names for one measurement, differing in what they say caused it.— “A second idea, often referred to as “sharpening” (Desimone, 1996; Wiggs & Martin, 1998), holds that while neural activity is decreasing on the average, the decreases are carried mainly by the cells that are poorly tuned and/or weakly responsive to the repeated stimuli with the “best” cells (i.e., most selective/responsive) instead…
sense`priming` NARROWED BY STIPULATION - BUT NOT BY THIS PAPER'S AUTHORS: `the complex relationship between repetition suppression (RS) and BEHAVIORAL PRIMING (HENCEFORTH REFERRED TO AS PRIMING)`. **VOICE: this sentence is from a PEER COMMENTARY printed with the article, not from Gotts, Chow and Martin** - it opens `Gotts et al. present four neural models` and continues `I strongly support Gotts et al.'s call`. So the stipulation binds the COMMENTATOR's text, and a reader who takes it as the target article's own definition has attributed a narrowing to the wrong authors.— “Gotts et al. present four neural models designed to capture the complex relationship between repetition suppression (RS) and behavioral priming (henceforth referred to as priming).”
sense`Explaining Away` / `Predictive Coding` - THE SAME MODEL, TWO LABELS, AND THE DISAGREEMENT IS PRINTED. `EWBANK AND HENSON APPEAR TO TAKE ISSUE WITH OUR USE OF THE LABEL “EXPLAINING AWAY” when referring to Friston's Bayesian model, PREFERRING INSTEAD “PREDICTIVE CODING”.` **This is a naming dispute conducted in the literature rather than reconstructed from it** - commentators object, authors answer, and both names go on being used. The authors' stated rationale is DISCRIMINATION: a label that separates the anti-symmetrical property from `the variety of models that utilize prediction in very different ways`. **Neither party thinks the model is in doubt; they disagree about what the name must exclude.**— “Ewbank and Henson appear to take issue with our use of the label “Explaining Away” when referring to Friston’s Bayesian model, preferring instead “Predictive Coding”.”
sense`priming` / `repetition suppression` - TWO CORE TERMS WHOSE REFERENTS DIFFER BY ORDERS OF MAGNITUDE IN TIME. `The terms “PRIMING” and “REPETITION SUPPRESSION” are COMMONLY USED TO REFER TO PHENOMENA OCCURRING ON TIME SCALES THAT CAN DIFFER BY SEVERAL ORDERS OF MAGNITUDE, ranging from seconds to days or even years.` **The words are stable and what they denote is not.** A corpus joining two papers on `priming` may be joining a 200-millisecond effect to one lasting a year, and nothing in either paper's use of the word records which.— “The terms “priming” and “repetition suppression” are commonly used to refer to phenomena occurring on time scales that can differ by several orders of magnitude, ranging from seconds to days or even years.”
