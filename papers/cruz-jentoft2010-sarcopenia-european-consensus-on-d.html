mechanismAND THE ABSENCE IS ENUMERATED, INCLUDING THE ADMINISTRATIVE ONE: `sarcopenia still has NO BROADLY ACCEPTED CLINICAL DEFINITION, CONSENSUS DIAGNOSTIC CRITERIA, INTERNATIONAL CLASSIFICATION OF DISEASES 9TH REVISION (ICD-9) CODES OR TREATMENT GUIDELINES.` A missing ICD code is a missing SLOT IN A CONTROLLED VOCABULARY, listed beside the missing definition as the same kind of lack - the clearest statement in this corpus that a definition and its administrative code are one problem— “While age-related sarcopenia is common and has huge personal and financial costs, sarcopenia still has no broadly accepted clinical definition, consensus diagnostic criteria, International Classification of Diseases 9th Revision (ICD-9) codes or treatment guidelines.”
mechanismAND A BETTER TERM WAS REJECTED BECAUSE THE WORSE ONE IS ALREADY KNOWN: `Some have argued that THE TERM DYNAPENIA IS BETTER SUITED to describe age-associated loss of muscle strength and function. However, SARCOPENIA IS ALREADY A WIDELY RECOGNISED TERM, SO REPLACING IT MIGHT LEAD TO FURTHER CONFUSION.` **THE EXACT MIRROR OF haas2018**: Banff DELETED the word `acute` because it confused clinicians; EWGSOP KEPT `sarcopenia`, against a term it concedes is better suited, because replacing it would confuse. Two consensus bodies, opposite decisions, the same reason - and neither decision is about the biology— “Some have argued that the term dynapenia is better suited to describe age-associated loss of muscle strength and function [ 20 ]. However, sarcopenia is already a widely recognised term, so replacing it might lead to further confusion.”
mechanismAND THE PREVALENCE RANGE IS ATTRIBUTED TO THE DEFINITION IN ONE SENTENCE: `DEPENDING ON THE LITERATURE DEFINITION USED FOR SARCOPENIA, the prevalence in 60-70-year-olds is reported as 5-13%, while the prevalence ranges from 11 TO 50% in people >80 years.` bellomo2004 and hoste2006 each gave a range and left the reader to infer the cause; this paper names it— “Depending on the literature definition used for sarcopenia, the prevalence in 60–70-year-olds is reported as 5–13%, while the prevalence ranges from 11 to 50% in people >80 years [ 71 ].”

mechanismAND THE INTENSION IS DECLARED TOO NARROW, WITH THE REASON: `muscle strength DOES NOT DEPEND SOLELY ON MUSCLE MASS, and the relationship between strength and mass IS NOT LINEAR. Thus, DEFINING SARCOPENIA ONLY IN TERMS OF MUSCLE MASS IS TOO NARROW and may be of limited clinical value.` The two-criterion rule is derived from a non-linearity rather than asserted— “The rationale for use of two criteria is: muscle strength does not depend solely on muscle mass, and the relationship between strength and mass is not linear [ 18, 19 ]. Thus, defining sarcopenia only in terms of muscle mass is too narrow and may be of limited clinical value.”
mechanismand the category is adopted BECAUSE the causes are unknown, not despite it: `We suggest it may be likewise helpful to RECOGNISE SARCOPENIA AS A GERIATRIC SYNDROME BECAUSE THIS VIEW PROMOTES ITS IDENTIFICATION AND TREATMENT EVEN WHEN THE EXACT CAUSES REMAIN UNKNOWN.` A class chosen for what it lets clinicians do, with the aetiological ignorance stated as part of the justification— “We suggest it may be likewise helpful to recognise sarcopenia as a geriatric syndrome because this view promotes its identification and treatment even when the exact causes remain unknown [ 5, 6 ].”
