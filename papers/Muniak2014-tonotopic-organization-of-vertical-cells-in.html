phenomenontonotopic organisation of dorsal cochlear nucleus vertical cells
claim-typeexistence
transferfrom cochlear anatomy: a frequency model built from AN innervation by cochlear origin, imported as an external criterion against which cell positions are predicted — “This model is based on the distribution of AN innervation in the CN with respect to cochlear origin, and makes no assumptions about nor imposes directionality to frequency organization throughout the volume of the nucleus.”
transferfrom psychoacoustics of the outer ear: the pinna transfer function and the frequency range of spectral notches, used to explain an anatomical cell-count gradient — “With the mouse ear, spectral notches are largely observed in the >20 kHz range ( Lauer et al., 2011 ), which is where our vertical cell counts are the lowest. Furthermore, the transfer function of the mouse ear shows overall gains in the 10 – 20 kHz range ( Lauer et al., 2011 ), where our cell counts are higher.”
mechanismAuditory-nerve input pattern sets each cochlear nucleus neuron's best frequency, so the cochleotopic auditory-nerve projection imposes tonotopy on vertical cells — “Given evidence that the pattern of AN input largely determines the BF of neurons in the CN ( Bourk et al., 1981 ; Fekete et al., 1984 ; Spirou et al., 1993 ; Ryugo and May, 1993 ), it is reasonable to conclude that the arrangement of vertical cell BFs would follow suit.”
mechanismVertical cell axons ramify locally inside an isofrequency slab, which is what makes their inhibition of pyramidal (type IV) cells fall at or near best frequency — “Thus, the near-BF inhibition of type IV units (pyramidal cells) by type II units is a result of the local ramification of vertical cell axons within an isofrequency slab.”
mechanismFrequency-specific inhibitory demand in the cochlear nucleus, set by the gains and spectral notches of the mouse ear transfer function, shapes how many vertical cells are found at each frequency — “Hence, the distribution of vertical cell numbers might relate to the overall inhibitory requirements in the CN with respect to frequency.”

limitationInjection size, a possible confound for the cell counts, could not be measured reliably. — “Quantifying the size of an injection site is notoriously difficult due to the ambiguity in determining the edge of the effective uptake region.”
limitationThe functional role of vertical cells is not settled by these anatomical results and needs further data. — “Overall, these results suggest that vertical cells provide rapid on- or near-BF inhibition both to cells of the DCN and AVCN, although more data are needed to better understand the function of these neurons.”

findingVertical cells labelled from low-best-frequency AVCN injections lie in the anteroventral DCN and those labelled from high-best-frequency injections lie in the posterodorsal DCN, so vertical cells are tonotopically arranged. — “This relationship indicated that cells labeled by injections with the lowest BFs were distributed in the most anteroventral region of the DCN, whereas cells matched to high frequencies were restricted to the posterodorsal regions.”
findingA single spatial axis captures three quarters of the positional variance, so the frequency arrangement of vertical cells is essentially one-dimensional. — “The first principal component accounted for 75% of the variance in the dataset ( Fig. 5D ), indicating vertical cells are largely organized along one dimension in the DCN with respect to frequency.”
findingA linear equation predicting log best frequency from the three normalised spatial coordinates fitted the data closely. — “This equation produced a strong and significant correlation between BF and spatial position ( R 2 = 0.87, P < 0.0001).”
findingBest frequencies predicted for each vertical cell by an independent model of auditory-nerve innervation matched the measured best frequencies, showing vertical cell tonotopy is aligned with the peripheral input map. — “This result suggests that the frequency organization of vertical cells is closely matched to the tonotopic organization of primary inputs from the periphery.”
findingAlmost nine in ten labelled vertical cells fell inside an independently reconstructed deep-layer surface, confirming that vertical cells are confined to the deep layer of the DCN. — “Only 11.8% of the labeled cells (148/1259) were located outside the boundary of the deep layers, separated by an average distance of 15.3 ± 11.4 µm ( Fig. 5C ).”
findingInjections at low best frequencies labelled more vertical cells than high-frequency injections, an unexplained asymmetry in cell numbers across the frequency axis. — “Low BF injections tended to label more vertical cells than high BF injections ( Fig. 5B ; R 2 = 0.32, P < 0.003), though the reason for this tonotopic feature is unclear.”
findingThe number of labelled vertical cells was not explained by injection size, because the extent of cochlear labelling correlated poorly with cell counts. — “There was a poor relationship, however, between these parameters ( R = 0.34, P = 0.092; see “Spread %” in Table 2 from Muniak et al., 2013 ), suggesting our counts were not significantly skewed by variations in injection size.”
findingPairs of vertical cells that were further apart in space differed more in best frequency, although only moderately, because cells of similar frequency can sit at opposite ends of adjacent frequency planes. — “Cells further separated in space tended to have greater differences in measured BF, though the relationship was moderate ( R 2 = 0.64, P < 0.0001).”
