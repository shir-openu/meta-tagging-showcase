sensebehavioral priming, OPERATIONALLY - and the inclusion criteria are the definition: word primes, INCIDENTALLY presented, a NON-OPPOSITE control group, and a BEHAVIOURAL dependent variable. Each clause excludes something. `Incidentally` rules out instruction; `behavioral dependent variable` rules out the judgement studies that most of this corpus's priming records use; and `non-opposite control` quietly excludes designs whose control condition primes the contrary concept, which would double the apparent effect— “in which word primes were incidentally presented to participants, with a non-opposite control group, prior to measuring a behavioral dependent variable. sensethe effect, as a NUMBER rather than as an existence claim: d(FE) = 0.332, d(RE) = 0.352 across 352 effect sizes from 133 studies. `Small` is the paper's own word for it. The corpus holds dozens of records asserting that priming happens; this is the one that says HOW MUCH, and the size is what most of the surrounding controversy is actually about— “Findings revealed a small behavioral priming effect ( d FE = 0.332, d RE = 0.352), which was robust across methodological procedures and only minimally biased by the publication of positive (vs. negative) results”
sensegoal activation versus perception-behavior, ADJUDICATED WITHOUT A WINNER BEING DECLARED. Two mechanisms this corpus has been collecting from separate papers, measured against each other for the first time, and the answer is both— “there was some evidence of persistence of goal effects over time. senseA MODERATOR NULL REPORTED IN THE ABSTRACT - expectancy, the perceived probability of achieving the goal, did not moderate priming. It was predicted to matter by two literatures that disagree about the DIRECTION (Forster: high expectancy strengthens pursuit; Locke & Latham: difficult goals intensify effort), so a null defeats both. A meta-analysis that puts its non-finding in the abstract alongside its finding— “There was no evidence that expectancy (ease of achieving the goal) moderated priming effects. The worst is yet to come for priming”
