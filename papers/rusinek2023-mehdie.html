content observations: finding · quantity · definition · variable · statistic · key-term — each grounded in a verbatim quotation from the paper
findingGeneral-purpose gazetteers carry almost none of the Semitic-script toponyms that Middle Eastern historical sources actually use. — “Geonames and the Getty Thesaurus of geographic names are two such resources, but they contain few, if any, toponyms in the Semitic scripts relevant to the sources of Middle Eastern History.”
findingWikidata, although multilingual, also matches these sources poorly. — “Even Wikidata, which is essentially multilingual, shows low recall when matching against them.”
findingA concrete gap: Wikidata holds a modern Hebrew variant of the toponym but not the archaic spelling the historical source uses. — “Wikidata currently includes a Hebrew alternative variant without the additional vowel, but not a variant with the more archaic letter”
findingMatching between two Semitic languages reduces ambiguity, whereas transliterating either into Latin script adds it. — “This shows the power of transliteration between semitic languages that reduces, rather than adds ambiguity”
findingThe authors draw a general conclusion for linked open data: prefer a bottom-up, intra-domain strategy over a centralised one. — “opens a meta-discussion on approaches for linked open data and the preference of a “bottom-up” intra-domain strategy over a centralised one”
