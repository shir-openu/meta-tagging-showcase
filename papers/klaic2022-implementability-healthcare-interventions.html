Adequate fidelityterm·dom will also require other enabling factors such as provider training and confirmed information flow. Without adequate fidelityterm·dom, it would be wasteful to conduct a feasibilityterm·key study. Factors such as appropriate resources, workforce, technology, and management will be required for feasibilityterm·key. If feasibilityterm·key (supported by acceptabilityterm·dom and fidelityterm·dom) in the research context is adequate, it is appropriate to consider testing acceptabilityterm·dom, fidelityterm·dom and feasibilityterm·key in the healthcare context. If adopted consistently in one healthcare context, it is appropriate to consider scaling the intervention to other settings, provider groups and patient groups. In each new setting, it would be wise to re-assess acceptabilityterm·dom, fidelityterm·dom and feasibilityterm·key as described above because adequate feasibilityterm·key in one setting at one time, whilst a positive sign, is not a guarantee that the intervention will be feasible in other settings. Discussion Based on the findings from the overview of reviewsterm·mth and the group consensus process, we propose a framework of implementabilityterm·dom of healthcare interventionsterm·dom which includes five key concepts, namely, acceptabilityterm·dom, fidelityterm·dom, feasibilityterm·key, scalabilityterm·dom and sustainabilityterm·key. The framework illustrates the interrelationship between the concepts and chronology, with acceptabilityterm·dom, fidelityterm·dom and feasibilityterm·key requiring investigation during early stages of the development of a healthcare implementationterm·dom, including during proof-of-principle studies and pragmatic evaluations of intervention effectiveness at one point in time and in one specific context. Acceptabilityterm·dom is a necessary but not sufficient condition for fidelityterm·dom, and similarly, fidelityterm·dom is a necessary but not sufficient condition for feasibilityterm·key. All three concepts are context- and population-dependent and will require reinvestigation as the healthcare interventionterm·dom is scaled to different settings and populations and over time. We argue that there is an association between the concepts, with acceptabilityterm·dom, fidelityterm·dom and feasibilityterm·key influencing the scalabilityterm·dom and sustainabilityterm·key of a healthcare interventionterm·dom. We are not suggesting that there is a causal relationship between the concepts, rather, scalabilityterm·dom and sustainabilityterm·key depend on the pre-conditions acceptabilityterm·dom, fidelityterm·dom and feasibilityterm·key of the healthcare interventionterm·dom, and these concepts should be re-examined over time, and as the healthcare interventionterm·dom is implemented with different populations or in different settings. This argument is consistent with other frameworks on scalabilityterm·dom and sustainabilityterm·key, including the Intervention Scalabilityterm·dom Assessment Tool [ 82 ] and the Dynamic Sustainabilityterm·key Framework [ 98 ], both of which suggest that feasibilityterm·key, acceptabilityterm·dom and fidelityterm·dom must be considered in the planning for scaling up and sustainabilityterm·key of a healthcare interventionterm·dom. The Consolidated Framework for Implementationterm·dom Research [ 12 ] suggests that the outer setting, including the social and economic context, can influence implementationterm·dom. Our proposed framework encourages the researcher to prospectively assess acceptabilityterm·dom, fidelityterm·dom and feasibilityterm·key in both the inner and outer contexts as the key stakeholders are likely to be different.

From the 252 reviewsterm·mth identified in the overview, the majority did not provide a definition of the concept. Rather, the reviewsterm·mth used measurement approaches which implied a definition. For example, feasibilityterm·key was typically defined by measuring components such as compliance to the intervention, dropouts, recruitment rates and adverse events. There was conflating of terms, with feasibilityterm·key and safety used interchangeably in several reviewsterm·mth, particularly drug feasibilityterm·key reviewsterm·mth. Although frameworks were identified for all five concepts, they were not frequently used in the reviewsterm·mth identified in the overview. Most of the frameworks included conceptual definitions and operationalisation of the concept, but these varied between frameworks. It is difficult to test the influence of these concepts on the implementabilityterm·dom of a healthcare interventionterm·dom without consistent definitions, descriptions, operationalisation and measurement approaches for these concepts.

Of all the concepts explored in the systematic overview, scalabilityterm·dom and sustainabilityterm·key of healthcare interventionsterm·dom were not often achieved. These findings suggest that healthcare interventionsterm·dom may be found to be effective, acceptable and feasible in the development or pilot phase, but this does not guarantee successful scale-up or sustainment of the intervention over time. We propose that the framework of implementabilityterm·dom can provide a dynamic, longitudinal perspective of intervention development where researchers consider acceptabilityterm·dom, fidelityterm·dom and feasibilityterm·key during the earlier phases of intervention development and implementationterm·dom, and iteratively re-evaluate these factors as the healthcare interventionterm·dom is scaled to different settings and over time.

Whilst there are some similarities in the concepts contained in these frameworks and our proposed framework of implementabilityterm·dom, the latter is explicitly concerned with the prospective and ongoing identification of factors that will influence scalabilityterm·dom and sustainabilityterm·key of a healthcare interventionterm·dom. Strengths and limitations To our knowledge, this is the first reviewterm·mth that considers all five key concepts in published reviewsterm·mth on healthcare interventionsterm·dom. The overview collated and described important information on concepts that are increasingly being assumed to influence implementationterm·dom of healthcare interventionsterm·dom. The development of a framework utilising well-established consensus methods is another strength. Although one author was responsible for most of the screening, data extraction and coding, independent extraction by a second author of 10% of the reviewsterm·mth confirmed reliability of the extraction process.

In order to the make the overview of reviewsterm·mth feasible, we only focused on publications that had one or more of the concepts in the title and/or abstract. Therefore, it is possible we may not have identified some reviewsterm·mth that were relevant. It must also be noted that the framework of implementabilityterm·dom of healthcare interventionsterm·dom is untested. We propose that it articulates some of the untested assumptions in the current literature on implementation scienceterm·key and have suggested some approaches for empirical evaluation of the framework.

We do not propose that interventions with high implementabilityterm·dom will automatically result in high uptake. As we argued in the background to this paper, the features of interventions may interact with top-down and bottom-up implementationterm·dom activities, and with contextual factors, to achieve consistent uptake into routine practice. Our argument is that these implementationterm·dom activities are more likely to be effective if implementabilityterm·dom of the intervention is high.

Conclusions The framework developed in this study can inform research that aims to prospectively and iteratively identify the likely implementabilityterm·dom of evidence-based healthcare interventionsterm·dom. We suggest that the framework be tested empirically through studies that examine the actual uptake of interventions, across settings and over time, compared with prospective assessments of the independent variables (acceptabilityterm·dom, fidelityterm·dom and feasibilityterm·key) and the outcome variables (scalabilityterm·dom and sustainabilityterm·key) in the framework. We recommend that, to avoid research wasteterm·key, implementabilityterm·dom should be assessed, and enhancements made, during the clinical evaluation stages of the development of interventions [ 1 , 3 ]. This would potentially accelerate their uptake into clinical practice.
