phenomenonauditory evoked potentials as a measure of anaesthetic depth
claim-typemethod
mechanismat ~40 stimuli per second each new stimulus arrives during wave Pa of the preceding response, so successive middle-latency responses overlap and progressively build up into the steady-state response — “With stimulus rates near 40/second, the next stimulus occurs during wave Pa of the AMLR evoked by the preceding stimuli, thus allowing progressive build-up of the 40 Hz ASSR by superimposition of the individual AMLR.”
mechanismthe 40 Hz ASSR is generated partly by anesthetic-sensitive synchronization of naturally occurring neuronal rhythms, and/or anesthetics disrupt the superimposition process by non-uniformly changing the refractory periods of AMLR components — “This therefore suggests that the 40 Hz ASSR partly arises from synchronization of naturally occurring neuronal rhythms63 (that are affected by general anesthetics64) or that general anesthetics interfere with the superimposition process (possibly by not uniformly affecting the refractory period of the AMLR components).”
mechanismgeneral anesthetics abolish consciousness by interfering with thalamocortical 40 Hz rhythms, which is why the 40 Hz response and consciousness disappear together — “An example is the hypothesis66 that general anesthetics impair consciousness by interfering with thalamocortical 40 Hz rhythms.”
relation — “Presence of PAMR led Pockett et al3 to conclude erroneously4 that the 40 Hz ASSR is not a suitable monitor of anesthesia.”
relation — “We have examined the effect of enflurane/N2O anesthesia on the AMLR and 40 Hz ASSR and found that attenuation of the 40 Hz ASSR was much more pronounced that predicted by alterations of the AMLR.52”
replicationuntested
limitationScope restricted to human studies for reasons of space — “Because of space constraints, this review will be limited to human studies.”
limitationThe association between ASSR attenuation and loss of consciousness is correlational, not demonstrated to be functional — “These observations suggest but do not prove that there is functional relationship between the attenuation of the 40 Hz ASSR and loss of consciousness.”
limitationApplicability to real-world multi-drug anaesthetic regimens is untested — “Applicability multi-drug regimen commonly seen in clinical practice remains to be assessed, as for BIS and other monitoring modalities.71”
limitationWhether ASSR parameters other than amplitude could support titration has not been examined — “Whether other parameters of the ASSR (phase alone or combined with amplitude, use of either stimulation frequencies.) could be useful has not been examined.”
limitationThe monitor and the patient's state may both be effects of the drug rather than related to each other, and the review says so of its own subject. — “Loss of consciousness and attenuation of the 40 Hz ASSR (or of the AMLR, BIS,.) could, however, be effects that both result from the presence of the anesthetic in the brain but that are otherwise functionally independent.”
limitationThe supporting evidence is explicitly said to fall short of proof. — “These observations suggest but do not prove that there is functional relationship”
limitationWhole components of the response are excluded from clinical use for being TOO sensitive, so the monitored signal is selected for stability rather than for sensitivity. — “The slow and late responses reflect higher level of processing and are considered too sensitive to anesthetic effect for routine clinical monitoring and will not be discussed here further.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingMost volatile and intravenous general anesthetics lengthen the latency and reduce the amplitude of the auditory middle latency response, and do so in proportion to concentration. — “There is ample evidence that most general anesthetics (halothane, enflurane, isoflurane, desflurane, sevoflurane, propofol, etomidate, althesin, xenon) increase the latency and decreased the amplitude of the AMLR in a concentration-dependent manner.”
findingNitrous oxide and ketamine leave the main AMLR components unchanged even at concentrations that abolish consciousness, so the AMLR does not track consciousness under these two drugs. — “N2O15 and ketamine17 cause no change of the main components of the AMLR, even at concentrations producing unconsciousness.”
findingKetamine raises rather than lowers the amplitude of the 40 Hz steady-state response, opposite to every other anesthetic reviewed. — “Unlike other anesthetics, ketamine increased the amplitude of the 40 Hz ASSR.”
findingReversing anesthetic hypnosis with physostigmine while the anesthetic concentration is held constant brings the 40 Hz ASSR back in proportion to the behavioural reversal, indicating that ASSR attenuation reflects impaired consciousness rather than mere drug presence. — “The attenuation of the 40 Hz ASSR by general anesthetics thus seems to reflect the impairment of consciousness since, antagonism of the hypnotic effect of the anesthetics by physostigmine is associated with an increase in the amplitude of the 40 Hz ASSR that matches the magnitude of the hypnotic reversal.”
findingUnder enflurane/N2O the 40 Hz ASSR was suppressed far more than could be predicted from the accompanying AMLR changes, so the steady-state response cannot be merely the sum of overlapping middle-latency responses. — “We have examined the effect of enflurane/N2O anesthesia on the AMLR and 40 Hz ASSR and found that attenuation of the 40 Hz ASSR was much more pronounced that predicted by alterations of the AMLR.”
findingThe 40 Hz ASSR amplitude cannot be used to titrate anesthetic dose once consciousness is lost because it is already almost fully suppressed at that point. — “The amplitude 40 Hz ASSR does not appear useful for titration because it is already markedly reduced at loss of consciousness.”
findingNoxious stimulation increases AMLR-derived measures, so the AMLR indexes the net balance between anesthetic suppression and stimulation-driven brain activation rather than anesthetic depth alone. — “These observations suggest that the AMLR reflects the balance between anesthetic suppression and brain activation caused by noxious stimulation.”
quantity2.1 ng/ml — sufentanil concentration producing half-maximal suppression of the 40 Hz ASSR — “The sufentanil IC50 was 2.1 ng/ml.”
quantitymore than 70% — reduction in 40 Hz ASSR amplitude when sevoflurane was titrated to unconsciousness (mean 0.85% end-tidal) — “Unconsciousness induced by individual titration of sevoflurane (mean 0.85% end-tidal concentration) reduced the ASSR amplitude by more than 70%,”
quantityabout 33 Hz / 15 Hz — coherent frequency of the steady-state response when awake versus when unconscious — “The CF is about 33 Hz in awake subjects and 15 Hz during unconsciousness.”
quantityz20% — reduction of the A-Line ARX index caused by the neuromuscular blocker vecuronium during steady-state anesthesia (i.e. muscle-artefact contribution to the index) — “Ge et al showed that the AAI is reduced by vecuronium by z20% during steady-state anesthesia in patients without surgical stimuli.”
quantity10 of 11 — propofol-anesthetized volunteers in whom physostigmine restored consciousness — “Physostigmine restored consciousness in 10 of 11 volunteers who had been rendered unconscious by propofol.”
quantitytwo of eight — sevoflurane-anesthetized subjects who regained consciousness after physostigmine (three more regained wakefulness only) — “Of eight subjects, two regained consciousness, three regained wakefulness (they opened their eyes but did not follow verbal commands) and three showed no behavioral changes.”

variableinhaled isoflurane concentration (0–0.5%) (independent) — “A study with human volunteers55 showed that the 40 Hz ASSR is attenuated in a concentration-dependent manner by isoflurane 0–0.5%; that the attenuation is nearly maximal at 0.5% isoflurane; that the 40 Hz ASSR is an excellent predictor of the level of consciousness.”

definitionauditory evoked potential (AEP) — “Auditory evoked potentials (AEPs) are changes of the electrical activity of the brain (i.e. the electroencephalogram—EEG), produced by auditory stimuli.”
definitiontransient AEP — “Transient AEPs are seen when rate of stimulus delivery is slow enough for the response to wear off before the next stimulus.”
definitionsteady-state AEP — “Steady-state AEPs are seen when rate of stimulus delivery is fast enough to cause overlap of the individual transient responses.”
definition40 Hz auditory steady-state response (40 Hz ASSR) — “The 40 Hz ASSR is a sustained, sinusoidal electrical response of the brain to auditory stimuli delivered at rates near 40 Hz”
definitionauditory middle latency response (AMLR) — “The AMLR consists of three main peaks: Na, Pa and Nb with nominal latency in awake subjects of 15–20, 25–30 and 40 millisecond, respectively”
definitioncoherent frequency (CF) — “Munglani et al58 examined the effect of isoflurane (0–0.8%) on the stimulus rate producing the largest ASSR with little or no power in the harmonics, rate called the coherent frequency (CF).”
definitionunintentional awareness — “The use of neuromuscular blocking drugs carries the risk of unintentional awareness during anesthesia, i.e. the accidental regaining of consciousness during surgery by a patient who is paralyzed and thus unable to signal his or her discomfort.”
definitionA-Line ARX index (AAI) — “The device also provide an index (A- Line ARX index—AAI) defined as the sum of absolute differences in the 20– 80 millisecond window of the AMLR.”
