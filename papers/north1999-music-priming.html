phenomenonmusical fit and in-store product choice— “This field study investigated the extent to which stereotypically French and German music could influence supermarket customers' selections of French and German wines.”
claim-typeexistence— “The results indicate that music influenced customers' product selections.”
method — evidence— “French and German music was played on alternate days alongside French and German wines in the alcoholic beverages section of a supermarket.”
builds-on Areni and Kim (1993)— “This theoretical framework has interesting parallels with Areni and Kim's (1993) arguments concerning musical fit and product selection discussed earlier.”
builds-on Martindale and Moore's (1988) preference-for-prototypes model— “Although the aim of Martindale and Moore's (1988) model is to explain preferences between aesthetic objects, it is relevant to consumer research because it suggests the role of knowledge activation in responses to music.”
senseTHE AUTHORS TAKE APART THEIR OWN EVIDENCE FOR UNAWARENESS, IN THREE MOVES. First the hedge is built into the report: `TAKEN PURELY AT FACE VALUE, the questionnaire data suggest that respondents were largely unaware of the effect that music had on their wine selections.` Then: `it REMAINS TO BE DETERMINED whether customers really were unaware ...: MAYBE CUSTOMERS DID REALIZE that the music was an attempt to influence their purchasing, BUT DID NOT LIKE TO ADMIT THIS to researchers.` **A self-report of unawareness is treated as evidence about what people will SAY**, which is a different quantity from what they knew - and most awareness checks in this corpus are reported as measurements of the second— “Second, it remains to be determined whether customers really were unaware of the effects of music on their purchasing: Maybe customers did realize that the music was an attempt to influence their purchasing, but did not like to admit this to researchers”
senseAND THE THIRD MOVE IS SUBTLER THAN IMPRESSION MANAGEMENT: `customers may not have explained their wine choice in terms of the music simply because THIS DID NOT SEEM TO THEM LIKE A RATIONAL EXPLANATION for their behavior.` Not concealment and not ignorance - a FOLK THEORY OF WHAT COUNTS AS A REASON. A shopper who half-noticed the music may still not offer it, because `the music was playing` is not the kind of thing that explains a purchase. So a null on a `why did you choose this` question is evidence about the respondent's model of explanation, and only then about awareness— “Indeed, customers may not have explained their wine choice in terms of the music simply because this did not seem to them like a rational explanation for their behavior”
senseTHE SAME BOUNDARY CONDITION money2013 GIVES, SIXTEEN YEARS EARLIER AND IN A DIFFERENT FIELD. Here: the effect holds `ONLY WHEN THERE IS AN ELEMENT OF UNCERTAINTY in product choice or when customers are in a state of LOW INVOLVEMENT with the decision process`. Vohs's 2015 reply to a failed replication says priming needs an AMBIGUOUS OUTCOME - `a situation that does not leave much room for interpretation is unlikely to show a consistent or large priming effect`. **Marketing in 1999 and social psychology in 2015, arriving independently at the condition that a prime needs somewhere to act**, and each citing its own literature (judgmental biases here, Loersch & Payne there)— “suggests that the heuristic influence of music would mediate behavior only when there is an element of uncertainty in product choice or when customers are in a state of low involvement with the decision process”
senseAN ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATION THE DATA CANNOT RULE OUT, STATED WITHOUT DEFENCE - AND THEN A REPLY THAT COSTS NOTHING. `Because the music was played FROM THE DISPLAY containing the wines, it may well have ATTRACTED THE ATTENTION of people who already intended to buy wine from one of the countries in question ... The present data does not allow us to test this alternative explanation.` The concession is total. The reply is that it does not matter: `even if this alternative explanation is correct ... the fact that music was able to attract them to a specific area of the store STILL SHOWS that music activated knowledge and was associated with the selection of matching products.` **The mechanism claim survives the confound because both routes require the same activation**— “Second, even if this alternative explanation is correct in the case of some customers, then the fact that music was able to attract them to a specific area of the store still shows that music activated knowledge and was associated with the selection of matching products”
controlthe French and German wines matched to each other on price and on degree of sweetness — held — rules out the music effect being a price or taste effect - if the French wine were cheaper or sweeter, the shopper's choice would follow the wine rather than the accordion— “The wines were of a reasonably affordable price to the typical shopper and were matched between the countries for their price and degree of sweetness.”
