phenomenonlong-term semantic priming— “In two experiments, we used different semantic content – word meaning versus category membership – to investigate the mechanisms accountable for long-term semantic priming.”
claim-typemechanism— “Evidence from the two experiments suggests that long-term semantic priming effects are due to different memory processes for different semantic content.”
method — evidence— “Two priming experiments were designed to be as structurally similar as possible, with one containing category exemplar and feature stimuli and the other containing synonym and antonym stimuli.”
mechanismThe memory process supporting long-term priming shifts with how the relevant part of semantic memory is organised associatively— “we propose that the memory processes underlying long-term semantic priming effects differ as a function of the associative organization of semantic memory for the stimulus domain in use.”
mechanismBecause categories are hierarchically organised under a shared label, a prime trial and a target trial repeat the very same label-retrieval and comparison operation, and that repetition is what speeds the target— “The repetition of this operation performed relative to the same category is likely to play a substantial role in the target trial facilitation compared to unprimed category trials.”
mechanismSynonyms and antonyms have no superordinate label to re-retrieve, so priming there can only come from incremental strengthening of the abstract semantic features the words share— “depends on increments of strength in abstract semantic representations that partially overlap for synonyms and antonyms.”
mechanismOperation-specific facilitation appears only in the category domain because of the hierarchical structure of category knowledge— “That is, operation-specific facilitation is uniquely found in the category domain because of the hierarchical structure of that knowledge.”
builds-on Woltz (2010) and Woltz et al. (2015)— “This is consistent with findings by Woltz (2010) and Woltz et al. (2015).”
replicates Woltz and Was (2007)— “Our findings support the conclusion that semantic priming of category comparisons is partly operationspecific, and this is similar to findings by Woltz and Was (2007).”
contradicts Woltz and Was (2007)— “This differs from earlier findings by Woltz and Was (2007), and suggests that some facilitation reflects a generalized increase in availability of category information regardless of category operation.”
sense**TWO RIVAL MECHANISMS, TWENTY YEARS APART, STATED AS ALTERNATIVES:** `The first explanation is that long-term semantic priming effects rely upon the INCREMENTAL STRENGTHENING OF ABSTRACT SEMANTIC MEMORY REPRESENTATIONS. The second explanation is that long-term priming is the result of MEMORY FOR PRIOR COGNITIVE OPERATIONS.` **One says the concept got stronger; the other says the subject remembers what they did.** They are not degrees of the same account - one is about the representation and the other about the act - and until this paper the literature had been arguing which— “The first explanation is that long-term semantic priming effects rely upon the incremental strengthening of abstract semantic memory representations. The second explanation is that long-term priming is the result of memory for prior cognitive operations”
sense**AND THE ANSWER IS NEITHER - IT IS BOTH, SPLIT BY CONTENT:** `Long-term semantic priming of WORD MEANINGS was dependent on strengthening abstract semantic memory representations and persistent priming of CATEGORY MEMBERSHIP was dependent on memory for prior cognitive operations.` **A dissociation inside a phenomenon the field had treated as one thing**, told apart only by varying the material while holding the task constant. This is the corpus's own thesis arriving as somebody else's empirical result: one term, two mechanisms, and no way to see it without the manipulation— “Long-term semantic priming of word meanings was dependent on strengthening abstract semantic memory representations and persistent priming of category membership was dependent on memory for prior cognitive operations”
sense**THE DESIGN POINT IS THE SAME ONE erb2019 MAKES, ONE RECORD APART:** `the priming tasks were designed to be AS SIMILAR AS POSSIBLE, while the PREVIOUS EVIDENCE CAME FROM DIFFERENT TASKS that could have been responsible for the difference in findings.` The earlier literature compared mechanisms across experiments differing in more than the variable of interest. **Two consecutive records now say a body of results is uninterpretable because a confound was not held constant** - erb2019 about associative-priming overlap, this about task differences - and neither is a critique paper. Both are ordinary empirical papers that had to clear the ground before they could ask anything— “the priming tasks were designed to be as similar as possible, while the previous evidence came from different tasks that could have been responsible for the difference in findings”
sense**AND THE ASYMMETRY IS EXPLAINED RATHER THAN NAMED - A CATEGORY HAS A LABEL AND A SYNONYM PAIR DOES NOT.** Evaluating whether roses and carnations share a category activates `flower`, and the next trial repeats that same evaluation against that same label; but `Deciding that placid rather than chaotic has a similar meaning to tranquil DOES NOT OCCUR WITH REFERENCE TO AN OVERARCHING CATEGORY LABEL with which the synonyms are exemplars.` **There is no operation to repeat, so only strengthening is left.** The mechanism is derived from the structure of the material rather than fitted to the result, which is what makes it a prediction for the next study rather than a description of this one— “Deciding that placid rather than chaotic has a similar meaning to tranquil does not occur with reference to an overarching category label with which the synonyms are exemplars”
