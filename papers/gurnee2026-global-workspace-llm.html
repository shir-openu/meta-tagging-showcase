extends the logit lens— “While the logit lens assumes that representations use the same coordinates in all layers, the Jacobian lens corrects for representational changes that take place across layers, allowing it to uncover meaningful information in earlier layers where the logit lens produces uninterpretable readouts.”

sense`J-space` - A NAME BORROWED FROM LINEAR ALGEBRA FOR SOMETHING THAT IS NOT A SUBSPACE. `THIS UNION OF CONES IS THE J-SPACE.` The suffix `-space` invites every habit a reader has about spans, bases and dimensions, and the object is **a union of polyhedral cones** - closed under nonnegative combination only. **The paper says so plainly and the name still does the misleading**, which is mechanism 28's shape (a name whose extension is wrong) arriving through a suffix rather than through a stem.— “This union of cones is the J-space.”
sense`J-space component` - DEFINED BY WHAT IT CAN BE WRITTEN AS, NOT BY A PROJECTION. `When we refer to the “J-SPACE COMPONENT” of a vector, WE MEAN THE COMPONENT THAT CAN BE WRITTEN AS SUCH A SPARSE (NONNEGATIVE) SUM.` In a subspace, `component` means an orthogonal projection and is unique. Here it means a best sparse nonnegative approximation - **the same word, a different mathematical object, and the difference follows directly from J-space not being a space.** **NO EDGE PROPOSED, AND THIS REFUSAL IS DIFFERENT FROM THE OTHERS TONIGHT.** `part-of` is the RIGHT type here; the paper simply never asserts the containment in words. limitationThe lens is acknowledged as an approximate and incomplete proxy for whatever the model's real workspace is.— “The Jacobian lens is an imperfect tool, which we believe only approximately and incompletely captures the model’s underlying workspace structure.”
limitationIt can only name concepts that happen to be single tokens in the vocabulary.— “This construction means that the set of concepts the lens can name is exactly the set of concepts that have a single-token name in the tokenizer's vocabulary.”
limitationThe readout is a flat bag of concepts and cannot show how they are bound or structured together.— “Even setting the vocabulary issue aside, the J-lens treats the workspace as a flat collection of independently active concept vectors.”
limitationReadouts are not always interpretable, and the rate of uninterpretable ones was not characterised systematically.— “In our experience this is the typical case at workspace-layer positions, but it is not the universal one: at some positions and layers, the top lens tokens are ones we cannot make sense of.”
limitationThe apparent emptiness of the early layers may be a fact about the model or an artefact of the lens; the authors cannot tell which.— “W e do not know whether this is a fact about the model or an artifact of the lens.”
limitationNo mechanism is offered for how a representation comes to enter the workspace.— “W e have not characterized what causes a representation to enter it.”
limitationThe authors decline to claim the J-space is sufficient for alignment monitoring, since automatic or well-practised misaligned behaviour could bypass it.— “W e do not feel comfortable making the stronger claim that monitoring the J-space is sufficient for alignment monitoring, or that any sophisticated plan the model might execute must be represented there.”
limitationFindings are from large production models only; scaling and training-time emergence are untested.— “W e have studied the workspace primarily in fully trained production LLMs of considerable size.”
limitationThe analogy to global workspace theory is explicitly partial: architectural features such as encapsulated processors and recurrent broadcast are not claimed.— “Despite these similarities, we do not claim that language models reproduce the full architecture global workspace theory ascribes to the brain”
