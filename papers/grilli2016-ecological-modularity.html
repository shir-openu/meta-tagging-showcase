Abstract The recently discovered twist-bend nematic phase, N tb, is a non-uniform equilibrium nematic phase that presents a spontaneous bend with a precession of t he nematic director, n, on a conical helix with a tilt angle θ and helical pitch P . The stabilityterm·key of the N tb phase has been recently demonstrated from the elastic point of view by extending the Frank elastic energy density of the nematic phase to include the symmetry element of helical axi s, t. In the present communication, we investigate the inﬂuence of an external bulk ﬁeld (magnet ic or electric) on the pitch of the N tb phase. For a material with positive magnetic susceptibilit y anisotropy, when the magnetic ﬁeld H is parallel to the helical axis, t, the ﬁeld modiﬁes the coupling parameter between the direct or and the helical axis, thus shifting the interval of values for wh ich this coupling results in the N tb phase. If H is perpendicular to t, it is shown that the pitch of the nematic director n precession increases with H, and at a critical value of Hc it becomes inﬁnite as in a cholesteric liquid crystal, that i s, one gets a uniform orientation of n. Our results include the b ehaviour of a cholesteric under ﬁeld as a limiting case. 2

I. INTRODUCTION Liquid crystals (LCs) are a fascinating example of collective behavior in nature. In con- trast to ordinary liquids, the orienting eﬀect over the anisometric m olecules by an external inﬂuence (such as a magnetic ﬁeld) is proportional to the volume of t he responding unit of LC molecules [1], resulting in strong aligning eﬀect for relatively small ﬁe lds. Whenever an external ﬁeld is used to align or reorient a LC media, a distortion in the orientation may occur [2]. Such astonishing eﬀect is the backbone of modern electro -optic devices (displays), where the average orientation of the molecules results from the co mpetition among external ﬁeld, anchoring strength and elasticity [3]. When the system is chiral, an applied ﬁeld yields a richer class of textures and transitions when compared to the no n-chiral counterpart, such as the appearance of ﬁngers [4–7], bubble domains [8, 9], and many ot hers [10, 11]. One im- portant example is the ﬁeld induced (magnetic, electric, mechanical) cholesteric to nematic transition, where the cholesteric helix is unwind when the ﬁeld is applied perpendicularly to the helix direction for positive dielectric anisotropy materials both for an unbound sam- ple [12, 13] and in conﬁned geometry [14, 15]. This unwinding is specially interesting in the vicinities of the transition cholesteric to Smectic A phase [16]. Recently, a new class of materials has been discovered in the LC ﬁeld w ith potential for presenting exciting orientational transitions as the ones aforeme ntioned. The twist bend nematic (N tb) phase is a heliconical structure with a nanoscale pitch favored by b ent-shaped molecules. It has been theoretically predicted years ago [17–19], bu t only recently the Ntb phase was experimentally observed in bent molecular dimers [20–22], t rimers [23] and, recently, in rigid bent-core materials [24] and in chiral dimers as well [2 5]. Sparked by these experimental achievements, new theoretical models have been pu blished recently [26–28], contributing to better understanding N tb materials. In this phase, the ground state presents a twist-bend deformation with the director precessing around a he lix axis maintaining a constant angle 0 ≤ θ ≤ π/2 [20]. From the optical scale point of view, the N tb phase forms micron size stripes resembling smectics. However, x-ray mea surements have shown no periodic variation in the electron density [21]. In fact, these stripe s have been attributed to a Helfrich-Hurault-type deformation caused by the undulation of t he pseudolayers forming the phase [29–31]. In a recent work, Challa et. al. [31] studied the eﬀect of high magnet ic ﬁelds on an 3

Ntb material. Nonetheless, their focus was centered on the temperat ure shift of the N tb to nematic caused by the ﬁeld. They observed however that the optic al stripes disappeared under the ﬁeld. Similar experiments have been conducted in [20], but t his time under electric ﬁeld, where a ﬁrst order like transition from the homeotrop ic to N tb texture happens. However, there is no data so far on the unwinding of the N tb helix. In this article, we take advantage of the recent elastic model for the phase [32] and calc ulate the pitch’s dependence with an applied magnetic ﬁeld. If the ﬁeld is applied parallel to helical axis , it modiﬁes the coupling parameter between the director and the helical axis, t hus shifting the interval of values for which this coupling results in the N tb phase. If applied perpendicular, it is shown that the pitch increases with H (magnetic ﬁeld). At a critical value Hc, the pitch is unwounded and an uniform director distribution occurs [12, 13]. As t he ﬁeld is increased, the helix unwinds until an uniform nematic phase forms. In these curves , each small bent-rod represents the director orientation at a given position in space. Recently, we have proposed a new elastic theory, in a very general form by using the symmetry p oint of view, that accounts for the N tb phase [32]. CONCLUSIONS We have theoretically investigated the eﬀect of an applied magnetic ﬁ eld on the director organization of a twist-bend nematic phase. We started out by rew riting the free energy found in [32] in terms of the parameters q0 and b0, associated with the natural distortions of the phase. Then, we analyzed two distinct cases: ﬁrst, the magne tic ﬁeld was applied parallel to the helical axis t; in the second case, H was applied perpendicularly to the direction of t. In both situations, x = sin 2(θ) was the same across the sample. When H ∥ t, the main eﬀect of the ﬁeld, for ﬁxed x, is of renormalizing the coupling parameter η, though the interval of possible values for η remains the same. Since the coupling parameter is associated to the phase transition from N tb to nematic, the ﬁeld shifts the transition point. In another words, the presence of the ﬁeld modiﬁes the way in which the director couple s with the helical axis and therefore alters the N tb range of equilibrium. This behavior agrees with recent studies on the eﬀect of ﬁeld on the N tb phase [31]. When H ⊥ t, there is a critical ﬁeld corresponding to the unwinding of the helix. This critical ﬁeld depends on the twist ela stic constant and on the wave number as expected, but also on x and on b0. If x = 0, there is no critical ﬁeld, 12

since the phase in this case is a nematic perfectly aligned. When x = 1 and b0 = 1, the critical ﬁeld reduces to the one well known critical ﬁeld of cholester ics. Interestingly, the molecular symmetry allows cholesteric phases ( x = 1) even when b0 ̸= 1.
