claim-typeexistence
mechanismIsoflurane reduces cochlear blood flow, blocks acetylcholine receptors and enhances GABA-gated currents, which raises medial olivocochlear efferent inhibition and so depresses auditory sensitivity in the anesthetized state — “The possible mechanisms underlying these impacts are changes in cochlear blood flow (CBF) (Nakashima et al., 2003; Xiao et al., 2014), the blockage of acetylcholine (ACh) receptors (Dilger et al., 1992), and enhanced gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA)-gated channel currents (Ming et al., 2001), which lead to increased inhibition mediated by medial olivocochlear (MOC) efferents (Sheppard et al., 2018).”
mechanismBehaviourally relevant high frequencies are over-represented in the mouse auditory cortex because the mouse hearing range extends much further upward, which is why the newly found regions are all high-frequency ones — “This finding suggests that the AC tonotopic maps are oriented more toward high frequencies than low frequencies, which is consistent with the wider spectrum of high frequencies in the mouse hearing range (Heffner, 1980).”
relation — “Despite the report of no difference between upward and downward FM sweep maps in a previous study (Issa et al., 2017), we found that the center of the slow sweep rate responsive area in AI shifts 350 µm rostrally between upward and downward FM sweep maps.”

limitationThe map of this cortex has been revised repeatedly, and the paper attributes the revisions to advances in technique rather than to new anatomy. — “However, during the past decades, technological advancements in neuroimaging and recording have led to significant updates in the tonotopic organization of the belt area.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingThree previously uncharacterised tonotopic regions of mouse auditory cortex - the dorsal anterior field, the posterior auditory field and the ventral posterior auditory field - were identified, all preferentially responsive to high frequencies and lying dorsal and caudal to the known areas. — “The newly identified regions were generally responsive to higher frequencies and were in dorsal and caudal AC regions.”
findingTonotopic maps were stable within a single 25-minute session, across three consecutive weeks in the same mouse, and across the twelve animals. — “Except for a few sparse pixels, the tonotopic maps were quite similar over time and between animals.”
findingHigh-frequency responses in AI-H and UF were larger in awake than in isoflurane-anesthetized mice, implying reduced high-frequency hearing sensitivity under anesthesia. — “In this study, the response amplitude at high frequencies (the H region of AI and UF) was greater in the tonotopic maps from awake compared to anesthetized mice.”
findingUnder anesthesia the cortical area encoding slow FM sweep rates expanded and the area encoding fast sweep rates shrank across AAF, DA and UF. — “The second difference was shown in the AAF-DA-UF regions, in which the response to slower sweep rates was expanded and the response to faster sweep rates was reduced in maps from the anesthetized compared to the awake state.”
findingContrary to an earlier report of no difference, upward and downward FM sweep maps do differ: the slow-rate centre in AI sits about 350 micrometres more rostral for downward sweeps. — “Despite the report of no difference between upward and downward FM sweep maps in a previous study (Issa et al., 2017), we found that the center of the slow sweep rate responsive area in AI shifts 350 µm rostrally between upward and downward FM sweep maps.”
findingUltrasonic vocalizations drove stronger responses than pure tones specifically in the regions tuned to fast FM sweep rates (AII, DP, PAF, VPAF and a zone between UF and AAF), and not in the high-frequency tonotopic regions. — “In our study, vocal stimuli elicited a stronger response than tonal stimuli in both anesthetized and awake states, and a higher V/T ratio was obtained in the regions (AII, DP, PAF, VPAF, and a region between UF and AAF) linked to the processing of faster FM sweep rates compared to regions that respond to high frequencies.”
findingBest frequency changes with distance along a tonotopic gradient as a sigmoid: middle frequencies change steeply over a short stretch of cortex while low and high frequencies occupy longer stretches. — “This sigmoid function reflects steeper frequency changes in a shorter distance for middle frequencies versus lower and higher frequencies.”
findingTone-evoked cortical activity stays near its source, so tonotopic maps computed from any averaging window between 100 ms and 1000 ms after onset come out identical. — “This similarity demonstrates that in the AC regions tonal stimuli evoke spatiotemporal responses that do not travel far from their initial source locations.”
findingThe tonotopic map spans 2 mm rostro-caudally and 1.9 mm dorso-ventrally, so the conventional 2x2 mm imaging window used in earlier work is too small to capture all of it. — “The distance between the farthest landmarks in the tonotopic map supports the idea that a window larger than 2 mm in each axis is necessary to include all cortical regions involved in the AC tonotopic organization.”

quantityR2 > 0.93 and 0.90 — Adjusted R-squared of the linear fit to the inverse-sigmoid frequency-distance relation, awake and anesthetized — “The associations were highly linear (R2 > 0.93 and 0.90 for awake and anesthetized states, respectively) and confirmed the sigmoid relationship between the best frequency and distance from the low-frequency landmark in a tonotopic gradient.”
quantityR2 > 0.95 and 0.87 — Fit quality of the inverse-sigmoid function for the FM sweep-rate/distance relation, awake and anesthetized — “A sigmoid function was obtained in the plots (Figures 6I and 6J for awake and anesthetized, respectively) after fitting an inverse-sigmoid function to each graph (R2 > 0.95 and 0.87 for awake and anesthetized states, respectively).”
quantity1.36 and 1.51 — Median vocalization-to-tone response ratio in the USV-favouring pixels, awake and anesthetized — “The median values of the V/T ratio for the regions favoring USVs (i.e., pixels with V/T > 1) were 1.36 and 1.51 for awake and anesthetized states, respectively.”
quantity2 mm and 1.9 mm — Extent of the auditory cortex tonotopic map along the rostral-caudal and dorsal-ventral axes — “The distance between the centers of the two farthest regions was 2 mm on the rostral-caudal axis and 1.9 mm on the dorsal-ventral axis.”

definitiontonotopic organization — “This tonotopic organization is characterized by a spectrum of gradients extended from neurons that preferentially respond to certain frequencies, ranging from high to low (Humphries et al., 2010).”
