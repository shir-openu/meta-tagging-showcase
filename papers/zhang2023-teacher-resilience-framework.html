The findings highlighted four broad dimensions of protective factors: those specifically related to the profession (such as self-efficacyterm·key beliefs and pedagogical competencies), emotional aspects (such as positive emotions and emotional management), social aspects (such as supportive relationships with students and colleagues), and motivational aspects (such as having a sense of purpose in one’s work) (e.g., intrinsic motivation, persistence, expectations, and goals). By gaining a deeper knowledge of these elements, educators and policymakers may design interventions and methods to increase teacher well-beingterm·key, job satisfaction, and student results ( Van Wingerden and Poell, 2019 ; Daniilidou et al., 2020 ; Cho et al., 2021 ).

Teacher resilienceterm·dom is an important area of study because teachers who do a good job and stay in the field have a positive effect on their students’ learning. Teacher resilienceterm·dom, along with their knowledge, skills, and other qualities, make up a patchwork of learning support that helps students do better in school. Teacher resilienceterm·dom sees itself as a concept that bridges the gap between the complicated contexts of practice and the people who work in them. Even though it has been defined in different ways, teacher resilienceterm·dom seems to be a mix of personality traits, developmental processes, and skills that teachers have learned ( Bobek, 2002 ; Benders and Jackson, 2012 ; Ebersöhn, 2014 ; Tenorio-Vilchez and Sucari, 2021 ). It has been called a key factor for teachers who work in poor urban areas ( Day and Hong, 2016 ; Suryaratri et al., 2020 ) and for teachers who work with kids who have special educational needs ( Mackenzie, 2012 ; Abdullah et al., 2019 ).

The job of a teacher is getting more difficult, demanding, and tiring ( Flores, 2020 ; Park et al., 2020 ). The motivations behind this study are to further explore the complex nature of teacher resilienceterm·dom and identify the factors that contribute to it. The data from the obtained studies has been downloaded in CSV format. The titles and abstracts of 172 articles were read one by one. It was examined according to the criteria of measuring the resilienceterm·dom level of teachers, using a quantitative measurement tool, and not having a review or meta-analysis study. In this review, nine articles that did not meet the criteria, such as studies on pre-service teachers, were excluded from the study. As a result, 22 articles were included in the data analysis.

Examining the research on teacher resiliency reveals two studies having a single author, while the remaining studies have multiple authors.
