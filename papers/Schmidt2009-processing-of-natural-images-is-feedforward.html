phenomenonvisuomotor response priming with natural images
claim-typeexistence
mechanismThe prime's feedforward wave arrives at motor areas first and drives the movement alone until the target's wave arrives and takes over; a longer SOA gives the prime a longer unopposed drive, which is why priming grows with SOA. — “Priming effects increase with prime–target SOA because the prime has more time to drive the response on its own when the target is further delayed.”
mechanismWhen no overt detour appears, opposing prime- and target-driven response tendencies conflict covertly and must be resolved before the movement can start, which shows up as a delayed onset instead. — “Even in the absence of detours, the delay of pointing onset probably reflects a covert conflict of opposing response tendencies from prime and target signals that must be resolved before movement onset can occur”
relation — “Our results extend our rapid-chase theory of response priming to the domain of natural image processing, linking visuomotor priming to feedforward processing of images (Schmidt et al., 2006; Vath & Schmidt, 2007).”
relation — “Our data strongly support the conclusion from previous studies (e.g., Bacon-Macé et al., 2007; Kirchner & Thorpe, 2006; Thorpe et al., 1996; VanRullen & Koch, 2003; Van- Rullen & Thorpe, 2001, 2002) that natural scenes can be classified on the basis of an initial feedforward sweep of processing (Bullier, 2001; Lamme & Roelfsema, 2000).”
relation — “These differences considered, there is no indication here that natural image classification is extraordinarily fast, or “ultra-rapid” (Kirchner & Thorpe, 2006; VanRullen & Thorpe, 2001), when compared with even more basic tasks such as simple shape classification.”
replicationuntested
limitationThe paper does not claim that the rapid classification is driven by image semantics specifically. — “However, we make no claim here that classification is based exclusively on the semantic content of the images.”
limitationIn the slow large-small task the independence criterion could not be clearly evaluated. — “Only in the large–small task, with its tardy response dynamics and small priming effects, does it become difficult to evaluate whether the independence criterion is met.”
limitationIn Experiment 1 the raw trajectories did not show the initiation criterion; the conclusion rests on the derived spatial priming functions. — “Admittedly, these issues are less clear in Experiment 1, in which both tasks failed to meet the initiation criterion, at least when judged by looking only at the raw trajectories (Figure 2).”
limitationMeeting the rapid-chase criteria shows behavioural equivalence to a feedforward system, not that every processing substage is feedforward. — “Importantly, note that the rapid-chase criteria do not guarantee that a stimulus– response system is strictly feedforward in all its substages (and they actually do not require such a strict assumption), but they reveal whether a system is behaviorally equivalent to a simple feedforward system.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingIn the animal-versus-object task, the time course of spatial priming matched the pattern the rapid-chase model predicts. — “In the animal–object task, the time course of spatial priming effects (Figure 4A) conformed to the pattern predicted by the rapid-chase model”
findingSpatial priming curves for every prime-target SOA followed one identical initial trajectory and separated only later, in SOA order, showing the target had no influence on the response when the prime first moved it. — “Crucially, the early time course of spatial priming was invariant for the different SOAs: All curves initially follow the time course of the curve for the longest SOA, in line with the independence criterion, and branch off only later one by one in the order of increasing SOAs.”
findingThe large-versus-small size-judgement task was the one case where the independence criterion could not be assessed, because its responses were slow and its priming effects small. — “Only in the large–small task, with its tardy response dynamics and small priming effects, does it become difficult to evaluate whether the independence criterion is met.”
findingThe authors attribute the slowness of the size-judgement task to its needing recurrent processing rather than a single feedforward sweep. — “It rather seems that the large–small classification cannot be performed in a single feedforward sweep and requires more extensive, recurrent processing.”
findingNatural-image categorisation was not unusually fast when benchmarked against simple ellipse-versus-rectangle shape classification, undercutting the label 'ultra-rapid'. — “These differences considered, there is no indication here that natural image classification is extraordinarily fast, or “ultra-rapid””
findingWhen prime and target categories conflicted, the pointing hand physically veered into the quadrant indicated by the prime before reversing toward the correct target. — “In inconsistent trials, however, the sensor tended to detour into the quadrant specified by the misleading prime before reversing and proceeding in the correct direction.”
findingPointing errors occurred almost only on inconsistent trials and grew with prime-target SOA, showing prime influence reaches the motor level. — “Errors occur mainly in inconsistent trials, and there increase with SOA, further showing that the impact of the primes goes all the way to the level of motor processing”
findingThe authors conclude that visuomotor priming reads out the first pass of information through the visuomotor system, before recurrent processing contributes. — “Our data thus suggest that visuomotor priming effects can capture the output of the very first pass of information through the visuomotor system, prior to contributions from recurrent processing.”
quantity380 msec — time after prime onset at which the hand began moving on consistent trials in the large-small task (versus roughly 300 msec in the animal-object task) — “animal–object task (Figure 2A) and roughly 380 msec after prime onset in the large–small task (Figure 2B).”
quantity280 msec / 220 msec — movement onset times after prime onset in the toy-animal-object and ellipse-rectangle tasks of Experiment 2 — “Indeed, movements started earlier, roughly 280 msec after prime onset in the toy-animal–object task and roughly 220 msec after prime onset in the ellipse–rectangle task”
quantity160 msec — lag of priming-effect onset in the large-small task relative to the ellipse-rectangle task — “those in the large– small task developed a full 160 msec after those in the ellipse– rectangle task.”
quantity33, 50, 67, 83, or 100 msec — the five prime-target stimulus onset asynchronies used, with primes shown for 33 msec — “The primes appeared for 33 msec at prime–target stimulus onset asynchronies (SOAs) of 33, 50, 67, 83, or 100 msec.”
quantityTwelve — total participants, six in each of the two experiments — “Twelve students from the University of Giessen (Experiment 1, 6 participants, 20–29 years of age, 1 male, 1 left-handed; Experiment 2, 6 participants, 21–30 years of age, 2 male, all right-handed) volunteered for course credit or for a payment of €8/h.”
quantity1.14% and 1.73% — trials discarded by the exclusion rules in Experiments 1 and 2 — “This procedure eliminated 1.14% and 1.73% of the trials in Experiments 1 and 2, respectively.”
quantityabout 80 msec — how much earlier spatial priming onset occurred in the animal-object task than in the large-small task — “This criterion was crossed about 80 msec earlier in the animal-object task than in the large–small task”
variableprime-target stimulus onset asynchrony (SOA) (independent) — “After a prime–target SOA of 33, 50, 67, 83, or 100 msec, two target images were presented at the same locations as the primes.”
variableprime-target consistency (independent) — “In consistent trials, primes and targets from the same category were presented at corresponding positions; in inconsistent trials, prime positions were switched.”
variableclassification task (animal-object, large-small, toy-animal-object, ellipse-rectangle) (independent) — “In each experiment, the participants took part in eight experimental sessions, four in each of the two respective tasks, with task order counterbalanced across participants.”
variablepointing trajectory position over time (dependent) — “Pointing responses were recorded by a Polhemus FASTRAK magnetic tracking device at a sampling frequency of 120 Hz.”
variableresponse error rate (dependent) — “Lower panels: Rates of response errors, which are defined as arrivals of the sensor at the nontarget position.”
definitionfeedforward (in neuronal signal flow) — “In the context of neuronal signal flow, feedforward indicates that a cell passes activation on to another cell before integrating any feedback or recurrent information from other cells about that signal.”
definitionrapid chase — “In a simple feedforward system that is reasonably fast and has sufficient temporal resolution, prime and target signals would engage in what we call a rapid chase, with the prime signal maintaining a lead over the target signal all the way to motor control.”
definitionrapid-chase criteria (initiation, takeover, independence) — “The feedforward properties of the system would show in the time course of the response and meet the following rapid-chase criteria: (1) Prime rather than target signals should determine the onset and initial direction of the response (initiation criterion); (2) target signals should be able to influence the response before it is completed (takeover criterion); and, crucially, (3) response kinematics should initially depend exclusively on prime characteristics and be independent of all target characteristics (independence criterion).”
definitionindependence criterion — “The initiation criterion guarantees that the prime wins the chase; the takeover criterion guarantees that the chase is sufficiently fast to take place within a single response; and the independence criterion establishes strict sequentiality of response control.”
definitionspatial priming function — “In addition, we defined a spatial priming function by subtracting trajectories in consistent trails from those in inconsistent trials”
