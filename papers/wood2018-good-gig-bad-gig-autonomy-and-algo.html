phenomenonremote gig workers experiencing genuine autonomy and severe overwork as two effects of the same system of control— “The autonomy resulting from algorithmic control can lead to overwork, sleep deprivation and exhaustion as a consequence of the weak structural power of workers vis-a-vis clients.”
claim-typeexistence— “Despite varying country contexts and types of work, we show that algorithmic control is central to the operation of online labour platforms. Algorithmic management techniques tend to offer workers high levels of flexibility, autonomy, task variety and complexity. However, these mechanisms of control can also result in low pay, social…
method — evidence— “Our analysis is primarily based on face-to-face semi-structured interviews with 107 workers. The worker informants consist of 36 workers in Southeast Asia (SEA, comprising 12 in the Philippines, five in Malaysia and 19 in Vietnam), and 71 in Sub-Saharan Africa (SSA, comprising 29 in Kenya, 23 in Nigeria and 19 in South Africa).”
object of study — evidence— “This article evaluates the job quality of work in the remote gig economy. Such work consists of the remote provision of a wide variety of digital services mediated by online labour platforms.”
research question — evidence— “The second aim of this article is therefore to identify the organisational forms (such as Taylorist and algorithmic controls) that could explain commonalities in the quality remote gig work across the platforms and countries that we study, and how these organisational forms are shaped by power and technology.”
contribution — evidence— “The identification of the ‘symbolic power’ ( Thompson, 1991) of platform reputations as an emerging form of marketplace bargaining power is an important contribution of this article.”
mechanismCONTROL AT THE END OF THE LABOUR PROCESS, NOT DURING IT. Only the delivered product is judged, so the worker is genuinely free in method, hours and place - and the same fact means the rating that governs all future work is assigned after the work is done and cannot be argued with— “What is notable about this algorithmic system of control in terms of job quality, compared to Taylorist forms of informational control, is that control operated at the end of the labour process rather than during it.”
mechanismNON-PROXIMITY DEFEATS SUPERVISION AND THE RATING RESTORES IT: clients in high-income countries cannot watch workers in SEA and SSA, so direct monitoring fails - and platform rating and ranking overcome exactly the spatial and temporal barriers that defeated it— “This weak structural power is an outcome of platform-based rating and ranking systems enabling a form of control which is able to overcome the spatial and temporal barriers that non-proximity places on the effectiveness of direct labour process surveillance and supervision.”
mechanismRANKING IS THE SANCTION: platforms do not dismiss low-rated workers, they filter work away from them, so the penalty is invisible, gradual and unappealable - continuing on the platform simply stops being a viable living— “online labour platforms, instead, use algorithms to filter work away from those with low ratings, thus making continuing on the platform a less viable means of making a living.”
mechanismDIRECT MONITORING WAS EASILY CIRCUMVENTED - keystroke counts and screenshots were worked around by timing and by a second monitor - which is why the reputational mechanism, not the surveillance mechanism, is the effective one— “For instance, frequencies of workers’ keyboard presses and mouse movements were tracked and shots taken of their screens. However, these monitoring mechanisms were easily circumvented.”
mechanismWORKERS WELCOMED THE MONITORING because the platform guaranteed payment for monitored work: surveillance was traded for protection against wage theft, so the same mechanism is a safeguard and a control depending on who is short of power— “In fact, the monitoring was often welcomed by workers, because the platform guaranteed payments for monitored work, protecting workers from non-payment and wage theft”
mechanismFORMAL CONTROL WITHOUT REAL CHOICE: workers hold genuine authority over where and when they work and have little real alternative to home and to unsocial hours, because client demand and weak structural power set the terms— “while algorithmic control provides remote gig workers with formal control over where they work, workers may have little real choice but to work from home, and this can lead to a lack of social contact and feelings of social isolation.”
mechanismAN UNREGULATED GLOBAL OVERSUPPLY OF LABOUR is what the platform connects clients to, and it is the source of the weak structural power that turns autonomy into overwork— “Online labour platforms thus facilitate clients in connecting with a largely unregulated global oversupply of labour.”
contradicts accounts which hold that ICTs bring Taylorist organisational forms to white-collar work - the 'assembly line in the head' and 'electronic sweatshop' readings - which this study's remote gig workers do not fit— “Thus remote gig work is a long way from being an ‘assembly line in the head’ ( Bain and Taylor, 2000) or ‘electronic sweatshop’”
contradicts Shapiro's characterisation of gig-work autonomy as 'autonomy over minute decisions', which the authors find far from minute for remote gig work— “Shapiro (2017) refers to the autonomy of local gig workers over things such as when to work and which orders to accept or reject as only constituting ‘autonomy over minute decisions’. Yet our findings suggest that for remote gig workers the level of discretion is far from minute.”
extends Silver's and Wright's concept of marketplace bargaining power, which this article extends with a symbolic form neither of them considers— “Marketplace bargaining power is a concept developed by Silver (2003) and Wright (2000), neither of whom consider the importance of symbolic forms of power.”
qualifies the literature on Taylorist informational control, which is predominantly based on call-centre research and was generalised into theories of effort-biased technology adoption— “Accounts of Taylorist informational control are predominately based on research in call centres. However, this research has informed more general theories regarding the adoption of technology being ‘effort biased’”
senseautonomy - IN THIS PAPER A REAL AND MEASURED FREEDOM THAT DOES NOT IMPLY GOOD WORK. 72% could choose the order of tasks and 74% their methods, and the same workers report overwork, sleep deprivation and exhaustion. The paper's whole argument is that autonomy and job quality come apart, so reading `autonomy` as a job-quality outcome inverts the finding— “This is echoed in our survey, where 72% of respondents felt able to choose and change the order in which they undertook online tasks, and 74% were able to choose or change their methods of work.”
sensealgorithmic control - CONTROL EXERCISED AT THE END OF THE LABOUR PROCESS RATHER THAN DURING IT. That single structural fact generates the whole pattern: because only the output is judged, the worker is free in how they work, and because the rating governs future work, the freedom is unusable— “What is notable about this algorithmic system of control in terms of job quality, compared to Taylorist forms of informational control, is that control operated at the end of the labour process rather than during it.”
sensealgorithmic management - NOT SUPERVISION BY MACHINE BUT CUSTOMER MANAGEMENT EXTENDED: the client is placed in the management circuit, and the platform's ranking turns their rating into a sanction. The controller is another user, not an algorithm with a policy— “Algorithmic management is an extension of ‘customer management’ strategies, which entails positioning customers ‘as agents in the management circuit’, so that ‘customers, rather than managers, are \[…\] the ones who must be pleased, whose orders must be followed, whose ideas…
senseplatform reputation - AN INDIVIDUAL RESOURCE IN THE LABOUR MARKET, alongside skills, rather than a signal about past work. It is what the paper means by reputation having `symbolic power`: it is held by the worker, cannot be transferred between platforms, and determines what work is reachable at all
