mechanismimplicit control cues must leave a memory trace that survives across trials, and only consciously represented events are strong enough to bridge that interval, so unconscious implicit cues never reach the control system — “Only conscious event representations might be strong enough to bridge longer time intervals.”
mechanismadapting to a statistical property such as conflict frequency requires accumulating information across many separate episodes over a long period, an accumulation that unconscious representations cannot support — “In these cases information of several such events has to be accumulated over a long period of time, and over a certain number of such events to extract, for example, the proportion of incongruent trials.”
mechanismcontrol is executed by if-then plans; explicit cues let participants specify both sides of the plan in advance, whereas implicit events leave the if-side and then-side vague, so the plan itself has to be revised and it is this revision that demands awareness — “It seems that, for example, adaptation to response conflict requires a specification or correction of if-then plans, and perhaps it is exactly this alteration of plans that requires awareness.”

Unconscious processing is granted only where the system was set up for it, so a null in an unprepared condition is not evidence against unconscious processing - it is outside the claim. replicationcontested
limitationThe review's own headline result is that the literature it surveys does not settle the question. — “To anticipate a main result, the outcome of this research program is ambiguous.”
limitationThe evidence base the review rests on may be distorted by selective publication of positive findings. — “Second, research on the consciousness-control link is perhaps particularly susceptible to publication bias.”
limitationThe authors name publication bias in their own literature: positive evidence for control without awareness is publishable and negative evidence is not — “Second, research on the consciousness-control link is perhaps particularly susceptible to publication bias. Positive evidence for control without awareness is exciting and may make it easier to be published in prestigious journals (or to be published at all), while negative evidence resides in less prestigious journals (or may not be published).”
limitationThe effects at issue are small, so studies claiming them need power that is not always present — “First, effects of unconscious stimuli are often small. Therefore, studies aiming to show such effects must have sufficient power to do so.”
limitationThe central claim is stated with an epistemic hedge: awareness seems essential, or else the evidence is merely not consistent — “However, if an event such as recent or frequent response conflict merely implicitly suggests the need of cognitive control, awareness of this event seems essential, or at least, evidence for the induction of control phenomena without awareness is not consistently found.”
limitationThe failures of implicit control might be failures of the manipulation rather than of the capacity — “For example, there might be adaptation to unconsciously induced response conflict, but sometimes the subliminally induced conflict was too small.”
limitationThe key term is admitted to be poorly defined — “Although not very well defined, it seems fair to say that cognitive control denotes those processes that configure the cognitive system to process stimuli in a specific manner, and re-configure the cognitive system when certain events tell the observer/actor to treat stimuli in a different way.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingWhen the need for control is signalled by a single explicit cue, that cue does not have to be consciously perceived for control to be recruited. — “If the need for control is conveyed by an explicit event, awareness of that event is dispensable.”
findingWhen the need for control is only implied by recent or frequent response conflict, awareness of that event appears to be necessary, and unconscious induction of control is not reliably obtained. — “However, if an event such as recent or frequent response conflict merely implicitly suggests the need of cognitive control, awareness of this event seems essential, or at least, evidence for the induction of control phenomena without awareness is not consistently found.”
findingExactly three control functions have so far been shown to be triggerable by subliminal explicit cues: task preparation, response inhibition, and orienting of attention. — “These are task preparation, response inhibition, and orienting of attention.”
findingMasked primes reliably activate task sets, facilitating the cued task and even biasing which task is freely chosen. — “In sum, there is little doubt that masked stimuli have the power to induce task sets.”
findingStrategic adaptation to how often conflict occurs is obtained only when the conflicting irrelevant information is consciously seen. — “Importantly, such strategic adaptation to conflict frequency occurs only when the irre-levant information is consciously perceived”
findingConflict adaptation requires that the prime in the preceding trial was visible, yet the control settings so established still act on masked primes in the current trial - so awareness is needed to invoke control, not to execute it. — “This observation implies that prime visibility is necessary to invoke cognitive control, whereas the control processes that alter information processing can operate on masked stimuli as well.”
findingContext-specific proportion-congruency adaptation appeared with visible primes but disappeared with subliminal primes, whose overall congruency effect was unaffected by the context cue. — “Importantly in contrast to the results of Experiment 1, this congruency effect was not affected by context information.”
findingTaken as a whole the literature is inconsistent: some experiments show control prompted by unnoticed events and others show it fails. — “Some studies suggest that cognitive control can be prompted by events that remain unnoticed, whereas other studies suggest that they cannot.”
findingThe authors argue that cognitive control itself does not depend on the actor having meta-knowledge about when or how control is being applied. — “We conjecture that cognitive control does not require meta-knowledge of when and how to apply it.”
findingcontrol cued by a single explicit signal appears to work without awareness; control that must be inferred from a pattern does not — “While the former type of control seems to work outside of awareness, the latter type of control seems to be restricted to consciously registered events that call for control.”
findingmasked primes speed and improve responses when mapped to the same response or category as the target — “Although the prime is heavily masked, and thus phenomenally “unaware”, it leaves a trace in behavior: Responses are usually faster and more accurate when prime and target are mapped to the same motor response”
findingthe literature on unconscious control is inconsistent, and the authors treat that inconsistency as the thing to be explained — “Here we give a brief review of this research, discuss possible reasons for inconsistencies in the empirical evidence, and suggest some lines of future research.”
findingthe field may be shaped by publication bias favouring positive evidence for unconscious control — “Positive evidence for control without awareness is exciting and may make it easier to be published in prestigious journals (or to be published at all), while negative evidence resides in less prestigious journals (or may not be published).”
