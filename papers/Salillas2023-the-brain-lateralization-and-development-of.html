findingThe authors' own meta-analysis of non-symbolic quantity tasks in school-age children found clusters in bilateral superior parietal lobe and in the right intraparietal sulcus, with the laterality index showing clear right asymmetry in the IPS.
findingFor symbolic quantity tasks the intraparietal sulcus flipped to a left-hemisphere preponderance, while every other activated area was right dominant.
findingThe same IPS region is therefore left-driven for symbolic numbers and right-driven for non-symbolic quantity in children, so parietal lateralization depends on stimulus format rather than on the numerical operation.
findingFor simple addition in children the meta-analysis returned a left-lateralized parietal cortex and SMA together with bilateral insula, IFG and MFG and a right occipital pole.
findingThe overall right lateralization reported for children by earlier meta-analyses is an artefact of those analyses being dominated by non-symbolic studies.
findingDevelopmentally, the right IPS encodes numerical magnitude from early life independently of age, experience or mathematical knowledge, whereas the left IPS is barely involved at first.
findingThe right hemisphere's contribution to arithmetic is not merely generic spatial support: it stores the operation-specific spatial layout for complex written procedures and, in the right insula, parses complex numbers containing zero.
findingTHE ARTEFACT NAMED: the overall right lateralization for number in children reported by earlier meta-analyses is attributed to the non-symbolic studies in the pool
