content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingAcross diverse large networks (WWW, citations, power grid), vertex connectivity follows a scale-free power-law P(k)~k^-γ — a universal feature independent of the systems particulars. — “the probability P (k) that a vertex in the network interacts with k other vertices decays as a power-law, following P (k) ∼ k−γ”
findingScale-free structure emerges from two generic mechanisms: continuous growth by adding vertices, and preferential attachment to already well-connected sites. — “networks expand continuously by the addition of new vertices, and new vertices attach preferentially to already well connected sites”
findingFour unrelated systems - actors, the web, the power grid and citations - all show power-law connectivity, with exponents between 2.1 and 4, which no existing network model predicts. — “share the com- 3 mon feature that the distribution of their local connectivity is free of scale, following a power-law for large k, with an exponent γ between 2.1 and 4”
findingBoth ingredients are necessary: removing preferential attachment gives an exponential distribution, and removing growth gives a distribution that never settles. — “The failure of models A and B indicates that both ingredients, namely growth and preferential attachment, are needed for the development of the stationary power-law distribution observed in Fig. 1.”
findingThe model's connectivity distribution is stationary - independent of time and of system size - which is what lets one model cover systems at different stages of growth. — “P (k) is independent of time (and, subsequently, independent of the system size m0 + t),”
findingSolved analytically, the model gives an exponent of 3 that does not depend on how many edges each new vertex brings. — “giving γ = 3, independent of m.”
