phenomenonIh and HCN channel diversity across inferior colliculus neuron types
claim-typeexistence
mechanismDifferential distribution of HCN channel subtypes across neuron classes (or, alternatively, second-messenger modulation of Ih) produces the observed differences in Ih kinetics and voltage dependence — “The differences in Ih kinetics and voltage dependence indicate that either HCN channel subtypes are differentially distributed in the various neuron types or that modulation of Ih via second messengers might be responsible for the observed differences.”
mechanismGABAergic neurons carry a slow Ih because their current is conducted by channels assembled from HCN2 and HCN4 subunits — “We conclude from these experiments that GABAergic neurons in the IC exhibit Ih that is mainly transmitted via ion channels composed of HCN2 and HCN4 subunits.”
mechanismFast Ih in excitatory neurons is carried by HCN1/HCN2 channels while slow Ih is carried by HCN2 and sometimes HCN4, so subunit composition sets the kinetic class — “In contrast, different types of excitatory neurons exist, with either fast Ih transmitted via HCN1/HCN2, or slow Ih transmitted via HCN2 and in some cases HCN4.”
mechanismBecause HCN4 is restricted to GABAergic cells and is cAMP-sensitive, neuromodulators that raise second-messenger levels would selectively retune the integrative properties of the inhibitory population — “Since HCN4 channels are primarily expressed in GABAergic neurons, neuromodulatory inputs that increase intracellular second messenger concentration (e.g., serotonin) may predominantly target the integrative properties of the GABAergic population”
mechanismA large, fast-activating Ih depolarizes the membrane after release from hyperpolarization and thereby enables rebound spiking in specific excitatory subclasses — “This indicates that rebound firing is facilitated by large and fast-activating Ih and mainly found in specific subclasses of IC neurons.”
relation — “This confirms previous results (Zhao et al. 2011) and shows that almost all neurons in the CIC expressing EYFP and channelrhodopsin are also GAD67(cid:2) and thus GABAergic.”
relation — “The same study also identified a population of GABAergic neurons with build-up or/and pause response, a firing pattern that we did not observe in the CIC.”
relation — “As HCN1 has been shown to be tonotopically organized in the rat IC (Koch et al. 2004) we quantified whether a similar differential distribution of HCN1 was also present in the mouse IC (Fig. 4D).”

findingEvery GABAergic inferior colliculus neuron sampled showed a slowly activating hyperpolarization-activated current of moderate amplitude, whereas some excitatory neurons showed a fast-activating, large-amplitude current. — “All GABAergic neurons from our sample displayed slow-activating Ih with moderate amplitudes, whereas a subset of excitatory neurons showed fast-activating Ih with large amplitudes.”
findingImmunostaining placed the fast-gating HCN1 channel around excitatory neurons and the slow-gating HCN4 channel perisomatically around most inhibitory neurons. — “This is in agreement with our finding that immunoreactivity against the fast-gating hyperpolarization-activated and cyclicnucleotide-gated 1 (HCN1) channel was present around excitatory neurons, whereas the slow-gating HCN4 channel was found perisomatically around most inhibitory neurons.”
findingGABAergic somata lacked HCN1 labelling entirely, so HCN1 appears restricted to excitatory cells while HCN2 is expressed in both classes. — “One interesting result of our study was a lack of HCN1 staining around VGAT-EYFP(cid:2) and thus GABAergic cell somata.”
findingAbout 15% of glutamatergic neurons fired only at stimulus onset, and this pattern always co-occurred with a large, fast Ih. — “About 15% of the glutamatergic neurons showed an onset spiking pattern, always in combination with large and fast Ih.”
findingUnlike cortex and hippocampus, membrane properties and firing patterns did not separate GABAergic neurons from roughly a third of the excitatory neurons in this nucleus. — “In contrast to many other brain regions, membrane properties and firing patterns were similar in GABAergic neurons and about one-third of the excitatory neurons.”
findingOnly the onset-firing excitatory neurons formed a distinct class; GABAergic neurons were statistically indistinguishable from many excitatory types on membrane properties and Ih. — “To conclude, VGAT(cid:2) neurons are not significantly different from many VGAT(cid:5) neuron types in terms of membrane properties and Ih, whereas the VGAT(cid:5) onset type neurons seem to be a separate class of neurons.”
findingHCN1 immunoreactivity showed no systematic dorso-ventral (tonotopic) gradient across the mouse central nucleus. — “No gradient of HCN1 staining was observed along the dorso-ventral (tonotopic) axis within the CIC.”
findingA hyperpolarizing step to about −90 mV evoked a rebound spike in every onset-firing excitatory neuron and in 80% of fast-Ih adapting excitatory neurons, but rarely in slow-Ih or GABAergic neurons. — “Also, 80% of the VGAT(cid:5) adapting firing neurons with fast Ih exhibited rebound spiking, whereas only few VGAT(cid:2) or VGAT(cid:5) slow-Ih neurons showed rebound responses.”
findingFiring rate versus injected-current relationships did not differ between GABAergic and any excitatory group. — “Averaging of these firing rate-level relationships did not yield any difference between VGAT(cid:2) and VGAT(cid:5) groups”
findingGABAergic neurons adapted their spike rate sharply within the first 200 ms and then stopped adapting, whereas excitatory neurons kept adapting through the whole stimulus. — “On average, VGAT(cid:2) neurons displayed a rapid spike rate adaptation during the first 200 ms but did not adapt much thereafter”
finding96% of EYFP-positive (GABAergic) neurons carried a perisomatic HCN4 ring. — “Ninety-six percent of EYPF-positive neurons showed HCN4(cid:2) rings (74 neurons out of 77 neurons).”
findingThe onset-firing excitatory neurons had low input resistance and fast, large HCN1-mediated Ih, matching the profile of principal neurons of the medial and lateral superior olive. — “These membrane properties resemble the membrane properties of principal neurons in the lateral and medial superior olive, which express high levels of HCN1 and low voltage-activated potassium (Kv1.x) channels”
findingIh current density was indistinguishable between GABAergic and slow-tau excitatory neurons but significantly larger in fast-tau excitatory neurons. — “Current density was similar in VGAT(cid:2) and VGAT(cid:5), slow tau neurons but significantly larger in VGAT(cid:5), fast tau neurons when compared with the other groups (Table 1 and Fig.”
