transferfrom primate and human neuropsychology: Posner's covert orienting paradigm, rebuilt for rats - the transfer this review argues may have failed in translation — “However, the procedural parameters of the tasks used in primates and rodents are necessarily different. For example, the location of the eyes on the rat’s head prevents it from fixating a central point like primates. Instead, in the rat version of this task, subjects must maintain a nosepoke into a central port for set period of time and await presentation of the cues and targets in adjacent ports. The dependent variable is also different in that rats must initiate a movement to withdraw the head out of the center port and then reposition the snout in the target port.”
transferfrom human clinical neuropsychology: the Wisconsin Card Sorting task, transferred as a rodent perceptual set-shifting procedure — “The fact that PPC damage impaired discrimination after an extra dimensional shift suggests that it may be involved in attention that is required when the meaning of a stimulus has changed.”
transferfrom associative learning theory: the Pearce-Hall account of attention as driven by violated prediction, used as the framework for interpreting lesion effects — “According to one influential theory ( Pearce & Hall, 1980 ), a CS will capture attention if its meaning surprisingly changes. replicationcontested
limitationBecause the anatomical definition of PPC has varied across studies, results cannot be compared cleanly and firm functional conclusions are hard to draw. — “Moreover, the regional boundaries of PPC have varied across studies, making it difficult to compare results and further complicating attempts to draw firm conclusions about the function of PPC in rats.”
limitationAny verdict on rat-primate functional homology for attention is premature given how few studies restricted damage to PPC as currently defined. — “At this time, drawing conclusions about the functional homology (or lack thereof) between the rat and primate PPC with regard to attention may be premature.”
limitationThe evidence base is almost entirely lesion/inactivation work, with very little electrophysiological recording during attentional manipulations. — “Moreover, there is a paucity of electrophysiological studies investigating neural activity in PPC during attentional manipulations.”
limitationThe review deliberately excludes the spatial learning and navigation literature on PPC. — “Studies involving spatial learning and navigation will not be included since they are the topic of several other articles in this issue.”
limitationthe regional boundaries of PPC have varied across studies, which the review states makes results incomparable — “Moreover, the regional boundaries of PPC have varied across studies, making it difficult to compare results and further complicating attempts to draw firm conclusions about the function of PPC in rats.”
limitationaspiration and electrolytic lesions cannot separate damage to an area from destruction of fibres of passage — “Moreover, the aspiration and electrolytic techniques used in these studies prevent a functional role from being ascribed to a specific cortical area distinct from destruction of fibers of passage.”
limitationmany of the informative lesions were not restricted to PPC, so their effects could have come from outside it — “Indeed, damage that included PPC produced symptoms of contralateral neglect and inattention ( Crowne et al., 1986 ; King & Corwin, 1993 ), but those studies did not restrict damage to PPC as it is currently defined and the effects could have been due to damage outside PPC.”
limitationthe rat cannot fixate a central point, so the covert-attention task had to be rebuilt and may not measure the same thing — “Eye movement is also unrestricted during the procedure and raises the issue of whether or not the same ‘covert’ attentional mechanisms are being measured.”
limitationa negative homology result may be a failed task translation rather than a real species difference — “Thus, although the current data may indicate that the rat and primate PPC do not have similar roles in covert attentional orienting, it seems highly likely that the procedures used with humans and monkey were not comparably reproduced in rats.”
limitationactivity in PPC does not establish that PPC is necessary for the task — “However, activity in PPC does not necessarily indicate that the region is essential for task performance.”
limitationthere is a paucity of electrophysiological studies of PPC during attentional manipulation — “Moreover, there is a paucity of electrophysiological studies investigating neural activity in PPC during attentional manipulations.”
limitationthe proposed subregions of rat PPC are treated as one area because no functional differences have been described — “Recent studies have begun to parcel the rat PPC into subregions based primarily on cytoarchitectural and histochemical differences ( Swanson, 1998 ; Palomero-Gallagher & Zilles, 2004 ; Pérez-Clausell, 1996 ). To date, few (if any) studies have attempted to describe the functional differences between these putative subregions. Thus, for the purpose of this review, the area defined above as PPC will be considered as a whole.”

findingReviewing the rat literature, the author concludes that current evidence does not establish that rat PPC serves the same attentional or working-memory functions as primate PPC. — “Yet, the current data do not entirely support the notion that PPC has a similar role in attention or working memory in rodents and primates.”
findingPPC damage spares attention that merely guides performance on well-learned monitoring tasks, so the region appears not to be critical for sustained attention. — “Thus, the available data suggest that PPC may not have a critical role in sustained attention.”
findingStudies of working memory in rats, especially non-spatial versions, have overwhelmingly failed to find a PPC contribution. — “In summary, most of the studies that have examined a specific role for PPC in working memory, particularly in non-spatial tasks, have produced null results.”
findingPPC lesions left orienting responses to a light intact, indicating no deficit in this overt form of attention. — “In this paradigm, lesions of PPC affected neither unconditioned nor conditioned orienting behavior, suggesting a limited role for PPC in these aspects of attention.”
findingBy contrast, PPC lesions selectively worsened learning after an extradimensional shift while sparing intradimensional performance. — “Lesions of the PPC exaggerated the normal difficulty in learning produced by the extradimensional shift while leaving performance on the intradimensional shift intact.”
findingRemoving cholinergic input to PPC abolished the surprise-induced enhancement of learning without affecting the consistent control condition. — “Using the task illustrated in Table 1 , removal of cholinergic input to the PPC was shown to selectively impair learning in the Surprise group when the light was paired directly with food ( Bucci, Holland, & Gallagher, 1998 ).”

findingthe regional boundaries of PPC have varied across studies, making results incomparable — “Moreover, the regional boundaries of PPC have varied across studies, making it difficult to compare results and further complicating attempts to draw firm conclusions about the function of PPC in rats.”
findinglesions restricted to PPC produced no somatosensory neglect, while larger lesions did produce neglect — “Using lesion coordinates that did restrict damage to PPC, Ward and Brown (1997) found no evidence of somatosensory neglect.”
findingno deficit in covert attentional orienting followed PPC damage in two studies using different definitions of the region — “In one study, damage was largely restricted to the PPC as currently defined ( Ward & Brown, 1997 ) while the study by Rosner and Mittleman (1996) included the larger area defined as PPC by Kolb and Walkey (1987) . Thus it is unlikely that the null results were merely due to insufficient damage to the PPC.”
findingPPC damage exaggerated the cost of an extradimensional shift while leaving intradimensional shifts intact — “The fact that PPC damage impaired discrimination after an extra dimensional shift suggests that it may be involved in attention that is required when the meaning of a stimulus has changed.”
findingcholinergic denervation of PPC removed surprise-induced learning enhancement but not consistent-condition learning — “Using the task illustrated in Table 1 , removal of cholinergic input to the PPC was shown to selectively impair learning in the Surprise group when the light was paired directly with food ( Bucci, Holland, & Gallagher, 1998 ). findinginactivating PPC mattered only when the task required working memory together with long-term memory — “The authors found that inactivating the PPC only had an effect when the task demands required both working memory and reliance on long-term memory, but not working memory alone.”
findingrat and primate PPC have very similar connectivity, yet the functional data do not support homology — “The rat and primate PPC have very similar patterns of thalamic and cortical connectivity, suggesting that these areas may also be functional homologues. Yet, the current data do not entirely support the notion that PPC has a similar role in attention or working memory in rodents and primates.”
