phenomenonperceptual (repetition) priming in monkeys— “We tested for priming in rhesus monkeys using procedures similar to those that have been effective in humans.”
claim-typenull-result— “Across 11 experiments, monkeys did not show priming, nor did they show any reliable benefit of having seen to-be-classified images.”
method — evidence— “We tested six adult male rhesus monkeys ( Macaca mulatta ; mean age at start of testing = 3.2 years).”
transferfrom human memory research (human cognitive psychology): the human occluded-picture-naming priming paradigm is converted into a classification task so it can be run on monkeys— “As in priming studies in which humans were asked to name partially occluded images that they either had or had not seen previously ( Mitchell, 2006 ; Snodgrass & Feenan, 1990 ), we required monkeys to classify partially occluded photographs that they either had or had not seen previously.”
mechanismThe visual processing that supports recognition is encapsulated from the processing that supports classification, so remembering an image cannot feed forward to help classify a degraded version of it.— “This suggests that the visual processing necessary for recognition is cognitively encapsulated from that necessary for classification.”
mechanismPigeons and monkeys classify by different routes — local features versus global shape — which is offered as the explanation for why priming appeared in pigeons but not here.— “Thus, it appears that unlike our monkeys, pigeons are more likely to process local rather than global features in classifications tasks.”
builds-on Brodbeck (1997)— “Following Brodbeck (1997) , we trained rhesus monkeys ( Macaca mulatta ) to categorize photographs according to content (e.g., birds, fish, flowers, people).”
contradicts Brodbeck (1997)— “We failed to replicate the findings of the single study of priming in nonhumans ( Brodbeck, 1997 ).”
contradicts Mcmahon & Olson (2007)— “Given the short delays used (mean = 1.9 seconds), it is likely the monkeys would have recognized the primes and, thus, this study does not provide evidence for implicit priming in monkeys.”
sensepriming as IMPLICIT - named by the paper as the critical feature of the theories it is testing, and stated as a double requirement: subjects identify primed material better AND cannot explicitly remember it. The effect is defined partly by a FAILURE that has to accompany it, which is why a study finding memory for the primes is testing something the definition cares about— “A critical feature of theories of the organization of human memory is that priming is implicit. Subjects are better at identifying primed material even when they cannot explicitly remember”
sensepriming defined by a PROPERTY LIST rather than by a mechanism - automatic, effortless, unconscious, neuroanatomically separate from sophisticated forms of memory such as episodic memory. Every item is a contrast with something else, and it is precisely this shape of definition that licenses the leap to evolutionary age: a capacity that needs none of the expensive machinery is assumed to have come first— “Because priming is automatic, effortless, unconscious, and neuroanatomically separate from sophisticated forms of memory such as episodic memory, researchers have theorized that it may be evolutionary old”
sensethe PHYLOGENETIC ASSUMPTION, named as an assumption in the abstract: priming is `commonly thought, WITH LITTLE EVIDENCE, to be widely distributed phylogenetically`. The paper's target is not a finding but a belief its field holds without having tested it - and the only prior nonhuman evidence it can find is one study in pigeons— “Such priming is commonly thought, with little evidence, to be widely distributed phylogenetically. Following Brodbeck (1997) , we trained rhesus monkeys”
sensesearch image versus perceptual priming - a neighbouring concept separated by two stated measurements rather than by definition: the search image is SHORT LIVED and is DIMINISHED BY DIVIDED ATTENTION, so it is classed as short-term ATTENTIONAL priming rather than the long-term PERCEPTUAL priming seen in humans. Two literatures using one word, told apart by duration and by whether attention matters— “The phenomenon of perceptual priming is also similar to, but distinct from, the concept of “specific searching image” in studies of insect predation by birds ( Tinbergen, 1960 ). After repeated exposure to cryptic prey, such as when foraging for camouflaged moths, birds may form a mental image that facilitates subsequent searches for that…
sense`priming` and `perceptual priming` are used at DIFFERENT GRAIN in different parts of the argument, and the split is systematic rather than casual: where the paper reasons about memory SYSTEMS - dissociation from explicit memory, survival in amnesia, evolutionary age - it writes bare `priming`; where it describes the visual effect it measures, and where it fences that effect off from the search image, it writes `perceptual priming`. The general term carries the theory and the specific term carries the experiment— “Thus, priming is cognitively distinct from explicit memory, long-lasting, early developing, and depends on neural systems distinct from those serving explicit memory. Because priming is automatic, effortless, unconscious, and neuroanatomically separate from sophisticated forms of memory such as episodic memory, researchers have theorized that…
sensea null defended rather than reported - the paper argues its own absence of evidence is not absence of power, on three separate grounds: priming is routinely seen in SINGLE human subjects, the pigeon result that motivated the study used FEWER subjects than this one, and the same animals demonstrably remembered the primes. The last is the one that matters, because it rules out the animals not having encoded the images at all— “Our null results are unlikely to be the product of low statistical power.
