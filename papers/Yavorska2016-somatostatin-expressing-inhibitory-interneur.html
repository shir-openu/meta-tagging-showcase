findingWhether SOM inhibition is subtractive or divisive is not a fixed property of the cell type but depends on the stimulus: small visual stimuli yield late subtractive inhibition, large stimuli fast divisive inhibition.
findingBecause their input synapses facilitate, SOM cells contribute little inhibition during low-frequency activity but strong inhibition during sustained high-frequency activity.
findingSOM cells sit outside the canonical thalamocortical feedforward path: they receive essentially no thalamic drive and are instead recruited by intracortical input.
findingLayer 4 SOM cells labeled in the X94 line invert the usual SOM output rule by inhibiting PV-positive fast-spiking interneurons instead of pyramidal neurons.
findingAssociative (fear) learning raises GABA and somatostatin expression in barrel-cortex SOM cells, implying stronger SOM inhibition supports circuit plasticity.
findingDuring motor learning SOM activity controls dendritic-spine stability on layer 2/3 apical tufts in opposite directions: activation destabilises spines, suppression hyperstabilises them.
findingBarrel-cortex SOM cells behave opposite to visual-cortex SOM cells, firing tonically at rest and being hyperpolarized and silenced by whisker stimulation.
