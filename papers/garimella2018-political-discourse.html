mechanismand that borrowed score has a stated meaning of its own - the FRACTION OF FACEBOOK USERS VISITING A DOMAIN WHO IDENTIFY THEMSELVES AS CONSERVATIVE ON THEIR PROFILE - which the authors read as the domain having a conservative or liberal bent in its coverage— “The score takes values between 0 and 1 and expresses the fraction of Facebook users who visit these pages that identify themselves as conservative on their Facebook profile. mechanismthe measurement is deliberately restricted to what can be scored: only tweets carrying a link to a news organisation with a known and independently derived leaning are counted at all— “To quantify the political leaning of content posted on Twitter, we consider only messages that contain a link to an online news organization with a known and independently derived political leaning. In particular, we use the dataset of the political leaning scores of news organizations described in Section 3.”
mechanismCONSUMPTION IS DEFINED THROUGH THE NETWORK: a user's produced content is the tweets they post, and their consumed content is the tweets posted by the accounts they follow— “For the content production of a user u, we consider tweets posted by user u. For the content consumption of a user u we consider tweets posted by users whom u follows.”
mechanismpartisanship is given a PARAMETERISED definition rather than a threshold - a user is delta-partisan when their production polarity lies within delta of either extreme - and the authors state the nesting property that follows, so a claim at one delta implies the claim at every larger one— “We say that a user is δ -partisan, for some value 0 ≤ δ ≤ 1 2 , if their production polarity is within δ from either extreme value min{p(u), 1 − p(u)} ≤ δ . (2) The smaller the value of δ the more partisan a user is. Note also that if a user u is δ -partisan then u is also δ ′-partisan for δ < δ ′ ≤ 1”
mechanismVARIANCE IS MEASURED ALONGSIDE THE MEAN over the same set of tweets, with the stated objective of quantifying the RANGE of opinions a user's produced content covers— “Besides the average political leaning of produced tweets, we also measure the variance in political leaning over the same set of tweets. The objective is to quantify the range of opinions of a user covered by the produced content.”

extends prior echo-chamber studies that measure either content or network alone— “In this paper, we propose measures to identify the existence of an echo chamber by using both the content being read/shared and the network that enables the content to propagate.”
sensean echo chamber - AS AGREEMENT BETWEEN TWO PER-USER SCORES: it exists, for this paper, when the leaning of the content a user receives agrees with the leaning of what they share; not a group, not a network structure, not an isolation measure, but a correlation computed per user - and the authors adopt it because the literature offers no consensus formal definition to inherit— “We say that an echo chamber exists if the political leaning of the content that users receive from the network agrees with that of the content they share. As there is no consensus on a formal definition in the literature, we opt for this definition, which is general enough and reasonably captures the essence of the phenomenon”
sensea gatekeeper - AN INDIVIDUAL USER, NOT A MEDIA SOURCE: communication studies gives the word to institutions that filter what passes into a channel, and this paper reassigns it to a person at a particular position on two scores, stating the earlier sense and supplying a bridging argument - that such users block information from one side, and hence can be considered gatekeepers— “Gatekeepers are defined in media and communication studies as media sources that act as filters (or ‘gatekeepers’) of information [27].
