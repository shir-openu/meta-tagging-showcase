limitationpolitical leaning is measured only from tweets that link to news sources of known, independently-derived leaning, not the message content itself — “To quantify the political leaning of content posted on Twitter, we consider only messages that contain a link to an online news organization with a known and independently derived political leaning.”
limitationAll data come from Twitter and are only a sample of social-media discussion. — “First, the datasets used are just a sample of all the discussions in social media, and they all come from Twitter.”
limitationResults are drawn from politically-savvy users and should not be assumed to generalise to the general public. — “However, as we focused on politically-savvy users on Twitter, the reader should not infer that our observations generalize immediately to other settings, or that echo chamber effects are as pronounced for the general public.”
limitationThe polarity measures depend on externally supplied political labels for news sources. — “Second, our production and consumption scores rely on external labeling of news sources along a political axis.”
limitationFollowing is used as a proxy for actual consumption; users may not read everything their neighbours post. — “Moreover, our analysis assumes that each user consumes all content produced by all their neighbors.”
limitationSome articles from the scored news domains may not be political. — “Finally, it is possible that not all news articles from the news sources we base the polarity measures are political.”
limitationThe causal relations between selective exposure, biased assimilation, algorithmic filtering and echo chambers are not addressed. — “Understanding how all these phenomena interact with each other and the precise causality relations is beyond the scope of this paper.”
limitationThe literature has no consensus definition of the paper's central term, so the authors chose one. — “As there is no consensus on a formal definition in the literature, we opt for this definition, which is general enough and reasonably captures the essence of the phenomenon”
limitationA term is borrowed from another field with its unit of analysis changed, which the authors state. — “Gatekeepers are defined in media and communication studies as media sources that act as filters (or ‘gatekeepers’) of information [27]. In our case, we consider consumption and production of content jointly, and define gatekeepers as users who consume content from both sides of the political spectrum but only produce content from one side”
limitationA second definition of the same category was tested and its results not presented. — “We also experimented with a different definition of gatekeepers – users who have a high consumption variance and low production variance. This definition captures a slightly broader set of users (compared to Equation 5), e.g., users who consume from both ends of the political spectrum and produce balanced ‘centrist’ content. The results were almost identical to the ones reported above in Section 5.3, and so we do not present them explicitly”
limitationWhat the identified users are actually doing is left open by the authors. — “Nevertheless, from our current analysis, it is not clear if such users act as open-minded net-citizens”
