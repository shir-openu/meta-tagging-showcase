mechanismcontent and network are measured JOINTLY at the level of the individual user, rather than content consumption alone, and the authors state that as the study's distinguishing move — “Unlike many previous works that focus on measuring only content consumption to quantify the echo-chamber effects, we study content consumption and production jointly at the level of individual users, and examine how different content profiles correlate with the network position of users. Though we are not the first to study echo chambers on Twitter, to the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to jointly use content and network to characterize echo chambers.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
mechanismPOLITICAL LEANING IS NOT INFERRED FROM THE TEXT AT ALL: scoring short snippets is called very challenging, so the leaning is inherited from an externally supplied per-domain ground truth of news organisations — “Obtaining a characterization of political leaning for short text snippets, such as tweets, is a very challenging problem, in general. To confront this challenge, we use a ground truth of political leaning scores of various news organizations with”
mechanismand that borrowed score has a stated meaning of its own - the FRACTION OF FACEBOOK USERS VISITING A DOMAIN WHO IDENTIFY THEMSELVES AS CONSERVATIVE ON THEIR PROFILE - which the authors read as the domain having a conservative or liberal bent in its coverage — “The score takes values between 0 and 1 and expresses the fraction of Facebook users who visit these pages that identify themselves as conservative on their Facebook profile. A value close to 1 (0) indicates that the domain has a conservative (liberal) bent in their coverage.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
mechanismthe measurement is deliberately restricted to what can be scored: only tweets carrying a link to a news organisation with a known and independently derived leaning are counted at all — “To quantify the political leaning of content posted on Twitter, we consider only messages that contain a link to an online news organization with a known and independently derived political leaning. In particular, we use the dataset of the political leaning scores of news organizations described in Section 3.”
mechanismCONSUMPTION IS DEFINED THROUGH THE NETWORK: a user's produced content is the tweets they post, and their consumed content is the tweets posted by the accounts they follow — “For the content production of a user u, we consider tweets posted by user u. For the content consumption of a user u we consider tweets posted by users whom u follows.”
mechanismpartisanship is given a PARAMETERISED definition rather than a threshold - a user is delta-partisan when their production polarity lies within delta of either extreme - and the authors state the nesting property that follows, so a claim at one delta implies the claim at every larger one — “We say that a user is δ -partisan, for some value 0 ≤ δ ≤ 1 2 , if their production polarity is within δ from either extreme value min{p(u), 1 − p(u)} ≤ δ . (2) The smaller the value of δ the more partisan a user is. Note also that if a user u is δ -partisan then u is also δ ′-partisan for δ < δ ′ ≤ 1” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
mechanismVARIANCE IS MEASURED ALONGSIDE THE MEAN over the same set of tweets, with the stated objective of quantifying the RANGE of opinions a user's produced content covers — “Besides the average political leaning of produced tweets, we also measure the variance in political leaning over the same set of tweets. The objective is to quantify the range of opinions of a user covered by the produced content.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
mechanismFIVE NON-POLITICAL DATASETS ARE COLLECTED AS A BASELINE for the political ones - tbt, ff, gameofthrones, love, foodporn - each built from one hashtag by parsing an archive, filtering to users with at least five tweets, and then taking all of those users' tweets — “To have a baseline for our measurements over the Political datasets, we also use five datasets that correspond to non-political topics, in particular: tbt (“throwback Thursday”), ff (“follow Friday”), gameofthrones, love, and foodporn. Each of these topics is associated with a particular hashtag (e.g., #tbt for tbt). The datasets are built as follows. First, we parse the tweets in the Internet Archive collection and select tweets that contain the corresponding hashtag for each topic during the month of June 2016. Second, we filter out users who have less than 5 tweets. Third, we obtain all tweets generated by these users.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
mechanismautomated accounts are excluded by minimum AND maximum thresholds on tweets per day, followers and friends, together with a requirement that the account be at least one year old at collection time — “For all the datasets, we perform simple checks to remove bots, using minimum and maximum thresholds for number of tweets per day, followers, friends, and ensure that the account is at least one year old at the time of data collection.”
mechanismthe paper STIPULATES 'gatekeeper' against the established sense and says so: in communication studies the term names news media sources acting as filters, and here it names an individual user who receives content from both leanings but produces only one — “Gatekeeping is a term commonly used in communication studies to refer to news media sources that act as filters of information [27]. Barzilai-Nahon [8] propose a model based on network theory for gatekeeping which generalizes the concept of gatekeeping for the Internet and applies to all information types (not just news). Several studies have looked at gatekeeping practices on Twitter [25, 39] and conclude that unlike in traditional media, any common user can become a gatekeeper on social media. The definition of these gatekeepers on social media also differs from the traditional gatekeepers in media organizations, due to the alternative information pathways available to social media users. In our case, we define gatekeepers as users who receive content from both political leanings, but only produce content from a single leaning, thus “filtering” information from one side.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]

limitationpolitical leaning is measured only from tweets that link to news sources of known, independently-derived leaning, not the message content itself — “To quantify the political leaning of content posted on Twitter, we consider only messages that contain a link to an online news organization with a known and independently derived political leaning.”
limitationAll data come from Twitter and are only a sample of social-media discussion. — “First, the datasets used are just a sample of all the discussions in social media, and they all come from Twitter.”
limitationResults are drawn from politically-savvy users and should not be assumed to generalise to the general public. — “However, as we focused on politically-savvy users on Twitter, the reader should not infer that our observations generalize immediately to other settings, or that echo chamber effects are as pronounced for the general public.”
limitationThe polarity measures depend on externally supplied political labels for news sources. — “Second, our production and consumption scores rely on external labeling of news sources along a political axis.”
limitationFollowing is used as a proxy for actual consumption; users may not read everything their neighbours post. — “Moreover, our analysis assumes that each user consumes all content produced by all their neighbors.”
limitationSome articles from the scored news domains may not be political. — “Finally, it is possible that not all news articles from the news sources we base the polarity measures are political.”
limitationThe causal relations between selective exposure, biased assimilation, algorithmic filtering and echo chambers are not addressed. — “Understanding how all these phenomena interact with each other and the precise causality relations is beyond the scope of this paper.”
limitationThe literature has no consensus definition of the paper's central term, so the authors chose one. — “As there is no consensus on a formal definition in the literature, we opt for this definition, which is general enough and reasonably captures the essence of the phenomenon”
limitationA term is borrowed from another field with its unit of analysis changed, which the authors state. — “Gatekeepers are defined in media and communication studies as media sources that act as filters (or ‘gatekeepers’) of information [27]. In our case, we consider consumption and production of content jointly, and define gatekeepers as users who consume content from both sides of the political spectrum but only produce content from one side” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
limitationA second definition of the same category was tested and its results not presented. — “We also experimented with a different definition of gatekeepers – users who have a high consumption variance and low production variance. This definition captures a slightly broader set of users (compared to Equation 5), e.g., users who consume from both ends of the political spectrum and produce balanced ‘centrist’ content. The results were almost identical to the ones reported above in Section 5.3, and so we do not present them explicitly”
limitationWhat the identified users are actually doing is left open by the authors. — “Nevertheless, from our current analysis, it is not clear if such users act as open-minded net-citizens”
