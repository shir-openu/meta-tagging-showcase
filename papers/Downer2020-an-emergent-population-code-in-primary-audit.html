phenomenonfeature-selective auditory attention
claim-typemechanism
mechanismBecause feature tuning co-varies within single neurons, a gain applied to individual cells cannot separate the two features; only re-weighting neurons at the population level recovers a feature-specific signal. — “In other words, A1 neurons rarely uniquely encode one sound feature or the other because feature tuning co-varies at the single-neuron level (similar to Fig. 3e-h). This prevents single-neuron tuning from providing a robust basis for attentional perceptual enhancement.”
mechanismMixed selectivity cancels the average effect of feature-specific gain, which is why single-neuron measures show nothing while the population does. — “Thus, when single neurons exhibit mixed selectivity in their tuning to different sensory features, FSG as a mechanism of attention exhibits no average attentional enhancement (Fig. 3g,h).”
mechanismThe attentional benefit is carried by higher-order correlations among neurons, i.e. it is an emergent property of the ensemble rather than a sum of single-cell effects. — “This suggests that our observed population enhancement of sound feature encoding constitutes an emergent effect, relying on higher-order correlations among A1 neurons.”
mechanismPopulation coding unfolds in stages over the stimulus: a fast feature-general change detection followed by finer feature discrimination. — “These temporal dynamics suggest that early population responses function to detect a rapid change, irrespective of feature, whereas sustained responses elicit finer-grained feature selectivity.”
relation — “Therefore, when comparing feature tuning across the more rigorous demands of different feature attention conditions, we do not observe tuning changes that have been found in several prior studies comparing A1 tuning between passive vs. active conditions and between attend-toward vs. attend-away conditions (Niwa et al., 2012b; Schwartz and David, 2017; von Trapp et al., 2016).”
relation — “This prompted us to conduct a population-level analysis, inspired by studies performed in PFC (Mante et al., 2013; Rigotti et al., 2013), to make sense of the heterogeneous single-neuron representations.”
replicationuntested
limitationThe analysis uses only firing rate, not spike timing. — “An important consideration of the present report is our exclusive use of firing rate, as opposed to spike-timing measures of neural activity.”
limitationBecause many bandwidth targets were also amplitude-modulated, phase locking could signal a target in both attention conditions, so the task may not have been demanding enough to reveal attentional effects on spike timing. — “It is important to note that the design of our task is such that, even when attending to the spectral feature, many target sounds are temporally modulated as well, and therefore phase-locking can signal the presence of a target in both attention conditions.”
limitationNeurons were pooled across sessions and trials resampled, so real correlated and intrinsic variability had to be reintroduced by modelling rather than measured simultaneously. — “An important consideration with this approach is that, by combining neurons across experimental sessions, and by randomly re-sampling trials, we fail to approximate correlated and intrinsic variability between and within neurons, respectively.”
limitationThe task may be too easy to force attention-related changes in A1 temporal dynamics. — “Thus, the present task may not sufficiently challenge the auditory system to drive attention-related changes in the temporal dynamics of A1 neurons’ spiking.”
limitationThe two levels of analysis disagree on the same data: attention does not enhance the single-neuron representation, while the population analysis shows enhanced encoding. — “attention does not enhance the single-neuron representation of attended features in our data. However, a population-level analysis reveals that ensembles of neurons exhibit enhanced encoding of attended sound features, and this population code tracks subjects’ performance.”
limitationThe physical stimulus differs between animals, because the values were tailored to each subject's own psychophysical thresholds rather than fixed. — “The precise AM and ∆BW values used during recording sessions were tailored to each subject’s psychophysical thresholds for detection of each sound feature (AM and ∆BW) in isolation, determined prior to recording.”
limitationThe condition labels are defined by a sensitivity criterion, not by a physical quantity: 'near threshold' means the level at which d-prime equals 1. — “Three values of each feature were used in recording sessions: one near psychophysical threshold (defined as the modulation level at which the subject’s sensitivity, measured using d’ (Wickens, 2002) was equal to 1), one slightly above threshold (d’ ~ 1.2), and one well above threshold (d’> 1.5).”
limitationAn inclusion decision was made and checked, and the check is reported rather than the criterion being justified in advance. — “Removing SUs with only 2 experimental blocks of isolation (n = 58) from analysis does not qualitatively affect our results nor main conclusions and interpretations.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingIndividual A1 neurons carried mixed selectivity, encoding both the spectral and temporal sound features and the attention condition. — “We find that single neurons tend to be highdimensional, in that they exhibit substantial mixed selectivity for both sound features, as well as task context.”
findingAttention produced no average enhancement of feature encoding in single A1 neurons. — “Contrary to common findings in many previous experiments, attention does not enhance the single-neuron representation of attended features in our data.”
findingSingle-neuron feature sensitivity measured by ROC area was statistically identical across the two attention conditions. — “Though we do observe some scatter around the unity line, neurons increase or decrease their AM ROC area with equal likelihood, (sign rank test, p = 0.74).”
findingAttention was the variable with a significant main effect in the largest number of neurons, yet interactions with feature tuning were rare. — “Context had a significant main effect in more neurons than either acoustic feature, as measured by the proportion of neurons with significant context coefficients”
findingAM and bandwidth tuning were positively correlated across neurons, so a single neuron's rate cannot disambiguate which feature changed. — “measured by the sign of the coefficient (Spearman correlation, r = 0.37, p < 0.001).”
findingAfter targeted dimensionality reduction, the population code decoded each sound feature better when that feature was attended. — “However, a population-level analysis reveals that ensembles of neurons exhibit enhanced encoding of attended sound features, and this population code tracks subjects’ performance.”
findingThe attentional advantage in the population code was large and highly reliable across simulated populations. — “Across 100 simulated neural populations, the average ROC enhancement is 0.151 for AM (p=2.41e-30)”
findingThe observed attentional modulation exceeded that of every surrogate population in which higher-order correlations had been destroyed. — “Comparing the attentional modulation index (z-score of ROC difference of attended relative to unattended feature) of the neural data (9c, vertical red line) to that of 1000 surrogate populations, we find no overlap between our observed attentional enhancement and that expected by the simpler single-neuron and pairwise covariance properties.”
findingTrial-by-trial population projections predicted whether the animal answered correctly, along each task-variable axis. — “The distribution of choice probability (CP) values across 100 simulated populations reveals a significant relationship between AM-axis projections and task performance, as evidenced by a mean CP across simulations of 0.573 (p=1.90e-39).”
findingBoth monkeys weighted the attended feature more heavily than the unattended one in their yes/no reports. — “Each subject performed the task above chance, as evidenced by a greater influence of the attended feature vs. the unattended feature on the probability of a “Yes” response (Figure 2; 47 total sessions, p = 8.98e-8 ; sign rank test, collapsed across monkeys).”
findingAttention did not alter spike timing: phase locking to the temporal envelope was unaffected by the attention condition. — “However, we find no evidence of attention-related effects on phase locking (Figure 11), suggesting that phase-locking may not be relevant for feature-selective attention in the context of this task in A1.”
findingPopulation subspace trajectories were initially feature-ambiguous and only separated by feature partway through the stimulus. — “Trajectories in both conditions exhibit an early, non-selective course wherein each stimulus projects to roughly the same area within the subspace.”

definitiontargeted dimensionality reduction (TDR) — “TDR is a method of calculating a weighted average of neural activity across a sample of neurons.”
definitionde-mixing by TDR — “TDR both reduces the dimensionality of the population response from # neurons to # projections and provides a way of “de-mixing” neural activity across a population of neurons with mixed selectivity.”
definitionfeature-specific gain (FSG) — “whereby the response of a neuron is determined by both its sensory response function and a multiplicative gain parameter that depends on the neuron’s preference for the attended feature, yielding larger responses when attention is directed toward the neuron’s preferred feature.”
definitionROC area as a neural sensitivity measure — “If a neuron’s ROC area is 0.5, that is interpreted as a failure to detect the presence of a feature.”
definitionchoice probability (CP) — “In order to quantify these effects, we calculated the ROC area between correct and incorrect response distributions, a metric commonly referred to as “Choice Probability” (CP) (Britten et al., 1992).”
definitionconditions versus variables in this study — “We refer to “variables” as the three dimensions along which all conditions vary, namely the two sound features (AM, ∆BW) and context (attention).”
definitionsurrogate population — “This method constructs surrogate populations by shuffling the original data labels, but then recovering (to the extent possible) the marginal and covariance features of the original data by transforming the shuffled data using a “readout” matrix.”
