phenomenonbidirectional optogenetic control of individual inhibitory neurons in freely-moving mice
claim-typemethod
transferfrom laser optics / photonics: The simple astigmatic Gaussian beam model, a solution of the paraxial Helmholtz equation from laser-beam physics, is imported to predict coupling efficiency and set the optical pathway lengths of the implantable neural device. — “Like the Gaussian beam model, the SAG model is a solution of the paraxial Helmholtz equation.”
mechanismChoosing an inhibitory (PV) opsin-expressing cell reverses the sign of the expected synaptic side-effect, so any blue-light-driven rate increase in the tagged cell must come from direct illumination rather than from trans-synaptic drive — “when the opsin-expressing cell is inhibitory, synaptic effects are reversed and any rate increase following blue light illumination stems from direct illumination (with the caveat of disynaptic dis-inhibition).”
mechanismOptically driven PV cells inhibit their postsynaptic pyramidal partners, so activating the PV cell lowers pyramidal firing and silencing it raises pyramidal firing — “These observations are consistent with inhibitory synaptic connections between the optically manipulated PV cells and the putative PYR”
mechanisma recorded neuron can inherit a rate change it never received light for — “a nonilluminated/opsin-free recorded neuron may inherit a rate increase/decrease via glutamatergic connections from directlyilluminated, opsin-expressing pre-synaptic neurons”
relation — “We have recently reported such an advance [16], [17], but that approach was tailored to specific wavelengths (405 and 638 nm), required custom-made parts (e.g. GRIN lenses), utilized a full nanofabrication facility, and was applied only to head-fixed anesthetized animals.”
relation — “This is not the first time that the same neuron is both activated and silenced optically: previous demonstrations were in brain slices [8], [26], in a head-fixed mouse [16], and in a freely-moving rat [15].”
differs inrate increase → ChR2 — PULSE DURATION DECIDES WHICH DIRECTION COUNTS AS EVIDENCE: a rate increase during 50 ms pulses and a rate DECREASE during 200 ms pulses both tag a cell as PV. The same cell class is identified by opposite signs of the same measure, separated only by how long the light was on — “as PVcells, based on a rate increase during 50 ms blue light pulses (p<0.01, Poisson test) or a rate decrease during 200 ms light pulses”
replicationuntested
limitationEach dual-color unit takes about six hours of manual assembly. — “The main disadvantage of the presented approach is assembly duration: assembling a single dual-color optical unit requires about six hours of work.”
limitationUnit weight over 0.5 g caps how many can be mounted on a small animal. — “A second limitation is the size of the unit: although lighter than optical tables by four or five orders of magnitude, each unit weighs over 0.5 g.”
limitationOnly a fraction of PV interneurons expressed both opsins, so not all blue-responsive cells were also red-responsive. — “A third limiting factor is the percentage of PV interneurons with double expression of both CaMKII and Jaws.”
limitationThe inference from a rate increase to direct illumination carries a named exception, and it is stated inside the sentence the conclusion is drawn from. — “any rate increase following blue light illumination stems from direct illumination (with the caveat of disynaptic dis-inhibition”
limitationThe design space is bounded by what is commercially available and by the physical envelope, not by what the experiment would ideally need. — “The physical requirements were met using ø3.8 mm Transistor Outline (TO) can LDs. To maximize the dynamic range of the light output, we chose LDs that can operate within the maximal current limitation of 100 mA and have low threshold currents.”
limitationThe design space was bounded by the model before it was bounded by experiment: butt-coupling was excluded on modelled grounds, so the reported geometry is the survivor of a simulated elimination rather than a measured comparison. — “the results of the predictive model indicated that a butt-coupling solution [15] cannot be extrapolated to the dual-source case simply by angling one or both of the diodes”

findingThe lens-free dual-color unit delivered 454±271 µW of 450 nm light and 8.65±2.26 mW of 638 nm light at the fiber tip at 100 mA, far above the 100 µW / 1 mW design thresholds. — “The light power measured at the tip of the fiber at a driving current of 100 mA was 454±271 µW and 8.65±2.26 mW for the 450 nm and 638 nm wavelengths, respectively”
findingThe blue and red illumination fields emerging from the shared multimode fiber overlapped spatially by 46%, a lower bound on the in-brain overlap. — “Nevertheless, the illumination profiles of two LDs coupled to the same fiber overlapped by 46%”
findingThe two wavelengths did not interact optically: their spectra at the fiber end were separate and no spectral interference occurred during simultaneous dual-color illumination. — “In contrast, the spectra at the end of the fiber (measured using CCS100 spectrometer, Thorlabs) did not overlap, and there was no spectral interference during dual-color illumination”
findingNine of 74 interneurons (12%) were controlled bidirectionally through the same fiber, raising their spike rate under blue light and lowering it under red light. — “Indeed, some of the units that modulated their activity during blue and red light application also increased their spike rate during blue light and decreased during red light (9/74 of the INT; 12%).”
findingBlue-light activation produced significantly larger effects than red-light silencing, which the authors attribute to less red light being applied or weaker expression of the red-sensitive opsin. — “Thus, blue light induced stronger effects than red light”
findingThe second-order effects on pyramidal cells were strictly sign-appropriate for inhibition: no pyramidal cell was excited by blue light and none was suppressed by red light. — “No PYR increased their firing rate during blue light, and no PYR decreased their firing rates during red light.”
findingOn the freely-moving mouse's head the laser can heated by no more than 7 °C even under prolonged pulsed illumination. — “We found that heating did not exceed 7 °C even during prolonged illumination (maximal current, 29 mA; pulse duration, 50 ms; duty cycle, 0.6%; Fig. 9A, right).”
findingWide-band recordings taken during both blue and red illumination were free of electromagnetic-interference artifacts, so the shielding and grounding worked at the power levels used. — “Visual inspection of the data recorded during both blue and red illumination in the dual-color mice revealed clean wide-band traces”
findingThe paper claims the first conclusive demonstration that a single identified neuron can be both activated and silenced in a freely-moving animal. — “Thus the present work shows conclusively, for the first time, that the same neuron can be both activated and silenced in a freely-moving animal.”
findingThe approach's main cost is labour: each dual-color optical unit takes roughly six hours to assemble. — “The main disadvantage of the presented approach is assembly duration: assembling a single dual-color optical unit requires about six hours of work.”
