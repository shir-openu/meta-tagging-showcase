phenomenonstructure of the Facebook social graph
claim-typeexistence
mechanismthe population is fixed by a STIPULATED definition of an active user - logged in within the last 28 days AND holding at least one friend - and the authors state that this is not the platform's own definition and that their statistics therefore differ from company figures — “We defined a user of Facebook as an active member of the social network if they logged into the site in the last 28 days from our time of measurement in May 2011 and had at least one Facebook friend. We note that this definition is not precisely Facebook’s ordinary definition of active user, and therefore some of our statistics differ slightly from company statistics.”
mechanismthe whole graph is analysed rather than sampled or crawled, and the stated reason is a defect of the alternatives rather than a preference: sampling and crawling cannot tell an active account from a stale one — “Then another thread of research has measured some large-scale network properties of the Facebook graph through sampling, crawling, and other methods to collect network data [22,23]. Notably, these methodologies have no way of distinguishing between active and stale accounts. Unlike these studies, we analyze the entire Facebook graph in anonymized form and focus on the set of active user accounts reliably corresponding to people.”
mechanismthe neighbourhood function is used in place of the diameter, because the diameter is an extremal measure that a single ill-connected path in a peripheral region can wildly distort, while a percentile of vertex pairs is robust to it — “The diameter is an extremal measure, and it is commonly considered less interesting than the full neighborhood function, which measures what percentile of vertex pairs are within a given distance. The exact diameter can be wildly distorted by the presence of a single ill-connected path in some peripheral region of the graph, while the neighborhood function and its average are thought to robustly capture the ‘typical’ distances between pairs of vertices.”
mechanismthe paper rejects the power-law model for its degree distribution and then DECLINES to fit a replacement, naming instead the features of the distribution it takes to be the relevant results — “We conclude, like Ref. [23], that strict power-law models are inappropriate for Facebook’s degree distribution. It is not our intent, though, to determine which parametric form best models the distribution. The relevant results are the monotonicity and curvature of the degree distribution, the degrees of typical users, the large variance in degrees, and the network’s sparsity.”
mechanismthe component structure is computed by a union-find algorithm with path compression that records components as edges are added, which is chosen because IT DOES NOT REQUIRE THE EDGES TO BE HELD IN MEMORY - the whole edge list is streamed past a single machine instead — “The NZ algorithm, a type of Union-Find algorithm with path compression, records component structure dynamically as edges are added to a network that begins completely empty of edges. When all edges are added, the algorithm has computed the component structure of the network. Crucially for our purposes, the NZ algorithm does not require the edges to be retained in memory. We apply it to the Facebook network on a single computer with 64GB of RAM by streaming over the list of edges.”
mechanismneighbourhood statistics are estimated from a stratified sample rather than the whole graph: users are drawn by reservoir sampling at each of a hundred log-spaced neighbourhood sizes, and the reported percentiles are stated to be empirical percentiles of that sample rather than of the population — “For analyzing network neighborhoods, 5,000 users were randomly selected using reservoir sampling for each of 100 log-spaced neighborhood sizes, creating a sample of 500,000 users for which the analysis was performed. The percentiles shown for the clustering coefficient and degeneracy for each neighborhood size are therefore empirical percentiles from this sample population of 5,000 users.”
mechanismthe path-length figures are produced by running the neighbourhood-function algorithm repeatedly on one machine and AVERAGING ACROSS THE RUNS rather than computing once — “For path length calculations, neighborhood functions were computed on a single 24-core machine with 72 GB of RAM using the HyperANF algorithm [48], averaging across 10 runs.”
mechanismthe edge relation is restricted to RECIPROCAL friendships only, and the authors date the restriction: the analysis predated the existence of subscriptions on the platform — “Our analysis predated the existence of ‘subscriptions’ on Facebook, and in this work we study only reciprocal Facebook friendships.”

limitationThe paper's own definition of an 'active' user departs from Facebook's, so its statistics differ from company figures. — “We note that this definition is not precisely Facebook’s ordinary definition of active user, and therefore some of our statistics differ slightly from company statistics.”
limitationCountry assignment relies on geo-IP data, which is imperfect and only reliable at national granularity. — “While imperfect, so-called geo-IP data is generally reliable on a national level.”
limitationThe analysis is acknowledged as non-exhaustive with respect to available network measures. — “While our computations have elucidated many aspects of the structure of the world’s largest social network, we have certainly not exhausted the possibilities of network analysis.”
limitationThe 'six degrees' confirmation is a statement about percentages of pairs in a friendship graph, not a repeat of the experiment that produced the phrase. — “As Figure 2 shows, fully 92% of all pairs of Facebook users were within five degrees of separation, and 99.6% were within six degrees”
limitationThe object studied is an abstraction the authors themselves call limited. — “This abstract view of human relationships, while certainly limited, has been very useful for characterizing social relationships”
limitationA small group of accounts sits far outside the typical user, so the average describes nobody in particular. — “The small population of users with abnormally high degrees, sometimes called hubs in the networks literature, have degrees far larger than the average or median Facebook user”
