mechanismRNase R digests linear RNA and not covalently closed circles, so ONE TREATMENT SEPARATES THE TWO HYPOTHESES: circles survive, and template-switching artefacts of a linear molecule would not— “transcripts with scrambled exons were generally resistant to RNaseR treatment, as expected for circular RNAs, but not for RNAs with scrambled exons generated by template switching or other artifacts of reverse transcription from a canonical linear RNA.”
mechanismcircular RNAs lack a poly-A tail, so they are enriched in the non-poly-adenylated fraction - a second, independent physical property pointing the same way— “In an analysis of publicly available RNA-Seq data from HeLa and H9 human embryonic stem cells, we found that RNAs with scrambled exons were enriched in non-poly-adenylated fractions.”
critiques the explanation of scrambled exons as reverse-transcription artefacts, which RNase R resistance and non-poly-adenylation both contradict— “Taken together, these findings provide strong evidence that most scrambled exon sequences we detected in human RNA were derived from circular molecules.”
critiques the EST-database literature on exon scrambling, as confounded by RT-PCR artefacts and selection bias in both directions— “The lack of direct experimental evidence makes interpretation of these studies difficult: EST database studies are fraught with confounding of RT-PCR artifacts and selection bias.”
contradicts the estimate that scrambled transcripts comprise less than one one-thousandth of a gene's output - here they are comparable to the canonical linear mRNA— “Indeed, for many genes, in both cancer and normal human cells, we found RNAs with circularly permuted exon orders at levels comparable to those of the canonical, linear mRNA.”
senseexon-scrambling - THE NAME THE PHENOMENON CARRIED FOR THIRTY YEARS, and the name is the verdict: a scramble is a mistake. Estimated at LESS THAN ONE ONE-THOUSANDTH of a gene's transcripts and widely explained as reverse-transcription artefact. This paper renames the same observation `circular RNA isoform` and puts it in the title as PREDOMINANT— “These scrambled transcripts were estimated to comprise less than one one-thousandth of DCC transcripts, and the phenomenon was dubbed exon-scrambling”
sensecircular RNA - AS ESTABLISHED HERE, BY EXONUCLEASE RESISTANCE rather than by sequence. The sequence evidence is the same evidence the artefact hypothesis uses; what separates them is a physical property of the molecule, and that is why the RNase R sentence is the paper— “transcripts with scrambled exons were generally resistant to RNaseR treatment, as expected for circular RNAs, but not for RNAs with scrambled exons generated by template switching or other artifacts of reverse transcription from a canonical linear RNA.”
sensethe artefact hypothesis - STATED WITH ARITHMETIC RATHER THAN DISMISSED: at a typical exon length of 150, 1 in 22,500 template switches lands on an exon boundary, and hundreds would therefore appear in an EST database. The rival explanation is given its own quantitative prediction before being tested against one— “If template switching occurs during reverse transcription, given a typical human exon length of 150 (the average of the lower 90th quantile), 1 out of 22500 template switches would be at exon-exon boundaries, and hundreds could be expected to be detected in an EST database.”
sensehow it was found - `Although our INITIAL GOAL was to discover cancer-specific chromosomal rearrangements`, and `WE WERE SURPRISED TO FIND` the permuted orders. The finding is a by-product of a search for something else, and the paper says so rather than reconstructing a hypothesis it did not have— “Although our initial goal was to discover cancer-specific chromosomal rearrangements by identifying the resulting fused or rearranged transcripts, we also investigated the possibility that the exon order specified by the genome sequence might be rearranged during RNA processing.”
