senseconceptual change - AS A COGNITIVE VARIABLE WITH A MEASUREMENT MENU: what the reviewed studies actually recorded was a profile of conception or understanding, remediation of misconception, or learning gains, nearly always from a pre- and post-test— “Conceptual change as a cognitive variable was measured or described in many methods. The most used were using profile of conception/understanding, remediation of misconception and learning gains”
sensemisconception - AS A CATEGORY ERROR: on the classical model a misconception is a concept assigned to the wrong ontological category, so change means altering what kind of thing the student takes the concept to be - a definition that makes some disagreements invisible— “Misconceptions develop when pupils assign concepts to wrong ontological categories, according to one distinction in the classical conceptual change model (Chi, Slotta, & De Leeuw, 1994). Conceptual change, according to this viewpoint, occurs when students alter their perceptions”
sensethe same object under several names - students arrive with prior ideas called alternative conceptions here, alternative beliefs in the exclusion rule, and misconceptions in the classical model, with no statement anywhere that the three are the same thing— “Science education researchers have obviously documented that the students enter the classrooms with pre-instructional alternative conceptions (Broughton, Sinatra, & Reynolds, 2010; Diakidoy et al., 2016; Vosniadou & Skopeliti, 2005;). These prior conceptions are often incomplete or incorrect”
senseconceptual change - THE ONTOLOGICAL READING (Chi, Slotta & De Leeuw 1994): change in what KIND OF THING the student takes the concept to be. On this reading a misconception is a concept filed under the wrong ontological category, so the change is a recategorisation.— “Misconceptions develop when pupils assign concepts to wrong ontological categories, according to one distinction in the classical conceptual change model (Chi, Slotta, & De Leeuw, 1994). Conceptual change, according to this viewpoint, occurs when students alter their perceptions of a concept's nature.”
senseconceptual change - THE AFFECTIVE READING (Pintrich et al. 1993): motivation, interest and self-efficacy are not conditions FOR the change but factors IN it. The paper calls this `another version`, i.e. a rival account of the same term.— “Another version considers the influence of affective factors on conceptual change such as motivation, interest and self-efficacy (Pintrich et al., 1993).”
senseconceptual change - THE MULTIDIMENSIONAL READING (Tyson et al. 1997): epistemological (classical), ontological AND affective aspects together. This one is defined by UNION of the other readings, which is why it cannot be scored on the same instrument as any of them.— “Lastly, several academics advocate for a multidimensional approach which considers epistemological (classical), ontological and affective aspects when considering conceptual change (Tyson et al., 1997).”
senseconceptual change - THE EXTENSION WIDENED OVER TIME, and the paper says so as its own finding: from cognitive-only to including student traits. A study from before the shift and one from after are not measuring the same construct, which is what makes the review's 50-study pooling a definitional question and not only a methodological one.— “The transition of conceptual change models from cognitive-only to an understanding of the role of student traits in learning, is possibly the utmost notable contemporary growth in the field of conceptual change research (Sinatra, 2005).”
