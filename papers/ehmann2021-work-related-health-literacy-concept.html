Introduction Individuals’ participation in the workforce plays a central role in providing them a livelihood and a basis for social insurances and social participation. Work is an influential factor during the life course and has a potentially large impact on one’s health and well-being [ 1 ]. At work, increasing complexity, rapid changes, and the altered working environment as a result of globalization and digitalization are causing—amongst other things—an increase in self-employed people and the blurring of work–life and other areas of life [ 2 , 3 , 4 ]. In consideration of changes in work environments, including tendencies toward greater flexibility and altered employment biographies, managing one’s own health is increasingly gaining in importance [ 7 , 8 ]. In Germany, with its 83 million inhabitants, there was a workforce of more than 46 million people in 2019 and 2020 [ 9 ].

1.1. Health Literacyterm·key Enhanced individual health literacyterm·key of employees can enable them to independently shape working conditions (hazards as well as protective measures) as a behavioral preventive measure and, thus, contribute to the implementation of structural preventive measures [ 10 ]. Work abilityterm·dom comprises individual as well as work-related factors [ 17 ]. 4. Discussion The aim of this scoping reviewterm·key was to present the current state of research on health literacyterm·key as an employee’s competence. The main research question relates to conceptualizations of health literacyterm·key in the workplaceterm·key. We started from a working definition of work-related health literacyterm·dom as an individual competence in relation to one’s own work abilityterm·dom and employabilityterm·key. This scoping reviewterm·key offered the opportunity to bundle articles using different country-specific terminologies (e.g., work-related health literacyterm·dom or occupational health literacyterm·dom). The literature search revealed different terms for health literacyterm·key in the workplaceterm·key and different approaches that overlap in main components (e.g., the time horizon). The synthesis led us to the result that health literacyterm·key in the workplaceterm·key, as individual competence is about knowledge and its application to actions related to safety and health at work. Depending on the research purpose, a definition (or conceptualization) of individual health literacyterm·key in the workplaceterm·key can either refer to the current work situation only or also include future viability. We continue to refer to this individual competence as work-related heath literacy. Individual work-related health literacyterm·dom is a resource for employees and is linked to the economic viability of companies [ 3 , 7 , 19 , 44 , 45 , 49 , 50 , 51 ].

Regarding interventions to promote individual work-related health literacyterm·dom, several interventions were identified that provided quantitative results after being implemented. Examples for these interventions are group trainings, workshops, and educational interventions. In the literature selection process, there were also articles describing (e.g., [ 71 , 72 , 73 ]) or qualitatively evaluating health literacyterm·key interventions in workplacesterm·key (e.g., [ 74 ])—these insights are important for the implementation of future interventions, but are not covered in this scoping reviewterm·key. Interventions addressing employees’ general health literacyterm·key were directed at specific groups of employees: the management level [ 64 ], employees in nursing homes [ 65 ], or agricultural workers [ 66 ]. One intervention targeted employees with mental health problems and addressed partly the general health literacyterm·key [ 60 ] and partly the mental health literacyterm·dom of the participating employees [ 59 ]. However, most of the interventions included in this scoping reviewterm·key aim at the promotion of mental health literacyterm·dom. This finding is in line with a recent literature reviewterm·mth on the application of the concept “health literacyterm·key” in companies [ 75 ]. Interventions targeting general health literacyterm·key were also less numerous than interventions targeting mental health literacyterm·dom [ 75 ]. In recent years, mental health literacyterm·dom was the area of most research activities in health literacyterm·key research [ 21 ]. Mental health literacyterm·dom refers to the knowledge and beliefs about mental disorders that support their detection, management, or prevention [ 76 ]. Mental health literacyterm·dom is, therefore, often considered in the context of the public or in relation to the corporate workforce; mental health literacyterm·dom interventions must be developed and applied in a context-specific manner [ 77 ]. A well-known example for the promotion of mental health literacyterm·dom in the workplaceterm·key are the mental health first aid training courses [ 78 ]. Since 2000, the training courses have spread out from Australia and are now available in many countries around the world [ 78 ]. These training courses are also offered for the workplaceterm·key but are not primarily related to individual work-related health literacyterm·dom, so they are not included in the results section of this scoping reviewterm·key. However, these interventions can also have a positive effect on one’s own mental health literacyterm·dom, as Kitchener and Jorm identified [ 79 ]. In some cases, mental health literacyterm·dom interventions showed significant effects on the primary outcome [ 55 , 59 , 61 , 63 ] or improvements in other outcomes [ 56 , 57 , 62 ]. However, all interventions contributed to raise awareness of mental health in the workplaceterm·key and to changes in attitudes toward mental health [ 56 , 57 , 61 , 62 , 63 ]. It was found generally that interventions increasing one’s own abilities should be more focused on the work or the working conditions and that health literacyterm·key can be improved through targeted interventions [ 55 , 60 , 75 ]. Other interventions to promote health literacyterm·key in the workplaceterm·key are interventions that are directed at specific employees (e.g., managers), but whose effects primarily affect the workplaceterm·key (i.e., structural preventive measures) rather than employees themselves. An example that illustrates this aspect is a training for managers helping to support the mental health needs of their employees (but not their own) [ 80 ]. Lack of work-relatedness was a frequent exclusion criterion ( n = 28). Interventions to promote health literacyterm·key in general but only using the workplaceterm·key setting (e.g., workplaceterm·key health promotion offers) were excluded because the immediate objective of the activities was not to maintain (or restore or improve) work abilityterm·dom. In our literature search, the term “health literacyterm·key” was required in the title or abstract; otherwise, articles were not included, which may have resulted in some articles with relevant content not being considered in this scoping reviewterm·key.

Interestingly, in none of the intervention studies reported (see Table 3 ) a comprehensive instrument to measure work-related health literacyterm·dom was applied. Beyond the validation studies described in Table 4 , we found two publications reporting on measurement instruments for measuring individual work-related health literacyterm·dom or occupational health literacyterm·dom. To date, there have been no publications evaluating interventions using any of these instruments. The various measures of outcomes in intervention studies promoting individual work-related or occupational health literacyterm·dom ( Table 3 ), and the measurement instruments available to date ( Table 4 ) indicate a need for validated measurement instruments designed for the workplaceterm·key context. Because no specific measurement instrument was available, in an intervention study among casting factory workers, general health literacyterm·key was measured and linked to workers’ perceptions of occupational health and safety training [ 14 ]. Suthakorn et al. [ 69 ] recommend the development of occupational health literacyterm·dom measurement instruments tailored to specific work settings in different cultures.

In addition to the instruments presented in Table 4 , there are two other measurement instruments, including a German one [ 81 ] translated from the original English version [ 82 ]. These instruments assess only the knowledge aspect of mental health literacyterm·dom in respect of one’s colleagues at work and, for this reason, were not included as measurement instruments in this reviewterm·mth. Furthermore, there is an instrument that asks educators about their knowledge regarding the mental health of their students [ 83 ]. This shows that “mental health literacyterm·dom” as a defined term has received attention and finds application in the context of employment.

For future instruments measuring individual work-related or occupational health literacyterm·dom, we recommend assessing all aspects of Sørensen et al.’s conceptualization [ 12 ]. Since more flexible career paths may be possible nowadays, we recommend also including relevant aspects of new forms of work to cover the individual aspect of employabilityterm·key into the development of instruments.

The relevance and the advantage of the issue “health literacyterm·key in the workplaceterm·key” may be found in relation to peoples’ changing work environments and employment biographies. [ 25 , 26 , 27 ] The COVID-19 pandemic imposes far-reaching impacts on societies, health care systems, workplacesterm·key, and individuals [ 84 ]. Changes in the nature of work, the use of technologies at work, business structures, status of employment, hierarchies as well as relationships at work as outlined in a report of the European Agency for Safety and Health at Work [ 2 ] are accelerating due to the COVID-19 pandemic and also affect workplacesterm·key where consequences of increasing digitalization had previously been slower [ 85 ].

As described in the background, health literacyterm·key can be valuable in promoting work abilityterm·dom. Work abilityterm·dom is the basis for employabilityterm·key and employment [ 17 ] and is becoming more important in view of changes in working and living conditions. Living conditions are also affected, as constant accessibility increases and the boundaries between work and private life can become increasingly blurred [ 2 ]. Interventions that are tailored to the needs of employees in companies may have a more successful implementation because they are close to the company, the sense of responsibility is given, and an added value can be seen for both employees and employers. For this reason, interventions to promote individual work-related health literacyterm·dom might be better accepted than offers (e.g., workplaceterm·key health promotion) to promote general health literacyterm·key in the company.

4.1. Limitations of the Study We aimed to identify all relevant articles on employee health literacyterm·key. However, there were more measures and interventions described or in planning not only for employees themselves. Articles on organizational health literacyterm·dom—in which health literacyterm·key is conceived of an attribute belonging to organizations or companies (and not to working individuals) [ 86 ]—were not included because we started from an individual characteristic with our working definition and the outcome “work abilityterm·dom” or “individual employabilityterm·key”; we wanted to address employees with their abilities to stay safe and healthy during their working life. We narrowed the inclusion criteria for articles on interventions. For example, our scoping reviewterm·key does not address what experience has been gained in implementing interventions to promote work-related or occupational health literacyterm·dom. There may be quantitative intervention studies that promote one aspect of health literacyterm·key without mentioning “health literacyterm·key” in the title/abstract; these articles were not retrieved via the literature search. Another limitation of this scoping reviewterm·key relates to the inclusion criteria. Thus, articles that focus on general health literacyterm·key and articles that focus on work–life balance were excluded. Articles on these areas may have an indirect impact on the individual work-related health literacyterm·dom and the maintenance of work abilityterm·dom or employabilityterm·key. In particular, articles on work–life balance are of special importance in a pandemic situation such as the current COVID-19 crisis with increased offers of mobile working. Additionally, emerging requirements regarding work-related health literacyterm·dom, such as those resulting from the pandemic, have not yet been considered in this scoping reviewterm·key.

Only articles in German and English were included, leaving some activities from other language countries out of the scope of this reviewterm·mth. In addition, due to the breadth of the research questions, relevant articles on a particular research question may not have been found and may not have received attention in this reviewterm·mth.

4.2. Future Research In this scoping reviewterm·key, we focused on an occupational work environment with employees working within an organization. Mobile workterm·dom and forms of more flexible work including self-employed or platform work [ 87 ] require a more detailed consideration.

We can confirm that there is a lack of reliable and valid measurement instruments for work-related or occupational health literacyterm·dom. To date, there is no standardized measurement instrument for individual health literacyterm·key that has been designed specifically for workplacesterm·key in general.

5. Conclusions and Recommendations The strength of individual work-related or occupational health literacyterm·dom is its direct relation to one’s own work abilityterm·dom and employabilityterm·key. Workplacesterm·key, where most people spend a significant proportion of their lives, can be advantages in terms of acceptance and implementation of measures. It can be suspected that the degree of acceptance of measures to increase work-related health literacyterm·dom will be higher than the acceptance of measures to promote general health literacyterm·key in the workplaceterm·key setting.

Considering the components of individual work-related health literacyterm·dom and the country- or environment-specific characteristics of workplacesterm·key, the applicability of the conceptualization of work-related health literacyterm·dom from other countries and different workplacesterm·key is not without obstacles. Social security and health care systems as well as labor market characteristics vary between countries. Thus, we recommend the development of country-specific instruments for the assessment of individual work-related health literacyterm·dom and to measure both mental and physical work-related health literacyterm·dom. In our view, a recommendation for future research is also to consider precarious working conditions as one aspect of working conditions.

The occupational safety and health of the future should also focus more on strengthening health literacyterm·key, but at the same time, should not consider itself released from the task of structural prevention [ 66 ], e.g., by fostering occupational safety culture [ 38 ]. Especially in awareness of the continuing shortages of skilled workers, demographic developments, and new, changed working conditions, individual health literacyterm·key is of growing importance. We encourage the development of targeted interventions with the aim of maintaining and promoting work abilityterm·dom as well as participation in employment.
