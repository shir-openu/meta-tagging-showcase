phenomenonresponse conflict monitoring in anterior cingulate cortex— “Specifically, we have hypothesized that the function of the ACC is to provide an on-line index of the degree of response conflict.”
claim-typemechanism— “The results indicate that the conflict-monitoring hypothesis, augmented by mechanisms for encoding stimulus history, can explain key phenomena associated with performance in sequential speeded response tasks.”
method — evidence— “Simulations conducted in a neural network model incorporating sequential priming mechanisms (developed in Cho et al., 2002) confirmed that a computational measure of response conflict was higher on low-frequency trials across all three tasks.”
mechanismRepeated high-frequency stimuli build residual activation (an expectancy) in one stimulus-response pathway, so a low-frequency stimulus must overcome that preactivation, and the resulting competition is what registers as conflict in ACC.— “Specifically, in the model, repeated presentations of high-frequency stimuli create an expectancy for continued future repetitions, which results in residual activity in the expected stimulus– response pathway.”
mechanismConflict at the decision layer produces ACC-indexed signal that lowers strategic priming, which raises control (slower, more accurate responding) on subsequent trials.— “The role of the conflict detector was to increase control by decreasing strategic priming in response to conflict.”
mechanismBecause the incorrect nontarget decision unit in one-response tasks is not connected to a response execution unit, transient wrong-channel activation never becomes an overt error, so the task difference arises downstream of conflict detection rather than at it.— “The difference in behavioral profiles across the two tasks is captured in the model by the simple fact that premature activation of the incorrect nontarget decision layer unit does not result in an overt behavior under a one-response task situation.”
extends Cho et al. (2002) sequential-priming choice-discrimination model— “The present model extends this previous work by adding the conflict-control loop (Botvinick et al., 2001) into the Cho et al. model.”
builds-on Usher and McClelland (2001) leaky competing accumulator architecture— “As was mentioned above, the basic architecture was developed in Usher and McClelland (2001) and was extended by Botvinick et al. (2001) and Cho et al. (2002).”
builds-on Braver, Barch, Gray, Molfese, and Snyder (2001) neuroimaging dataset— “The neuroimaging data used for the present study were originally reported in Braver et al. (2001).”
sense**THE MECHANISM IS A CHAIN OF THREE VERBS AND EACH ONE IS DOING WORK:** the ACC `DETECTS the presence of response conflict and CONVEYS this information to other brain regions, enabling subsequent ADJUSTMENTS IN COGNITIVE CONTROL`. Detect, convey, adjust. **A claim that a region is `active during` something cannot be wrong in an interesting way; this one can** - it predicts that the signal precedes the adjustment, that removing it removes the adjustment, and that its size tracks the amount of competition rather than the difficulty— “indicates that the anterior cingulate cortex (ACC) detects the presence of response conflict and conveys this information to other brain regions, enabling subsequent adjustments in cognitive control.”
sense**AND IT MEETS erb2019 HEAD-ON WITHOUT EITHER PAPER KNOWING.** This model predicts `the dynamics of ACC activity related to TRIAL SEQUENCE EFFECTS` - performance on a trial depending on whether the previous one conflicted, which is the congruency sequence effect. **erb2019, merged into this corpus this morning, finds that associative priming overlap decides whether a CSE is evidence of control at all.** So the corpus holds the computational account and the paper questioning what its central evidence measures, seventeen years apart, neither citing the other. **This sense was first attached to the conflict-theory sentence, which does not contain the words `trial sequence effects` at all** - the audit caught it before the gate did, which is the one step in this pipeline that has no automated equivalent— “In addition, the model captured detailed aspects of behavioral reaction time and accuracy data, predicted the dynamics of ACC activity related to trial sequence effects, and provided evidence for the functional role of conflict information in performance monitoring and optimization.”
sense**THE ORDER OF EVENTS IS REPORTED HONESTLY, WHICH IS RARER THAN IT SHOULD BE:** `In order to test this idea, however, EXISTING IMPLEMENTATIONS OF THE CONFLICT MODEL HAD TO BE AUGMENTED so as to be responsive to the effects of stimulus frequency on processing.` The model did not handle frequency; it was extended; then it fitted. **A model extended to fit the data it then fits is a weaker result than one that predicted it**, and stating the sequence lets a reader price that difference instead of having to reconstruct it from the methods— “In order to test this idea, however, existing implementations of the conflict model had to be augmented so as to be responsive to the effects of stimulus frequency on processing.”
controlthe mapping of stimuli onto the target and nontarget categories was counterbalanced across subjects — held — rules out that a category effect is a stimulus effect - that the target set is simply easier— “However, the mapping of stimuli to target/R1 and nontarget/ R2 categories was counterbalanced across subjects.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingThe simulated conflict measure was higher on low-frequency trials in every task condition, matching the ACC imaging result.— “There was significantly greater conflict for low- versus high-frequency events in all task conditions (all ps , .001).”
findingThe model reproduced the puzzling exception that accuracy to low-frequency targets in the oddball (one-response) task is near perfect and not degraded by low frequency.— “In this condition, low-frequency responses were nearly perfectly accurate and were not significantly different from responses to high-frequency [F(1,63) = 0.297, p = .588] or equal-frequency [F(1,63) = 0.133, p = .717] events.”
findingSimulated conflict tracked not only global stimulus frequency but the fine-grained five-trial sequential history of stimuli.— “The high correlation observed between conflict and empirical data behavioral performance (accuracy, r = .934; RT, r = .891) indicates that conflict not only tracks global frequency but also the local sequential history of stimuli.”
findingA reanalysis of the imaging data confirmed the model's novel prediction that ACC activity fluctuates with local sequence even under globally equal frequencies.— “the empirical data from the ACC corresponds rather well with the conflict predictions obtained from the model (r = .856, p , .001), accounting for 73% of the variance present in the sequential pattern of ACC activation”
findingTrials whose four preceding stimuli were repetitions had the fastest reaction times and fewest errors, in both human and simulated data.— “In fact, the four categories ending in RR had the four lowest RTs and error rates in both the behavioral and the simulated data.”
findingRemoving the conflict-monitoring control loop degraded the model's ability to predict trial-by-trial performance, showing the loop makes a real contribution.— “The performance of the model with no conflictmonitoring control loop was slightly less effective at predicting performance, thus indicating a contribution of this component of the model.”
findingSubjects showed speed-accuracy adjustments after slow (high-conflict) trials as well as after errors, exactly as the intact conflict-control model predicted.— “There were significant adjustments in RT following errors [t(60) = 6.50, p , .001], in error rate following slow RT trials [t(64) = 25.76, p , .001], and in both RT and error rate following fast RT trials [RT, t(64) = 22.6, p , .01; error rate, t(64) = 4.81, p , .001].”
