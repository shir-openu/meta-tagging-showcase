limitationThe paper describes its own data collection two incompatible ways. Methods: interviews of about 30 minutes, audio-recorded and transcribed verbatim. Data availability: a survey of 10 questions answered as real-time feedback posted directly on a forum. These are different instruments producing different data, and the abstract uses both words in one sentence ('surveyed 48 undergraduates ... through semi-structured interviews'). Nothing in the paper reconciles them.— “Data collection was carried out through a survey of 10 questions distributed via the Personal Learning Environment (PLE) platform, where students provided real-time…
limitationThe 90% agreement is computed after the two coders built the themes together: the preliminary codes were 'collaboratively discussed and categorized into potential themes by both the first and second authors', and only then did each researcher independently apply them. The figure measures consistency of application, not independent arrival at the same scheme.
limitationThe sample is selected on the exposure under study - students who chose to enrol in an 'AI in Academic Writing' course - and the paper offers that selection as what makes the data informative rather than as a threat to it. Perceptions of AI are then reported from a group defined by having opted into using AI.
limitationThe paper recommends that future work 'employ quantitative methods alongside qualitative approaches to validate observed trends', while its own results are reported almost entirely as counts out of 48 - 35, 29, 25, 24, 22, 21, 18, 15, 10, 9 students and so on. The trends it asks future work to validate quantitatively are already stated as numbers here.
