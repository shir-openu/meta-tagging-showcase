mechanismAI supplies instant feedback and flags common mistakes, which triggers student self-reflection and thereby develops cognitive and thinking skills — “Thematic analysis revealed that AI tools are instrumental in providing instant feedback and highlighting common mistakes, which contribute to cognitive and thinking skill development (Theme 4).”

limitationThree limits are stated together: a small sample, exclusive reliance on qualitative data with subjective bias, and no long-term effects. — “The study acknowledges several limitations, including the limited sample size, which restricts the generalizability of the findings, and the exclusive reliance on qualitative data, which may introduce subjective biases. Additionally, the study does not address the long-term effects of AI usage in education”
limitationThe central construct is measured through instruments derived from a taxonomy, so the result inherits that taxonomy's division of cognition. — “Cognitive skills, such as memory, attention, comprehension, and reasoning abilities, are measured through tools developed with guidance from Bloom’s Taxonomy.”
limitationThe paper describes its own data collection two incompatible ways. Methods: interviews of about 30 minutes, audio-recorded and transcribed verbatim. Data availability: a survey of 10 questions answered as real-time feedback posted directly on a forum. These are different instruments producing different data, and the abstract uses both words in one sentence ('surveyed 48 undergraduates ... through semi-structured interviews'). Nothing in the paper reconciles them. — “Data collection was carried out through a survey of 10 questions distributed via the Personal Learning Environment (PLE) platform, where students provided real-time feedback directly on the forum.”
limitationThe 90% agreement is computed after the two coders built the themes together: the preliminary codes were 'collaboratively discussed and categorized into potential themes by both the first and second authors', and only then did each researcher independently apply them. The figure measures consistency of application, not independent arrival at the same scheme. — “preliminary codes were then collaboratively discussed and categorized into potential themes by both the first and second authors. This collaborative phase was critical for maintaining a rigorous and unbiased coding process. Each researcher independently applied these codes”
limitationThe sample is selected on the exposure under study - students who chose to enrol in an 'AI in Academic Writing' course - and the paper offers that selection as what makes the data informative rather than as a threat to it. Perceptions of AI are then reported from a group defined by having opted into using AI. — “Students were selected through convenience sampling, focusing on those who had chosen to enroll in the “AI in Academic Writing” course. This approach allowed us to gather insights from participants who were already interested and engaged in using AI tools as part of their academic development.”
limitationThe paper recommends that future work 'employ quantitative methods alongside qualitative approaches to validate observed trends', while its own results are reported almost entirely as counts out of 48 - 35, 29, 25, 24, 22, 21, 18, 15, 10, 9 students and so on. The trends it asks future work to validate quantitatively are already stated as numbers here. — “Future research should consider larger and more diverse sample populations to enhance the generalizability of findings and employ quantitative methods alongside qualitative approaches to validate observed trends comprehensively.”

findingThe most frequently reported benefit was that generative AI helped with homework and exam preparation by offering problem-solving strategies, refining articles and creating course content (35 of 48 students). — “For instance, 35 students reported that generative AI is particularly effective in assisting with homework and exam preparation by offering problem-solving strategies, refining articles, and creating course content.”
findingForty-two students said AI raised their enthusiasm by supplying learning resources and suggestions tailored to their own learning behaviour, interests and past performance. — “Specifically, 42 students noted that AI provides customized learning resources and interests, and suggestions tailored to their learning behavior, historical performance, making learning more targeted and effective.”
findingThirty-three students credited generative AI with fostering their creativity by supplying diverse perspectives and innovative solutions. — “33 students noted that AI fosters creativity by offering diverse perspectives and innovative solutions.”
findingThe dominant student concern was that heavy reliance on AI would erode their ability to think independently (22 of 48 students). — “22 students worry that excessive reliance on AI could diminish their ability to think independently.”
findingTwenty-one students said AI-generated content was sometimes erroneous or inaccurate, so they had to verify and filter it themselves. — “21 students reported that the content generated by AI tools could sometimes be erroneous or inaccurate, necessitating user verification and filtering.”
findingTwenty-five students were worried about data security and privacy because of the volume of personal data AI systems process. — “25 students expressed concerns about data security and privacy, especially given the large volumes of personal data that AI systems often handle.”
findingFifteen students said the AI tools failed to fully understand their queries and returned answers below their expectations. — “15 students reported that AI tools sometimes failed to fully comprehend their queries, resulting in responses that fell short of their expectations.”
