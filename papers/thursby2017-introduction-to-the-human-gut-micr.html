mechanismfibre deficiency makes the community switch to eating the host's own secreted mucins, which thins the mucus barrier and lets pathogens reach the epithelium— “The erosion of the colonic mucus barrier under dietary fibre deficiency is associated with a switch of the gut microbiota towards the utilisation of secreted mucins as a nutrient source \ [83\].”
mechanismcross-feeding: acetate or lactate produced by one species is converted to butyrate by another, so fermentation products do not accumulate— “For example, acetate produced by fermentation of resistant starch by _R. bromii_ \ [103\] or lactate produced by lactic acid bacteria such as lactobacilli and bifidobacteria provides substrate for other microbiota members such as _Eubacterium hallii_ and _Anaerostipes caccae_ which convert it into butyrate \ [104, 105\].”
mechanisma decreasing butyrate gradient from lumen to crypt is proposed to control epithelial turnover - proliferation at the crypt bottom, apoptosis nearer the lumen— “A decreasing gradient of butyrate from lumen to crypt is suggested to control intestinal epithelial turnover and homeostasis by promoting colonocyte proliferation at the bottom of crypts, whilst increasing apoptosis and exfoliation of cells closer to the lumen \ [144\].”
mechanismantibiotics liberate fucose and sialic acid from mucins, and the pathogens that cannot cleave those sugars themselves expand on what the commensals released— “For example, _S._ Typhimurium and _C. difficile_ utilise fucose and sialic acid liberated by the gut microbiota, and increasing sialic acid levels post-antibiotic treatment favour their expansion within the gut \ [135\].”
mechanismbacteria without a sialidase carry a nan cluster instead and depend on other members of the community for their carbon - dependence as a structural feature, not a deficiency— “Many bacteria, including pathogens such as _Salmonella_ Typhimurium or _C. difficile_, lack a sialidase but harbour a ‘nan cluster’ dedicated to sialic acid metabolism, and thus rely on other members of the gut microbiota to provide them with this source of carbon \ [107\].”
mechanismthe germ-free deficit in CD4+ T-cell expansion is reversed completely by a single molecule, polysaccharide A from the B. fragilis capsule— “This deficiency can be completely reversed by the treatment of GF mice with polysaccharide A from the capsule of _B. fragilis_ \ [163\].”
mechanismhost FUT2 genotype decides whether H and ABO antigens appear in the mucosa, and that host genetic difference shifts the abundance of many bacterial groups— “For example, the presence (secretor) or absence (non-secretor) of H and ABO antigens in GI mucosa, determined by the FUT2 genotype (a gene expressing an α1,2-fucosyltransferase), affects the abundance of many bacterial groups \ [78\].”

conceptgut microbiota — bacteria, archaea and eukarya colonising the GI tract, taken as one co-evolved community
conceptenterotype — one of three types named for a dominant genus, derived from 33 samples, and whose EXISTENCE this paper calls controversial
conceptcore microbiota — the organisms common to all individuals - proposed at the organism level and re-proposed at the functional level in the same passage
conceptdysbiosis — an altered microbial composition, defined by what it disrupts rather than by what it is
conceptsuperorganism — host plus resident microorganisms as one entity - a name the paper attributes to others rather than adopting
conceptcommunity types — classifications of individual microbiota arrangements that are predictive of each other - the successor term to `enterotype`
conceptmicrobiota-accessible carbohydrates — the fraction of dietary fibre the gut community can actually use; the paper's lever connecting Western diet to reduced diversity
conceptcross-feeding — one species' fermentation product is another's substrate - the mechanism by which co-operation widens the community
conceptglycoside hydrolases — the enzyme repertoire that decides which glycans a species can eat, and therefore where it can live
conceptsegmented filamentous bacteria — commensals that adhere to the epithelium rather than staying in the lumen, and so can activate the immune system directly
conceptfunctional redundancy — why the immune system shapes the community by STRATIFICATION rather than by targeting species - losing one member changes little
conceptpattern recognition receptors — the epithelial sensors through which the host reads microbial molecules and distinguishes beneficial from pathogenic
conceptsecretory IgA — the antibody that co-localises with bacteria in the OUTER mucus layer and keeps them off the epithelial surface
conceptgnotobiotic — animals with a known, defined microbiota - the design that lets a growth impairment be transmitted and then reversed by named species
