findingThe paper's central claim is that three specific skills - AI literacy, prompt engineering knowledge and critical thinking - are what schools need to remedy the problems AI creates in education.— “In order for all parties to be best prepared for using AI in education, based on a case study and a subsequent literature analysis, there are three necessary skills that can remedy these problems, which are AI literacy, knowledge about prompt engineering, and critical thinking.”
findingIn the author's own trial, GPT-3.5 answered a lost-object reasoning task incorrectly under plain input-output prompting but correctly under Tree-of-Thought prompting.— “The present author’s experiments have indicated that GPT-3.5 provides false answers to this task when asked with Input–Output prompting. However, the responses turned out to be correct when asked with ToT-prompting.”
findingThe case-study university settled on a middle-ground policy in which student use of ChatGPT and other LLMs is neither banned nor left unrestricted.— “For the members of the AI-Taskforce and the university’s leadership, this has come to mean that the use of ChatGPT and other LLMs are neither prohibited nor allowed without restrictions.”
findingRequiring students to document how they critically evaluated the AI's output was the element of the guidelines the authors considered novel relative to other institutions.— “This is why we considered our third point in the documentation requirements (the need for critical reflection) our most crucial innovation – something that we did not find in other schools and universities.”
findingThe written AI guidelines are undermined because they work only if students voluntarily read and follow them, and compliance cannot be verified.— “One of the most substantial issues is the fact that their effectiveness hinges on student compliance, which is not guaranteed.”
findingThe main barrier to teachers adopting AI tools in class is fear rooted in not knowing enough about the technology and expecting to use it wrongly.— “Discussions with teachers have shown that one of the biggest constraints to implement AI tools in the classroom is their fear of using them, predominantly due to the fact that they might not know enough about them and assuming that they might use them wrongly.”
findingA year after ChatGPT's release, almost all students in the case-study classrooms had already used it.— “Discussions with students in the classroom at KFH have shown that one year after the introduction of ChatGPT, only few have not yet used it.”
findingThe institution has nobody formally responsible for tracking AI developments, leaving it to individual staff on their own time.— “One of the marked challenges at the KFH is the ostensible fact that there is no dedicated person or group that is tasked with staying on top of the matter.”
findingThe university offers no workshops or training programmes to raise teacher or student competence with AI.— “at KFH there are currently no workshops and programs helping both teachers and students…
