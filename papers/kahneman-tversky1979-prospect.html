mechanismA PHENOMENON IS NAMED AT THE MOMENT IT IS INTRODUCED, AND THE NAME CARRIES THE MECHANISM: people underweight outcomes that are MERELY PROBABLE compared with outcomes obtained with CERTAINTY, and THIS TENDENCY, CALLED THE CERTAINTY EFFECT, is what contributes to risk aversion for sure gains and risk seeking for sure losses— “In particular, people underweight outcomes that are merely probable in comparison with outcomes that are obtained with certainty. This tendency, called the certainty effect, contributes to risk aversion in choices involving sure gains and to risk seeking in choices involving sure losses.”
mechanismTHE SECOND NAMED TENDENCY IS EXPLICITLY A FACT ABOUT DESCRIPTION: people DISCARD COMPONENTS SHARED BY ALL PROSPECTS under consideration, and THIS TENDENCY, CALLED THE ISOLATION EFFECT, LEADS TO INCONSISTENT PREFERENCES WHEN THE SAME CHOICE IS PRESENTED IN DIFFERENT FORMS. The inconsistency is produced by the form, not by the choice— “In addition, people generally discard components that are shared by all prospects under consideration. This tendency, called the isolation effect, leads to inconsistent preferences when the same choice is presented in different forms.”
mechanismA FORMAL OPERATIONALISATION IS REPORTED AS FAILING TO CAPTURE THE INTUITIVE NOTION IT STANDS FOR: the aversion to probabilistic insurance is called A RATHER PUZZLING CONSEQUENCE of the risk aversion hypothesis, and the conclusion drawn is that THE INTUITIVE NOTION OF RISK IS NOT ADEQUATELY CAPTURED BY THE ASSUMED CONCAVITY of the utility function for wealth. The formalism and the word have come apart, and the paper says which is at fault— “This is a rather puzzling consequence of the risk aversion hypothesis of utility theory, because probabilistic insurance appears intuitively riskier than regular insurance, which entirely eliminates the element of risk.
