limitationthe analysis is restricted to prospects with objective/standard probabilities — “The present discussion is restricted to prospects with so-called objective or standard probabilities.”
limitationThe findings rest on hypothetical rather than real choices, which raises validity and generalisability questions the authors acknowledge. — “The reliance on hypothetical choices raises obvious questions regarding the validity of the method and the generalizability of the results.”
limitationThe order in which editing operations are applied, and hence the final edited prospect, is not determined by the theory and is left unstudied. — “A detailed study of this problem is beyond the scope of the present treatment.”
limitationThe theory's account of insurance and gambling is admittedly incomplete. — “Although prospect theory predicts both insurance and gambling for small probabilities, we feel that the present analysis falls far short of a fully adequate account of these complex phenomena.”
limitationThe treatment covers only the simplest two-prospect choice task, not bidding or other production tasks. — “Finally, it should be noted that the present treatment concerns the simplest decision task in which a person chooses between two available prospects.”
limitationHow compound or many-outcome prospects are reduced to simple ones has not been investigated. — “The manner in which complex options, e.g., compound prospects, are reduced to simpler ones is yet to be investigated.”
limitationThe critique is aimed at one of the criticised theory's two stated uses, not at both. — “It has been generally accepted as a normative model of rational choice [24], and widely applied as a descriptive model of economic behavior, e.g. [15, 4]. Thus, it is assumed that all reasonable people would wish to obey the axioms of the theory [47, 36], and that most people actually do, most of the time”
limitationThe proposed theory is stated by its own authors to have normatively unacceptable consequences. — “The equations of prospect theory retain the general bilinear form that underlies expected utility theory. However, in order to accomodate the effects described in the first part of the paper, we are compelled to assume that values are attached to changes rather than to final states, and that decision weights do not coincide with stated probabilities. These departures from expected utility theory must lead to normatively unacceptable consequences”
limitationThe demonstrations rest on a named academic sample and one national currency. — “The demonstrations are based on the responses of students and university faculty to hypothetical choice problems. The respondents were presented with problems of the type illustrated below. Which of the following would you prefer? A: 50% chance to win 1,000, B: 450 for sure. 50% chance to win nothing; The outcomes refer to Israeli currency”

findingProspect theory critiques expected-utility theory as a descriptive model: choices show pervasive violations, so value is assigned to GAINS and LOSSES relative to a reference point (not final assets), and probabilities are replaced by decision weights. — “This paper presents a critique of expected utility theory as a descriptive model of decision making under risk, and develops an alternative model, called prospect theory... value is assigned to gains and losses rather than to final assets and in which probabilities are replaced by decision weights.”
findingThe value function is concave for gains, convex for losses, and steeper for losses than gains (loss aversion); low probabilities are overweighted, contributing to both insurance and gambling. — “The value function is normally concave for gains, commonly convex for losses, and is generally steeper for losses than for g... Overweighting of low probabilities may contribute to the attractiveness of both insurance and gambling.”
