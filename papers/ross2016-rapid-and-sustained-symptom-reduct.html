phenomenonclinically significant anxiety and depression in cancer patients, present in 30-40% in hospital settings and associated with worse medical outcomes— “Enduring clinically significant anxiety and/or depressive symptoms are common in patients with cancer, present in 30–40% of patients in hospital settings”
claim-typeexistence— “Prior to the crossover, psilocybin produced immediate, substantial, and sustained improvements in anxiety and depression and led to decreases in cancer-related demoralization and hopelessness, improved spiritual wellbeing, and increased quality of life.”
method — evidence— “In this double-blind, placebo-controlled, crossover trial, 29 patients with cancer-related anxiety and depression were randomly assigned and received treatment with single-dose psilocybin (0.3 mg/kg) or niacin, both in conjunction with psychotherapy.”
object of study — evidence— “In this double-blind, placebo-controlled, crossover trial, 29 patients with cancer-related anxiety and depression were randomly assigned and received treatment with single-dose psilocybin (0.3 mg/kg) or niacin, both in conjunction with psychotherapy.”
research question — evidence— “The primary outcomes were anxiety and depression assessed between groups prior to the crossover at 7 weeks.”
contribution — evidence— “In conjunction with psychotherapy, single moderate-dose psilocybin produced rapid, robust and enduring anxiolytic and anti-depressant effects in patients with cancer-related psychological distress.”
mechanismTHE MYSTICAL EXPERIENCE IS THE ROUTE, AND THE PAPER SAYS IT IS ONE ROUTE AMONG SEVERAL: intensity of the subjective mystical experience significantly mediated clinical benefit at 6 weeks, and the authors add that it is likely one of several mediators rather than the mechanism— “The mystical experience is likely to be one of several mediators that transmit the effect of psilocybin to changes in anxiety and/or depression.”
mechanismA PSYCHO-SPIRITUAL MECHANISM OF ACTION - the mystical state of consciousness - offered as a candidate alongside the neurobiological account rather than instead of it— “This finding suggests a potential psycho-spiritual mechanism of action: the mystical state of consciousness.”
mechanismDIFFICULT EXPERIENCES ARE TREATED AS PART OF THE THERAPY, not as harm to be prevented: challenging encounters with cancer-related fearful imagery are read as the working-through, which is why transient anxiety is monitored and managed rather than counted as an adverse outcome— “Difficult experiences are not necessarily pathological and can be understood as part of the therapeutic process (e.g. working through cancer-related psychological or existential distress through challenging encounters or emotionally charged confrontations with cancer-related fearful imagery or symbolism)”
replicates prior trials of psilocybin in NORMAL VOLUNTEERS (Griffiths et al. 2006, 2008, 2011) and in patients with terminal cancer (Grob et al. 2011), whose mystical-type effects this trial reproduces— “Compared to the control, psilocybin produced mystical-type experiences, consistent with prior trials of psilocybin administration in normal volunteers ( Griffiths et al., 2006, 2008, 2011) and patients with terminal cancer ( Grob et al., 2011) ( Figure 7 (top)).”
confirms the adverse-event profile reported in earlier psilocybin trials in normal volunteers and in terminal cancer patients, which this trial's transient and tolerable events match— “psychiatric AEs (transient anxiety, transient near-psychotic symptoms) attributable to psilocybin are all known AEs of psilocybin, were transient, tolerable, and consistent with prior trials of psilocybin administration in normal volunteers ( Griffiths et al., 2006, 2008, 2011), and patients with terminal cancer ( Grob et al., 2011).”
confirms the cardiovascular safety, time course and magnitude observed in previous psilocybin studies in healthy volunteers and advanced cancer patients— “The medical safety, time course, and magnitude of effects on these cardiovascular measures were consistent with those observed in previous studies of psilocybin in healthy volunteers ( Griffiths et al., 2006, 2011) and patients with advanced cancer ( Grob et al., 2011).”
confirms open-label LSD-assisted psychotherapy trials for terminal cancer distress, in which the mystical experience was described as integral to the therapeutic effect— “This result matches with descriptive historical data from open-label LSD-assisted psychotherapy trials for psycho-spiritual distress associated with terminal cancer, in which the mystical experience was reported as being an integral part of the therapeutic effect”
confirms open-label psilocybin trials for tobacco addiction and alcoholism, which report the same correlation between mystical experience and outcome— “It is further corroborated by recent open-label trials using psilocybin-assisted psychotherapy to treat tobacco addiction ( Garcia-Romeu et al., 2014; Johnson et al., 2014) and alcoholism ( Bogenschutz et al., 2015) showing significant correlations between the mystical experience and improved clinical outcomes.”
critiques existing pharmacotherapeutic and psychosocial treatment for cancer-related anxiety and depression, whose efficacy is called mixed and limited and for which no FDA-approved pharmacotherapy exists— “There are no US Food and Drug Administration approved pharmacotherapies for cancer-related psychological distress, the onset of clinical improvement with anti-depressants is delayed, relapse rates are high, and significant side effects compromise treatment adherence”
senseplacebo - IN THIS TRIAL NIACIN, AN ACTIVE PLACEBO CHOSEN TO PRODUCE A NOTICEABLE SOMATIC EFFECT, not an inert capsule. The companion trial in the same issue used a very low dose of psilocybin instead. Two teams reached the same result through two different solutions to the same problem - that an inert placebo is unblindable against a psychedelic— “In this double-blind, placebo-controlled, crossover trial, 29 patients with cancer-related anxiety and depression were randomly assigned and received treatment with single-dose psilocybin (0.3 mg/kg) or niacin, both in conjunction with psychotherapy.”
sensemystical experience - A MEASURED MEDIATOR AND EXPLICITLY NOT THE ONLY ONE: the authors say it is `likely to be one of several mediators`, which is a weaker and more careful claim than the abstract's `mediated the therapeutic effect`— “The mystical experience is likely to be one of several mediators that transmit the effect of psilocybin to changes in anxiety and/or depression.”
sensedifficult experience - NOT AN ADVERSE EVENT IN THIS FRAMEWORK. Transient anxiety or paranoia is to be monitored and managed, and is read as part of the therapeutic process rather than as harm, which changes what the safety numbers of a psychedelic trial mean— “Difficult experiences are not necessarily pathological and can be understood as part of the therapeutic process (e.g. working through cancer-related psychological or existential distress through challenging encounters or emotionally charged confrontations with cancer-related fearful imagery or symbolism)”
controlan ACTIVE placebo - niacin - rather than an inert one, so the control arm produces a noticeable somatic effect and participants cannot infer condition from feeling nothing — partial— “This trial was limited by a relatively small sample size, a non-nationally representative cancer patient population (e.g. 62% women, 90% Caucasian), which decreases generalizability, a crossover design that limited the interpretation of clinical benefits after the crossover, and the use of a control with limited blinding.” — rules out participants inferring their arm from feeling nothing - niacin produces a noticeable somatic effect, though the authors still record the blinding as limited— “In this double-blind, placebo-controlled, crossover trial, 29 patients with cancer-related anxiety and depression were randomly assigned and received treatment with single-dose psilocybin (0.3 mg/kg) or niacin, both in conjunction with psychotherapy.”
controlboth arms received the same psychotherapy, so the psychological support is held constant and the comparison is of the drug rather than of the treatment package — held— “In this double-blind, placebo-controlled, crossover trial, 29 patients with cancer-related anxiety and depression were randomly assigned and received treatment with single-dose psilocybin (0.3 mg/kg) or niacin, both in conjunction with psychotherapy.” — rules out the psychotherapy rather than the drug as the active ingredient - both arms received the same psychological support— “In this double-blind, placebo-controlled, crossover trial, 29 patients with cancer-related anxiety and depression were randomly assigned and received…
controlprimary outcomes were assessed BETWEEN GROUPS BEFORE the crossover at 7 weeks, which preserves a clean parallel-group comparison inside a crossover design — held — rules out carryover from the first dose contaminating the efficacy estimate - the primary comparison is between groups BEFORE the crossover
controlthe trial was pre-registered - ClinicalTrials.gov NCT00957359 — held — rules out outcome-switching and selective reporting - the trial was registered before it ran
