mechanismScaffolding lowers cognitive load by structuring the task so learners attend only to the parts relevant to the learning goals, which is why constructivist teaching does not overload working memory — “These researchers argue that in constructivist approaches such as problem-based learning and inquiry learning, the teachers can provide scaffolding that decrease cognitive load by structuring a task that guide the learners to focus on aspects of the task that are relevant to the learning goals.”
relation — “In contrast to Kirschner et al’s point of view, I assert that the elements of constructivist approaches allow for flexible adaptation of guidance, making these instructional approaches more compatible with the manner in which our cognitive structures are organized.”
relation — “In agreement with to Hmelo- Silver et al (2007), I argue that some of these approaches, in particular constructivist, cannot be equated with minimally guided instruction.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingThe paper argues constructivist approaches cannot be classed as minimally guided instruction because they allow flexible adaptation of guidance and so fit how cognitive structures are organised. — “In contrast to Kirschner et al’s point of view, I assert that the elements of constructivist approaches allow for flexible adaptation of guidance, making these instructional approaches more compatible with the manner in which our cognitive structures are organized.”
findingIts concluding claim is that scaffolding is what makes constructivist approaches compatible with human cognitive architecture, because scaffolding reduces working memory load. — “The reason is that these constructivist approaches allow for flexible adaptation of guidance and reduce working memory load by giving scaffolding, so that these approaches are compatible with the manner in which our cognitive structures are organized.”
findingConstructivist approaches such as problem-based and inquiry learning supply learners with scaffolding covering both how to do the task and why it should be done that way, so learners are not left to construct everything alone. — “In constructivist approaches such as problem-based learning and inquiry learning, students are provided with so-called scaffolding to support students’ learning of both how to do the task as well as why the task should be done that way (Hmelo-Silver, 2007).”
findingReviewing the evidence, the paper concludes the claim that constructivist approaches do not work is not well supported and that evidence points the other way. — “Even in this limited review of research on constructivist approaches, it is clear that the claim that “constructivist approaches do not work” is not well supported and in fact, there are supports for the alternative.”
findingRealistic Mathematics Education is classified as a constructivist approach, and it differs from constructivism in being a theory of learning and instruction specific to mathematics rather than a general theory of learning. — “The constructivism theory is a theory of learning in general, while the realistic mathematics theory is a theory of learning and instruction specifically relating to mathematics.”
findingThe paper stops short of claiming constructivism is superior to direct instruction, accepting instead that working memory limits are real and both approaches have a place. — “I also do not claim that constructivist approaches are better than other instructional guidance such as direct”
quantityfive to nine items — Working memory capacity for not-previously-learned information, the limit the paper reasons from (attributed to Miller, 1994) — “Miller (1994) in his research has found that working memory only can hold about five to nine items or elements of information that have not been previously learned or known, in a certain time.”
definitionminimally guided instruction — “They define minimally guided instruction as “one in which learners, rather than being presented with essential information, must discover or construct essential information for themselves””
definitionscaffolding — “The term scaffolding is defined by Reiser (2004) as the process by which the teacher or more knowledgeable peer assist a learner, altering the learning task so the learner can solve problems or accomplish tasks that would otherwise be out of reach.”
definitionshort-term / working memory — “Short-term or working memory is defined as the memory that is used for all conscious activities such as reading the text and it is the only memory that we can monitor (Sweller, 2004; Kirschner, 2002).”
definitionlong-term memory — “Long-term memory, in contrast, is defined as the repository that consists of large and relatively permanent store of information, knowledge and skills (Sweller, 2004; Kirschner, 2002).”
definitioncognitive load theory — “Cognitive load theory is based on the assumption that human cognitive architecture can only process a limited amount of information in working memory in a certain time (Kirschner, 2002).”
definitionintrinsic cognitive load — “Intrinsic cognitive load takes place in the mind of learners when the elements of the to-be-learned material are highly interconnected (Kirschner, 2002).”
definitionextraneous cognitive load — “the extraneous cognitive load is imposed by conventional instruction in which the limitation of working memory is rarely taken into account (Kirschner, 2002).”
definitiongermane cognitive load — “The germane cognitive load is defined as the amount of resources devoted to foster the learning process.”
definitionconstructivist theory (its essence) — “The essence of constructivist theory is the idea that knowledge is not transmitted directly from one knower to another or from teacher to the students.”
