method — evidence— “To begin to address the large-scale structural organization of cellular networks, we have examined the topologic properties of the core metabolic network of 43 different organisms based on data deposited in the WIT database 19.”
object of study — evidence— “this database provides description for 6 archaea, 32 bacteria and 5 eukaryota”
research question — evidence— “Our first goal was to identify the structure of the metabolic networks, i.e., to establish if their topology is best described by the inherently random and uniform exponential model 21 (Fig. 1a and b), or the highly heterogeneous scale-free model 2 (Fig. 1c and d).”
contribution — evidence— “A unique feature of metabolic networks, as opposed to that seen in non-biological scale-free networks, is the apparent conservation of the network diameter in all living organisms.”
transferfrom network science / statistical physics: applying complex-network (scale-free) analysis to the large-scale organization of cellular metabolism— “has benefited from recent advances in understanding the generic properties of complex networks”
mechanismgrowth with preferential attachment: new nodes join by attaching to already well connected ones, which is what produces the power law— “the emergence of power law distribution is intimately linked to the growth of the network in which new nodes are preferentially attached to already established nodes 2”
mechanismhub dominance: a few highly connected substrates carry the connectivity, so removing them raises the diameter sharply and breaks the network into isolated clusters— “upon the sequential removal of the mostconnected nodes the diameter of the network rises sharply, the network eventually disintegrating into isolated clusters that are no longer functional”
mechanismerror tolerance: because most substrates are sparsely connected, removing random ones leaves the average distance unchanged
mechanismincreasing per-substrate connectivity is what holds the diameter constant as organisms get more complex
