mechanismgrowth with preferential attachment: new nodes join by attaching to already well connected ones, which is what produces the power law — “the emergence of power law distribution is intimately linked to the growth of the network in which new nodes are preferentially attached to already established nodes 2”
mechanismhub dominance: a few highly connected substrates carry the connectivity, so removing them raises the diameter sharply and breaks the network into isolated clusters — “upon the sequential removal of the mostconnected nodes the diameter of the network rises sharply, the network eventually disintegrating into isolated clusters that are no longer functional”
mechanismerror tolerance: because most substrates are sparsely connected, removing random ones leaves the average distance unchanged — “when randomly chosen M substrates were removed, -mimicking the consequence of random mutations of catalyzing enzymes-, the average distance between the remaining nodes was not affected, indicating a striking insensitivity to random errors”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingAcross 43 organisms from all three domains of life, metabolic networks share the SAME scale-free topology (power-law degree distribution) as non-biological complex networks — a common blueprint dominated by a few highly connected hubs. — “these metabolic networks display the same topologic scaling properties demonstrating striking similarities to the inherent organization of complex non-biological systems”
findingThis scale-free organization implies metabolic networks are robust and error-tolerant, following the same design principles as other scale-free networks. — “complies with the design principles of robust and error- tolerant scale-free networks”
findingMetabolic networks in all three domains of life are scale-free: the number of reactions a substrate takes part in follows a power law. — “our results convincingly indicate that the probability that a given substrate participates in k reactions follows a power-law distribution, i.e., metabolic networks belong to the class of scale-free networks”
findingThe network diameter is the same in all 43 organisms regardless of how many substrates they have - which is not what network growth predicts, and the paper calls it unprecedented. — “we find that the diameter of the metabolic network is the same for all 43 organisms, irrespective of the number of substrates found in the given species”
findingThe identity of the hub substrates is essentially the same in every organism; species-specific differences appear only among the sparsely connected substrates. — “we find that the ranking of the most connected substrates is practically identical for all 43 organisms”
findingThe network survives random removal of substrates but not removal of its hubs - robust to error, fragile to attack. — “when randomly chosen M substrates were removed, -mimicking the consequence of random mutations of catalyzing enzymes-, the average distance between the remaining nodes was not affected, indicating a striking insensitivity to random errors”
