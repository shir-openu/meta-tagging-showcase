phenomenonlong-range connectivity of the mouse posterior parietal cortex
claim-typeexistence
mechanismAbsence of a direct visual-to-motor cortical route forces visual decision signals through PPC, so inactivating PPC impairs visual but not whisker-based decisions — “There is essentially no direct connection between VIS and MO (Wang et al. 2012) and the main indirect connection from VIS to MO is through the PPC (Zingg et al. 2014).”
mechanismReciprocal PPC-thalamic loops sustain persistent choice-selective firing during a delay — “The current study showed that the PPC has a strong reciprocality with LP, PO, and LD, suggesting the possibility that the reciprocal connectivity with thalamic nuclei may be a general connectivity motif that supports the persistent activity.”
mechanismTracing and axon-volume quantification disagree because one reports where cell bodies sit and the other where synapses land — “For instance, when L5 neurons receive inputs on their distal dendrites, an experimental approach will show L5, where cell bodies are located, whereas bioinformatic analysis will show L1, where the synapses occur.”
relation — “The same method has been used in previous brain-wide quantitative analyses of projection patterns (Oh et al. 2014; Harris et al. 2019), and thus was used in this study for comparative analysis of inputs to/outputs from PPC.”
relation — “Taking advantage of AAV1, which is a widely used anterograde tracer with transsynaptic potential (Zingg et al. 2017), in combination with reporter mouse line (Gt(ROSA)26Sortm9(CAG-tdTomato), Ai9), we selectively labeled the neuronal cell bodies that receive VISa inputs.”
relation — “We also observed inputs from LGd, VPL, VPM and VAL in mice, which were not reported in rats (no reported data in BAMS).”
relation — “It has been reported in rats that light projections from the ZI to the VISrl exist, however, we found no clear evidence of projections to the PPC (see Supplementary Figs 9 and 10k).”

replicationuntested
limitationThe PPC is small and its anatomical borders are weakly agreed on in mouse, so injection targeting could be imprecise. — “This leaves a potential problem considering that the PPC is a relatively small brain region with a weak consensus on anatomical borders in the mouse brain.”
limitationFew injections were available per structure in the atlas dataset, giving incomplete regional coverage. — “First, a relatively limited number of injections for each structure in the connectivity dataset can result in incomplete coverage of the brain regions, which may display a high level of intraregional variability.”
limitationThe quantified signal is axonal, not synaptic, so laminar projection patterns must be read cautiously. — “However, detailed projection patterns need to be interpreted with care since the detected signal is primarily based on axonal structure instead of synaptic inputs per se.”
limitationThe LD and LP injections did not fill those nuclei, so the apparent mouse-rat thalamic difference is not yet established. — “As LD and LP injections examined in this study did not fill the entire LD or LP, the complete regional injections or additional nonoverlapping subregional targeting of mouse LD and LP are required to demonstrate a true species difference.”
limitationTwo ways of delineating the same region are reported not to match. — “However, it has been reported that even with functional retinotopic mapping by intrinsic optical imaging, using voltage-sensitive dyes or Ca2+ responses, the boundaries of VISa and VISrl were not clearly defined or mismatched with the architectonic definition of the PPC”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingThe mouse posterior parietal cortex sends its heaviest cortical output to secondary motor cortex, primary visual cortex and the barrel field of primary somatosensory cortex. — “The PPC predominantly projected to secondary motor, primary visual, and the barrel field of the primary somatosensory cortex (MOs, VISp, and SSp-bfd; Figs 2 and 3).”
findingPPC output to the thalamus is concentrated in three associative nuclei, LP, PO and LD. — “Strong PPC projections were found in the associative thalamic regions, LP, PO, and LD.”
findingThe heaviest inputs to the PPC come from ipsilateral secondary motor, auditory and visual cortex plus primary somatosensory cortex. — “The largest inputs arise from ipsilateral secondary cortical regions (motor, auditory, and visual) and the primary somatosensory region (Fig. 5).”
findingHypothalamic output of the PPC is essentially confined to the zona incerta, a connection not previously reported. — “Within the hypothalamus, moderate projections from the PPC appeared almost exclusively in the ZI, for both VISa and VISrl”
findingWhat separates PPC from neighbouring visual cortex is that it gets more multimodal spatial input and less primary visual input. — “In summary, the enhanced multimodal spatial input and decreased primary visual input distinguishes the PPC from adjacent regions of the visual cortex.”
findingThe strongest reciprocal cortical partners of PPC are MOs, SSp-bfd, VISal, VISam and VISpm. — “In the cortex, MOs, SSp-bfd, VISal, VISam, and VISpm showed the greatest mutual connection, for which pairs both VISa and VISrl showed the projection strength above the threshold (Fig. 11a,b).”
findingThe strongest reciprocal thalamic partners of PPC are LP, PO, LD and PF. — “In the thalamus, LP, PO, LD, and PF were found to have the strongest reciprocal connections (Fig. 11c,d).”
findingNeuromodulatory input to PPC is dominated by cholinergic fibres, with weaker serotonergic and histaminergic input. — “In summary, there was a distinct cholinergic input to the PPC, with much lighter serotonergic and histaminergic inputs to the PPC; patterns were similar in adjacent regions.”
findingThe posterior thalamic nucleus projects topographically, its dorsomedial neurons reaching VISa and its lateral neurons reaching VISrl. — “Dorsomedial PO neurons projected to VISa, whereas neurons in lateral portion of PO projected to VISrl almost exclusively (Fig. 9c,d).”
findingCompared with rats, mice have lighter LD and LP projections to PPC but clear ventrobasal (VPL, VPM) and LGd input that rats were not reported to have. — “On the other hand, LD and LP projected more lightly to the PPC in mice than in rats, whereas the ventrobasal areas and the LGd appeared lightly, but clearly, in mice (Figs 5, 8, and 9).”
findingThe single largest mouse-rat discrepancy in PPC output is the infralimbic area, strongly innervated in rat and only trace-labelled in mouse. — “The greatest species differences in projection between mice and rats were noted in ILA.”
findingTop-down cortical inputs from ILA and ACAd and higher-order thalamic input from VAL and PO terminate mainly in layer 1 of PPC. — “Top–down cortical inputs (ILA and ACAd) and high-order thalamic nuclei (VAL and PO) send their projections primarily to L1.”
findingOverall the mouse PPC connectome closely resembles the rat's, with a minority of clear species differences. — “We found that the connectivity of the mouse PPC was remarkably similar to that of rats overall, while clear differences were also observed in some areas.”
