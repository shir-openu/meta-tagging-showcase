claim-typemechanism— “Here we use a paradigm requiring processing on relatively ‘local’ and ‘global’ scales and provide evidence suggesting that the auditory system extracts fine-detail acoustic information using short temporal windows and uses long temporal windows to abstract global acoustic patterns.”
method — evidence— “Here, to explore mechanisms of temporal integration and test certain predictions of the different integration models, we investigated in a series of four psychophysical experiments the perceptual performance at shorter and longer scales by manipulating the timescale of acoustic information while biasing the auditory processes implementing…
object of study — evidence— “Integrating information over time, as we define it operationally here, serves as a basic information accumulation process in the auditory system to guarantee that enough information is supplied for extracting meaningful regularities on a certain timescale.”
research question — evidence— “Therefore, by hypothesis, a single integration process may not suffice for the variety of auditory tasks that the system successfully solves.”
contribution — evidence— “Behavioral task performance that requires processing fine-detail information does not improve with longer stimulus length, contrary to predictions of previous temporal integration models such as the multiple-looks and the spectro-temporal excitation pattern model.”
mechanismsampled acoustic information is passed to a second, representational stage of limited capacity, so what reaches perception is not the full set of samples— “In a second stage, the sampled information is channeled to a representational stage with limited information capacity and probably with a time constant of hundreds of milliseconds.”
mechanismwhen too much detail is squeezed into that limited-capacity representation the system smooths or summarises it, destroying the local detail that the task requires— “However, the limited information capacity of the representation stage forces the auditory system to smooth or summarize all information.”
mechanisminserting gaps lowers the amount of information per unit time, letting local detail be represented precisely— “After intervals are inserted between segments, information per unit time decreases, so local details can be represented in a more precise manner.”
mechanismstretching a stimulus with gaps pushes segments outside the single long temporal window, so the global pattern can no longer be sampled— “As the long temporal window is around hundreds of milliseconds, inserting intervals stretches the stimuli and makes few segments fall within one long temporal window, so a global pattern of the stretched stimuli cannot be sampled.”
mechanismtwo windows run concurrently: about 30 ms for parsing acoustic structure at high resolution, about 200 ms for extracting the global pattern— “one temporal window, of ~30 ms or less in which acoustic structure is parsed and analyzed with relatively high temporal resolution; and another temporal window of ~200 ms in which acoustic information is extracted at a more global scale”
mechanismthe two models are separated by a prediction, not by plausibility: more stimulus should mean better detail discrimination under multiple-looks and STEP, and need not under 2TWI— “In contrast, the 2TWI model suggests that increased stimulus length may not necessarily increase performance, because representation and processing of local details might function best only at a time range of tens of milliseconds”
mechanismthe older account is leaky integration with a time constant of hundreds of milliseconds, under which performance rises with duration and plateaus above 200 ms— “Previous models of temporal integration typically assume leaky integration with a time constant of hundreds of milliseconds, based on studies such as loudness summation 15 , 16 , signal detection in noise 17 , 18 , and temporal integration at threshold 13 , 19 , 20 , 21 , 22 , in which listeners’ performance increases continuously with sound…
mechanismmultiple-looks resolves the paradox by sampling fast and concatenating - so the long timescale is BUILT from short samples rather than being independent— “The multiple-looks model suggests that temporal integration is a process of multiple timescales instead of one, but the longer timescale is assembled on the basis of concatenated short samples.”
contradicts the multiple-looks model (Viemeister) and the spectro-temporal excitation pattern (STEP) model (Moore)— “However, we find that the performance of discrimination between two stimuli with different fine-grained information decreases, which is inconsistent with the prediction of a multiple-looks model and a STEP model.”

findingMaking a sound longer improved listeners' ability to judge its overall global pattern but did not improve their ability to compare its fine acoustic detail.
findingLengthening the individual segments had the opposite effect from lengthening the whole stimulus: it helped the local task and hurt the global task.
findingWith longer stimuli listeners shifted their decision criterion, increasingly calling two genuinely different sounds 'the same'.
findingInserting silent gaps between segments raised sensitivity in the local task while lowering it in the global task.
findingVarying the silent interval between the two sounds of a pair from 500 to 1500 ms left performance unchanged, so memory decay cannot explain the stimulus-length effect.
findingGlobal frequency-trend discrimination still improved with stimulus length when the onset and offset frequency differences were equated, ruling out a simple spectral-edge cue.
findingThe local-task result contradicts the multiple-looks and STEP models, which predict better discrimination of fine detail as more samples become available.
findingHolding the number of segments constant while stretching the stimulus with gaps made performance better, not worse, so working-memory load does not drive the effect.
findingThe pattern replicates earlier reports that longer sounds aid global statistical perception while blocking access to local acoustic detail.
findingFine-detail performance does not improve with longer stimuli - the prediction that separates the old models from the new one fails for the old ones.
findingHearing a sound as one unitary event takes more than hundreds of milliseconds.
findingThe field's two bodies of evidence conflict: integration studies plateau above 200 ms, resolution studies find thresholds of 2-30 ms.
findingThe timescales are not arbitrary - speech carries phonemic, syllabic and intonational information at three separate ranges.

concepttemporal integration — accumulating acoustic information over time until there is enough of it to extract a regularity at a given timescale
concepttemporal window — the span of time over which the system accumulates before reading out - here two of them, ~30 ms and ~200 ms, running concurrently
conceptmultiple-looks — Viemeister's account: sample the sound fast, store the samples, concatenate them - so long timescales are built out of short ones
conceptresolution-integration paradox — the field's own contradiction: integration studies imply a slow accumulator, resolution studies imply a fast one, and both are well evidenced
conceptleaky integration — the older single-process account, with a time constant of hundreds of milliseconds and performance rising continuously with duration
conceptspectro-temporal excitation pattern — Moore's internal representation of a sound as a weighted vector of samples or 'intelligent looks'
concepttimescale — the temporal grain at which a kind of information lives - phonemes, syllables and intonation each occupying a different one
