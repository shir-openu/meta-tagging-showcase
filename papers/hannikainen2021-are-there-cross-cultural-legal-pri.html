phenomenonlay beliefs about procedural constraints on law— “In our present study, we pursue a related prediction: namely, that people around the world share an intuitive grasp of the formal properties of law—as well as its content.”
claim-typeexistence— “Confirming our preregistered prediction, people reported that such laws cannot exist, but also (paradoxically) that there are such laws.”
method — evidence— “In the present report, we evaluate this thesis through an experiment administered in 11 different countries.”
transferfrom modal logic (philosophy/formal logic): an axiom of modal logic — that what is actual is thereby possible — is imported to turn the two response patterns into a demonstrable contradiction and so to derive the experimental prediction— “According to most systems of modal logic, if there exists even a single retrospective law, it follows that retrospective laws certainly could exist—in virtue of axiom M.”
mechanismJudgments about possible laws are generated from a prototype or essentialized concept of law, assembled from simpler concepts such as norm, fairness and punishment, that is only weakly tied to experience of actual laws— “Such a concept could be a prototype, or essentialized belief—arising from the simpler concepts of “norm,” “fairness,” and “punishment,” for example—and which depends weakly on one’s experience with actual laws.”
mechanismAsking about possible rather than actual laws shifts attention from surface features to the deeper criteria for category membership, such as the purpose or moral dimension of law— “Potentially, by comparison to empirical reasoning about actual laws, modal reasoning about possible laws draws attention toward deeper criteria for category membership (i.e., could true laws lack these procedural properties?), such as the purpose of laws”
mechanismPrescriptive reasoning bleeds into descriptive judgment: asked whether laws necessarily have a property, participants also weigh whether that property is desirable — a mechanism the paper proposes and then partly disconfirms— “Our present findings could be understood as a manifestation of the latter phenomenon: When prompted to reason about whether laws necessarily exhibit certain procedural constraints, most participants also considered whether such constraints would be desirable or undesirable.”
extends Donelson and Hannikainen (2020)— “In sum, generalizing the findings of Donelson and Hannikainen (2020), participants in the Possible condition tended to report that laws could not violate various procedural principles even while participants in the Actual condition recognized that they often do.”
builds-on Donelson and Hannikainen (2020) materials— “The stimuli were adapted from Donelson and Hannikainen (2020) and translated into eight additional languages by native speakers: Dutch, German, Hindi, Khmer, Lithuanian, Polish, Portuguese, and Spanish.”
sensemodality - alethic: necessity and possibility, what could be the case— “Collectively, these are referred to as alethic modals, insofar as they purport to assess questions of necessity and possibility.”
sensemodality - deontic: permission and obligation, what is allowed or desirable — and the paper states that although the two are easily distinguished IN THOUGHT, natural language expresses them imperfectly— “Though these varieties of modality are easily distinguished in thought, natural languages tend to offer imperfect ways of doing so—raising the possibility that participants interpreted the statements in our experiment as deontic rather than alethic modals.”
senselaw - the concept: what participants report laws NECESSARILY are, which the paper reports as defying their own grasp of how legal systems actually work— “These results document cross-culturally and –linguistically robust beliefs about the concept of law which defy people’s grasp of how legal systems function in practice.”
senselaw - the practice: what participants report actual laws are like, where the same people acknowledge the principles are violated— “participants in the Possible condition tended to report that laws could not violate various procedural principles even while participants in the Actual condition recognized that they often do.”
