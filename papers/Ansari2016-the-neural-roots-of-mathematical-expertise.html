findingThe author's own Neurosynth reverse-inference meta-analysis shows that brain regions associated with numerical processing are largely anatomically separate from those associated with language. — “This analysis provides support for the finding that the processing of numerical information is (largely) anatomically distinct from the brain circuitry that is thought to underpin linguistic functions.”
findingThe commentary concludes that the absence of language-area recruitment in expert mathematicians refutes the claim that high-level mathematical ability is grounded in language systems. — “By showing that expert mathematicians do not recruit brain areas associated with language when engaged in mathematical thinking, Amalric and Dehaene ( 1 ) refute the hypothesis that higher level mathematical abilities are grounded in language systems of the brain.”
findingThe commentary argues the overlap between advanced mathematical reflection and elementary number processing implies a deep continuity between rudimentary symbolic number skills and advanced mathematics. — “the data speak against the notion that basic number concepts have little to do with advanced mathematics and instead suggest a deep connection between rudimentary symbolic number processing and advanced mathematics”
findingThe author qualifies the refutation: language may still matter for the initial acquisition of expertise or through functional connectivity rather than direct recruitment. — “Notwithstanding, it is possible that language may have played a role in the initial acquisition of mathematical expertise or that such a relationship exists at the level of functional connectivity with language areas of the brain rather than through their direct recruitment.”
findingThe author raises an untested alternative account, that experts may simply attend differently to statements in their domain, and proposes a non-mathematical expert control group to separate domain-general attention from domain-specific processing. — “Nevertheless, it is possible that the mathematical experts attended differently to the mathematical statements because they are particularly salient to them.”
findingThe commentary cautions that the expertise-related differences in ventral visual cortex are correlational and cannot establish that training caused them. — “Importantly, as Amalric and Dehaene ( 1 ) acknowledge, such correlational data cannot be used to infer causality.”
