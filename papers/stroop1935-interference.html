phenomenoncolor-word interference— “Interference in serial verbal reactions has been studied by means of newly devised experimental materials.”
claim-typeexistence— “But the interference of conflicting word stimuli upon the time for naming 100 colors (each color being the print of a word which names another color) caused an increase of 47.0 seconds or 74.3 percent of the normal time for naming colors printed in squares.”
method — evidence— “Seventy college undergraduates (14 males and 56 females) were used as subjects.”
mechanisminterference from automaticity: the highly-practiced automatic response (reading a word) competes with a slower, less-automatic response (naming the ink color), so conflicting color words slow color naming - the Stroop effect— “a given association can function automatically even though some effect of a previous contrary association remains”
builds-on prior experimental research on interference and inhibition— “Interference or inhibition (the terms seem to have been used almost indiscriminately) has been given a large place in experimental literature.”
replicates Woodworth-Wells (1911), Brown (1915) and Ligon (1932) on the sex difference in colour naming— “This reliable sex-difference favoring the females in naming colors agrees with the findings of Woodworth-Wells (1911), Brown (1915), Ligon (1932), etc.”
builds-on Peterson and Barlow's (1928) hypothesis about practice and individual differences— “These findings lend some support to Peterson's hypothesis, "Subjects of normal heterogeneity would become more alike with practice on the simpler processes or activities, but more different on the more complex activities" (Peterson and Barlow, 1928, p. 228).”
builds-on Peterson's prediction about the reading of colour names printed in conflicting colours— “This difference is not reliable which is in agreement with Peterson's prediction made when the test was first proposed.”
sense**THE ASYMMETRY WITH BOTH HALVES, WHICH IS THE FINDING - NOT THE HALF THAT GETS QUOTED:** `The interference of conflicting color stimuli upon the time for reading 100 words ... caused an increase of ONLY 2.3 SECONDS OR 5.6 PERCENT over the normal time for reading the same words printed in black. THIS INCREASE IS NOT RELIABLE. But the interference of conflicting word stimuli upon the time for naming 100 colors ... caused an increase of 47.0 SECONDS OR 74.3 PERCENT.` **One direction is a null and the other is enormous.** A record that holds only `colour-word interference` has half of this paper; the whole of it is that interference runs one way and not the other, and that fact is what needs explaining— “The interference of conflicting color stimuli upon the time for reading 100 words (each word naming a color unlike the ink-color of its print) caused an increase of only 2.3 seconds or 5.6 percent over the normal time for reading the same words printed in black. This increase is not reliable. But the interference of conflicting word stimuli…
sense**AND STROOP'S EXPLANATION IS A TRAINING HISTORY, NOT AN ARCHITECTURE:** `The word stimulus has been associated with the SPECIFIC response 'to read,' while the color stimulus has been associated with VARIOUS responses: 'to admire,' 'to name,' 'to reach for,' 'to avoid,' etc.` **One-to-one against one-to-many.** Not automaticity, not a module, not a race between pathways - a difference in how many responses each stimulus class has been attached to over a lifetime. **It is in the paper's own SUMMARY, not buried in a discussion** - and because it is a claim about learning rather than about structure, it is testable by training, which is exactly what Stroop then does in Experiment 3. sense**AND HE TESTED IT THE ONLY WAY THAT COULD SETTLE IT - BY TRAINING THE NULL DIRECTION UNTIL IT STOPPED BEING NULL.** Eight days, 200 reactions a day, naming the colours of conflicting words: `1. It DECREASED the interference of conflicting word stimuli to naming colors BUT DID NOT ELIMINATE IT. ... 5. It INCREASED THE INTERFERENCE OF CONFLICTING COLOR STIMULI UPON READING WORDS.` **The direction that was `not reliable` became reliable once it was trained**, and the direction that was enormous shrank. That is the training account making a prediction and collecting it, and it is stronger evidence than the headline asymmetry it explains— “It decreased the interference of conflicting word stimuli to naming colors but did not eliminate it. 2. It produced a practice curve comparable to that obtained in many other learning experiments. 3. It increased the variability of the group. 4. It shortened the reaction time to colors presented in color squares. 5. It increased the…
sense**AND THE INTERFERENCE HE BUILT DECAYED FASTER THAN THE ONE HE FOUND, WHICH IS A SEPARATE CLAIM:** `it is evident that the newly developed interference DISAPPEARS VERY RAPIDLY with practice. From one day to the next the mean decreases from 34.8 to 22.0 seconds. This indicates that RENEWING the effectiveness of OLD associations which are being opposed by newly formed ones is EASIER than STRENGTHENING NEW associations in opposition to old well established ones.` A trained interference is real but shallow; the lifetime one is not. **Age of an association, not just its strength** - and it is measured here in 1935 with a stopwatch— “it is evident that the newly developed interference disappears very rapidly with practice. controlthe colour order constructed so that no colour repeats consecutively in a row or column, and each appears TWICE in every row and column — held — rules out regularity of occurrence - a predictable sequence lets the reader anticipate the next colour, so a naming-speed difference would measure prediction rather than interference. Balancing across rows and columns also equalises how often each colour is named— “The colors were arranged so as to avoid any regularity of occurrence and so that each color would appear twice in each column and in each row”
