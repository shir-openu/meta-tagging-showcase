mechanismTHE MACHINE BUILDS ITS OWN REPRESENTATION WITHOUT REGARD FOR HUMAN COMPREHENSION - optimisation over training data does not naturally accord with human semantic explanation, so the opacity is a property of the method and not of anyone's choice or skill— “When a computer learns and consequently builds its own representation of a classification decision, it does so without regard for human comprehension. Machine optimizations based on training data do not naturally accord with human semantic explanations.”
mechanismUSEFULNESS REQUIRES COMPLEXITY: a machine learning algorithm CAN be implemented so its logic is fully comprehensible, but such an instance is unlikely to be useful - accuracy carries a degree of unavoidable complexity— “Though a machine learning algorithm can be implemented simply in such a way that its logic is almost fully comprehensible, in practice, such an instance is unlikely to be particularly useful. Machine learning models that prove useful (specifically, in terms of the ‘accuracy’ of classification) possess a degree of unavoidable complexity.”
mechanismTHE OPACITY IS IN THE INTERPLAY, NOT IN EITHER PART: the dataset may be large but comprehensible and the code may be written with clarity, and the complexity arises between them, in the mechanism of the algorithm operating on data— “While datasets may be extremely large but possible to comprehend and code may be written with clarity, the interplay between the two in the mechanism of the algorithm is what yields the complexity (and thus opacity).”
mechanismTHE DECISION LOGIC CHANGES AS THE ALGORITHM LEARNS, so reading the code as written does not tell you what it does after training— “The internal decision logic of the algorithm is altered as it ‘learns’ on training data.”
mechanismDIMENSIONALITY MANIPULATION BREAKS THE FEATURE-TO-DIMENSION CORRESPONDENCE: with PCA or the kernel trick the relation is not one-to-one, so a 'weighted input' can no longer be traced to a human-readable property— “it is often the case that the relationship between a feature and a dimension in the model is not one-to-one.”
mechanismINTUITION FAILS AT HIGH DIMENSIONS - each of thousands of features subtly and imperceptibly shifts the classification, so debugging and improvement get harder as inputs are added— “Domingos (2012) notes that ‘intuition fails at high-dimensions.’ In other words, reasoning about, debugging, or improving the algorithm becomes more difficult with more qualities or characteristics provided as inputs, each subtly and imperceptibly shifting the resulting classification.”
mechanismSOME ALGORITHMS ARE OPAQUE IN ORDER TO WORK: spam, scam and fraud detection face an adversary who adapts, so disclosure would defeat the system - opacity here is functional rather than merely protective— “Network security applications of machine learning deal explicitly with spam, scams, and fraud and remain opaque in order to be effective.”
mechanismSECRECY MAY BE COVER RATHER THAN COMPETITIVE NECESSITY: Pasquale's reading is that the adversary is regulation itself, which makes the same observable opacity a different problem with a different remedy— “he argues that a kind of adversarial situation is indeed in play, one where the adversary is regulation itself.”
critiques proposals for code or algorithm audits as a way to evaluate for discriminatory classification, which the paper argues underestimate what reading the code of a complicated system entails and are non-specific about classes of algorithm— “A call for code ‘audits’ (where this means reading the code) and the employment of ‘auditors’ may underestimate what this would entail as far as the number of hours required to untangle the logic of the code within a complicated software system.”
qualifies Pasquale's definition of opacity as 'remediable incomprehensibility' and the regulatory-disclosure remedy that follows from it— “In reference to this, he defines ‘opacity’ as ‘remediable incomprehensibility.’”
qualifies the emerging literature on the politics of algorithms, which treats algorithmic opacity as largely intentional corporate self-protection— “One argument in the emerging literature on the ‘politics of algorithms’ is that algorithmic opacity is a largely intentional form of self-protection by corporations intent on maintaining their trade secrets and competitive advantage.”
extends scholarship on digital inequality, which has focused on the distribution of computational resources and skills rather than on subjection to computational classification— “The goal of this article was to look more deeply into machine learning algorithms and the nature of their ‘opacity’, relating this to sociological interests in classification and discrimination.”
senseopacity - THREE DIFFERENT THINGS UNDER ONE WORD, and the paper's whole point is that they are routinely conflated: (1) intentional corporate or state secrecy, (2) the fact that reading code is a specialist skill, (3) the mismatch between high-dimensional mathematical optimisation and human-scale semantic reasoning. Only the third is intrinsic to machine learning— “Three distinct forms of opacity include: (1) opacity as intentional corporate or institutional self-protection and concealment and, along with it, the possibility for knowing deception; (2) opacity stemming from the current state of affairs where writing (and reading) code is a specialist skill and; (3) an opacity that stems from the mismatch…
senseopacity - AND THE SECOND AND THIRD SENSES ARE THE PAIR THAT GET CONFUSED, both reading as 'this is technical and complex'. The paper separates them because they have opposite remedies: education fixes illiteracy and cannot touch dimensionality— “This third form of opacity (often conflated with the second form as part of the general sense that algorithms and code are very technical and complex) is the particular focus of this article.”
senseopacity (Pasquale's sense) - 'REMEDIABLE INCOMPREHENSIBILITY', which builds the remedy into the definition. Burrell's third form is precisely the case that definition cannot cover, since no disclosure makes it comprehensible— “In reference to this, he defines ‘opacity’ as ‘remediable incomprehensibility.’”
sensecode audit - IN THIS PAPER, TWO INCOMPATIBLE THINGS: reading the source, which is what the legal literature proposes, and observing the algorithm in action on data, which is what the third form of opacity actually requires. The paper's critique of audits turns entirely on which one is meant— “These are challenges not just of reading and comprehending code, but being able to understand the algorithm in action, operating on data.”
