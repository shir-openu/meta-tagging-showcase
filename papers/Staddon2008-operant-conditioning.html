mechanismReinforcement acts as a selective agent on emitted behaviour, its strength set by how soon the reinforcer follows the response, how often the pairing occurs, and how well the response predicts the reinforcer— “Reinforcement is the selective agent, acting via temporal contiguity (the sooner the reinforcer follows the response, the greater its effect), frequency (the more often these pairings occur the better) and contingency (how well does the target response predict the reinforcer).”
mechanismThe predictive properties of the situation build, through innate food-related response tendencies, the repertoire of activities out of which reinforcement can then select— “The general principle that emerges from these experiments is that the predictive properties of the situation determine the repertoire, the set of activities from which consequential, operant, reinforcement can select.”
mechanismA food-predicting stimulus recruits built-in mechanisms that bias the animal's repertoire towards food-getting acts, which is why behaviour appears before any operant reinforcement
mechanismArousal has a bitonic effect because increasing predictiveness simultaneously raises response vigour and narrows the repertoire, so past a point the effective response drops out
mechanismMatching emerges because reinforcement probability on a VI schedule falls as responses accumulate and rises with time away, so any reward-following rule produces it
