transferfrom linguistics (natural language processing): the Skip-gram word-embedding architecture (word2vec), applied to graph nodes— “We proceed by extending the Skip-gram architecture to networks”
mechanismbiased (2nd-order) random-walk sampling of node neighborhoods— “We use a 2nd order random walk approach to generate (sample) network neighborhoods for nodes”

extends DeepWalk [24]— “The sampling strategy in DeepWalk can be seen as a special case of node2vec with p = 1 and q = 1.”
extends DeepWalk [24] and LINE [28] (rigid sampling strategies)— “Our algorithm node2vec overcomes this limitation by designing a flexible objective that is not tied to a particular sampling strategy and provides parameters to tune the explored search space (see Section 3).”
sensenode similarity - AS HOMOPHILY: two nodes count as alike when they sit in the same tightly knit community, which is one of the two organising principles the paper says networks mix— “nodes in networks could be organized based on communities they belong to (i.e., homophily); in other cases, the organization could be based on the structural roles of nodes in the network (i.e., structural equivalence)”
sensenode similarity - AS STRUCTURAL EQUIVALENCE: two nodes count as alike when they play the same role, EVEN IN DIFFERENT COMMUNITIES - the paper's own figure has u alike to s1 under the first sense and alike to s6 under this one, so the two senses pick out different pairs— “For instance, in Figure 1, we observe nodes u and s1 belonging to the same tightly knit community of nodes, while the nodes u and s6 in the two distinct communities share the same structural role of a hub node”
sensenetwork neighborhood - AS A SAMPLED SET, NOT A RADIUS: not restricted to immediate neighbours, and its shape changes with the sampling strategy, so the neighbourhood is a design choice rather than a property of the graph— “The neighborhoods NS(u) are not restricted to just immediate neighbors but can have vastly different structures depending on the sampling strategy”

for which uselimitation — every sampled walk and the estimates built on it: SAMPLE REUSE CAN INTRODUCE SOME BIAS IN THE OVERALL PROCEDURE— “Note that sample reuse can introduce some bias in the overall procedure.”
for which uselimitation — the claim that the search strategy contributes: IN THE WORST CASE, THE SEARCH PHASE HAS LITTLE BEARING ON LEARNED REPRESENTATIONS IN WHICH CASE NODE2VEC IS EQUIVALENT TO DEEPWALK— “In the worst case, the search phase has little bearing on learned representations in which case node2vec is equivalent to DeepWalk.”
for which uselimitation — the recommendation of the Hadamard operator: WE WOULD LIKE TO EXPLORE THE REASONS BEHIND THE SUCCESS OF HADAMARD OPERATOR OVER OTHERS - it performs best and the paper cannot say why— “As a future work, we would like to explore the reasons behind the success of Hadamard operator over others, as well as establish interpretable equivalence notions for edges based on the search parameters.”
for which uselimitation — the learned embeddings as a model of a network neighbourhood: the likelihood is factorised by assuming each neighbourhood node is observed independently of every other one given the source, and the paper adopts this to make the optimisation tractable— “In order to make the optimization problem tractable, we make two standard assumptions: • Conditional independence. We factorize the likelihood by assuming that the likelihood of observing a neighborhood node is independent of observing any other neighborhood node given the feature representation of the source”
for which uselimitation — node2vec read as word2vec applied to graphs: the neighbourhood in language is a sliding window over consecutive words, which the paper states does not transfer because networks are not linear, so the neighbourhood is redefined as a sampled set— “Feature learning methods based on the Skip-gram architecture have been originally developed in the context of natural language [21]. Given the linear nature of text, the notion of a neighborhood can be naturally defined using a sliding window over consecutive words. Networks, however, are not linear, and thus a richer notion of a neighborhood…
for which uselimitation — a comparison against spectral clustering or similar baselines: those methods assume a specific relationship between network structure and the task - graph cuts being useful for classification - which the paper calls reasonable in many scenarios but unsatisfactory for generalising across diverse networks— “For instance, spectral clustering makes a strong homophily assumption that graph cuts will be useful for classification [29]. Such assumptions are reasonable in many scenarios, but unsatisfactory in effectively generalizing across diverse networks”
replicationuntested
limitationReusing walk samples across source nodes trades statistical purity for efficiency and can bias the procedure.— “Note that sample reuse can introduce some bias in the overall procedure.”
limitationWhen the search phase does not matter for a given network, node2vec collapses to the DeepWalk baseline and offers no gain.— “In the worst case, the search phase has little bearing on learned representations in which case node2vec is equivalent to DeepWalk.”
limitationThe paper does not explain why the Hadamard edge operator works best, nor give equivalence notions for edges.— “As a future work, we would like to explore the reasons behind the success of Hadamard operator over others, as well as establish interpretable equivalence notions for edges based on the search parameters.”
limitationThe likelihood is factorised by assuming each neighbourhood node is observed independently of the others given the source, and the paper gives tractability as the reason.— “In order to make the optimization problem tractable, we make two standard assumptions: • Conditional independence. We factorize the likelihood by assuming that the likelihood of observing a neighborhood node is independent of observing any other neighborhood node given the feature representation of the source”
limitationThe construct borrowed from language does not carry over, and the paper says why: text is linear and networks are not, so the neighbourhood had to be redefined.— “Feature learning methods based on the Skip-gram architecture have been originally developed in the context of natural language [21]. Given the linear nature of text, the notion of a neighborhood can be naturally defined using a sliding window over consecutive words. Networks, however, are not linear, and thus a richer notion of a neighborhood…
limitationPrior methods are criticised for assuming a particular relationship between network structure and the prediction task, with spectral clustering named as an example.— “For instance, spectral clustering makes a strong homophily assumption that graph cuts will be useful for classification [29]. Such assumptions are reasonable in many scenarios, but unsatisfactory in effectively generalizing across diverse networks”
