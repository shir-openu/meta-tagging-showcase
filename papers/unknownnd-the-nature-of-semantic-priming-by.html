sense**A NULL WITH ITS OWN POSITIVE CONTROL, IN THE SAME APPARATUS:** `when symbolic and nonsemantic mechanisms were prevented, allowing only for a genuinely EMBODIED semantic priming, NO EFFECT WAS FOUND. Conversely, FACILITATION EMERGED IN THE SAME EXPERIMENTAL PARADIGM when embodied priming was prevented and symbolic priming was allowed.` **Six experiments, and the machine works when you leave the other route open.** A failed manipulation cannot selectively succeed - which is the objection every unconscious-priming null has to answer, and this one answers it by construction rather than by argument. Same structure as hesselmann2016 (Q-818) at three times the scale— “The critical finding is that when symbolic and nonsemantic mechanisms were prevented, allowing only for a genuinely embodied semantic priming, no effect was found. Conversely, facilitation emerged in the same experimental paradigm when embodied priming was prevented and symbolic priming was allowed”
sense**AND THE LEVEL CLAIM IS EXACT:** `we found NO EVIDENCE that unconsciously perceived words can activate SENSORIMOTOR processes, ALTHOUGH THESE WORDS ARE PROCESSED UP TO THE SEMANTIC LEVEL.` **Unconscious words reach meaning and stop before simulation.** The `although` is the whole value: this is not a claim that unconscious processing is shallow - the words got to semantics - but that the chain BREAKS at a specific link. A corpus of masked-priming records mostly argues about whether meaning was reached at all; this one grants that and asks what comes after— “we found no evidence that unconsciously perceived words can activate sensorimotor processes, although these words are processed up to the semantic level”
sense**AND THE CONCLUSION IS OFFERED AS A CONDITION, NOT A MECHANISM:** `We thus conclude that sensorimotor activations MIGHT NEED CONSCIOUS ACCESS to emerge during language understanding.` `might need` is inside the quote. **What makes this more than a hedge is that it is falsifiable in an obvious way** - show sensorimotor activation from an unconscious word under any conditions and it is gone - and the authors have just spent six experiments failing to do that themselves— “We thus conclude that sensorimotor activations might need conscious access to emerge during language understanding”
sense**AND IT SITS AGAINST dehaene1998 IN THIS CORPUS, WITHOUT CONTRADICTING IT.** dehaene1998 concluded that an unreportable numeral was `unconsciously processed according to task instructions, ALL THE WAY DOWN TO THE MOTOR SYSTEM`. This paper finds no sensorimotor activation from unconscious words at all. **Both can be right, because `reaches the motor system` names two things:** dehaene1998's is a TRAINED RESPONSE MAPPING - a digit compared to 5, a hand prepared, an arbitrary rule applied - and Bottini's is SIMULATION OF MEANING, the claim that understanding `up` re-enacts upward experience. An unconscious stimulus can drive the first and fail the second. **Same shape as Q-821's two senses of `nonconscious`, and the corpus currently indexes both pairs under one heading**— “we found no evidence that unconsciously perceived words can activate sensorimotor processes, although these words are processed up to the semantic level”

conceptembodied semantic priming — the claim under test: that understanding a word re-enacts the sensorimotor experience of its referent, and that this happens automatically enough to occur unconsciously. **The route that produced NO effect once the other two were closed off**— “The critical finding is that when symbolic and nonsemantic mechanisms were prevented, allowing only for a genuinely embodied semantic priming, no effect was found. Conversely, facilitation emerged in the same experimental paradigm when embodied priming was prevented and symbolic priming was allowed”
conceptsymbolic priming — the disembodied alternative - association between word forms and meanings with no simulation. **The route that DID produce facilitation, in the same paradigm**, which is what makes the embodied null interpretable rather than a failed experiment— “The critical finding is that when symbolic and nonsemantic mechanisms were prevented, allowing only for a genuinely embodied semantic priming, no effect was found. Conversely, facilitation emerged in the same experimental paradigm when embodied priming was prevented and symbolic priming was allowed”
conceptsensorimotor — what Embodied Semantics says must activate during comprehension - and what these six experiments could not detect from unconscious words, though the words reached meaning— “we found no evidence that unconsciously perceived words can activate sensorimotor processes, although these words are processed up to the semantic level”
conceptconscious access — the condition the authors end up proposing: simulation may require it, even though semantic processing does not. A boundary drawn between two levels that the unconscious priming literature usually treats as one chain— “We thus conclude that sensorimotor activations might need conscious access to emerge during language understanding”
