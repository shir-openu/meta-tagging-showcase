phenomenonconscious versus unconscious masked priming— “In the current study, we used a paradigm to reliably disentangle conscious and unconscious processing based on a trial-by-trial prime awareness assessment.”
claim-typemethod— “We present an alternative methodology for comparing conscious and unconscious cognition.”
method — evidence— “In our study, we used a priming version of the Stroop paradigm, in which primes are color words printed in black, and targets are colored symbols.”
mechanismPriming may cause awareness rather than the reverse: a strong priming effect on a trial generates metacognitive cues (response speed, urge to press the wrong key) that in turn help the participant identify the prime— “However, it could also be the case that trials where a strong priming effect is present lead to some metacognitive awareness (e.g., awareness of speed of RTs, awareness of the urge to press the wrong response button, a notion of the difficulty of the trial; e.g., Marti et al., 2010 ) and this in turn might aid prime identification.”
mechanismParticipants who see few primes adopt a more conservative criterion for calling a trial conscious, so their conscious trials contain only the most visible primes and yield inflated priming effects— “This latter effect could be due to the fact that participants with mostly uncertain or unconscious trials are more conservative in deciding when a trial was truly conscious and only do this on trials where the prime was extremely visible for them, which could lead to stronger priming effects.”
contradicts Francken et al. (2011), who claimed priming effects are independent of prime awareness once signal strength is matched— “The results of the current study directly oppose those of Francken et al. (2011) , who claimed that priming effects are independent of prime awareness.”
extends Lamy et al. (2009), who categorised trials as conscious or unconscious with a trial-by-trial awareness measure— “Lamy et al. (2009) also presented stimuli for an intermediate duration followed by a mask, and categorized trials as either conscious or unconscious based on a trial-by-trial awareness measure.”

senseTHE CONFOUND, stated in one sentence and inherited by every subliminal-versus-supraliminal comparison in this corpus: in traditional masking paradigms the unconscious primes are also the WEAKER primes, so a smaller unconscious effect `might just be the result of a difference in signal strength, rather than a difference in awareness`. Two variables move together and the design cannot say which one did the work— “In traditional masking paradigms, unconscious primes typically have a lower signal strength than conscious primes, confounding the comparison between conscious and unconscious processing. As an alternative approach, we categorized trials with identical stimulus strength as either conscious, uncertain, or unconscious based on the…
senseawareness as a PROPERTY OF A TRIAL rather than of a condition - the paradigm's whole move. Every prime has identical perceptual strength; the trials are sorted AFTERWARDS by what the participant reports seeing on that trial, into conscious, uncertain and unconscious. The experimenter no longer decides which trials were unconscious, and awareness stops being something the design asserts and becomes something it measures— “Crucially, in all these conditions, the primes have identical perceptual strength. Significant priming was observed for all conditions, but the effects for conscious trials were significantly stronger, and no difference was observed between uncertain and unconscious trials”
sensethe result in two halves, and the second is a NULL that shapes the first: significant priming in ALL THREE trial types, with conscious trials significantly stronger - and NO difference between uncertain and unconscious. So awareness matters with signal strength held constant, but the boundary behaves like a step between `saw it` and `did not clearly see it` rather than a gradient across the middle— “Significant priming was observed for all conditions, but the effects for conscious trials were significantly stronger, and no difference was observed between uncertain and unconscious trials. Thus, awareness of the prime has a large impact on congruency effects, even when signal strength is controlled for”
senseA METHODS PAPER REFUSING TO CROWN ITS OWN METHOD: `The aim of the current paper is not to suggest abolishing objective prime awareness measures OR TO PROVIDE A PERFECT MEASURE of prime awareness, but rather to explore alternative measures where consciousness is inferred from INTROSPECTION and where visual strength ... is perfectly matched.` It states what it trades - objective performance for self-report - instead of claiming to have escaped the trade-off— “The aim of the current paper is not to suggest abolishing objective prime awareness measures or to provide a perfect measure of prime awareness, but rather to explore alternative measures where consciousness is inferred from introspection and where visual strength in conscious and unconscious conditions is perfectly matched”

findingReliable priming appeared in all three subjectively defined conditions, including trials participants said they could not identify at all.
findingWith prime duration held identical, the reaction-time congruency effect was about three to four times larger on conscious trials (140 ms) than on uncertain (48 ms) or unconscious (33 ms) trials.
findingThe error-rate data reproduced the reaction-time pattern, with significantly larger congruency effects on conscious than on uncertain or unconscious trials.
findingUncertain trials, where participants identified the prime correctly but without confidence, produced priming no larger than fully unconscious trials.
findingParticipants' confidence ratings tracked objective performance: prime identification was 88% correct when they said they were certain versus 73% when uncertain.
findingBetween-subject differences in how many trials fell into each awareness category did not distort the reaction-time priming results.
findingThe study concludes that prime awareness itself, not signal strength, drives the size of priming effects.
