transferfrom cognitive psychology of memory: the self-reference effect, imported as the motivating prediction that personally relevant stimuli should recruit more neurons— “One virtually infallible method of enhancing memory is simply to relate the to-be-remembered information to one's self.”
transferfrom rodent hippocampal electrophysiology: the sparse-coding expectation, carried across from animal MTL recordings and used to frame what a human selective response means— “In animals, selective sparse coding has also been observed in recordings from the MTL ( 15 , 16 ).”
transferfrom clinical neuropsychology of temporal lobe epilepsy: famous faces and names as a probe of semantic memory integrity, reused here as a graded familiarity stimulus set— “Furthermore, famous faces and names have long been used to query the integrity of recent and remote semantic memory in patients with temporal lobe epilepsy (TLE) ( 3 , 6 – 11 ).”
mechanismPersonally relevant images are linked to more, and more varied, past experiences, so a larger number of MTL neurons are recruited to encode them— “Taken together, these findings suggest that personally relevant photographs are encoded by a larger number of neurons than photographs of unfamiliar people, because these photographs are associated with a larger number and perhaps wider variety of experiences.”
mechanismViewing a familiar face activates a richer network of associations than an unfamiliar one, and that richer network is what drives extra MTL cells into responding— “The images of experimenters and family members and, to a lesser degree, famous people, are all likely to elicit a richer network of associations than unknown faces, and thus recruit more MTL neurons.”
mechanismIncidental (unintentional) recollection of the autobiographical network attached to an item is the route by which personal relevance enhances encoding— “One mechanism by which the self-referent effect might operate is via the incidental recollection of a rich network of information related to past experiences with that particular item.”
mechanismIn the amygdala the driver is not personal relevance but a combination of novelty and emotional significance, which is why recently met experimenters outrank long-known family members there— “Notably, in the amygdala, photographs of the experimenters generated the largest proportion of responses, suggesting that these cells may represent other related variables, such as a combination of novelty and emotional significance.”
mechanismthe experimenters themselves are used as a stimulus category, which manufactures personal relevance the authors control rather than having to find it— “pictures of the researchers performing the experiments at the University of California at Los Angeles (UCLA), who had daily contact with the patients”
mechanismrelevance is turned into an ordered scale — unknown, famous, family, experimenter — so the prediction is a gradient rather than a single contrast— “Overall, we found that the different categories of faces (unknown, famous, family, and experimenters) generated different proportions of responses across the MTL [χ 2 (3) = 28.2, P < 0.001].”
mechanismselectivity is defined by an exclusion rule rather than by a positive criterion: cells responding to more than five image sets are dropped so that promiscuous units cannot dominate the counts
mechanismresponsiveness requires three conditions together — a five-SD threshold, a minimum absolute spike count, and a significance test — so no single loose criterion carries a unit through
