phenomenonnegative visuospatial priming
claim-typenull-result
mechanismIsolation rearing lowers dopamine responsivity in the prefrontal cortex, so amphetamine no longer pushes PFC dopamine past the level that disrupts negative priming — “The above considerations suggest that isolation rearing-induced reductions in DA responsivity in the PFC might account for the observation of the attenuated AMPH effects on NP in isolates compared with socials.”
mechanismNegative priming depends on an optimal (inverted-U) level of prefrontal dopamine transmission, which amphetamine shifts away from optimum — “Optimal DA transmission levels in the PFC may therefore be required for maximum levels of PFC-controlled functions”
mechanismRats inhibit responding at the distractor location during the prime, and must overcome that inhibition when the target later appears there, which lowers accuracy on negative trials — “Having to overcome that inhibition in order to correctly respond when, during a negative trial, this location then goes on to display the target stimulus, is what presumably leads to the decreased accuracy in negative trials as compared to baseline trials and thus NP.”
mechanismBlunted prefrontal serotonin release after amphetamine in isolates removes the drug-driven rise in premature responding — “it is plausible that reduced 5-HT responsivity in the PFC may also contribute to the attenuation of AMPH effects on premature responses observed in this study.”
relation — “In conclusion, the present findings replicate and expand our previous report that NP can be measured in rats and is sensitive to disruption by AMPH.”
relation — “We recently developed and validated a rodent NP task ( Amitai et al., 2013 ).”
effect directionisolation rearing → negative visuospatial priming — no-change — “isolation rearing did not disrupt negative visuospatial priming”
replicationuntested
limitationOnly one amphetamine dose was tested, so hypo- versus hypersensitivity in isolates cannot be decided. — “It must be noted, however, that no definitive conclusions regarding hypo-versus hypersensitivity to the effects of AMPH in isolates can be drawn based on the present study, since only one dose of AMPH was used.”
limitationBecause many more isolates failed to reach criterion and were excluded, a priming deficit in the excluded animals could have been missed. — “It is possible that the exclusion of a larger number of isolates due to their inability to reach criterion task performance could have masked a NP deficit in isolates (if the rats excluded due to their inability to learn the tasks were specifically ones that would have exhibited lower levels of NP).”
limitationThe task may have a ceiling that hides group differences or drug-induced increases in priming. — “It remains possible that this NP task, in its current form, cannot detect increases in NP beyond a certain point, and thus may not be able to detect greater NP in socials due to ceiling effects.”
limitationThe proposed prefrontal monoaminergic explanation is not measured in this study. — “This phenomenon may reflect reduced monoaminergic activity in the PFC, but further research is needed to more firmly establish the neurochemical underpinnings of the observations in this study.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingIsolation-reared rats that managed to learn the task showed negative priming that was indistinguishable from that of socially reared rats, contrary to the authors' hypothesis of an NP deficit. — “Although fewer isolation-reared animals learned the complex NP task, those that learned exhibited unaffected NP compared with socially reared rats.”
findingA low dose of D-amphetamine abolished negative priming and raised motor impulsivity in socially reared rats. — “Consistent with previous reports, D-amphetamine impaired NP and increased motor impulsivity in socially reared rats.”
findingThe same dose of D-amphetamine left both negative priming and motor impulsivity intact in isolation-reared rats, indicating altered dopaminergic sensitivity. — “In contrast, D-amphetamine did not affect NP or motor impulsivity in isolation-reared rats.”
findingSignificantly fewer isolation-reared than socially reared rats reached criterion performance in Experiment 1, showing a learning deficit for this complex task. — “When analyzed, this difference in reaching criterion by group was statistically significant (Pearson χ 2 = 8.7, p < 0.01), indicating fewer isolates attained criterion than socials.”
findingAmphetamine abolished negative priming by selectively lowering accuracy on baseline trials rather than by changing accuracy on negative trials. — “Notably, both in our previous and in the current study, AMPH disrupted NP not by altering accuracy during negative trials, but by selectively decreasing accuracy in the baseline trials”
findingBecause the same isolates showed prepulse-inhibition deficits but intact negative priming, the two inhibitory-processing measures are dissociable. — “This finding also suggests that while PPI and NP are both measures of inhibitory processing, they are dissociable and may be mediated by somewhat different substrates and mechanisms.”
quantityn = 23 socials and n = 14 isolates — Final animal numbers analysed in Experiment 1 after exclusions — “As a result, final rat numbers were n = 23 socials and n = 14 isolates in Experiment 1 (three socials and 15 isolates excluded).”
quantityn = 19 socials and n = 18 isolates — Final animal numbers analysed in the amphetamine experiment — “For Experiment 2, final rat numbers were n = 19 socials and n = 18 isolates (seven socials and 11 isolates excluded).”
