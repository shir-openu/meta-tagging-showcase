phenomenonnegative visuospatial priming— “Negative visuospatial priming (NP) represents a quantifiable measure of inhibitory information processing that is disrupted in several neurodevelopmental and psychiatric disorders, including schizophrenia.”
claim-typenull-result— “In contrast with our hypothesis, however, isolation rearing did not disrupt negative visuospatial priming in rats, despite inducing PPI deficits consistent with various developmental and psychiatric disorders characterized by impaired information processing, including schizophrenia.”
method — evidence— “We also further explored the role of monoaminergic signaling in NP and the effects of isolation rearing by challenging both socially reared and isolation-reared rats with D-amphetamine during the NP task.”
mechanismIsolation rearing lowers dopamine responsivity in the prefrontal cortex, so amphetamine no longer pushes PFC dopamine past the level that disrupts negative priming— “The above considerations suggest that isolation rearing-induced reductions in DA responsivity in the PFC might account for the observation of the attenuated AMPH effects on NP in isolates compared with socials.”
mechanismNegative priming depends on an optimal (inverted-U) level of prefrontal dopamine transmission, which amphetamine shifts away from optimum— “Optimal DA transmission levels in the PFC may therefore be required for maximum levels of PFC-controlled functions”
mechanismRats inhibit responding at the distractor location during the prime, and must overcome that inhibition when the target later appears there, which lowers accuracy on negative trials— “Having to overcome that inhibition in order to correctly respond when, during a negative trial, this location then goes on to display the target stimulus, is what presumably leads to the decreased accuracy in negative trials as compared to baseline trials and thus NP.”
mechanismBlunted prefrontal serotonin release after amphetamine in isolates removes the drug-driven rise in premature responding— “it is plausible that reduced 5-HT responsivity in the PFC may also contribute to the attenuation of AMPH effects on premature responses observed in this study.”
replicates Amitai et al., 2013 (the rat negative-priming task and its disruption by amphetamine)— “In conclusion, the present findings replicate and expand our previous report that NP can be measured in rats and is sensitive to disruption by AMPH.”
builds-on Amitai et al., 2013— “We recently developed and validated a rodent NP task ( Amitai et al., 2013 ).”
senseA NULL AGAINST THE AUTHORS' OWN HYPOTHESIS, WITH A POSITIVE CONTROL IN THE SAME ANIMALS: `In contrast with our hypothesis, however, isolation rearing DID NOT DISRUPT negative visuospatial priming in rats, DESPITE INDUCING PPI DEFICITS consistent with various developmental and psychiatric disorders.` The manipulation demonstrably worked - prepulse inhibition was disrupted - and the measure demonstrably did not move. A null with an internal manipulation check is a different object from a null on its own, and this corpus should be able to tell them apart— “In contrast with our hypothesis, however, isolation rearing did not disrupt negative visuospatial priming in rats, despite inducing PPI deficits consistent with various developmental and psychiatric disorders characterized by impaired information processing”
senseTHE SELECTION PROBLEM STATED IN THE SENTENCE THAT REPORTS THE RESULT: `Although FEWER ISOLATION-REARED ANIMALS LEARNED the complex NP task, those that learned exhibited unaffected NP compared with socially reared rats.` The comparison runs on survivors - the isolates who got far enough to be measured - so the null holds on a sample the manipulation has already filtered. The authors put that in the abstract rather than leaving it to a reader, which is the shape this corpus wants from a limitation— “Although fewer isolation-reared animals learned the complex NP task, those that learned exhibited unaffected NP compared with socially reared rats”
sensePRIMING AS AN INSTRUMENT, DECLARED IN THE FIRST SENTENCE: negative visuospatial priming `represents A QUANTIFIABLE MEASURE of inhibitory information processing that is disrupted in several neurodevelopmental and psychiatric disorders, including schizophrenia`. The clinical claim attaches to the PROCESS, not to the priming effect - priming is the dial, inhibition is the quantity, and the disorder is a property of the quantity. Second Amitai record where priming is a measuring device rather than a phenomenon— “Negative visuospatial priming (NP) represents a quantifiable measure of inhibitory information processing that is disrupted in several neurodevelopmental and psychiatric disorders, including schizophrenia”
senseTHE TITLE'S FINDING, AND IT IS A NULL INSIDE A NULL: `In contrast, D-amphetamine DID NOT AFFECT NP or motor impulsivity in isolation-reared rats.` The drug disrupts negative priming in socially reared animals and does not in isolates - so the rearing that failed to disrupt the measure directly nonetheless changed how the measure responds to a drug. The discussion offers reduced prefrontal dopamine responsivity as the reason, hedged twice: `suggest that` and `might account for`— “In contrast, D-amphetamine did not affect NP or motor impulsivity in isolation-reared rats. These data confirm a monoaminergic influence on NP behavior and indicate that rats reared in isolation have altered dopaminergic sensitivity.”
controltarget and non-target locations varied pseudorandomly so each location held the stimuli an equal number of times per session while remaining unpredictable trial to trial — held — rules out a location bias and an anticipation strategy at once - equal frequency removes the first, unpredictability removes the second, and the paper states both— “The location of the target and non-target stimuli varied pseudorandomly between trials, so that each location contained the stimuli an equal number of times per session, but their location was not predictable on any given trial.”
controlbaseline and negative trials alternated pseudorandomly, each type equally often per session and not predictable in advance — held — rules out the animal preparing for the trial type it expects next, which would make the priming effect a preparation effect— “Baseline and negative trials alternated pseudorandomly, so that that each trial type was presented equally often during each session, but the trial type was not predictable on any given trial.”
