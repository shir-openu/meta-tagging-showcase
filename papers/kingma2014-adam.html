phenomenonstochastic gradient-based optimization— “Stochastic gradient-based optimization is of core practical importance in many fields of science and engineering.”
claim-typemethod— “We introduce Adam, an algorithm for first-order gradient-based optimization of stochastic objective functions, based on adaptive estimates of lower-order moments.”
method — evidence— “To empirically evaluate the proposed method, we investigated different popular machine learning models, including logistic regression, multilayer fully connected neural networks and deep convolutional neural networks.”
mechanisminitialisation bias from zero-initialised moving averages The moment estimates start at zero, so early estimates are biased toward zero; the correction divides by one minus the decay rate raised to the timestep.— “these moving averages are initialized as (vectors of) 0’s, leading to moment estimates that are biased towards zero, especially during the initial timesteps”
mechanismautomatic annealing via the signal-to-noise ratio As an optimum is approached the ratio falls, so the effective step shrinks with no schedule imposed.— “the SNR value typically becomes closer to 0 towards an optimum, leading to smaller effective steps in parameter space: a form of automatic annealing”
mechanisminvariance of the update to gradient rescaling Rescaling the gradient by c scales the first moment by c and the second by c squared, and the two cancel in the ratio.— “The effective stepsize ∆t is also invariant to the scale of the gradients; rescaling the gradientsg with factorc will scaleˆmt with a factorc andˆvt with a factorc2, which cancel out”
builds-on AdaGrad (Duchi et al., 2011) and RMSProp (Tieleman & Hinton, 2012)— “Our method is designed to combine the advantages of two recently popular methods: AdaGrad (Duchi et al., 2011), which works well with sparse gradients, and RMSProp (Tieleman & Hinton, 2012), which works well in on-line and non-stationary settings; important connections to these and other stochastic optimization methods are clarified in…
extends RMSProp (Tieleman & Hinton, 2012)— “RMSProp also lacks a bias-correction term; this matters most in case of a value of β2 close to 1 (required in case of sparse gradients), since in that case not correcting the bias leads to very large stepsizes and often divergence, as we also empirically demonstrate in section 6.4.”
builds-on the online learning framework of (Zinkevich, 2003)— “We analyze the convergence of Adam using the online learning framework proposed in (Zinkevich, 2003).”
sensesignal-to-noise ratio - here the ratio of the two bias-corrected moment estimates, used as confidence in the gradient DIRECTION, not a power ratio— “With a slight abuse of terminology, we will call the ratio ˆmt/√ˆvt the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR )”
senseregret - the summed gap between the online prediction and the best fixed parameter in hindsight, over the whole sequence— “the regret, that is the sum of all the previous difference between the online prediction ft(θt) and the best fixed point parameterft(θ∗) from a feasible setX for all the previous steps”
sensesecond raw moment - the UNCENTERED variance of the gradient, not the variance— “The moving averages themselves are estimates of the 1 st moment (the mean) and the 2nd raw moment (the uncentered variance) of the gradient”
sensetrust region - the region within which the current gradient estimate is taken to carry enough information, bounded here by the stepsize hyper-parameter— “This can be understood as establishing a trust region around the current parameter value, beyond which the current gradient estimate does not provide sufficient information”
controlthe optimiser comparison run both with and without dropout noise — held — rules out an advantage that holds only in the absence of the regularisation practitioners actually use— “In figure 1, Adagrad outperforms SGD with Nesterov momentum by a large margin both with and without dropout noise.”
