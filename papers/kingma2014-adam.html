relation — “We analyze the convergence of Adam using the online learning framework proposed in (Zinkevich, 2003).”
differs inAdam optimizer → RMSProp — whether a bias-correction term is present, and the paper names the regime in which it decides the outcome: beta2 close to 1, which is what sparse gradients require — “RMSProp also lacks a bias-correction term; this matters most in case of a value of β2 close to 1 (required in case of sparse gradients), since in that case not correcting the bias leads to very large stepsizes and often divergence”
differs inAdam optimizer → Sum-of-Functions Optimizer (SFO) — memory scaling: SFO's requirement is linear in the number of minibatch partitions — “The Sum-of-Functions Optimizer (SFO) (Sohl-Dickstein et al., 2014) is a quasi-Newton method based on minibatches, but (unlike Adam) has memory requirements linear in the number of minibatch partitions of a dataset, which is often infeasible on memory-constrained systems such as a GPU”
replicationuntested
limitationThe convergence analysis covers only convex objectives, not the non-convex problems on which Adam is mostly used. — “Although our convergence analysis does not apply to non-convex problems, we empirically found that Adam often outperforms other methods in such cases.”
limitationThe Lp-norm generalisation of the update rule is numerically unstable except in the infinity-norm limit. — “Such variants become numerically unstable for large p.”
limitationIn convolutional nets the second moment estimate vanishes and poorly approximates the cost surface. — “The second moment estimate is therefore a poor approximation to the geometry of the cost function in CNNs comparing to fully connected network from Section 6.2.”
limitationOn the CNN experiment the gain over SGD with momentum is only marginal. — “Though Adam shows marginal improvement over SGD with momentum, it adapts learning rate scale for different layers instead of hand picking manually as in SGD.”
limitationThe convergence guarantee is proved only for convex objectives, and the paper says so in the same sentence in which it reports the non-convex results. — “Although our convergence analysis does not apply to non-convex problems, we empirically found that Adam often outperforms other methods in such cases”
