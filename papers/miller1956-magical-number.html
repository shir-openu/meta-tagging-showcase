method — evidence— “I shall begin my case history by telling you about some experiments that tested how accurately people can assign numbers to the magnitudes of various aspects of a stimulus.”

transferfrom information theory (Shannon / communication theory): analyzing the limits of human perception and memory using information theory (channel capacity, bits)— “the results are analyzed in terms of the concepts of information theory”
transferfrom communication engineering (information theory): Shannon's information measure, the channel and its capacity, carried into psychology - and the paper is explicit that the experiments would not exist without it— “Since these experiments would not have been done without the appearance of information theory on the psychological scene, and since the results are analyzed in terms of the concepts of information theory, I shall have to preface my discussion with a few remarks about this theory.”
transferfrom statistics: A translation table offered in the other direction, so psychologists could read the information measure as the variance they already knew— “The "amount of information" is exactly the same concept that we have talked about for years under the name of "variance."”
mechanismthe human observer behaves like a limited-capacity communication channel: absolute judgment saturates at a channel capacity of about 2.5 bits (~7 categories)— “This asymptotic value we take to be the channel capacity of the observer”
mechanismTreating the observer as a communication channel makes the limit measurable: input information is raised until transmitted information stops rising, and that asymptote is the capacity— “This asymptotic value we take to be the channel capacity of the observer: it represents the greatest amount of information that he can give us about the stimulus on the basis of an absolute judgment.”
mechanismImmediate memory holds a fixed number of chunks rather than a fixed number of bits, which is why it behaves unlike absolute judgment— “The span of immediate memory seems to be almost independent of the number of bits per chunk, at least over the range that has been examined to date.”
mechanismBecause the limit is in chunks, packing more information into each chunk raises how much can be held without raising the count— “Since the memory span is a fixed number of chunks, we can increase the number of bits of information that it contains simply by building larger and larger chunks, each chunk containing more information than before.”
mechanismRecoding is the operation that builds bigger chunks: group the input, name the group, remember the name— “There are many ways to do this recoding, but probably the simplest is to group the input events, apply a new name to the group, and then remember the new name rather than the original input events.”
mechanismAdding dimensions and accepting cruder judgments on each raises the span of absolute judgment far beyond seven— “91] more dimensions and requiring crude, binary, yes-no judgments on each attribute we can extend the span of absolute judgment from seven to at least 150.”
builds-on information theory (channel capacity)— “This asymptotic value we take to be the channel capacity of the observer: it represents the greatest amount of information that he can give us about the stimulus on the basis of an absolute judgment.”
replicates Hayes (10), memory-span experiment with five kinds of test material— “There is nothing wrong with Hayes's experiment, because Pollack ( 16 ) repeated it much more elaborately and got essentially the same result.”
replicates Hake and Garner (8), absolute judgment of visual position— “The Hake-Garner experiment has been repeated by Coonan and Klemmer.”
builds-on Külpe (14), abstraction experiment— “These multidimensional judgments are strongly reminiscent of the abstraction experiment of Külpe ( 14 ).”
extends information theory as imported into psychology— “Informational concepts have already proved valuable in the study of discrimination and of language; they promise a great deal in the study of learning and memory; and it has even been proposed that they can be useful in the study of concept formation.”
critiques the assumption that immediate memory is limited by the same quantity as absolute judgment— “If the amount of information in the span of immediate memory is a constant, then the span should be short when the individual items contain a lot of information and the span should be long when the items contain little information.”
senseseven - the span of absolute judgment: about seven categories distinguishable along one dimension, a limit in BITS— “I would propose to call this limit the span of absolute judgment , and I maintain that for unidimensional judgments this span is usually somewhere in the neighborhood of seven.”
senseseven - the span of immediate memory: about seven items retained, a limit in CHUNKS and almost independent of the bits in each— “Everybody knows that there is a finite span of immediate memory and that for a lot of different kinds of test materials this span is about seven items in length.”
senseseven - the span of attention: about six objects taken in at a glance, a third limit with a similar number and a different mechanism— “I have just shown you that there is a span of absolute judgment that can distinguish about seven categories and that there is a span of attention that will encompass about six objects at a glance.”
senseamount of information - as the paper translates it for the reader: variance, in the ordinary statistical sense— “The "amount of information" is exactly the same concept that we have talked about for years under the name of "variance."”
senseamount of TRANSMITTED information - covariance or correlation between input and output, a different quantity that shares most of its name— “There are two simple rules to follow. Whenever I refer to "amount of information," you will understand "variance." And whenever I refer to "amount of transmitted information," you will understand "covariance" or "correlation."”
differs inthe assumption that immediate memory is limited by the same quantity as absolute judgment — THE AXIS IS INFORMATION PER ITEM.
