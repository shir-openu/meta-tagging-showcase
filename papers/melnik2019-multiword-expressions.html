mechanismthe idiom whose common characterization is rigidity is searched for in a twenty-billion-word corpus, chosen because the earlier corpora were too small to distinguish an idiom that cannot vary from one that is simply too rare for its variants to appear — “In order to verify that the idiom's common characterization as a rigid idiom is not an epiphenomenon of its low frequency, we consulted a much larger corpus: enTenTen13 (Baroni et al. 2009), a 20 billion word English corpus, available on SketchEngine (Kilgarriff et al. 2004).”
mechanismidioms are cross-classified on two dimensions at once - figuration, whether a literal meaning can be pictured, and transparency, whether the route from that picture to the idiomatic meaning can be recovered - in place of the single decomposable/non-decomposable cut — “In this chapter we consider an alternative categorization of idioms. More precisely, we cross-classify idioms according to two dimensions: figuration and transparency. Figuration reflects the degree to which the idiom can be assigned a literal meaning. These features are particularly important when our goal is to cast a wide net to retrieve variations in general, and in particular non-canonical…”
mechanisma query wide enough to catch variants also returns results that are not instances of the idiom, so each result is inspected by hand to remove the false positives — “Nevertheless, a wide net comes at a cost; not all the retrieved results are necessarily instances of the idiom. Often, only a manual inspection of each result can weed out the false positives.”
mechanismthe endpoint is switched from frequency to existence: rather than how often each variation occurs, the study verifies that every type of variation occurs at least once for each idiom — “For this reason we do not present quantitative data with regard to the distribution of the canonical idiom and its variations. We did, however, verify the occurrence of all types of variation for each idiom.”
mechanismargument-structure variation is observable in Hebrew because it is carried by combining one consonantal root with different morphological templates — “Hebrew presents an interesting case in this respect, since argument structure variations are associated with the combination of one consonantal root with different morphological templates (Doron 2008; 2003).”

content observations: finding · quantity · definition · variable · statistic · key-term — each grounded in a verbatim quotation from the paper
findingThe paper's central question is whether idiom behaviour is idiosyncratic or predictable from general constraints. — “Is information regarding their flexibility encoded for each idiom individually, or can the behavior of idioms be predicted from general principles?”
findingDecomposability is rejected as the criterion, and transparency and figuration put in its place. — “We propose an alternative classification that builds on the notions of transparency and figuration”
findingThe hypothesis is directional and therefore testable: more transparent and more figurative idioms should bend more. — “We propose an alternative classification that builds on the notions of transparency and figuration. We hypothesize that the more transparent and figurative an idiom is, the more likely it is to be "transformationally productive".”
findingIdioms are not uniformly rigid; some keep their idiomatic meaning outside their canonical form. — “Not all idioms, however, are equally rigid, as some maintain their idiomatic meaning even when they do not appear in their canonical form.”
definitionsemantic idiomaticity — “The most prominent type of idiosyncrasy ascribed to MWEs is their semantic idiomaticity; their meaning cannot be systematically derived from the meanings that their parts have when they are used independently”
variabletransparency and figuration of the idiom (independent) — “using a large corpus of Modern Hebrew”
