phenomenonidiom flexibility is a continuum rather than a two-way split, with every idiom examined showing some flexibility
claim-typecritique
mechanismthe idiom whose common characterization is rigidity is searched for in a twenty-billion-word corpus, chosen because the earlier corpora were too small to distinguish an idiom that cannot vary from one that is simply too rare for its variants to appear — “In order to verify that the idiom's common characterization as a rigid idiom is not an epiphenomenon of its low frequency, we consulted a much larger corpus: enTenTen13 (Baroni et al. 2009), a 20 billion word English corpus, available on SketchEngine (Kilgarriff et al. 2004).”
mechanismidioms are cross-classified on two dimensions at once - figuration, whether a literal meaning can be pictured, and transparency, whether the route from that picture to the idiomatic meaning can be recovered - in place of the single decomposable/non-decomposable cut — “In this chapter we consider an alternative categorization of idioms. More precisely, we cross-classify idioms according to two dimensions: figuration and transparency. Figuration reflects the degree to which the idiom can be assigned a literal meaning. Transparency (or opacity) relates to how easy it is to recover the motivation for an idiom's use, or, in other words, to explain the relationship between its literal meaning and its idiomatic one.”
mechanismqueries are written in a corpus query language over morphological features - part of speech, lemma, clitic - combined with logical operators, which the authors call particularly important for casting a wide net over non-canonical word orders, discontinuous elements and morphological inflections — “SketchEngines's Corpus Query Language (CQL) provides a way of defining complex queries which target morphological features of words (e.g., POS, lemmas, clitics) and which make use of logical operators (and/or/not). These features are particularly important when our goal is to cast a wide net to retrieve variations in general, and in particular non-canonical word orders, discontinuous elements and various morphological inflections.”
mechanisma query wide enough to catch variants also returns results that are not instances of the idiom, so each result is inspected by hand to remove the false positives — “Nevertheless, a wide net comes at a cost; not all the retrieved results are necessarily instances of the idiom. Often, only a manual inspection of each result can weed out the false positives.”
mechanismthe endpoint is switched from frequency to existence: rather than how often each variation occurs, the study verifies that every type of variation occurs at least once for each idiom — “For this reason we do not present quantitative data with regard to the distribution of the canonical idiom and its variations. We did, however, verify the occurrence of all types of variation for each idiom.”
mechanismargument-structure variation is observable in Hebrew because it is carried by combining one consonantal root with different morphological templates — “Hebrew presents an interesting case in this respect, since argument structure variations are associated with the combination of one consonantal root with different morphological templates (Doron 2008; 2003).”
mechanismthe modifier question is split in two: modifications of all types count as variations, while it is the modifier's semantic SCOPE that can provide evidence about whether idiom parts are assigned individual idiomatic meanings — “The question of whether a modifier is internal or external relates to the issue of decomposability, but not to the question of flexibility. When idiom parts are syntactically modified in an idiom this is certainly an instance of variation; the idiom does not appear in its canonical form. Thus, modifications of all types are variations. The semantic scope of the modifier can provide evidence with respect to whether idiom parts are assigned individual idiomatic meanings or not.”

limitationThe generalisation rests on a limited set of Hebrew verbal idioms. — “Naturally, this generalization, which is based on our work on only a limited set of Hebrew verbal idioms, requires further investigation.”
limitationThe criterion under critique is described as impressionistic and circular: flexibility is taken as evidence for the very property meant to predict it. — “determining whether an idiom is decomposable or not is rather impressionistic, and is prone to circularity, where its flexibility is taken as evidence for its decomposability.”
limitationThe originating proposal supplied neither precise definitions of its two categories nor a procedure for telling them apart. — “Nunberg et al. (1994) do not provide precise definitions for the two categories, or a specific procedure for distinguishing between them.”
limitationAn independent corpus study classifying by paraphrase reported the boundary between the two categories as fuzzy. — “She classified those for which she found an appropriate paraphrase as decomposable. Nevertheless, she observed that the boundary between the two categories is fuzzy.”
limitationTwo psycholinguistic studies of the same construct disagree on whether speakers can apply it reliably. — “In contrast, Titone & Connine (1994) did not find reliable agreement regarding decomposability in their study. The authors suggest that grouping idioms into these categories may rely on a type of linguistic knowledge that is not easily accessed.”
limitationThe rigidity of the field's standard example is shown to depend on corpus size; variation appears only in a very large corpus. — “However, since this idiom is used relatively rarely and idiom variations in and of themselves are relatively infrequent, non-canonical instances of it and other infrequent idioms can only be empirically attested in very large corpora. This, we believe, is an important methodological finding, which at this point in time, with the availability of large annotated corpora, cannot be overlooked.”
limitationThe authors' own prediction about cranberry idioms was not borne out, and they say so. — “We were especially surprised by the behavior of the cranberry idioms. Our initial expectation was that the lack of transparency and figuration would render these idioms more rigid. Our corpus findings, however, reveal a different picture.”
limitationNeither proposed dimension accounts for idiom behaviour on its own; the paper attributes the effect to an interaction. — “Thus, we propose that neither transparency nor figuration alone can account for the behavior of idioms. Our findings suggest that there is an interaction between the two dimensions.”
