mechanismFigure-ground contextual modulation in V1, generated by recurrent feedback, is carried on past stimulus offset and thereby converts the sensory representation into a working-memory trace. — “We therefore propose that contextual modulation in the primary visual cortex is a correlate of the process that forms a bridge between sensory activity and working memory.”
mechanismTwo processes act on V1 during the delay in opposite directions: one suppresses overall firing while a second preserves the figure-versus-ground difference. — “Thus, whereas one mechanism suppresses overall activity in V1 during the delay, another mechanism seems to be able to maintain the difference in figure versus ground signals.”
relation — “Thus, whereas one mechanism suppresses overall activity in V1 during the delay, another mechanism seems to be able to maintain the difference in figure versus ground signals. In that sense, the maintained activity is different from delay-period activity recorded in temporal (13) or prefrontal (14) cortex. This difference may also explain why in functional magnetic resonance imaging studies, very little delay activation is found in early visual areas (15).”

The distractor condition supplies the missing half: modulation persists when the figure is the target and decays when the same figure is the distractor, so 'the continuation of modulation is NOT A PASSIVE PROCESS BUT IS RELATED TO AN ACTIVE STORAGE of information needed for the animal's goal'. limitationThe signal recorded in V1 is stated to depend on feedback from higher areas, so the site of the recording is not the site of the process. — “Contextual modulation in V1 depends on feedback from higher visual areas (7–9), which implies that it is a specific correlate of recurrent processing”
limitationThe early figure-ground signal is the same whether the animal later remembers or forgets; only the late persistence separates them. — “Thus, for correct trials as well as for incorrect trials, the figure is equally well segregated from the ground, as indicated by the presence of contextual modulation in the first 250 ms after stimulus onset”
limitationThe memory signal decays with the length of the delay, so its strength depends on how long the animal is asked to wait. — “This indicates that the strength of contextual modulation is related to the delay period, where modulation becomes weaker for longer delays”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingAfter the figure was removed from the screen, V1 responses to the figure stayed stronger than responses to the ground throughout the delay, so figure-ground contextual modulation continues in the absence of the stimulus. — “During the delay period, the figure response remained stronger than the ground response (Fig. 2B) (P , 1024 for all delay periods).”
findingThe strength of the maintained figure-ground modulation shrinks as the required memory delay gets longer. — “This indicates that the strength of contextual modulation is related to the delay period, where modulation becomes weaker for longer delays.”
findingCorrect and incorrect trials did not differ in the initial figure-ground signal, so the difference between them lies only in whether the signal was maintained. — “Thus, for correct trials as well as for incorrect trials, the figure is equally well segregated from the ground, as indicated by the presence of contextual modulation in the first 250 ms after stimulus onset.”
findingIn the late part of the delay, contextual modulation vanished entirely on trials where the monkey later made the wrong saccade, while it continued on trials answered correctly. — “The decrease of modulation was stronger for incorrect trials, and contextual modulation for the incorrect trials disappeared completely in the late part of the delay period (Fig. 3, B and D), whereas modulation for the correct trials continued (Fig. 3, A and C).”
findingModulation was maintained only when the remembered figure was the behaviourally relevant target and fell away when the same figure was a distractor, showing that the maintenance is an active, goal-dependent storage rather than a passive after-effect. — “These results indicate that the continuation of modulation is not a passive process but is related to an active storage of information needed for the animal’s goal.”
findingDuring the delay V1 shows two dissociable effects at once: overall firing is suppressed while the figure-versus-ground difference is preserved. — “Thus, whereas one mechanism suppresses overall activity in V1 during the delay, another mechanism seems to be able to maintain the difference in figure versus ground signals.”
findingThe maintained signal cannot be visual persistence, because it lasts up to about 2 s whereas visual persistence for such stimuli lasts only tens of milliseconds, and it survives replacement of the display. — “It is highly unlikely that the maintained activity is a neural correlate of visual persistence, i.e., the lasting visibility of a stimulus after its physical disappearance.”
findingThe monkeys detected the figure well overall but their performance dropped as the delay lengthened, confirming the task taxes short-term memory. — “Detection of the figure was high and declined for longer delay periods (Fig. 2A), indicating that the task requires short-term memory processes.”
findingThe same continued modulation appeared with a static orientation-defined texture figure followed by a mask, so persistence is not an artefact of the motion stimulus. — “In a control experiment, we observed the same phenomenon when a static, rather than a moving, stimulus was used in the same delayed-response task.”
