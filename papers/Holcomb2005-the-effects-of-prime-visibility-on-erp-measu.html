phenomenonmasked priming and the N400— “In these studies, the prime stimulus is presented very briefly and is then immediately obscured by either a pattern mask (e.g., a series of letters or symbols occupying the same location on the screen as the prime) or the target word itself.”
claim-typeexistence— “Taken together, these findings provide strong evidence that the N400 repetition effects at 40 ms in this experiment were due in large part to automatic unconscious processes and were not strictly the result of conscious leakage from consciously perceived primes.”
method — evidence— “In two experiments, the effect of the duration (40, 80 and 120 ms) of pattern masked prime words on subsequent target word processing was measured using event-related potentials.”
mechanismForm information is extracted from the prime automatically while semantic extraction consumes capacity-limited attentional resources, so prime visibility gates semantic but not repetition priming— “The complete pattern of priming effects obtained in the present study can be explained if one assumes that form information can be extracted automatically (i.e., without attention-demanding resources), while the extraction of semantic information is either not automatic at all and depends on the allocation of capacity-limited resources or…
mechanismRepetition priming works by pre-activating shared representations whereas semantic priming works by establishing post-target connections between related representations, the latter requiring attention— “One way to avoid this relatively ad-hoc assumption would be to argue that the critical difference between repetition and semantic priming is that repetition priming is driven primarily by the pre-activation of representations that are shared by prime and target, while semantic priming involves, to a greater extent, establishing connections…
mechanismThe N400 indexes a resonance process at a form–meaning interface in which prime-derived form or meaning information helps the lexical processor settle on one form–meaning association— “Improving the processing of either form or meaning will help the lexical processor settle on a single form–meaning association via a resonance process involving the activation of similar form and similar meaning representations.”
contradicts Brown and Hagoort— “The pattern of results observed in these experiments would seem to call into question the interpretation of the functional significance of the N400 offered by Brown and Hagoort [ 2 ] and others who have maintained that the N400 is exclusively sensitive to post-lexical semantic processes.”
contradicts Deacon et al. and Kiefer et al. reports of unconscious masked semantic priming— “Moreover, these findings draw into question previous reports of masked semantic priming that have claimed that the effects are due to unconsciously processed primes [ 6 , 14 , 15 ].”
replicates Misra and Holcomb; Schnyer et al.— “Like several previous studies [ 21 , 31 ], robust effects of repetition in the region of the N400 component were found for target words preceded by very briefly displayed masked prime words.”
extends Misra and Holcomb— “One limitation with the Misra and Holcomb [ 21 ] study was that their unmasked (identifiable) and masked (unidentifiable) priming data for immediate repetitions (i.e., the trials with the largest effects) were collected in different experiments in different participants.”
senseTHE DISSOCIATION IS MADE OF CORRELATIONS, NOT OF MEANS, AND THAT IS THE DESIGN. One measure (the N400), one design, two kinds of priming, and the question is whether each effect's size tracks how well the individual participant could see the prime: `unlike repetition priming in Experiment 1, for semantic priming in Experiment 2, VISIBILITY OF PRIMES AT 40 MS ... PREDICTED THE N400 EFFECT`. Individual differences are the instrument - a median split on d-prime - so the NULL in Experiment 1 is the informative half, not the weak one. It is also the paper's defence against its own worst confound: if repetition priming ran on conscious leakage, the participants who leaked more would prime more, and they do not— “So, unlike repetition priming in Experiment 1, for semantic priming in Experiment 2, visibility of primes at 40 ms (as measured by button presses to probe items) predicted the N400 effect for targets following very briefly presented primes”
senseTHE AUTOMATIC/CONTROLLED LINE MOVES OFF THE REPRESENTATION AND ONTO THE MECHANISM: `IT IS NOT THE TYPE OF REPRESENTATION (form or meaning) that determines the automaticity of priming effects, BUT RATHER THE TYPE OF MECHANISM utilized (pre-activation versus establishing connections).` The older account - form is automatic, meaning is not - would have to say the repetition effect is mostly form, and the authors call that assumption AD HOC in their own words rather than defending it. What replaces it is a distinction between raising the activation of a shared representation and building a link between two different ones, and only the second needs attention— “One way to avoid this relatively ad-hoc assumption would be to argue that the critical difference between repetition and semantic priming is that repetition priming is driven primarily by the pre-activation of representations that are shared by prime and target, while semantic priming involves, to a greater extent, establishing connections…
senseTHEY NAME THE RESULT THAT WOULD REFUTE THEM, which almost nothing else in this corpus does: `finding evidence of N400 priming effects on masked TARGETS would strengthen the case for the N400 being a direct reflection of automatic processing and WOULD FORCE THOSE OF US WHO HAVE ASSUMED that it reflects a post-lexical stage of comprehension TO RETHINK OUR POSITION.` A named observation, a design that would produce it (mask the target instead of the prime), and the authors' own position placed at risk by it. The corpus holds many predictions; this is a stated disconfirmer— “On the other hand, finding evidence of N400 priming effects on masked targets would strengthen the case for the N400 being a direct reflection of automatic processing and would force those of us who have assumed that it reflects a post-lexical stage of comprehension to rethink our position”
senseA SECOND, SHARPER TEST IS SPECIFIED WITH ITS DISCRIMINATING PREDICTION: translation equivalents in bilinguals (arbre-TREE). `Modulation of the N400 by priming with translation equivalents SHOULD NOT BE SENSITIVE TO PRIME VISIBILITY since the same semantic representation would be PRE-ACTIVATED by the translation equivalent in the other language.` That separates the two accounts cleanly - a translation equivalent is semantically related but shares no form, so the form-versus-meaning account predicts it behaves like semantic priming while the pre-activation account predicts it behaves like repetition priming. The prediction is stated in the direction that would embarrass the authors' preferred story if it failed— “Modulation of the N400 by priming with translation equivalents should not be sensitive to prime visibility since the same semantic representation would be pre-activated by the translation equivalent in the other language”

NOR THE KIEFER STUDIES CONTROLLED PRIME PROCESSING and therefore it is not clear whether their participants allocated similar attentional resources to primes in their various conditions.` The go/no-go animal-probe task exists to hold attention on the prime epoch constant across durations, because otherwise a shorter prime is also a less-attended one and the two are confounded. So the corpus now holds Kiefer's gating account, this paper's methodological objection to the evidence for it, and both papers' own hedges— “Neither the Deacon et al. nor the Kiefer studies controlled prime processing and therefore it is not clear whether their participants allocated similar attentional resources to primes in their various conditions”
