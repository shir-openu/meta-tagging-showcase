phenomenonmasked priming and the N400
claim-typeexistence
mechanismForm information is extracted from the prime automatically while semantic extraction consumes capacity-limited attentional resources, so prime visibility gates semantic but not repetition priming — “The complete pattern of priming effects obtained in the present study can be explained if one assumes that form information can be extracted automatically (i.e., without attention-demanding resources), while the extraction of semantic information is either not automatic at all and depends on the allocation of capacity-limited resources or relies to a greater extent on capacity-limited resources.”
mechanismRepetition priming works by pre-activating shared representations whereas semantic priming works by establishing post-target connections between related representations, the latter requiring attention — “One way to avoid this relatively ad-hoc assumption would be to argue that the critical difference between repetition and semantic priming is that repetition priming is driven primarily by the pre-activation of representations that are shared by prime and target, while semantic priming involves, to a greater extent, establishing connections across related representations (e.g., as in theories such as compound-cue [ 26 ]).”
mechanismThe N400 indexes a resonance process at a form–meaning interface in which prime-derived form or meaning information helps the lexical processor settle on one form–meaning association — “Improving the processing of either form or meaning will help the lexical processor settle on a single form–meaning association via a resonance process involving the activation of similar form and similar meaning representations.”
relation — “The pattern of results observed in these experiments would seem to call into question the interpretation of the functional significance of the N400 offered by Brown and Hagoort [ 2 ] and others who have maintained that the N400 is exclusively sensitive to post-lexical semantic processes.”
relation — “Moreover, these findings draw into question previous reports of masked semantic priming that have claimed that the effects are due to unconsciously processed primes [ 6 , 14 , 15 ].”
relation — “Like several previous studies [ 21 , 31 ], robust effects of repetition in the region of the N400 component were found for target words preceded by very briefly displayed masked prime words.”
relation — “One limitation with the Misra and Holcomb [ 21 ] study was that their unmasked (identifiable) and masked (unidentifiable) priming data for immediate repetitions (i.e., the trials with the largest effects) were collected in different experiments in different participants.”
differs inrepetition priming → semantic priming — WHETHER THE EFFECT TRACKS HOW WELL THE PRIME WAS SEEN. Semantic priming correlates with prime visibility across participants; repetition priming does not. Same measure, same design, so the difference is in the priming and not in the instrument - and on the authors' account it is a difference of MECHANISM (pre-activation versus establishing connections) rather than of representation — “prime visibility correlated highly with semantic priming and not with repetition priming”
effect directionpre-activation → repetition priming — increases — “repetition priming is driven primarily by the pre-activation of representations that are shared by prime and target”
replicationcontested
limitationThe 40 ms condition is acknowledged to be partly contaminated by trials on which the prime was consciously perceived. — “We acknowledge that our results in the 40 ms condition are undoubtedly contaminated to some degree by conscious leakage (18% of masked animal probes were correctly detected, suggesting that participants could sometimes consciously process primes at 40 ms).”
limitationConscious leakage can never be fully eliminated by shortening prime duration. — “In fact, we believe it is impossible to completely eliminate this possibility, as to do so would most likely require the utilization of primes of such short duration that all priming effects would likely be precluded (no one argues that there is a lower limit past which no perceptual processing can take place!).”
limitationThe data do not show that the process the N400 reflects is itself exclusively automatic, only that it is sensitive to automatic priming. — “It is important to note, however, that contrary to the claims of Deacon and others, neither we (nor they) have demonstrated that the process directly reflected by the N400 is exclusively automatic.”
limitationThe columnar analysis strategy multiplies the number of statistical comparisons. — “Although this approach does increase the number of comparisons, this is offset by both a more complete description of the data and by a cautious interpretation of analyses where only a single column produces a significant effect.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingRobust N400 repetition priming appeared even for primes displayed too briefly to be identified. — “Like several previous studies [ 21 , 31 ], robust effects of repetition in the region of the N400 component were found for target words preceded by very briefly displayed masked prime words.”
findingFor repetition priming, individual prime visibility was unrelated to the size of the N400 effect at the shortest prime duration, arguing against conscious leakage as its source. — “Using several approaches, we found a lack of any apparent relationship between prime visibility and the size of the N400 repetition effect at short prime durations which suggest that conscious leakage had a minimal impact on priming at 40 ms.”
findingFor semantic priming, by contrast, prime visibility at 40 ms did predict the size of the N400 effect. — “So, unlike repetition priming in Experiment 1, for semantic priming in Experiment 2, visibility of primes at 40 ms (as measured by button presses to probe items) predicted the N400 effect for targets following very briefly presented primes.”
findingDirect between-experiment comparison showed repetition priming produced a larger N400 effect than semantic priming. — “Specifically, repetition priming (Experiment 1) produced a larger overall priming effect than semantic priming (Experiment 2).”
findingNo post-N400 late positivity effect of repetition was found, even for clearly visible primes, unlike earlier unmasked studies. — “Unlike Misra and Holcomb [ 21 ] and a number of other previous unmasked ERP repetition priming studies (e.g., [ 24 , 27 – 30 ]), there was no clear evidence of an effect on the post-N400 late positivity.”
findingThe authors conclude that previously reported masked semantic priming effects were plausibly driven by conscious leakage on a subset of trials rather than by unconscious processing. — “A more likely explanation in light of the current findings is that these previous reports obtained their semantic priming at short masked prime durations because of conscious leakage on some percentage of trials.”
quantityn = 22 (18 analysed) — participants recruited for Experiment 1 (four later excluded) — “Twenty-two right-handed, native English speaking Tufts University students (10 male, mean age = 19.75) received $20 compensation for participation in this experiment.”
quantity63% vs. an expected 23% — size of the 40 ms N400 repetition priming effect relative to the 120 ms effect, versus what conscious leakage alone predicts — “Fifth, across all 18 subjects, the size of the N400 priming effect in the 40 ms condition was 63% of the effect in the 120 ms condition across the midline sites, but based on probe categorization performance, the priming effect at 40 ms should have been only 23% of that at 120 ms.”
quantity42% vs. an expected 25% — same comparison for semantic priming in Experiment 2, where the gap was not significant — “Across all 16 subjects, the size of the N400 priming effect in the 40 ms condition was 42% of the effect in the 120 ms condition across the midline sites, but based on probe categorization performance the priming effect at 40 ms should have been 25% of that at 120 ms.”
quantityr 2 = 0.434, P = 0.006 — relationship between 40 ms prime visibility (d-prime) and the semantic N400 relatedness effect at Cz — “This possibility was supported by the existence of a significant relationship between 40 ms d-prime values and the N400 relatedness effect at Cz ( r 2 = 0.434, P = 0.006).”
quantity18% — proportion of masked animal probes detected at 40 ms, the authors' estimate of conscious leakage — “We acknowledge that our results in the 40 ms condition are undoubtedly contaminated to some degree by conscious leakage (18% of masked animal probes were correctly detected, suggesting that participants could sometimes consciously process primes at 40 ms).”
quantity~1 µV (vs. ~4 µV previously) — difference in N400 repetition priming between masked and visible primes in this study compared with Misra and Holcomb — “Misra and Holcomb [ 21 ] found differences of approximately 4 µV between the two types of priming while the difference in the current study was closer to 1 µV averaged across sites.”
variableprime duration (40, 80, 120 ms) (independent) — “In addition to the two levels of repetition (repeated/unrepeated), there was another factor of prime duration (40 ms, 80 ms and 120 ms), which resulted in six types of critical trials (45 pairs/condition—see Table 1 ).”
variablerepetition (repeated vs. unrepeated target) (independent) — “Separate analyses of variance (ANOVAs) were performed using the within-subject factors of prime duration (short, medium and long) and repetition (repeated, unrepeated)”
variablerelatedness (semantically related vs. unrelated target) (independent) — “As in Experiment 1, there were two experimental variables: relatedness (related/unrelated) and prime duration (40 ms, 80 ms and 120 ms), which resulted in six types of critical trials of 40 pairs/condition (see Table 3 ).”
variablemean target ERP amplitude in three latency windows (N400) (dependent) — “Mean target amplitudes for the same three latency windows used by Misra and Holcomb [ 21 ] were used to quantify the target ERPs in this experiment: 150–300 ms post-target onset, 300–500 ms post-target onset and 500–750 ms post-target onset.”
variableprime visibility group (high vs. low 40 ms d-prime) (moderator) — “To help determine if prime visibility accounted for any of the ERP results (see below), participants were subdivided into two groups based on a median split of their d-prime scores in the 40 ms prime duration condition.”
variableprime–target SOA held constant at 500 ms (control) — “with the variable post-mask blank screen designed to keep the prime–target SOA constant at 500 ms”
definitionmasked priming — “In these studies, the prime stimulus is presented very briefly and is then immediately obscured by either a pattern mask (e.g., a series of letters or symbols occupying the same location on the screen as the prime) or the target word itself.”
definitionconscious leakage — “One question that dogs all subliminal priming studies is the issue of whether the obtained effects may be due to residual conscious processing of the primes on some percentage of the trials (a phenomena we will call “conscious leakage”) [ 4 ].”
definitionprime / target — “The first item, or “prime”, serves to establish some type of context and the second item, or “target”, is usually manipulated so that it either fits or does not fit into the prime context.”
