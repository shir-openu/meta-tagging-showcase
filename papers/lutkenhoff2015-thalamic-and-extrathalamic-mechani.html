claim-typemechanism
mechanismWillful behaviour and arousal are supported by separate circuits, so thalamic damage removes organised willful behaviour while extrathalamic (basal ganglia) damage removes behavioural and electrocortical arousal — “At a circuit level, the implication of our findings is that, at least in the context of DOC, different aspects of consciousness, including willful behavior and arousal, might be mediated by thalamic and extrathalamic mechanisms, respectively.”
mechanismLoss of corticopetal thalamo-prefrontal projections (the mesocircuit) removes the drive that sustains organised behaviour and integrates information across cortex — “On the one hand, the negative association between motor/communication scores and atrophy along the anterior and dorsomedial regions of thalamus is consistent with the mesocircuit theory according to which corticopetal projections from thalamus to prefrontal cortex are crucial for sustaining organized behavior26 and integrating information across different regions of cortex.29”
mechanismPartial basal forebrain atrophy spares the coma-inducing threshold but impairs the fast cortical disinhibition that transiently amplifies sensory processing — “comalike state seen in animal models upon complete lesion of this region,18 but might nonetheless have been sufficient to affect the mechanisms of fast cortical disinhibition.”
relation — “On the one hand, the negative association between motor/communication scores and atrophy along the anterior and dorsomedial regions of thalamus is consistent with the mesocircuit theory according to which corticopetal projections from thalamus to prefrontal cortex are crucial for sustaining organized behavior26 and integrating information across different regions of cortex.29”
relation — “Third, paralleling postmortem examinations,50 we have also shown in vivo that patients suffering from nontraumatic brain injury exhibit a more widespread and left-lateralized degree of thalamic atrophy, consistent with the well-known poorer prognosis associated with nontraumatic etiology.1”
replicationuntested
limitationStructural neuroimaging is descriptive, so the findings cannot be given a causal interpretation — “Furthermore, the use of structural neuroimaging, a descriptive methodology, to pinpoint neuroanatomical components of consciousness, as measured with standard clinical methods, does not allow a causal interpretation of our findings.”
limitationThe atlas-based segmentation relies on priors estimated mostly from healthy volunteers — “Second, the atlas-based component of our approach is both an asset, allowing improved estimation and segmentation of individual regions, and a liability, given its reliance on priors estimated mostly from healthy volunteers.”
limitationThe proposed model is an approximation that omits corticosubcortical connectivity, temporal dynamics, neurotransmitter action and further arousal-related regions — “Third, it should also be noted that our proposal, as well as the mesocircuit model of DOC,7 are to be understood as approximations that do not take into full account the extent of corticosubcortical connectivity, temporal dynamics,56 the multiple mechanisms of action of neurotransmitters, and a number of additional regions known to be important for the regulation of arousal and cortical function.34”
limitationAdditional clinical factors that shape a given patient's atrophy pattern could not be addressed and would need larger samples — “Nonetheless, it must be recognized that there are a number of additional factors that might well play an important role in determining the specific pattern of atrophy observed in a given patient that we could not address (eg, whether pharmacologic coma was used, the presence of some patients undergoing steroid administration) and that will likely require even larger samples than the present one to allow full statistical evaluation.”
limitationA structure the literature calls crucial could not be assessed by the technique used. — “parabrachial/precoeruleus complex, which has been previously shown to be crucial for this process (but could not be assessed with the present technique”
limitationThe outcome analysed is a set of rotated components, not the clinical subscales themselves. — “However, because of the correlations between CRS-R subscales, and the negative effects of excessive multicollinearity on regression analysis,20 we performed a data reduction (principal component analysis”
limitationOne region entered the analysis on the strength of evidence from animal models. — “This region was added to the analysis because of its causal role in the maintenance of cortical (and behavioral) arousal in animal models”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingCompared with healthy volunteers, patients with disorders of consciousness showed significant atrophy in every subcortical structure examined. — “Compared to healthy volunteers (ie, analysis #1), patients exhibited significant atrophy across all examined regions.”
findingThe behaviourally defined diagnostic categories were not distinguishable by subcortical pathology: vegetative-state, MCS-minus and MCS-plus patients did not differ from one another in any region. — “No significant differences were detected in the comparison of VS versus MCS2 patients, VS versus MCS1 patients, and MCS2 versus MCS1 patients.”
findingTotal CRS-R score varied inversely with atrophy in the left globus pallidus, left putamen and small ventromedial parts of the basal forebrain. — “As shown in Figure 3A, the CRS-R total score (analysis #2) correlated inversely with atrophy in large portions of the left globus pallidus (76% of vertices), left putamen (85% of vertices), and small sections of the bilateral ventromedial basal forebrain (8% and 2% of right and left vertices, respectively; Fig 4C).”
findingMotor and communication ability tracked thalamic pathology: the Motor-Communication component correlated negatively with atrophy in bilateral anterior and dorsomedial thalamus, left putamen and right hippocampus. — “Motor–Communication component scores exhibited a significant negative correlation with the degree of atrophy in bilateral anterior and dorsomedial thalamus (61% and 27% of vertices in the left and right hemisphere, respectively), small segments of left putamen (36% of vertices), and right medial and posterolateral hippocampus (25% of vertices; see Fig 3C).”
findingArousal tracked basal ganglia rather than thalamic pathology: the Arousal component correlated negatively with atrophy in bilateral putamen and globus pallidus. — “The Arousal component was negatively associated with extensive atrophy in bilateral putamen (69% and 63% in left and right hemisphere, respectively), globus pallidus (92% and 16% of vertices), and a small segment of left posterior hippocampus (3% of vertices; see Fig 3D).”
findingArousal showed no association with thalamic atrophy, contrary to the traditional view of thalamic intralaminar nuclei as the arousal-critical structure. — “More surprising is the absence of association between patients’ level of arousal and thalamic atrophy.”
findingSensory (audiovisual) responsiveness was associated specifically with atrophy of small ventromedial segments of the basal forebrain. — “The Audiovisual component was negatively associated with the degree of atrophy in small bilateral ventromedial segments of the basal forebrain (3% and 5% of vertices in the left and right hemisphere, respectively; see Fig 4D).”
findingPatients with nontraumatic injury had more widespread and left-lateralised thalamic atrophy than patients with traumatic injury, mirroring the worse prognosis of nontraumatic aetiology. — “Third, paralleling postmortem examinations,50 we have also shown in vivo that patients suffering from nontraumatic brain injury exhibit a more widespread and left-lateralized degree of thalamic atrophy, consistent with the well-known poorer prognosis associated with nontraumatic etiology.1”
findingThe only clinical-group contrast that reached significance was between the two extremes of the spectrum, with vegetative-state patients showing more left putamen, globus pallidus and right hippocampal atrophy than patients emerging from MCS. — “Consistent with the result reported for total CRS-R, VS patients exhibited, as compared to eMCS, significant atrophy in left putamen (85% of vertices) and globus pallidus (69% of vertices), as well as in small sections in medial and anterior right hippocampus (6% of vertices).”
