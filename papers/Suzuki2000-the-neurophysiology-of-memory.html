phenomenonmedial temporal lobe neuronal correlates of recognition and associative memory
claim-typeexistence
mechanismBecause the hippocampus receives polymodal rather than unimodal input, it is positioned to combine the cortex's stimulus-selective signals into complex conjunctions or to abstract a general output signal from them, which is why its mnemonic code is abstracted rather than stimulus-specific — “The hippocampus is in a privileged position to either combine the stimulus-selective mnemonic information in cortex into more complex conjunctions or to abstract, from the stimulus-selective information, a more general “output” signal.”
mechanismThe occasional stimulus-selective, cortex-type recognition signals seen in the hippocampus are inherited through the direct anatomical projections from perirhinal and entorhinal cortex to area CA1 — “These “cortexlike” recognition signals may be conveyed via the direct projections from both the perirhinal and the entorhinal cortices to area CA1.”
mechanismAt long delays interference builds up, so contextual/episodic conjunctive information becomes the useful cue for recognition, which is why hippocampal damage bites hardest at long delays — “At longer delay intervals, when interference can play a detrimental role, information about the context or episode in which the stimulus was seen could be particularly helpful to successful performance.”
mechanismCortical stimulus-stimulus association works by a cue acquiring the capacity to generate the neural representation of the stimulus it was paired with, producing pair-coding and pair-recall firing — “Thus, within these cortical areas, stimulus-stimulus associations may be represented by the acquired capacity of a stimulus to generate the neural representation of another stimulus with which it has been associated.”
relation — “Yet the evidence showing differences between these areas suggests that parahippocampal cortical neurons may participate in associative memory by developing representations of complex perceptual stimuli that can be activated by their associates (i.e., pair-coding neurons of Sakai and Miyashita52), whereas neurons of the hippocampal formation participate in associative memory by representing conjunctions of independent perceptual stimuli as well as behavioral events (i.e., conjunctive coding neurons of Wood et al.37).”
effect directionABBA version → match enhancement — increases — “the proportion of neurons exhibiting match enhancement was substantially higher”
replicationuntested
limitationThe parahippocampal/postrhinal cortex is excluded from the review because too little is known about its mnemonic activity. — “Much less is known about the patterns of mnemonic activity in the parahippocampal/postrhinal cortex, and therefore this region will not be discussed.”
limitationNo direct comparison exists that would identify which testing conditions produce similar versus different coding in cortex and hippocampus. — “A compelling direct comparison that reveals the critical aspects of these different testing conditions has yet to be made.”
limitationThe scope of associative-memory signals across the medial temporal lobe is not yet established. — “Additional comparative studies are needed to determine the full scope of neural signals underlying associative memory across the medial temporal lobe.”
limitationA specific empirical gap: intervening stimuli have not been tested against selective delay activity in rat perirhinal/entorhinal cortex. — “The effect of intervening stimuli on the selective delay activity in the perirhinal or entorhinal cortices of rats has not been examined.”
limitationThe anatomy is used to support two opposite expectations, both stated in the same passage. — “On the one hand, the fact that each of these structures differs markedly in the amount and content of convergent cortical input suggests that each area may be able to process or combine information to encode, store, or retrieve memory in different ways. This view suggests that each area may be characterized by distinct patterns of mnemonic activity”
limitationHow many neurons show the effect depends on which version of the task was run. — “However, the proportion of neurons exhibiting match enhancement was substantially higher during performance of the ABBA version of the task compared to the standard version”
limitationThe neuropsychological literature is stated to give only indirect insight into neural activity. — “neuropsychological studies on braindamaged patients or experimental animals can provide only indirect insights into the specific patterns of neural activity that allow us to form and retain new long-term declarative memories”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingAcross humans, monkeys and rats the patterns of memory-related firing in the medial temporal lobe are largely the same, so mnemonic signals are conserved across species. — “These cross-species comparisons show that the patterns of mnemonic activity observed throughout the medial temporal lobe are largely conserved across species.”
findingPerirhinal and entorhinal neurons signal recognition with stimulus-selective suppressed or enhanced responses to a repeated item, whereas hippocampal neurons signal an abstracted match/nonmatch outcome that generalises over all stimuli. — “In contrast to the stimulus-selective recognition signals observed in cortex, neurons of the hippocampal formation convey abstracted recognition memory signals.”
findingEntorhinal selective delay activity survives multiple intervening test stimuli while perirhinal delay activity is abolished by even one intervening stimulus, dissociating the two cortical areas. — “By contrast, selective delay activity in the perirhinal cortex was disrupted by the presentation of even a single intervening stimulus.”
findingMatch-suppression neurons suppress to any stimulus repetition including behaviourally irrelevant repeats, whereas match-enhancement neurons respond only when the behaviourally relevant (target) stimulus repeats. — “By contrast, the match-enhancement neurons were enhanced only when the behaviorally relevant stimulus was repeated.”
findingMatch suppression is not sensory fatigue or habituation, because the suppression is abolished when the same stimulus reappears as the sample on the next trial. — “The match-suppression response cannot be explained by sensory fatigue or habituation, because the suppression disappears when the same stimulus is shown as a sample on the following trial.”
findingEntorhinal place cells keep the same spatial firing pattern when a rat is moved between a circular and a square environment, while hippocampal place cells almost always remap to independent patterns. — “By contrast, hippocampal neurons nearly always showed different and independent patterns of spatial activity in the two environments.”
findingLesion evidence dissociates the two regions by delay: entorhinal/perirhinal damage impairs recognition even at short delays, whereas hippocampus-limited damage impairs recognition most strongly at delays of many minutes to hours. — “In contrast to lesion studies involving the cortical medial temporal lobe areas, damage limited to the hippocampus produces the most striking recognition impairments when delay intervals are long (many minutes to hours)”
findingAbout a third to two fifths of human hippocampal and entorhinal neurons recorded at retrieval are modulated by whether the item is new or old, mostly by enhancing their response to previously seen stimuli. — “The responses of 35% (10 of 29) of cells recorded in the human hippocampus and 40% (8 of 20) of cells recorded in the entorhinal cortex in the retrieval phase were influenced by whether a stimulus was new or old.”
findingCortical and hippocampal areas contribute different forms of associative coding: perirhinal/inferotemporal pair-coding and pair-recall neurons link one stimulus to its learned associate, while hippocampal neurons code conjunctions of stimulus with place and behavioural event. — ““Pair-recall” neurons increased their firing rate during the delay period following presentation of the associate of the optimal cue, leading up to a robust response to that cue.”
quantity47% ... 94% — proportion of visually responsive monkey entorhinal (47%) versus perirhinal (94%) neurons that respond selectively to particular visual stimuli — “For example, in monkeys, 47% of the visually responsive neurons in the entorhinal cortex18 and 94% of the visually responsive neurons in the perirhinal cortex19 respond selectively to particular visual stimuli.”
quantity35% ... 11% — proportion of sampled rat lateral entorhinal (35%) versus perirhinal (11%) neurons with odor-specific firing — “In rats, 35% of the sampled lateral entorhinal neurons and 11% of the sampled perirhinal neurons exhibit odor-specific firing properties.”
quantity15 of 120 or 12.5% — share of responsive hippocampal neurons that differentiated match from nonmatch presentations — “some neurons in this region respond differentially to stimuli when they are shown as matches compared to when the same stimuli are shown as nonmatches (15 of 120 or 12.5% of the responsive neurons)”
quantity13 of 91 or 14% — share of responsive rat hippocampal neurons carrying an abstracted match-nonmatch signal independent of odor identity — “some neurons signaled the match-nonmatch status of an odor irrespective of the odor identity (“abstracted” match-nonmatch signal; 13 of 91 or 14% of the responsive neurons)”
quantity18 of 91 or 20% ... 4 of 91 or 4% — share of responsive hippocampal neurons coding match-nonmatch status conjointly with position (20%), or with both position and odor identity (4%) — “A second subset of neurons in this task signaled the abstracted match-nonmatch signals in conjunction either with the position in which it occurred (18 of 91 or 20% of the responsive neurons) or with both position as well as odor identity (4 of 91 or 4%)”
quantity24 hours — longest delay across which repetition suppression to a once-novel stimulus persists — “For some neurons, the firing rate is suppressed when the novel stimulus is repeated, even after a delay interval of 24 hours.”
quantity74 neurons in 3 regions — size of the human single-unit sample in the recognition study whose results matched the animal cortical pattern — “Despite the relatively small number of neurons examined (74 neurons in 3 regions), the overall pattern of activity was strikingly similar to the findings described for the perirhinal and entorhinal neurons in rats and monkeys.”
quantity24 — number of fractal stimulus patterns used in the paired-associate study of inferotemporal and perirhinal neurons — “examined responses of anterior inferotemporal and perirhinal neurons to 24 fractal stimulus patterns”
