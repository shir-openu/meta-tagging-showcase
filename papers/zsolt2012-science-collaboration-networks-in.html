limitationThe paper states it cannot readily explain why Serbian sociologists do not co-author. — “The question why the Serbian sociologists do not cooperate during the writing of scientific works cannot be answered easily.”
limitationThe paper notes that its comparison is between scientific communities of very different sizes, so the author/coauthor distributions are not directly comparable. — “Considering the fact that the issue is about comparing scientific communities of various sizes, the samples of such a distribution of authors’ and coauthors’ works are quite different.”
limitationThe field measures a thing it rarely defines: the paper states that very few works define what science collaboration is. — “However, there are very few works which define what science collaboration is.”
limitationThe term rests on social convention, and what one researcher counts as collaboration another counts as a loose grouping. — “Katz and Martin (1997) concluded that the term of “collaboration” is hard to define, because it is in great measure a thing of social conventions between scientists. That which by some would be considered as collaboration, others could define as loose grouping or collection of informal connections”
limitationEach study narrows the term to one aspect, so the operational definitions differ between the works being compared. — “Researchers of the scientific networks more commonly opt for just one of its aspects and therefore narrow their operational definitions of science collaboration networks”

findingAcross three Serbian sociology journals for 2000-2010, single-authored papers overwhelmingly dominate: 89.1% of published texts against only 10.9% coauthored. — “The proportion of author works in total number of published texts is 89.1%, whereas the proportion of coauthor works is merely 10.9%.”
findingAmong the coauthored papers, collaboration between colleagues in the same institution is twice as frequent as collaboration across institutions. — “The conclusion is that the participation of coauthors who are employed in the same institutions is twice as higher than the participation of the authors who cooperate with the colleagues from the other scientific institutions (see table 1).”
findingThe authors conclude from these frequencies that coauthorship networks among Serbian sociologists are underdeveloped. — “Based on this insight, we can conclude that the scientific networks of coauthors’ works of sociologists in Serbia are not quite developed.”
findingSerbian sociologists at the start of the 21st century collaborated three times less than the sociologists in Moody's international data. — “Therefore, it does not come as a surprise that the cooperation of the Serbian sociologists at the beginning of the 21st century was three times smaller than the cooperation of the sociologists portrayed in Moody’s research.”
findingKronegger and colleagues, applying the same methodology as Moody, arrive at a different network structure for sociology, which the authors attribute to the size of the national scientific community and its dependence on ministry funding. — “Even though Kronegger and his associates used the same methodology as Moody did, we have seen that the results of their research point out different network structures which are realised by the sociologists.”
findingAlthough sociology is taught at three Serbian universities, none of their study programmes contains a course on the theory or analysis of social networks. — “there is not one subject which is dedicated to the theory and analysis of social networks in their study programmes.”
findingIn all four Slovenian disciplines examined, scientists preferentially form new coauthorships with the coauthors of their coauthors, closing triangles - a signature of small-world clustering within network borders. — “In all four disciplines, scientists are inclined to form new coauthorship connections with coauthors of their coauthors, which is irrefutable confirmation of high grouping inside network borders and a clear indicator of small-world network structures.”
