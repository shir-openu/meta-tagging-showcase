phenomenonscientific coauthorship networks in sociology— “Science collaboration networks occupy an important place in the analysis of social networks.”
claim-typeexistence— “Based on this insight, we can conclude that the scientific networks of coauthors’ works of sociologists in Serbia are not quite developed.”
method — evidence— “We have analysed coauthors’ works according to the criterion of belonging to the same or different scientific/educational institution.”
mechanism1990s isolation and economic collapse produced minimal science funding and minimal international contact, which suppressed the coauthorship that funding and contact would otherwise generate— “Among all else, it produced minimal investment in science, as well as hard and minimal international cooperation.”
mechanismprojects framed around very general themes let each associate work on his own issue without leaving his own profession, so participation never turns into joint problem-solving and hence not into coauthorship— “Problems related to the project are mostly chosen based on such general notion which could enable the project work of the associates who could tackle various issues, without the obligation to step out of their own profession.”
mechanismthe absence of distinguished scientists who gather younger associates leaves no nuclei around which collaboration networks could form— “In Serbian Sociology there are no distinguished scientists who tend to gather younger associates”
extends Moody (2004), The Structure of a Social Science Collaboration Network— “Therefore, it does not come as a surprise that the cooperation of the Serbian sociologists at the beginning of the 21st century was three times smaller than the cooperation of the sociologists portrayed in Moody’s research.”

for which uselimitation — any result about science collaboration networks: the paper states very few works define what science collaboration is, so studies of it need not be studies of the same thing— “However, there are very few works which define what science collaboration is.”
for which uselimitation — a comparison of collaboration rates between studies or communities: the term is hard to define because it is largely a matter of social conventions between scientists, and what some count as collaboration others would call a loose grouping or informal connection— “Katz and Martin (1997) concluded that the term of “collaboration” is hard to define, because it is in great measure a thing of social conventions between scientists. That which by some would be considered as collaboration, others could define as loose grouping or collection of informal connections”
for which uselimitation — a synthesis across collaboration-network studies: researchers commonly take one aspect only and narrow their operational definitions accordingly, so the definitions differ between the studies being combined— “Researchers of the scientific networks more commonly opt for just one of its aspects and therefore narrow their operational definitions of science collaboration networks”
for which uselimitation — a citation-based measure of influence: the same count aggregates ceremonial citation, a demonstration of knowing the literature, with diplomatic non-citation, the purposeful omission of authors from a rival group— “Cole (1970) speaks about “ceremonial citation” as a demonstration in literature knowing and “diplomatic non-citation” as purposeful non-citation of authors from rival group etc.”
