limitationThe commentary's own caution: without confidence ratings, better performance on match trials cannot be fully separated from the neural difference attributed to match — “Although the authors carefully considered and discounted such an effect, without confidence rating data, it is difficult to entirely exclude the possibility that subjects may have been more confident in making (correct) match, as opposed to mismatch, judgments.”
limitationThe central conclusion about maintenance rests on an absence of difference, which the commentary flags as a null result — “Although caution is advised in interpreting such a null finding, this result does suggest that persistent neural firing in the hippocampus does not occur during the delay period of short-term memory tasks, as is thought to occur in the entorhinal cortex.”

findingHippocampal activity measured during the sample (encoding) phase predicted whether the later recognition judgment would be correct. — “This revealed that hippocampal activity during the sample phase predicted successful recognition judgments in the test phase”
findingA subsequent-memory effect in the perirhinal cortex appeared only in the sample phase, consistent with that region encoding item-specific rather than associative information. — “Indeed, a subsequent memory correlation was observed in the perirhinal cortex selectively during the sample phase, in line with proposals that this neural region is critical for the encoding of item-specific information.”
findingHippocampal activity during the 11 s delay did not differ between later-correct and later-incorrect trials, arguing against persistent hippocampal firing as the maintenance mechanism. — “Interestingly, there were no significant differences in hippocampal activity as a function of accuracy during the delay period.”
findingOn correct trials, hippocampal activation was higher for match displays than for either type of mismatch display. — “The authors used a region-of-interest analysis to demonstrate that hippocampal activation during correct trials was greatest in relation to match displays (compared with mismatch-position and mismatch-swap displays).”
findingThe direction of the hippocampal match/mismatch response reverses when novelty is incidental rather than the object of an explicit recognition judgment, with activation then peaking for mismatch. — “Interestingly, when the novelty/familiarity of associative information is incidental to the task at hand (i.e., subjects are not required to make explicit recognition memory judgments), a qualitatively different pattern of findings has been observed with hippocampal activation maximal under conditions of mismatch rather than match”
findingThe hippocampus contributes to short-term memory for novel associations at encoding and retrieval but apparently not during active maintenance. — “These results suggest that the hippocampus plays an important role in the encoding and retrieval, but perhaps not the active maintenance, of novel associative information in short-term memory.”
findingFinding a hippocampal subsequent-memory effect at test as well as at sample excludes the alternative that sample-phase activity merely reflected encoding of the individual objects rather than the object-location associations. — “This finding rules out an otherwise problematic explanation that greater neural activity during the sample phase predicts subsequent success not through the encoding of object-location associative information, but rather the objects (e.g., drums, birdbath) themselves.”
