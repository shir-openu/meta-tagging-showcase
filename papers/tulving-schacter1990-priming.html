phenomenonpriming (implicit memory)— “Priming is a nonconscious form of human memory, which is concerned with perceptual identification of words and objects and which has only recently been recognized as separate from other forms of memory or memory systems.”
claim-typemechanism— “We believe that priming and perceptual identification are expressions of a single perceptual representation system (PRS), which exists separately from but interacts closely with other memory systems (10, 11).”
method — evidence— “The evidence we have reviewed converges on the PRS hypothesis-that is, priming reflects the enhancement of the neural computations and correlated cognitive operations of the perceptual representation system, PRS.”
mechanismpriming reflects a distinct perceptual representation system (PRS) - a pre-semantic, implicit memory that operates without conscious recollection, separate from but interacting with episodic and semantic memory— “Priming is a type of implicit memory; it does not involve explicit or conscious recollection of any previous experiences”
extends the authors' own earlier priming article (reference 9), which conjectured an as yet little understood memory system— “In an early article on priming, we conjectured that the effects we had observed might "reflect the operation of some ... as yet little understood memory system" other than semantic or episodic memory [p. 341 in (9)].”
builds-on Warrington and Weiskrantz's experiments on learning in densely amnesic patients (reference 4)— “One of the triggers for the study of priming turned out to be experiments by Warrington and Weiskrantz (4) showing that densely amnesic patients, who were severely impaired in their ability to remember recently seen information, exhibited near-normal learning when they were tested by methods that tapped what we now know is priming.”
contradicts processing theories of priming that treat memory as a unitary cognitive system (reference 49)— “These processing theories are usually based on a limited domain of data, such as cognitive psychology experiments with normal subjects.”
sense**THE PAPER THAT MADE PRIMING A MEMORY SYSTEM, AND IT SAYS HOW RECENT THAT WAS:** `Priming is a NONCONSCIOUS FORM OF HUMAN MEMORY, which is concerned with perceptual identification of words and objects and which has ONLY RECENTLY BEEN RECOGNIZED AS SEPARATE from other forms of memory or memory systems.` **Not an effect, not a task - a system.** Most of this corpus inherits that framing without arguing for it; this is where it was argued, and the `only recently` is the authors dating their own claim— “Priming is a nonconscious form of human memory, which is concerned with perceptual identification of words and objects and which has only recently been recognized as separate from other forms of memory or memory systems”
sense**AND THE SPLIT INSIDE THE WORD, MADE IN 1990 BY THE PEOPLE WHO OWNED IT:** `priming is an expression of a PERCEPTUAL REPRESENTATION SYSTEM that operates at a PRE-SEMANTIC level ... CONCEPTUAL PRIMING, however, seems to be based on the operations of SEMANTIC MEMORY.` **Two phenomena under one term, separated by naming the systems rather than the tasks.** This is Q-806's one-name-many-concepts problem being SOLVED inside a discipline - and it is worth the corpus noting that the solution was to split the referent and keep the word, which is exactly what soil science and psychology have not done with `priming effect`— “priming is an expression of a perceptual representation system that operates at a pre-semantic level; it emerges early in development, and access to it lacks the kind of flexibility characteristic of other cognitive memory systems. Conceptual priming, however, seems to be based on the operations of semantic memory”
sense**AND THE ARGUMENT FOR A NEW SYSTEM IS TAXONOMIC, NOT EXPERIMENTAL:** `Priming resembles PROCEDURAL memory in that it enhances perceptual skills. It also resembles SEMANTIC memory in that it involves cognitive representations of the world.` A new system is established by showing it sits between two accepted ones and belongs to neither. **That is why this paper is cited by records in this corpus that share no method with it** - the claim is about how to carve the field, and a carving can be adopted by anyone— “Priming resembles procedural memory in that it enhances perceptual skills. It also resembles semantic memory in that it involves cognitive representations of the world”
sense**AND THE UNAWARENESS IS OF THE MEMORY, NOT OF THE STIMULUS - WHICH IS A DIFFERENT CLAIM FROM EVERY MASKED-PRIMING RECORD HERE:** `A PERSON PERCEIVING A FAMILIAR OBJECT is not aware that what is perceived is AS MUCH AN EXPRESSION OF MEMORY AS IT IS OF PERCEPTION.` dehaene1998, Finkbeiner2011 and hesselmann2016 all work to make the STIMULUS unavailable to report. **Tulving and Schacter are saying the stimulus is seen perfectly well and its status as a memory is what goes unnoticed.** Two senses of `nonconscious priming` that the corpus has been filing under one heading— “A person perceiving a familiar object is not aware that what is perceived is as much an expression of memory as it is of perception”
controlthe materials built so that the target word could not be predicted either from the picture or from the first two words of the phrase — held — rules out the priming effect being ordinary predictability - if the target can be guessed from the context, a faster response measures guessing rather than implicit memory— “247 materials had been constructed in such a manner that the target was not predictable either from the picture or from the first two words of the phrase.”
