mechanismTHE EXPLANATION'S VOCABULARY IS NOT THE MODEL'S: an interpretable representation is defined separately from the features the classifier actually uses - presence or absence of a word, or of a contiguous patch of similar pixels - while the model may work over embeddings or a three-channel tensor — “Before we present the explanation system, it is important to distinguish between features and interpretable data representations. As mentioned before, interpretable explanations need to use a representation that is understandable to humans, regardless of the actual features used by the model. For example, a possible interpretable representation for text classification is a binary vector indicating the presence or absence of a word, even though the classifier may use more complex (and incomprehensible) features such as word embeddings. Likewise for image classification, an interpretable representation may be a binary vector indicating the “presence” or “absence” of a contiguous patch of similar pixels (a super-pixel), while the classifier may represent the image as a tensor with three color channels per pixel.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
mechanismINTERPRETABILITY IS DEFINED AS AUDIENCE-RELATIVE, not as a property of the model class: the authors state that a linear model, a gradient vector or an additive model MAY OR MAY NOT be interpretable, because the criterion has to take the user's limitations into account — “An essential criterion for explanations is that they must be interpretable, i.e., provide qualitative understanding between the input variables and the response. We note that interpretability must take into account the user’s limitations. Thus, a linear model [24], a gradient vector [2] or an additive model [6] may or may not be interpretable.”
mechanismand the audience-relativity is made concrete rather than left as a caveat: practitioners may be able to read a small Bayesian network where laymen are better served by a few weighted features — “Finally, we note that the notion of interpretability also depends on the target audience. Machine learning practitioners may be able to interpret small Bayesian networks, but laymen may be more comfortable with a small number of weighted features as an explanation.”
mechanismTHE IMPOSSIBILITY IS STATED BEFORE THE CRITERION IS WEAKENED: an explanation cannot be completely faithful unless it IS the complete description of the model, so the requirement imposed instead is that it be faithful in the vicinity of the instance being explained — “Although it is often impossible for an explanation to be completely faithful unless it is the complete description of the model itself, for an explanation to be meaningful it must at least be locally faithful, i.e. it must correspond to how the model behaves in the vicinity of the instance being predicted.”
mechanismAND THE IMPLICATION IS STATED TO RUN ONE WAY ONLY: local fidelity does not imply global fidelity - a feature that matters globally may not matter locally, and the reverse - while global fidelity would imply local, and globally faithful interpretable explanations are said to remain a challenge — “We note that local fidelity does not imply global fidelity: features that are globally important may not be important in the local context, and vice versa. While global fidelity would imply local fidelity, identifying globally faithful explanations that are interpretable remains a challenge for complex models.”
mechanismthe explainer is required to treat the model as a BLACK BOX, and the reason given is not only that current classifiers are uninterpretable but that model-agnosticism buys the ability to explain classifiers that do not exist yet

mechanismTHE TRADE-OFF IS PUT IN THE OBJECTIVE RATHER THAN LEFT TO JUDGEMENT: unfaithfulness in the neighbourhood is minimised while a separate measure of the explanation's own complexity is held low enough for a human to read, and the authors note the same formulation admits different explanation families, fidelity functions and complexity measures — “In order to ensure both interpretability and local fidelity, we must minimize L(f, g, πx) while having Ω(g) be low enough to be interpretable by humans. The explanation produced by LIME is obtained by the following: ξ(x) = argmin L(f, g, πx) + Ω(g) (1) g∈G This formulation can be used with different explanation families”
mechanismthe neighbourhood is explored BY SAMPLING rather than by assumption, precisely to keep the method model-agnostic: no assumption is made about the model, and the local loss is approximated by drawing samples weighted by proximity to the instance — “We want to minimize the locality-aware loss L(f, g, πx) without making any assumptions about f , since we want the explainer to be model-agnostic. Thus, in order to learn the local behavior of f as the interpretable inputs vary, we approximate L(f, g, πx) by drawing samples, weighted by πx.”
mechanismsamples are drawn both near the instance and FAR from it, weighted by proximity - which is what lets a globally complex model still be approximated locally, and what the authors say makes the method fairly robust to sampling noise — “The primary intuition behind LIME is presented in Figure 3, where we sample instances both in the vicinity of x (which have a high weight due to πx) and far away from x (low weight from πx). Even though the original model may be too complex to explain globally, LIME presents an explanation that is locally faithful (linear in this case), where the locality is captured by πx. It is worth noting that our method is fairly robust to sampling noise since the samples are weighted by”
mechanismA CLASSIFIER AT 94% HELD-OUT ACCURACY IS SHOWN TO BE UNTRUSTWORTHY BY ITS EXPLANATIONS: the words it relies on are said to have no connection to either class, and one of them appears in 22% of training examples with 99% of those in a single class - and removing the headers only substitutes the names of prolific posters, which would not generalise either — “Although this classifier achieves 94% held-out accuracy, and one would be tempted to trust it based on this, the explanation for an instance shows that predictions are made for quite arbitrary reasons (words “Posting”, “Host”, and “Re” have no connection to either Christianity or Atheism). The word “Posting” appears in 22% of examples in the training set, 99% of them in the class “Atheism”. Even if headers are removed, proper names of prolific posters in the original newsgroups are selected by the classifier, which would also not generalize.”
