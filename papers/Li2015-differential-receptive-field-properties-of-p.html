mechanismPV neurons pool a wider range of excitatory inputs and receive stronger subthreshold drive, so more of that input crosses spike threshold, producing broad, weakly selective spike tuning— “The broader/weaker frequency tuning of PV neurons was attributed to a broader range of synaptic inputs and stronger subthreshold responses elicited, which resulted in a higher efficiency in the conversion of input to output.”
mechanismSOM spiking is late mainly because the excitatory synaptic input itself arrives late, with longer synaptic integration adding to the delay— “Therefore, the delayed spiking of SOM neurons could be mainly attributed to their delayed synaptic input, while a longer integration time may also contribute to their late spiking.”
mechanismNon-selective local connectivity from excitatory cells onto inhibitory (especially PV) cells pools inputs with many different preferred frequencies onto one interneuron— “which would result in a pooling of excitatory inputs with a wide range of preferred frequencies in layer 2/3 inhibitory cells”
mechanismSOM neurons bypass the direct ascending (feedforward) pathway and are instead driven through slower horizontal circuits, which is why their input is delayed— “On the other hand, SOM neurons receive delayed excitatory input, suggesting that unlike PV and excitatory neurons, SOM neurons do not receive direct ascending inputs.”
mechanismPV cells pool excitatory input from neurons with a wide range of preferred frequencies because excitatory-to-PV connectivity is unselective— “On the other hand, connectivity between excitatory and inhibitory (in particular PV) neurons is much less selective ( Yoshimura and Callaway 2005 ; Hofer et al. 2011 ; Wilson et al. 2012 ), which would result in a pooling of excitatory inputs with a wide range of preferred frequencies in layer 2/3 inhibitory cells.”
mechanismPV cells also convert input to output more efficiently, so a broad input becomes an even broader spike tuning— “The broader/weaker frequency tuning of PV neurons was attributed to a broader range of synaptic inputs and stronger subthreshold responses elicited, which resulted in a higher efficiency in the conversion of input to output.”
mechanismPV cells are driven at the same time as excitatory cells, consistent with shared feedforward input from layer 4— “PV neurons receive their excitatory drive at the same time as excitatory neurons, and their spiking activity occurs before excitatory neurons. These results suggest that PV neurons, similar as excitatory cells, receive feedforward ascending inputs, likely from layer 4”
mechanismSOM cells receive delayed input, suggesting horizontal rather than ascending drive— “On the other hand, SOM neurons receive delayed excitatory input, suggesting that unlike PV and excitatory neurons, SOM neurons do not receive direct ascending inputs. Instead, they are more likely driven by excitation through horizontal circuits”
mechanismthe two types target different parts of the pyramidal cell - perisomatic for PV, distal dendrite for SOM - which is why they can act on different input streams— “PV neurons preferentially target perisomatic regions where ascending inputs are most likely located, while SOM neurons preferentially target distal dendritic domains where feedback excitatory inputs are most likely located”
contradicts Moore and Wehr (2013) on PV frequency tuning breadth— “The discrepancy between Moore and Wehr study and our current result may be due to different targeting methods (optogenetics aided indirect targeting vs. visually guided direct targeting).”
replicates Moore and Wehr (2013) on PV intensity tuning— “Nevertheless, PV neurons showed significantly lower ISIs than excitatory cells ( P < 0.01, K–S test), indicating that PV neurons were less intensity-tuned, which is consistent with the observation in a recent study ( Moore and Wehr 2013 ).”
builds-on Wu et al. (2008) intracellular study of rat auditory cortex layer 4
