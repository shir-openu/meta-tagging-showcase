claim-typemethod
mechanismAny break in the ground path (CSF to ground screw to copper wire to copper mesh to ground pin) leaves the headstage ungrounded, which is why movement artifacts and 50/60 Hz line noise appear in the recording — “the electrical path of the grounding should be checked, as any circuit interruption between the cerebrospinal fluid, the ground screw, the copper wire, the copper mesh flaps and the ground pin on the connector, would result in a large movement artifacts and/or line noise (50 Hz or 60 Hz).”
mechanismA micro-drive protruding above a too-short Faraday cage picks up environmental electrical fields, acting as an antenna and injecting noise into the signal — “If the Faraday cage is not high enough, the protruding micro-drive may act as an antenna.”
mechanismThe copper-mesh cylinder encloses the headgear and shunts environmental fields and whisker-generated charge to ground, which is how it suppresses noise and slow-wave artifacts — “The cylinder also serves as an electrical shield against environmental noise and the slow wave artifacts produced by the charged whiskers in the behaving animal.”
mechanismPlacing the reference over the cerebellum exploits the fact that cerebellar LFP amplitude and midline muscle artifact are both minimal, so the referenced signal carries less contamination — “This site is chosen because cerebellar LFP is the smallest of all cortical regions and muscle artifacts are minimal at this midline location.”
mechanism — “As a result of these competing requirements, different drives suite different applications.”
mechanism — “This ensures that during advancement, the probe shanks move into brain tissue without 'cutting' through it.”
mechanism — “Before fixing the probe to the drive, add extra insulation to the bonding area of the probe to prevent cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) or humidity from producing short-circuits:”
mechanism — “In addition, the electrical path of the grounding should be checked, as any circuit interruption between the cerebrospinal fluid, the ground screw, the copper wire, the copper mesh flaps and the ground pin on the connector, would result in a large movement artifacts and/or line noise (50 Hz or 60 Hz).”
mechanism — “The antenna effect can be prevented by grounding the drive as well (solder another copper wire between the drive and the copper-mesh).”

limitationThe authors state the correlational limit of the whole method: no amount of recording settles cause and effect — “However, given the correlational nature of these measurements, the cause-effect relationship among neuronal activity patterns remains inevitably ambiguous.”

limitationInjury during preparation or afterwards destroys the unit activity the method exists to capture — “Similarly, any injury to the brain tissue (while preparing the brain surface for implantation, or from bumping into the probe or drive once it is implanted) would result in damaging the cells and jeopardizing the recording of unit activity.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingCommercially available silicon probes with flexible cables and small multichannel preamplifiers have made simultaneous recording from 64-128 sites in a behaving rodent as easy as recording from 2 wire-electrode sites a decade earlier. — “Today, it is as easy to record from 64 to 128 sites simultaneously in a behaving rodent as from 2 sites with wire electrodes just a decade ago.”
findingSilicon-based multiple-site devices are effective for ensemble recording because they scale to many sites, occupy small volume, and have controlled geometric design. — “Multiple-site recording with silicon-based devices are particularly effective because of their scalability, small volume and geometric design.”
findingIn a rat exploring an open field, 32-channel CA1 recordings show a prominent 8 Hz theta oscillation in the local field potential with unit spikes superimposed across several shanks and recording sites. — “Note the prominent 8 Hz (theta band) oscillation of the local field potential during exploration with superimposed spiking on multiple shanks and sites”
findingThe recorded electrophysiological signals depend both on which brain structure the probe sits in and on what the animal is doing at the time. — “Electrophysiological signals (local field potential and unit activity) vary depending on the recorded structure and the current behavior of the animal.”
findingEven large-scale silicon-probe recordings cannot establish cause and effect between neuronal activity patterns, because the measurements are purely correlational. — “However, given the correlational nature of these measurements, the cause-effect relationship among neuronal activity patterns remains inevitably ambiguous.”
findingThe authors project that refined large-scale probes combined with new mathematical and modelling work will allow recording from a representatively large fraction, or perhaps all, of the neurons in the volume a multi-shank probe surveys. — “Recent theoretical and experimental studies with silicon probes 17,19 indicate that with properly refined large-scale recording methods, combined with new mathematical insights and modeling studies, one will be able to record from a representatively large fraction or perhaps every neuron from the brain volume surveyed by a multiple shank silicon probe”
findingrecording from 64 to 128 sites in a behaving rodent is now as easy as two sites were a decade earlier — “Today, it is as easy to record from 64 to 128 sites simultaneously in a behaving rodent as from 2 sites with wire electrodes just a decade ago.”
findingthe example recordings show theta-band field oscillation with spiking superimposed across shanks during exploration — “Note the prominent 8 Hz (theta band) oscillation of the local field potential during exploration with superimposed spiking on multiple shanks and sites (examples of spikes indicated by arrowheads).”
findingrecording every neuron in the surveyed volume is projected as reachable, and would still not resolve causation — “Recent theoretical and experimental studies with silicon probes 17,19 indicate that with properly refined large-scale recording methods, combined with new mathematical insights and modeling studies, one will be able to record from a representatively large fraction or perhaps every neuron from the brain volume surveyed by a multiple shank silicon probe (thousands of cells in ~1 μm 3;5-17 ).”
