phenomenonnegative priming
claim-typeexistence
mechanismpresenting a stimulus in one modality automatically activates its features in the other modality, so an ignored sound can prime (and slow) a later picture — “Second, the presentation of a stimulus in one modality may automatically activate features in other modalities.”
mechanismdistractor inhibition is applied only to distractors that interfere: nonconflict distractors need no inhibition, hence no later cost — “It could be assumed that nonconflict prime distractors do not interfere with the prime reaction, so that they need not be inhibited.”
mechanismthe probe target retrieves the earlier prime episode, which carries a 'no response was made' tag that conflicts with the current requirement to respond — “Within the episodic retrieval account, negative priming may be caused by the probe target’s cuing the retrieval of the perceptually similar prime display in which the distractor representation contains the information that no response was (to be) made to that stimulus.”
mechanismacoustic traces of ignored sounds persist in a long auditory store better than visual distractor traces persist in visual short-term memory, producing facilitation that offsets or reverses the negative-priming cost — “It seems possible that the acoustic aspects of auditory distractor representations are maintained more efficiently in a “long auditory store” (see Cowan, 1984) than the perceptual aspects of the representations of visual distractors are maintained in visual short-term memory.”
relation — “This demonstration is consonant with the finding of auditory-to-visual negative priming by Driver and Baylis (1993).”
relation — “To our knowledge, only Driver and Baylis (1993, Experiment 1) have investigated cross-modal negative priming, but only the auditory-tovisual side of it.”
effect directionconflict → negative priming — increases — “Negative priming was observed only after conflict”
differs indistractor inhibition → episodic retrieval — WHETHER ANYTHING IS SUPPRESSED AT ALL. Inhibition says the ignored item is actively damped and is still damped when it returns. Episodic retrieval says nothing is suppressed - the probe RETRIEVES the earlier display, which carries a tag that no response was to be made, and that tag conflicts with responding now. **Same prediction for the conflict result, opposite claims about what is in the head between the two trials** — “the observed data pattern is most easily explained”
replicationreplicated
limitationPrime conflict is unavoidably confounded with whether the prime and probe responses alternate or repeat, which makes the key finding ambiguous in principle. — “Although this unavoidable confound renders the interpretation of the present finding ambiguous in principle, we believe that the conflict/nonconflict nature of the primes, and not the prime–probe response alternation/repetition, determined the observed pattern of performance.”
limitationThe unexpected positive priming after nonconflict primes in the auditory-auditory condition can only be accounted for speculatively. — “We can only speculate about the underlying mechanisms.”
limitationThe positive priming found on nonconflict prime trials in the auditory-auditory condition was not predicted. — “The positive priming on nonconflict prime trials in the auditory–auditory condition was unexpected.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingNegative priming appeared only when the attended and ignored members of the prime pair called for different responses; nonconflict primes produced none. — “Negative priming was observed, but only if the attended and the ignored primes evoked different responses.”
findingThe conflict-only pattern of negative priming held for visual stimuli, for auditory stimuli, and in both cross-modal directions (auditory-to-visual and visual-to-auditory). — “This pattern—negative priming after conflict, but not after nonconflict, primes—was demonstrated with visual stimuli and replicated with auditory stimuli, as well as across modalities, both auditory to visual and visual to auditory.”
findingCross-modal negative priming was about equally strong in both directions, so the effect is not specific to the auditory-to-visual switch previously reported. — “In addition, the present data show that auditory-to-visual and visual-toauditory negative priming is fairly symmetrical.”
findingIn the auditory-auditory condition, nonconflict-prime trials produced positive priming (faster responses to the previously ignored sound) instead of the null effect seen in the other three presentation conditions. — “In trials with nonconflict probes in the auditory–auditory condition, we observed positive priming, rather than no priming, as in the other presentation conditions.”
findingWhether the probe pair itself was conflicting did not reliably change the size of negative priming. — “A 4 3 2 analysis of variance with presentation condition as a between-subjects variable and probe type as a withinsubjects variable showed that the probe type variable failed to reach the conventional level of significance”
findingCollapsing across the conflict/nonconflict prime distinction would have shown significant negative priming in the visual-visual condition while falsely concluding there is none in the auditory-auditory condition, which may explain the near absence of published auditory negative-priming work. — “ignoring the conflict–nonconflict prime distinction would have led to the finding of significant negative priming in the visual–visual condition, but it would have led us to erroneously accept the null hypothesis of no negative priming in the auditory–auditory condition.”
findingProbe responses were faster after visual than after auditory probes and slower after conflicting than after nonconflicting probes, while prime modality had no effect on probe speed. — “Most obviously, (1) probe reactions were faster after visual than after auditory probes (669 vs. 897 msec), whereas prime modality did not have an effect, and (2) probe reactions were slower following conflict, as opposed to nonconflict, probes (819 vs. 747 msec).”
findingBecause the ignored prime's response category was matched between ignored-repetition and control trials, the observed cross-modal effects cannot be explained by response-level information and must reflect stimulus identity. — “This implies that any performance differences between control and ignored repetition trials must be due to the stimulus identity and cannot be due to the response category associated with it.”
