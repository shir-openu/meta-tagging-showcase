claim-typemethod
transferfrom attitude psychology: The tri-component ABC model of attitudes is imported as the organizing scheme for a marketing/consumer-behaviour priming framework. — “In developing the priming framework, each type of prime is categorized by priming outcome: affective, behavioral, and cognitive, which follows suit with the tri-component ABC model of attitudes (Breckler, 1984).”
mechanismFollowing a primed procedure consumes cognitive resources, which keeps the primed representation active and slows its decay relative to a declarative prime. — “Because greater cognitive resources are devoted to following the procedure, decay of procedural primes is much slower than primes involving declarative knowledge, and this is borne out in many empirical findings.”
mechanismUncertainty about a stimulus makes the consumer recruit the prime together with existing memory to resolve that uncertainty, which produces assimilation. — “In other words, assimilation effects are greatest when there is uncertainty surrounding a stimulus, and therefore, the prime is used with existing knowledge in memory to solve the uncertainty.”
mechanismHigh motivation raises processing depth, which triggers correction of the judgment for the perceived persuasion attempt and so flips assimilation into contrast. — “When motivation is high, processing is higher, leading to correction in judgments to account for persuasion.”
relation — “This holistic priming framework extends Janiszewski and Wyer’s (2014) priming review to show the importance of priming limitations, especially in relation to survey research (i.e., measurement discrepancy).”
relation — “The offered framework has benefited from the review of priming by Janiszewski and Wyer (2014) and the theorizing of the tri-component model (Breckler, 1984).”
effect directionprime → knowledge activation — increases — “only a prime if it increases knowledge activation”
differs inprime → target — WHICH SIDE OF THE MANIPULATION EACH IS ON. The prime is what raises activation; the target is what the prime is applied to and where the outcome is measured. The two definitions are mutually referring - neither can be stated without the other - so this is a distinction internal to one paradigm rather than between two things that exist independently — “the target is what the prime is applied to in an effort to produce specific outcomes”
replicationuntested
limitationThe paper deliberately excludes the content-versus-process priming distinctions covered by the prior review it builds on, so its coverage of priming distinctions is partial by design. — “Specific distinctions made by Janiszewski and Wyer (2014), such as the nuances distinguishing content versus process priming, are not further discussed here in order to focus on new contributions.”
limitationThe framework's central measurement-discrepancy proposal is asserted rather than empirically tested, and the authors state it still needs testing. — “There is a great need for further testing of measurement discrepancy issues to identify if measurement similarity provides new and more accurate insight into consumer behavior.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingThe review sorts every priming technique into three outcome classes - affective, behavioral and cognitive - borrowed from the tri-component ABC model of attitudes. — “In developing the priming framework, each type of prime is categorized by priming outcome: affective, behavioral, and cognitive, which follows suit with the tri-component ABC model of attitudes (Breckler, 1984).”
findingPriming theories are grouped into two prospective theories - spreading activation and expectancy - and two retrospective theories - semantic matching and compound-cue. — “There are two main prospective priming theories: (i) spreading activation theory and (ii) expectancy theory, as well as two main retrospective priming theories: (i) semantic matching theory and (ii) compound-cue theory.”
findingCompound cue theory and spreading activation theory cannot be combined into one account because they rest on incompatible underlying models of memory. — “Thus, while some theories may be able to work together as Neely and Keefe (1989) describe, compound cue theory and spreading activation, in particular, are in direct opposition to one another because of their foundation in different models of memory.”
findingProspective and retrospective theories differ only in process; both converge on the same endpoint of increased knowledge activation. — “Both prospective and retrospective theories lead to the same end result of increased knowledge activation.”
findingA stimulus or independent variable counts as a prime only when it raises knowledge activation that then shapes the response to a target. — “Some researchers also refer to the prime as the stimulus or independent variable; however, a stimulus or an independent variable is only a prime if it increases knowledge activation that influences response to a target.”
findingThe commonest measurement discrepancy in survey-based priming work is priming behavior but measuring the effect with cognitive purchase-intention questions. — “One of the most common discrepancies occurs when researchers create surveys that use behavioral priming methods to encourage a consumer to purchase a product, yet measure the effects of priming through cognitive-based purchase intention questions.”
findingConsumers respond to a prime either consistently with it or reactantly against it, and the paper attributes the reactant (contrast) case to consumer traits and states such as skepticism and concern about persuasion rather than to processing nodes alone. — “We argue that reactance (i.e., apparent contrast effects) may stem from trait and state variables such as a consumer’s tendency toward skepticism or manipulation of their concern for persuasion.”
findingProcedural primes persist far longer than declarative primes, whose activation typically decays within seconds. — “Because greater cognitive resources are devoted to following the procedure, decay of procedural primes is much slower than primes involving declarative knowledge, and this is borne out in many empirical findings.”
quantity0.49 versus 0.25 — Average effect size of associative priming studies compared with semantic priming studies, as reported in the meta-review the paper cites — “In a meta-review of semantic priming studies, Lucas (2000) found that associative priming studies resulted in greater effect sizes (average of 0.49) in comparison with semantic priming studies (average of 0.25).”
quantity0.26 — Size of the increase in priming effects attributed to the associative boost (a prime that is both semantically and associatively related) — “Lucas (2000) also shows that the associative boost (i.e., both semantic and associative relationships) increases priming effects by 0.26.”
definitionpriming — “McNamara (2005) defines priming as “an improvement in performance in a perceptual or cognitive task, relative to an appropriate baseline, produced by context or prior experience” (p. 3).”
definitionprime and target — “Within the priming paradigm, the prime is the item used to manipulate or increase knowledge activation, and the target is what the prime is applied to in an effort to produce specific outcomes (Higgins et al., 1985).”
definitionbehavioral priming — “Behavioral priming, sometimes also referred to as social priming, results in increased participation in prime-activated behaviors.”
definitionprocedural priming — “Procedural priming falls under the umbrella of behavioral priming but focuses specifically on priming a process, such as strategies or methods for processing information.”
definitiongoal priming — “Goal priming, another type of behavioral priming, focuses on activation of end-goal states thereby leading individuals to behave in ways consistent with goal attainment (Forster et al., 2009).”
definitioncognitive priming — “Cognitive priming refers to changes in thought based upon the presence of a prime (Myers and Hansen, 2012).”
definitioncategory priming — “Category priming is a type of cognitive priming that occurs when a subset of terms (e.g., high class) is primed thereby influencing response to a target.”
definitionsemantic priming — “Semantic priming, another form of cognitive priming, considers how a word, phrase, sign, or symbol can influence response to a stimulus.”
definitionassociative priming — “Semantic priming occurs as a result of direct semantic relation between words (e.g., bronze is a type of gold), whereas associative priming occurs because of common relations developed in the mind that are not necessarily semantically related (e.g., dogs are associated with bones).”
definitionassociative boost — “Moss et al. (1995) note that priming studies can feature both associative and semantic elements resulting in greater effect sizes through what is called an associative boost.”
definitionconceptual versus perceptual priming — “As McNamara and Holbrook (2003) describe, conceptual priming focuses on meaning, while perceptual priming focuses on the form of the stimulus.”
definitionrepetition priming — “Simple repetition priming is described by Eysenck (2004) as the “more efficient processing of a stimulus when it has been presented and processed previously” (p. 313).”
definitioncontextual priming — “Contextual priming, also known as environmental cueing, has been defined in communications as manipulation or activation of knowledge using marketing cues that precede or surround a target advertisement (Yi, 1990a, 1990b).”
definitionmasked priming — “In contrast to priming techniques that allow seconds, minutes, or even longer between presentation of the prime and the target, masked priming shows a prime for a short time (sometimes just 50–60 ms) with a target immediately following the prime (Kinoshita and Lupker, 2003).”
definitionspreading activation theory — “Spreading activation theory states that a prime activates nodes in memory that are associated with the prime (Quillian, 1967).”
definitionexpectancy theory — “In contrast to spreading activation theory, expectancy theory posits that upon exposure to a prime, one’s mind automatically creates a set of expected targets, often words (Posner and Snyder, 1975).”
definitionsemantic matching theory — “Turning to retrospective theories, semantic matching theory states that individuals are exposed to both the prime and target and then use the prime to make sense of the target (Neely and Keefe, 1989).”
definitioncompound cue theory — “Once presented with the prime and target, this compound cue in short-term memory is matched to compounds already existing in long-term memory (Ratcliff and McKoon, 1988).”
definitionprospective theories of priming — “Prospective theories of priming describe a prime as activating knowledge, which then influences response to a target; thus, the majority of the priming process occurs before exposure to the target.”
definitionpriming method (as distinct from priming technique) — “Priming techniques can be partnered with a priming method (e.g., conceptual, perceptual, repetition, or contextual priming) to produce a priming outcome.”
