transferfrom attitude psychology: The tri-component ABC model of attitudes is imported as the organizing scheme for a marketing/consumer-behaviour priming framework.— “In developing the priming framework, each type of prime is categorized by priming outcome: affective, behavioral, and cognitive, which follows suit with the tri-component ABC model of attitudes (Breckler, 1984).”
mechanismFollowing a primed procedure consumes cognitive resources, which keeps the primed representation active and slows its decay relative to a declarative prime.— “Because greater cognitive resources are devoted to following the procedure, decay of procedural primes is much slower than primes involving declarative knowledge, and this is borne out in many empirical findings.”
mechanismUncertainty about a stimulus makes the consumer recruit the prime together with existing memory to resolve that uncertainty, which produces assimilation.— “In other words, assimilation effects are greatest when there is uncertainty surrounding a stimulus, and therefore, the prime is used with existing knowledge in memory to solve the uncertainty.”
mechanismHigh motivation raises processing depth, which triggers correction of the judgment for the perceived persuasion attempt and so flips assimilation into contrast.— “When motivation is high, processing is higher, leading to correction in judgments to account for persuasion.”
extends Janiszewski and Wyer (2014) review of priming in marketing— “This holistic priming framework extends Janiszewski and Wyer’s (2014) priming review to show the importance of priming limitations, especially in relation to survey research (i.e., measurement discrepancy).”

findingThe review sorts every priming technique into three outcome classes - affective, behavioral and cognitive - borrowed from the tri-component ABC model of attitudes.— “In developing the priming framework, each type of prime is categorized by priming outcome: affective, behavioral, and cognitive, which follows suit with the tri-component ABC model of attitudes (Breckler, 1984).”
findingPriming theories are grouped into two prospective theories - spreading activation and expectancy - and two retrospective theories - semantic matching and compound-cue.— “There are two main prospective priming theories: (i) spreading activation theory and (ii) expectancy theory, as well as two main retrospective priming theories: (i) semantic matching theory and (ii) compound-cue theory.”
findingCompound cue theory and spreading activation theory cannot be combined into one account because they rest on incompatible underlying models of memory.— “Thus, while some theories may be able to work together as Neely and Keefe (1989) describe, compound cue theory and spreading activation, in particular, are in direct opposition to one another because of their foundation in different models of memory.”
findingProspective and retrospective theories differ only in process; both converge on the same endpoint of increased knowledge activation.— “Both prospective and retrospective theories lead to the same end result of increased knowledge activation.”
findingA stimulus or independent variable counts as a prime only when it raises knowledge activation that then shapes the response to a target.— “Some researchers also refer to the prime as the stimulus or independent variable; however, a stimulus or an independent variable is only a prime if it increases knowledge activation that influences response to a target.”
findingThe commonest measurement discrepancy in survey-based priming work is priming behavior but measuring the effect with cognitive purchase-intention questions.— “One of the most common discrepancies occurs when researchers create surveys that use behavioral priming methods to encourage a consumer to purchase a product, yet measure the effects of priming through cognitive-based purchase intention questions.”
findingConsumers respond to a prime either consistently with it or reactantly against it, and the paper attributes the reactant (contrast) case to consumer traits and states such as skepticism and concern about persuasion rather than to processing nodes alone.— “We argue that reactance (i.e., apparent contrast effects) may stem from trait and state variables such as a consumer’s tendency toward skepticism or manipulation of their concern for persuasion.”
findingProcedural primes persist far longer than declarative primes, whose activation typically decays within seconds.— “Because greater cognitive resources are devoted to following the procedure, decay of procedural primes is much slower than primes involving declarative knowledge, and this is borne out in many empirical findings.”
quantity0.49 versus 0.25 — Average effect size of associative priming studies compared with semantic priming studies, as reported in the meta-review the paper cites the paper’s own words: “Lucas (2000) found”
quantity0.26 — Size of the increase in priming effects attributed to the associative boost (a prime that is both semantically and associatively related)
