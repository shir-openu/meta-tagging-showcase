mechanism**THE OLD NUMBER WAS ARBITRARY AND THE 1973 MEETING SAID SO**: `it was recognised that this upper limit of normal mPAP of 25 mmHg WAS SOMEWHAT EMPIRICAL AND ARBITRARILY DEFINED. However, THIS CONSERVATIVE CUT-OFF VALUE ALLOWED PHYSICIANS TO DISCRIMINATE SEVERE PH due to primary PH FROM OTHER FORMS OF PH ... characterised by a lower mPAP.` **The threshold was chosen to separate the severe cases from everything else**, not to mark where normal ends - a working boundary that became the definition of a disease for 45 years— “In the report of the meeting, it was recognised that this upper limit of normal mPAP of 25 mmHg was somewhat empirical and arbitrarily defined [ 2 ]. However, this conservative cut-off value allowed physicians to discriminate severe PH due to primary PH from other forms of PH (mainly due to lung diseases) characterised by a lower mPAP.”
mechanism**AND IT WAS KEPT FOR ITS CONSEQUENCES, NOT ITS ACCURACY**: `This definition REMAINED UNCHANGED during the subsequent WSPH meetings FROM 1998 TO 2013, AT LEAST IN PART TO PRECLUDE POTENTIAL OVERDIAGNOSIS AND OVERTREATMENT OF PH.` Four international meetings declined to move a number they knew was arbitrary, because of what moving it would do. **A definition held in place by its downstream effects is the cleanest instance of this in the corpus**— “This definition remained unchanged during the subsequent WSPH meetings from 1998 to 2013 [ 4 – 6 ], at least in part to preclude potential overdiagnosis and overtreatment of PH.”
mechanism**AND THE NEW NUMBER WAS ALREADY IN THE 1961 REPORT**: `In 1961, a report of the World Health Organization (WHO) Expert Committee on Chronic Cor Pulmonale mentioned clearly that the mean pulmonary arterial pressure DOES NOT NORMALLY EXCEED 15 mmHg ... and that the value was little affected by age AND NEVER EXCEEDED 20 mmHg.` The 2018 threshold is 20 mmHg. **The evidence for the new boundary is 57 years older than the boundary it replaces**, and the paper cites it as its first reference— “In 1961, a report of the World Health Organization (WHO) Expert Committee on Chronic Cor Pulmonale mentioned clearly that the mean pulmonary arterial pressure (mPAP) does not normally exceed 15 mmHg when the subject is at rest in a lying position, and that the value was little affected by age and never exceeded 20 mmHg [ 1 ].”
mechanism**AND THE NEW ONE IS A PERCENTILE**: from `1187 NORMAL SUBJECTS FROM 47 STUDIES`, `mPAP at rest was 14.0+/-3.3 mmHg`, so `TWO STANDARD DEVIATIONS would suggest mPAP >20 mmHg as above the upper limit of normal ( i.e. ABOVE THE 97.5TH PERCENTILE). THIS DEFINITION IS, THEREFORE, NO LONGER ARBITRARY, BUT BASED ON A SCIENTIFIC APPROACH.` **The replacement for an arbitrary cut is a distributional convention** - mean plus two standard deviations is a choice about how much of a normal population to call abnormal, and 2.5% is as much a convention as 25 mmHg was— “Considering this mPAP of 14 mmHg, two standard deviations would suggest mPAP >20 mmHg as above the upper limit of normal ( i.e. above the 97.5th percentile). This definition is, therefore, no longer arbitrary, but based on a scientific approach.”
mechanism**AND THE COMPANION CRITERION IS ADMITTED TO BE ARBITRARY, FOUR PARAGRAPHS LATER**: `This cut-off value of PVR >=3 WU IS ALSO QUITE ARBITRARY since some recent data suggest that PVR >2 WU could be also considered abnormal. In this sense, the use of a cut-off value of PVR >=3 WU IS CONSERVATIVE.` **One definition, two thresholds: one de-arbitrarised by a percentile and one defended as arbitrary and conservative**, and the same document says both— “This cut-off value of PVR ≥3 WU is also quite arbitrary since some recent data suggest that PVR >2 WU could be also considered abnormal [ 6 ]. In this sense, the use of a cut-off value of PVR ≥3 WU is conservative, suggesting the presence of a manifest pre-capillary PH.”
mechanismAND A NUMBER ALONE IS DECLARED INSUFFICIENT TO NAME A DISEASE: `Whatever the mPAP cut-off value considered for defining PH (>=25 or >20 mmHg), it is important to emphasise that THIS VALUE USED IN ISOLATION CANNOT CHARACTERISE A CLINICAL CONDITION AND DOES NOT DEFINE THE PATHOLOGICAL PROCESS PER SE.` The pressure can be raised by cardiac output, by a shunt, by wedge pressure or by blood viscosity - so the definition needs a second measurement to say WHERE the resistance is— “Whatever the mPAP cut-off value considered for defining PH (≥25 or >20 mmHg), it is important to emphasise that this value used in isolation cannot characterise a clinical condition and does not define the pathological process per se .”
mechanism**AND THE PATIENTS ADDED BY THE CHANGE HAVE NO EVIDENCE BASE**: `a change in the haemodynamic definition of PH due to PVDs DOES NOT IMPLY TREATING THESE ADDITIONAL PATIENTS, but highlights the importance of close monitoring in this population. PROSPECTIVE TRIALS ARE REQUIRED TO DETERMINE WHETHER THIS PH POPULATION MIGHT BENEFIT FROM SPECIFIC MANAGEMENT.` **A diagnosis is extended to people for whom no treatment is indicated and none has been tested** - stated plainly, in the section that makes the change— “Nevertheless, a change in the haemodynamic definition of PH due to PVDs does not imply treating these additional patients, but highlights the importance of close monitoring in this population.
