phenomenontask-phase-specific firing in basal forebrain ensembles— “The current study examined the spiking activity of basal forebrain neuron populations across multiple phases of a selective attention task, addressing, in particular, the issue of complexity in ensemble firing patterns across time.”
claim-typeexistence— “Among the full population of BF neurons, multiple distinct and complex forms of task-phase-specific activity are realized, forming unique population firing patterns for all task phases.”
method — evidence— “In the present work, large ensemble recordings of BF neurons were obtained during performance of a selective attention task with the goal of filling, to some extent, this gap in knowledge.”
mechanismAnatomical organisation of local interconnections and of extrinsic afferents partitions basal forebrain neurons into distinct groups, which is why firing patterns cluster and why pair-wise trial-to-trial variability tracks pattern similarity.— “The foregoing results suggest that, for complex behavioral tasks, BF neurons are organized into distinct groups by virtue of the anatomical organization of their local interconnections, by their extrinsic afferents, or both.”
mechanismWhichever subset of basal forebrain neurons is active at a given moment raises responsiveness specifically in the cortical targets those neurons reach, so task-phase-specific firing produces task-phase-specific cortical modulation.— “currently active set of BF neurons yields enhanced responsiveness among the subset of efferent targets reached by those neurons.”
mechanismSimultaneous peaks and troughs in GABAergic and glutamatergic basal forebrain neurons could amplify inhibition of the cortical target, because GABAergic drive rises while excitatory glutamatergic drive is withdrawn.— “In an admittedly simplistic scenario, for instance, GABA and glutamate neurons could reach simultaneous peaks and troughs in activity, yielding amplification of GABAergic inhibition through coincident disfacilitation of excitatory glutamatergic input.”
builds-on Golmayo et al., 2003— “The model is consistent with experimental work demonstrating that different sub-regions of prefrontal cortex can activate different BF neuron sub-groups which, in turn, can differentially impact responsiveness in primary somatosensory and visual cortices ( Golmayo et al., 2003).”
builds-on Gill et al., 2000— “Both possibilities are supported by work showing that neurons of the prelimbic subregion of prefrontal cortex exhibit activity correlates to all phases of a task requiring stimulus detection and choice of an instrumental response, and that such activity correlates are strongly dependent on BF efferents (Gill et al., 2000).”
extends Wilson and Rolls, 1990a,b; Tindell et al., 2004; Lin and Nicolelis, 2008— “BF neurons having dual responses to relevant light flashes or auditory tones as well as reward delivery have previously been observed ( Wilson and Rolls, 1990a,b; Tindell et al., 2004; Lin and Nicolelis, 2008 ).”
sensea task phase - DEFINED BY A POPULATION FIRING-RATE VECTOR: each phase is identified by a unique vector across the recorded population, and most categories of task-phase-specific firing are said to have counterparts with opposing patterns, so a 'phase' here is a point in population activity space and its complement— “Unique population firing-rate vectors defined each task phase and most categories of task-phase-specific firing had counterparts with opposing firing patterns”
sensethe basal forebrain signature - ALSO PRESENT IN PARIETAL CORTEX: an analogous set of task-phase-specific firing patterns was observed in posterior parietal cortex neurons, so the pattern is not diagnostic of the structure the title names— “An analogous set of task-phase-specific firing patterns was also observed in a population of posterior parietal cortex neurons”
sensea category of task-phase-specific activity - AN OUTPUT OF CLUSTERING: the distinct categories were revealed by clustering techniques applied to the full population, and the abstract gives their number as 'a large number', so the entities are individuated by the method rather than counted— “Clustering techniques applied to the full population revealed a large number of distinct categories of task-phase-specific activity patterns”

findingDistinct sub-populations of basal forebrain neurons reached their peak firing rate at every phase of the task, including the pure locomotion period between nose-poke and return to the arena centre, not only at stimulus onset and reward.— “First, distinct subsets of BF neurons reach peak activity at each task phase, including, for example, the time period directly between nose-poke and return to the center plate.”
findingNearly every category of task-phase-specific firing had a mirror-image counterpart category whose mean firing pattern was strongly negatively correlated with it, so at each task phase one group of neurons peaked while another fell to a trough.— “The mean task-phase-specific firing pattern for nearly every grouping of BF neurons has a counterpart in another grouping for which the mean task-phase-specific pattern is strongly negatively correlated”
findingApart from persistence before the light flash and after reward, no basal forebrain ensemble firing pattern recurred at a later task phase, so each task phase was marked by its own distinct population firing vector.— “While persistence in BF ensemble firing patterns is found over the time period preceding light flash onset and after reward obtainment, further evidence for pattern recurrence is not found, directly evidencing the fact that BF networks are capable of generating a large repertoire of distinct firing patterns.”
findingPosterior parietal cortex neurons, clustered independently by the same algorithm, produced groups that each had both a strongly matching and a strongly opposing basal forebrain counterpart pattern.— “Remarkably, despite independence in the application of the algorithm to the BF and PPC populations, mean rate vectors for nearly all PPC groups had BF counterparts with highly similar and highly dissimilar patterns.”
findingNeuron pairs whose mean firing patterns were similar also co-varied trial by trial: 62% of the 14,020 positively signal-correlated pairs had noise correlations at least two standard deviations above chance.— “Across 14,020 pairs having positive signal correlations, 62% exhibited noise correlation values two standard deviations or more greater than that expected by chance.”
findingMost firing peaks (82.8%) and most firing troughs (62.2%) were statistically significant against a randomized-light-time control, and a third of all neuron-by-phase mean rates were significant, far above the 1% expected.— “Across all neurons and task phases, fully 33.8% of the observed mean firing rates yielded significance, a value far above that expected (1%) as well as that observed (1.02%) for the same set of comparisons for two separate randomizations”
findingDuring the anticipatory period before the light flash — when attention to the visual field should be highest — basal forebrain mean firing was at its lowest and few neurons peaked, arguing against a role confined to anticipatory attention.— “Among the full population of BF neurons, mean activity rates were actually lowest during this time period, and associated with peak activity in only a few neurons.”
findingThe largest proportion of neurons peaked, and population mean rate was highest, in the period just after the light flash, pointing to a basal forebrain role in initiating and maintaining responses to relevant stimuli.— “Instead, the time period just after light flash was associated with peak firing in the largest proportion of neurons as well as the highest mean rate across the full population.”
