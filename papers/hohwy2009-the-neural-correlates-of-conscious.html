limitationA CONTROL GROUP ARGUED AWAY RATHER THAN OMITTED. Owen's case that the vegetative-state patient's sustained SMA response is voluntary rests on its duration and on its changing to order - and the author notes that this logic, if it works, makes a control group unnecessary, since ANY brain activity time-locked to the requests would show the same thing. He calls that consequence puzzling rather than wrong.— “it is slightly puzzling that this logic removes the need for comparison with a control group: consideration solely of the VS patient’s SMA activity is enough since any pattern of activity anywhere in the brain that is time-locked to the requests could demonstrate the voluntariness.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingBecause content-based NCC studies hold the subject's overall conscious state fixed rather than manipulating it, that presupposed conscious state remains an uncontrolled potential cause of the content's being conscious, i.e. a confound.— “this shows that presupposing conscious states at least potentially introduces a confounding factor in the standard content-based NCC approach.”
findingWhat the content-based approach can legitimately identify is the neural correlate of a content being selected for conscious presentation rather than not selected, which is a different target from a content's being conscious.— “The target seems to be to find the neural correlate of a content’s being selected for conscious presentation, rather than not selected, on the background assumption that the creature is already in an overall conscious state.”
findingThe same experimental method would serve researchers who care only about explicit representation and are agnostic about whether their subjects are conscious, so the method is not specifically directed at consciousness at all.— “They can be completely neutral about whether the creature is in fact in an overall conscious state or not and yet gain very fruitful results using this method.”
findingIn state-based studies there is a trade-off: the more carefully conscious content is matched across conditions, the weaker the grounds for believing the two conditions still differ in being conscious versus unconscious rather than being two conscious conditions.— “The lesson is that the better content is matched across conditions, the less certain we become that the conditions contrast the unconscious state with the conscious state rather than merely contrasting conscious states with conscious states.”
findingTaking sustained, instruction-locked brain responses as evidence of consciousness makes voluntary control the criterion of consciousness, which substitutes volition for phenomenality and so changes the explanatory target.— “Now the mark of consciousness is voluntary control rather than what philosophers refer…
findingThe alternative route of inferring state consciousness from the presence of a content NCC also fails, because the content-based approach already assumes the subject is in a conscious state and therefore cannot independently establish it.
findingThe paper concludes that neither the content-based nor the state-based paradigm, pursued on its own, can deliver the neural correlates of consciousness.
findingThe remedy proposed is not to conjoin the two approaches' results but to build a new paradigm that targets the causal, mechanistic interplay between content processing and overall conscious state across contents and across conscious and unconscious state types.
