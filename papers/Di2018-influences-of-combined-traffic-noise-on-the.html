transferfrom toxicology: the hormesis biphasic dose-response model, with noise dose defined by analogy to an ingested toxicant dose (sound level times duration)— “Corresponding to the dose of toxicity that was ingested by organisms, the exposure dose of noise for organisms was the value of exposure sound level times exposure duration.”
mechanismA low-dose stressor breaks homeostasis and triggers a compensatory response that overshoots the inhibition, producing facilitation early on.— “Under the condition of low-dose stimulation, the compensatory response produced by organisms could be greater than the inhibitory response of stimuli, which resulted in the excitatory effect of stimuli.”
mechanismWith continued exposure the compensatory response is exhausted, so the inhibitory effect of the noise comes to dominate.— “It could be caused by a gradual exhaustion of compensatory response.”
mechanismGlutamate acting on hippocampal NMDA receptors admits calcium and thereby changes synaptic plasticity, which is the substrate of learning and memory.— “Subsequently, Mg 2+ , which blocked the channel of Ca 2+ , was removed, and the Ca 2+ flowed into the cell, which induced the change of synaptic plasticity.”
mechanismThe behavioural swing is mediated by the hippocampal glutamate level, which is raised by short exposure and lowered by long exposure.— “Thus, it could be speculated that the effects of CTN on the ability of learning and memory in mice were regulated and controlled by the level of Glu in the hippocampus.”
mechanismThe direction of the effect reverses with exposure duration, which the paper identifies as hormesis - excitation at low dose, inhibition at high— “Hormesis was the common biphasic dose-effect model in toxicology and was characterized by low-dose excitement and high-dose inhibition.[ 22 ]”
mechanismDose for noise is not the level but the level multiplied by the duration, which is why the same decibel figure sits on both sides of the curve— “Corresponding to the dose of toxicity that was ingested by organisms, the exposure dose of noise for organisms was the value of exposure sound level times exposure duration.[ 25 ]”
mechanismThe reversal is attributed to a compensatory response that outruns the insult at first and is then exhausted— “Under the condition of low-dose stimulation, the compensatory response produced by organisms could be greater than the inhibitory response of stimuli, which resulted in the excitatory effect of stimuli.”
mechanismThe behavioural reversal is tracked by glutamate, which rises early and falls late while GABA stays flat— “Obviously, the level of Glu in mice rose after short-term exposure (6–10 days) to 70 dB(A) CTN, but the long-term exposure (26–30 days, 36–40 days) led to a decrease in the Glu level in mice.”
mechanismGlutamate has its own inverted-U: more release aids learning until excitotoxicity sets in— “Nonetheless, too much release of Glu could cause an excitatory toxic effect on the neurons, which resulted in a decline in the learning and memory ability.”
