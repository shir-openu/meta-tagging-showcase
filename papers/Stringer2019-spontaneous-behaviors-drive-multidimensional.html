findingOngoing (spontaneous) population activity in mouse visual cortex reliably encodes a latent signal spanning at least 100 linear dimensions rather than one or two noise-like factors. — “Ongoing population activity in visual cortex reliably encoded a latent signal of at least 100 linear dimensions, and possibly many more.”
findingThe single largest dimension of ongoing activity tracks arousal and drives roughly half the neurons up while driving the other half down, so it is not a global rate increase. — “The largest dimension correlated with arousal and modulated about half of the neurons positively and half negatively.”
findingA high-dimensional description of facial movement predicts about twice as much neural variance as the three standard arousal measures (running, pupil diameter and whisking) combined. — “This multidimensional behavior measure predicted approximately twice as much variance as the three arousal variables”
findingThe same multidimensional behaviour-related activity structure is not confined to visual cortex but is present in neurons sampled across the forebrain. — “suggesting that a multidimensional structure of ongoing activity is distributed throughout the brain”
findingThe neural subspaces encoding visual stimuli and encoding behaviour share only a single dimension, which is the population mean firing rate; all the finer patterns of increase and decrease are orthogonal between the two. — “Thus, overlap in the spaces encoding sensory and behavioral variables arises primarily because both can change the mean firing rate of the population: the precise patterns of increases and decreases about this change in mean were essentially orthogonal”
findingStimulus identity can be decoded accurately from the stimulus-only subspace but not at all from the behaviour-only or spontaneous-only subspaces, which perform no better than random dimensions. — “Decoding from the stimulus space yielded a cross-validated error rate of 10.1 ± 4.0 %; activity in the spontaneous- or behavior-only spaces yielded errors of 53.1 ± 6.4 % and 56.8 ± 6.7 %, no better than randomly-chosen dimensions”
findingTrial-to-trial response variability decomposes into multiplicative gain modulation inside the stimulus subspace plus additive ongoing activity in dimensions orthogonal to it. — “Trial-to-trial variability in population responses to repeated stimulus presentations reflected a combination of multiplicative modulation in the stimulus-space, and additive modulation in orthogonal dimensions.”
findingThalamic neurons are the most strongly behaviour-modulated population recorded, with 35.7% of their total variance predictable from facial video. — “with neurons in thalamus predicted best (35.7% of total variance, 59% of estimated maximum)”
finding97% of the variance in the first shared component was reliable, leaving 3% for independent noise — “The fraction of reliable variance in the first SVC was 97% ( Fig. 1K,L ), implying that only 3% of the variance along this dimension reflected independent noise.”
findingreliable spontaneous variance decayed across dimensions as a power law of exponent 1.14, steeper than for stimulus responses but still high-dimensional — “The magnitude of reliable spontaneous variance was distributed across dimensions according to a power law of exponent 1.14 ( Fig. 1M ). This value is larger than the power law exponents close to 1.0 seen for stimulus responses ( 35 ), but still indicates a high-dimensional signal.”
findingthe three explicit arousal variables together explained about half the variance of the first neural principal component — “These arousal-related variables correlated with each other ( Fig. S4A,B ), and together accounted for approximately 50% of the variance of the first neural PC ( Fig. 1E , Fig. S4C ).”
findingabout 57% of neurons correlated positively with the first PC and the rest negatively, so two sub-populations alternate — “Correlation with the first PC was positive or negative in approximately similar numbers of neurons (57% ± 6.7% SE positive), indicating that two large sub-populations of neurons alternate their activity ( Fig. 1F,G ).”
findingmultidimensional facial behaviour predicted roughly twice the variance the three arousal variables did — “This multidimensional behavior measure predicted approximately twice as much variance as the three arousal variables ( Fig. 2D-J , Movie S3 ).”
findingprediction kept improving up to at least 16 behavioural dimensions, so visual cortex encodes at least that many motor dimensions — “Prediction quality continued to increase with up to 16 dimensions of video-graphic information (and beyond, in some recordings), suggesting that visual cortex encodes at least 16 dimensions of motor information.”
