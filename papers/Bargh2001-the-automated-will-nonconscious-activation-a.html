phenomenonnonconscious goal pursuit— “Our argument is that goals can be triggered outside of awareness and then run to completion, attaining desired outcomes ( Bargh, 1990 ; Bargh & Chartrand, 1999 ; Bargh & Gollwitzer, 1994 ).”
claim-typeexistence— “Taken together, the results of the five experiments support the proposal that behavioral goals can become activated without any consciously made choice required.”
method — evidence— “Five experiments are reported in which the goal either to perform well or to cooperate was activated, without the awareness of participants, through a priming manipulation.”
mechanismRepeated, consistent pursuit of a goal in a given situation forms an automatic situation-to-goal association, so situational cues alone later activate the goal without conscious choice.— “In other words, over time the goal representation will develop an automatic association with the features of those situations in which that goal has been repeatedly and consistently chosen and pursued in the past.”
mechanismAn activated but unfulfilled goal-directed action tendency accumulates strength over time (unlike perceptual activation, which decays), which is why the behavioural priming effect grows with delay.— “Until the goal is acted on, however, the action tendency is posited to increase in strength over time, thus pressing for realization.”
replicates Ovsiankina (1928)— “This finding replicates the classic finding of Ovsiankina (1928) in the case of conscious goal pursuit.”
builds-on J. A. Bargh, 1990— “It is proposed that goals can be activated outside of awareness and then operate nonconsciously to guide self-regulation effectively (J. A. Bargh, 1990 ).”
builds-on Atkinson and Birch (1970)— “According to Atkinson and Birch’s (1970) “dynamic theory of action,” goal-directed action tendencies consume their strength to the degree they are acted on, reaching their lowest level of activation immediately after the goal is attained.”
sensenonconscious goal pursuit - the claim in one sentence, and note how much is packed into it: goals can be ACTIVATED OUTSIDE OF AWARENESS, then OPERATE nonconsciously, and do so EFFECTIVELY - guiding self-regulation rather than merely biasing a judgement. Three separate assertions (activation without awareness, autonomous operation, functional success), any of which could fail while the others held— “It is proposed that goals can be activated outside of awareness and then operate nonconsciously to guide self-regulation effectively (J. A. Bargh, 1990 ). Five experiments are reported in which the goal either to perform well or to cooperate was activated, without the awareness of participants, through a priming manipulation”
sensegoal versus construct - THE DISAMBIGUATION THIS CORPUS NEEDS AND RARELY GETS. A prime made of `win, compete, succeed, strive` could have activated a construct that changes how the situation is CONSTRUED, or a goal that DRIVES behaviour. The paper separates them by time course and states the falsifier first: NO model of construal effects predicts an increase over time, so the same prime is required to produce a decreasing perceptual effect and an increasing behavioural one. A crossover, not a difference in size— “No model of priming or accessibility effects on perception or construal predicts an increase in effect over time ( Bargh, 1994 ; Higgins, 1989 , 1996 ; Wyer & Srull, 1989 ). The dissociation we predicted, if goal-priming effects are not mediated by perceptual construal, is straightforward. The effects of the same priming manipulation…
senseDEFINITION BY DIAGNOSTIC SIGNATURE - goal pursuit is identified by CONTENT-FREE features (vigour, persistence against obstacles, resumption after disruption) taken from the study of consciously held goals. Because the criteria are content-free they can be applied to a goal the participant cannot report, which is the only reason the nonconscious version is testable at all. The definition is doing methodological work, not just fixing a meaning— “Goal pursuit (i.e., striving for the desired outcome), however, is also characterized by content-free features, such as vigorous acting toward goal attainment, persistence in the face of obstacles, and resumption after disruption”
sensethe control is built into the definition: participants are given ONE AND THE SAME TASK with the goal activated for some and not others, so that `we were able to hold the content of the task goal constant across all participants` and let the content-free features be the only thing that varies. Borrowing a content-free definition and then holding content constant is the same decision made twice, at the level of theory and of design— “we were able to hold the content of the task goal constant across all participants so that we could examine the content-free features of nonconscious goal pursuit and be able to compare these to the known content-free features of conscious goal pursuit”
controlExperiment 3 used a DISSOCIATION PARADIGM specifically to test the perceptual-construal account — held — rules out that the prime changed how the situation was PERCEIVED rather than activating a goal - the leading alternative explanation for behavioural priming— “Experiment 3 used a dissociation paradigm to rule out perceptual-construal alternative explanations.”
controla control group was subliminally exposed to words UNRELATED to impression formation — held — rules out that the effect comes from subliminal exposure as such rather than from the content of the primed words— “Instead, one group had been previously primed (subliminally) with words related to impression formation, and a control group was subliminally exposed to words unrelated to impression formation.”
controlextensive questioning during debriefing looked for any sign that participants were aware of the priming manipulation's effect — held — rules out that the effect is conscious compliance rather than nonconscious activation— “In both experiments, extensive questioning of the participants during debriefing revealed no signs of conscious awareness of the effect that the priming manipulation might have had on the subsequent task.”

limitationThe intention-behaviour correlations rest on small per-cell samples.— “Because the number of participants on which these correlations are based is somewhat small, these results should be interpreted with caution.”
limitationRetrospective self-report is a weak index of what participants were actually aware of during the task.— “Nevertheless, it must be acknowledged that any such memory-based self-report measure of prior conscious states is not the most sensitive or powerful measure of actual awareness during the experimental tasks ( Ericsson & Simon, 1980 ).”

findingParticipants primed with high-performance words found more words on unrelated word-search puzzles than participants primed with neutral words.— “High-performance-goal primed participants found a mean of 26.0 words, compared with a mean of 21.5 words for participants in the neutral priming condition.”
findingPriming the goal of cooperation increased the number of fish returned to the common pool, as did an explicit conscious cooperation goal.— “It revealed a main effect for priming, F (1, 56) = 3.93, p = .05, as well as a main effect for conscious goal, F (1, 56) = 5.99, p < .02.”
findingThe goal-priming effect did not depend on a concurrent conscious goal in the same direction, ruling out the 'piggybacking' account.— “Moreover, in line with our hypothesis that nonconscious-goal activation does not require the preexistence of a conscious goal in the same direction (the piggybacking issue), participants in the no-conscious-goal condition showed the same increase in cooperation due to goal priming as did participants in the conscious-goal condition.”
findingA five-minute delay after the identical prime weakened the impression-formation (perceptual) effect but strengthened the task-performance (motivational) effect, a crossover dissociation.— “Most importantly for the present hypotheses, however, primed participants’ achievement-related impressions of the target person became less extreme over time, t (57) = 1.83, p < .04, whereas the performance of primed participants on the word-search task increased over time, t (91) = 2.50, p < .01.”
findingPerformance-goal primed participants were far more likely than neutral-primed participants to keep working after an explicit instruction to stop, showing persistence against an obstacle.— “A substantially greater proportion of participants in the high-performance–goal priming condition (57%) than in the neutral priming condition (22%) continued to work on the task after the stop signal was given.”
findingAfter an interruption, performance-goal primed participants more often resumed the unfinished intellectual task instead of switching to a pretested-more-enjoyable alternative.— “As predicted, a considerably higher proportion of high-performance–goal primed participants (66%) chose to continue working on die interrupted Scrabble task than did nonprimed participants (32%).”
findingSelf-reported strength of intention predicted behaviour only for consciously held goals, not for primed ones, indicating the priming effect was not mediated by conscious intention.— “The self-reported intentions of participants with a nonconsciously held goal to cooperate were unrelated to their actual cooperation, even though these participants showed a non-conscious cooperation-goal effect on their behavior.”
