phenomenonnonconscious goal pursuit
claim-typeexistence
mechanismRepeated, consistent pursuit of a goal in a given situation forms an automatic situation-to-goal association, so situational cues alone later activate the goal without conscious choice. — “In other words, over time the goal representation will develop an automatic association with the features of those situations in which that goal has been repeatedly and consistently chosen and pursued in the past.”
mechanismAn activated but unfulfilled goal-directed action tendency accumulates strength over time (unlike perceptual activation, which decays), which is why the behavioural priming effect grows with delay. — “Until the goal is acted on, however, the action tendency is posited to increase in strength over time, thus pressing for realization.”
relation — “This finding replicates the classic finding of Ovsiankina (1928) in the case of conscious goal pursuit.”
relation — “It is proposed that goals can be activated outside of awareness and then operate nonconsciously to guide self-regulation effectively (J. A. Bargh, 1990 ).”
relation — “According to Atkinson and Birch’s (1970) “dynamic theory of action,” goal-directed action tendencies consume their strength to the degree they are acted on, reaching their lowest level of activation immediately after the goal is attained.”
differs ingoal-directed action tendencies → perceptual construal — WHAT DELAY DOES TO THEM - construal effects DECREASE over time, goal-directed action tendencies INCREASE. A crossover on the same manipulation, and the increase is the half no accessibility or construal model predicts, which is what makes it a falsifier rather than a comparison — “predicts an increase in effect over time”
replicationuntested
limitationThe intention-behaviour correlations rest on small per-cell samples. — “Because the number of participants on which these correlations are based is somewhat small, these results should be interpreted with caution.”
limitationRetrospective self-report is a weak index of what participants were actually aware of during the task. — “Nevertheless, it must be acknowledged that any such memory-based self-report measure of prior conscious states is not the most sensitive or powerful measure of actual awareness during the experimental tasks ( Ericsson & Simon, 1980 ).”
limitationThe intention questionnaire may not have been sensitive enough to detect real differences in cooperation motivation. — “However, the lack of a difference between those participants explicitly instructed to cooperate (i.e., the conscious-goal condition) and those not given such instructions raises the issue of whether the intention questionnaire was sufficiently sensitive to capture actual differences in the motivation to cooperate during the task.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingParticipants primed with high-performance words found more words on unrelated word-search puzzles than participants primed with neutral words. — “High-performance-goal primed participants found a mean of 26.0 words, compared with a mean of 21.5 words for participants in the neutral priming condition.”
findingPriming the goal of cooperation increased the number of fish returned to the common pool, as did an explicit conscious cooperation goal. — “It revealed a main effect for priming, F (1, 56) = 3.93, p = .05, as well as a main effect for conscious goal, F (1, 56) = 5.99, p < .02.”
findingThe goal-priming effect did not depend on a concurrent conscious goal in the same direction, ruling out the 'piggybacking' account. — “Moreover, in line with our hypothesis that nonconscious-goal activation does not require the preexistence of a conscious goal in the same direction (the piggybacking issue), participants in the no-conscious-goal condition showed the same increase in cooperation due to goal priming as did participants in the conscious-goal condition.”
findingA five-minute delay after the identical prime weakened the impression-formation (perceptual) effect but strengthened the task-performance (motivational) effect, a crossover dissociation. — “Most importantly for the present hypotheses, however, primed participants’ achievement-related impressions of the target person became less extreme over time, t (57) = 1.83, p < .04, whereas the performance of primed participants on the word-search task increased over time, t (91) = 2.50, p < .01.”
findingPerformance-goal primed participants were far more likely than neutral-primed participants to keep working after an explicit instruction to stop, showing persistence against an obstacle. — “A substantially greater proportion of participants in the high-performance–goal priming condition (57%) than in the neutral priming condition (22%) continued to work on the task after the stop signal was given.”
findingAfter an interruption, performance-goal primed participants more often resumed the unfinished intellectual task instead of switching to a pretested-more-enjoyable alternative. — “As predicted, a considerably higher proportion of high-performance–goal primed participants (66%) chose to continue working on die interrupted Scrabble task than did nonprimed participants (32%).”
findingSelf-reported strength of intention predicted behaviour only for consciously held goals, not for primed ones, indicating the priming effect was not mediated by conscious intention. — “The self-reported intentions of participants with a nonconsciously held goal to cooperate were unrelated to their actual cooperation, even though these participants showed a non-conscious cooperation-goal effect on their behavior.”

variablechoice of task after the interruption (resume Scrabble vs. rate cartoons) (dependent) — “The dependent variable was the participant’s choice of task following the interruption: the Scrabble word-construction task on which they had already started or the objectively more enjoyable cartoon-humor rating task.”
definitionautomatic (nonconscious) goal pursuit — “Our argument is that goals can be triggered outside of awareness and then run to completion, attaining desired outcomes ( Bargh, 1990 ; Bargh & Chartrand, 1999 ; Bargh & Gollwitzer, 1994 ).”
definitioncontent-free features of goal pursuit — “Goal pursuit (i.e., striving for the desired outcome), however, is also characterized by content-free features, such as vigorous acting toward goal attainment, persistence in the face of obstacles, and resumption after disruption ( Heckhausen, 1991 ; Lewin, 1926 ; Wicklund & Gollwitzer, 1982 ).”
