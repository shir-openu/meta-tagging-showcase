phenomenoncomodulation masking release— “Comodulation masking release (CMR) is a psychoacoustic phenomenon whereby adding coherently modulated noise to an existing masker makes signals easier to perceive ( Hall et al., 1984 ).”
claim-typemechanism— “Transient opsin-mediated silencing of auditory cortex during the priming period almost completely abolished these improvements, suggesting that cortical processing may play a direct and significant role in detection of quiet sounds in noisy environments.”
method — evidence— “Recordings were made in primary auditory cortex from a total of 21 sites at different penetrations, in 6 animals.”
mechanismProlonged exposure to the opening portion of the noise primes auditory cortex to the masker's spectrotemporal statistics, which is what later permits the signal to be segregated from it— “We hypothesized that exposure to the early noise portion (between 0 and 400 ms) primes the auditory system to the spectrotemporal statistics of the masker to facilitate masking release.”
mechanismSlow adaptation to temporal coherence across frequency builds up over the precursor period and is what implements across-channel masking release, rather than instantaneous cancellation— “This latter observation suggests that slow adaptation to temporal coherence across-frequency is necessary to implement this form of CMR.”
mechanismAcross-channel maskers rarely drive envelope-locked cortical firing, so there is less locking to be suppressed by the signal, which is why across-channel CMR is smaller than within-channel CMR— “With across-channel maskers, as used here, the probability of noise locking is reduced, which in turn reduces the opportunity for locking suppression.”
mechanismBlue light drives ChR2-expressing parvalbumin interneurons, whose activation inhibits pyramidal cells and thereby transiently silences cortical processing of the precursor— “Blue light stimulation (150 ms, 473 nm, 5 mW) of cortical tissue caused transient activation of PV + cells, which in turn produced strong inhibition of cortical pyramidal cells ( Fig. 6 B , C ).”
contradicts Klink et al. (2010), behavioural across-channel CMR in mouse— “Klink et al. (2010 ) did observe that detection thresholds were lower in across-channel comodulated backgrounds, but these differences did not reach significance, in contrast to the relatively large neuronal CMR observed here.”
contradicts the equalization cancellation model of across-channel CMR (Buus, 1985; Piechowiak et al., 2007)— “However, our data demonstrate inconsistencies with the EC model.”
contradicts Nelken et al. (1999), envelope locking of cortical cells to the masker— “Previous studies also reported that most cortical cells demonstrate locking of spiking activity to the envelope of the noise ( Nelken et al., 1999 ), which we have not observed.”
senseA FOURTH KIND OF PRIME IN THIS CORPUS, AND THE ONLY ONE THAT IS NOT A REPRESENTATION. The prime is `PRIMING PERIODS OF NOISE ALONE` - an interval of acoustic exposure. Nothing is activated, retrieved or represented; a circuit is given time in the noise and gets better at separating a signal from it. Beside a word priming a concept, a picture priming a word and a sentence priming a structure, this is priming as ADAPTATION of a processing stage, and the same word covers all four— “Improvements in detection were largest following priming periods of noise alone, indicating that cortical segregation is enhanced over time”
senseTHE CAUSAL EVIDENCE IS AN INTERVENTION IN THE PRIMING WINDOW ITSELF, which almost nothing else in this corpus has: `TRANSIENT OPSIN-MEDIATED SILENCING of auditory cortex DURING THE PRIMING PERIOD almost completely abolished these improvements`. Not a correlation between prime and effect and not a dissociation - the structure is switched off while the prime is playing and the benefit does not appear. And the paper still hedges the interpretation: cortical processing `MAY play a direct and significant role`— “Transient opsin-mediated silencing of auditory cortex during the priming period almost completely abolished these improvements, suggesting that cortical processing may play a direct and significant role in detection of quiet sounds in noisy environments”
senseTHE EFFECT IS A FUNCTION OF TIME, stated as such: improvements were LARGEST following priming periods, `indicating that cortical segregation is ENHANCED OVER TIME`. The benefit accrues during exposure rather than being triggered by an event, which is what distinguishes this sense of priming from the single-prime designs elsewhere in the corpus - there the interval is a delay between two events, here it is the duration of the cause— “Consistent with psychophysical CMR, we observed that priming exposure to the masker improves signal representation in single A1 neurons. However, the mechanism(s) that underlie this effect remain unclear”
senseAND IT SAYS WHICH PART OF THE EFFECT IS NOT CORTICAL: `active cancellation via ongoing lateral inhibition may be PRIMARILY A SUBCORTICAL PHENOMENON (e.g., via wideband inhibition in cochlear nucleus)`. The paper is not only claiming a role for its own structure; it is assigning a rival mechanism elsewhere in the pathway. A positive claim and a placement of what it does not explain, in one sentence— “Our results indicate that active cancellation via ongoing lateral inhibition may be primarily a subcortical phenomenon (e.g., via wideband inhibition in cochlear nucleus) ( Pressnitzer et al., 2001 ; Neuert et al., 2004 ; Ernst and Verhey, 2006 ). We also note that perceptual masking release can also occur in the absence of masking components…

findingCoherently modulated flanking noise bands greatly increased signal-evoked spiking in mouse A1, producing a neuronal correlate of true (across-channel) comodulation masking release, whereas simply adding incoherent flanking energy did not.— “However, coherent modulation of flanking noise bands produced large enhancements in signal-evoked activity, consistent with psychoacoustic measurements and corresponding to a true release from masking ( Fig. 1 D , G ).”
findingThe population-level improvement came entirely from neurons whose receptive fields were centred at or near the 20 kHz signal frequency, not from recruitment of off-frequency neurons.— “Thus, increases in sensitivity to the signal were largely restricted to neurons tuned to frequency bands adjacent to, and encompassing, the signal frequency.”
findingContrary to the equalization-cancellation account, ongoing noise-evoked firing was facilitated rather than suppressed when bandwidth increased, so there is no cortical cancellation of the masker at the signal frequency.— “Surprisingly, increasing bandwidth produced a significant increase in firing rate for both broadband (CM and IM) conditions relative to the NB condition across this population ( Fig. 3 ; sign test, CM, p ≪…
findingOngoing noise responses during the signal window carried no information about masking release, ruling out active cancellation as the cortical mechanism.
findingCells whose onset response to broadband noise was suppressed relative to narrowband noise (negative across-frequency interaction) were the cells that later showed strong masking release, making the early noise response a predictor of CMR.
findingRemoving the 400 ms precursor of comodulated noise sharply reduced cortical signal sensitivity, showing that prior exposure to the masker (priming) is required for large across-channel CMR in A1.
findingOptogenetically silencing A1 during the early noise period reduced subsequent signal-evoked responses, establishing a causal role for cortical processing in masking release.
findingThe same laser manipulation had no effect when no early noise was present, confirming the deficit came from disrupting precursor processing and not from the light itself.
findingSensitivity differences between the best- and worst-tuned subpopulations were largest in comodulated noise (15.7 dB SNR) and smallest in incoherently modulated noise (6.12 dB SNR).

conceptpriming — THE PRIME IS A PERIOD OF NOISE. No word, no picture, no concept - an interval of acoustic exposure that leaves the cortex better able to pull a tone out of that same noise afterwards. Closest to adaptation of anything the corpus holds under this word
conceptcomodulation masking release — the psychoacoustic phenomenon being explained: a tone is EASIER to hear in noise when the noise is coherently modulated across frequency, which is counterintuitive because there is more masker, not less
conceptauditory cortex — the site, and the paper's claim is a DIRECT role rather than a correlate - established by switching it off during the priming window
conceptsegregation — what the priming period improves - the separation of overlapping sounds into discrete objects, which the abstract frames as the auditory system's central task
conceptdetection — the read-out, and it is measured in SINGLE NEURONS - the sensitivity of A1 cells to brief tones embedded in masking noise, not a behavioural report
