content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingVIP neurons in mouse V1 are activated in tight correlation with locomotion even in complete darkness, unlike nearly all neighbouring non-VIP neurons. — “We found that the neural activity of VIP neurons in mouse V1 is closely correlated with the locomotion even without visual stimulation, when most other neurons in the visual cortex show only spontaneous activity.”
findingAdding visual stimulation did not strengthen the VIP-locomotion coupling; it significantly weakened it, so the locomotion signal is not confounded with the sensory response it modulates. — “Interestingly, we found that the cross-correlation between the VIP neurons’ calcium responses and locomotion was not increased by visual stimulation with moving bars, and indeed was significantly reduced (0.28±0.03, mean±SEM, n=44, p <0.0001 comparing with no visual stimulation group, Mann-Whitney U-test)”
findingThe three inhibitory classes respond differently to running: SST neurons are suppressed, PV neurons respond heterogeneously, and VIP neurons are consistently driven. — “The average cross-correlation of SST neuron activity with locomotion was negative at time-zero, and the distribution of zero-time cross-correlation had a single negative peak”
findingBlocking nicotinic cholinergic receptors locally in V1 removed most of the VIP locomotion response, while blocking glutamatergic input did not. — “The local blockade of nicotinic cholinergic input, but not of glutamatergic input, reduced the response of VIP neurons to locomotion by more than two thirds, and measurements in vitro disclosed powerful nicotinic cholinergic input to VIP neurons.”
findingMonosynaptic rabies tracing identified the cholinergic nucleus of the diagonal band of Broca in basal forebrain as a direct input source to upper-layer V1 VIP neurons. — “Interestingly, such retrograde tracing identified the nucleus of the diagonal band of Broca (NDB), a basal forebrain nucleus enriched in cholinergic neurons, as a prominent source of input to upper layer VIP neurons in V1”
findingOptogenetically driving VIP neurons in stationary mice reproduced the locomotion-induced gain increase, and selectively killing VIP neurons removed it. — “Optogenetic activation of VIP neurons increased V1 visual responses in stationary awake mice, artificially mimicking the effect of locomotion, and photolytic damage of VIP neurons abolished the enhancement of V1 responses by locomotion.”
findingThe gain-and-loss experiments together establish VIP activation as both necessary and sufficient for the locomotion-driven enhancement of excitatory visual responses. — “Taken together, these findings indicate that VIP neuron activation is both necessary and sufficient for the increase in the visual response of excitatory cells produced by locomotion.”
findingVIP neurons in somatosensory and auditory cortex are also modulated by locomotion, though more weakly than in V1, suggesting a general cortical arrangement. — “The responses of VIP neurons in primary auditory cortex were also associated with locomotor activity”
findingLocomotion raises response gain without altering orientation tuning, and optogenetic VIP activation reproduced this selectivity-preserving profile. — “Running increases visual responses without changing stimulus selectivity (OSI) ( Niell and Stryker, 2010 ).”
findingVIP neurons track locomotion in complete darkness, when neighbouring neurons show only spontaneous activity — “To our surprise, while non-VIP neurons ( Figure 1B , green arrow) showed only low-frequency spontaneous calcium spikes similar to those when the animal was stationary, the calcium responses of VIP neurons were greatly elevated during locomotion”
findingVIP neurons are the majority of locomotion-responsive cells in V1 in the absence of visual stimulation — “While 20/28 imaged VIP neurons had a zero-time cross-correlation larger than 0.4, only 3/77 non-VIP neurons had a zero-time cross-correlation larger than 0.4 ( Figure 2C ), indicating that VIP neurons constitute the majority of the neurons responsive to locomotion in the absence of visual stimulation in mouse V1.”
findingvisual stimulation does not increase, and in fact reduces, the coupling between VIP activity and locomotion — “Interestingly, we found that the cross-correlation between the VIP neurons’ calcium responses and locomotion was not increased by visual stimulation with moving bars, and indeed was significantly reduced (0.28±0.03, mean±SEM, n=44, p <0.0001 comparing with no visual stimulation group, Mann-Whitney U-test)”
findingVIP, PV and SST neurons differ significantly from one another in their locomotion coupling — “On average, the zero-time cross-correlation of VIP neurons was 0.58±0.03 (n=21), significantly different from that of PV neurons (0.30±0.06, n=40, p =0.01, rank-sum test), and SST neurons was −0.19±0.05 (n=11, p <0.005 comparing with 0, t-test), which were also different from each other ( Figure 3D , p <0.0001, one-way ANOVA)”
findingSST neurons are suppressed by locomotion — “the calcium responses of SST cells were suppressed by locomotion ( Supplemental Figure 3C ). The average cross-correlation of SST neuron activity with locomotion was negative at time-zero, and the distribution of zero-time cross-correlation had a single negative peak”
findingnicotinic blockade cuts the VIP locomotion response sharply while glutamatergic blockade does not — “In contrast, a similar injection of the nAChRs antagonists mecamylamine (MEC) and methylycaconitine (MLA) (1 and 0.1mM, respectively) did not block the visual responses of nearby non-VIP neurons ( Supplemental Figure 4A–F ), but dramatically reduced the responses of VIP neurons to locomotion”
findingthe nucleus of the diagonal band is a prominent monosynaptic input to upper-layer V1 VIP neurons — “Interestingly, such retrograde tracing identified the nucleus of the diagonal band of Broca (NDB), a basal forebrain nucleus enriched in cholinergic neurons, as a prominent source of input to upper layer VIP neurons in V1”
findingalmost no direct motor cortex input was found, but barrel and auditory cortex input was — “While we labeled more than 50 upper layer VIP neurons in V1, only 0–1 neurons per mouse were retrogradely labeled in motor cortex or the border between motor and cingulate cortex ( Figure 5G ), suggesting at most very sparse direct input from motor cortex.”
findingoptogenetic VIP activation in stationary mice raised visual responses by about a third without changing selectivity — “Photo-activation of VIP neurons in awake stationary mice significantly increased the visual response of layer 2/3 neurons by 32.4±8.3% (mean±s.e.m.; n=19, p <0.02, paired Wilcoxon signed rank test) without producing locomotion or changing the orientation selectivity index (OSI)”
