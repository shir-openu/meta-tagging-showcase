mechanismBecause a computer sees only low-level data (pixels, letters) and not meaning, analysts extract many additional features in the hope that together they carry enough information to bridge that gap. — “Trying to “close the semantic gap” (this is the standard phrase in computer science publications) is one of the motivations for using multiple features.”
mechanismHuman side-by-side comparison breaks down as collections grow, which is what forces the substitution of computed distance in feature space for perceptual comparison. — “Manual comparison does not scale well for big data.”
mechanismThe need to compute by hand constrained the founders of statistics to one- and two-variable methods, and that constraint still shapes how data is imagined today. — “The practicality of manual computation was an important consideration for the people who were consolidating statistics in the beginning of the 20th century.”
relation — “Moretti’s point certainly applies to all other humanities fields; and it applies even more to the analysis of contemporary culture.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingClassical statistics and data science use variables in opposite ways: statistics isolates one independent and one dependent variable in order to explain a phenomenon, while data science piles up many features in order to automate a cognitive task. — “Statistics and quantitative social science that uses it ideally wants to isolate one independent and one dependent variable, because the goal is understanding the phenomenon. Data science wants to use many features in the hope that together they contain the right information for automating recognition, classification, or another cognitive task.”
findingThe feature-space concept lets the humanities' oldest method, comparison, be scaled up to very large cultural datasets. — “The concept of a geometric feature space allows us to take the most basic method of humanities – a comparison – and extend it to big cultural data.”
findingRepresenting images as points in a feature space converts visual difference into geometric distance, which is what makes machine comparison of millions of images possible. — “In such representation, the visual difference between images is equated with a distance in feature space.”
findingAutomatic classification, the single most common application of data science, is the least valuable one for the humanities, which should instead use computation to question existing categories. — “Among the contemporary applications of data science, probably the most common is automatic classification. However, in my view it is the least interesting one for humanities.”
findingDimension reduction makes the shape of a high-dimensional space visible but at the cost of interpretability, because each plotted axis stands for a combination of features rather than one feature. — “This is the serious challenge of dimension reduction algorithms – while they allow us to represent data using scatterplots where we can see the structure of a space easily, it can be quite challenging to interpret the meaning of each dimension.”
findingPlotted on average brightness and average saturation, van Gogh's Paris paintings spread more widely than his Arles paintings, most of which occupy the same region as the Paris works. — “The visualization shows that on these dimensions, van Gogh’s Paris paintings have more variability than his Arles paintings.”
findingIn a PCA map of roughly 6000 Impressionist paintings, the bright canvases the public associates with Impressionism are a minority; at least half of the works are darker and closer to conventional nineteenth-century painting. — “One interesting finding is that the types of images popularly associated with Impressionism (lower left part) constitute only a smaller part of the larger set of artworks created by these artists.”
findingInstagram tags from the 2014 Kyiv Maidan protests split into two barely-connected clusters, generic global Instagram tags and locally specific event tags, indicating two separate user populations. — “The visualization suggests that there is little interaction between these two types of tags: one group of Instagram users was using generic tags while another group was primarily tagging the local and specific events.”
