mechanismMisconceptions are robust, so an intervention that does start replacing them needs time before the replacement is measurable - which is why the benefit appears only at the delayed post-test.— “Thus, although a teaching approach or educational intervention could facilitate change from misconception to accurate understanding of scientific concepts, it may also require more time to explicitly show its effectiveness.”
mechanismThe three dialogue types complement one another - deliberation supplies critical reasoning, consensual co-construction repairs the social damage of disputes - so the productive profile is a mix, not one pure type.— “Thus, deliberative argumentation, disputative argumentation, and consensual co-construction might supplement each other to promote conceptual change in students.”
mechanismHearing group members voice both misconceptions and correct understandings makes a student self-reflect and spot the flaws in their own account, which is the route to conceptual change.— “collaborative argumentation provided opportunities for students to listen to others’ misconceptions and accurate understandings, prompting students to self-reflect and perceive the flaws in their own incomplete ideas”
mechanismHearing others' misconceptions makes a student's own incomplete idea visible to them, which is offered as the route from dialogue to conceptual change— “For example, collaborative argumentation provided opportunities for students to listen to others’ misconceptions and accurate understandings, prompting students to self-reflect and perceive the flaws in their own incomplete ideas.”
mechanismDisagreement forces re-examination of evidence that was skipped the first time— “In addition, collaborative argumentation also provided opportunities for students to be trapped in a “tug-of-war” situation, prompting students to re-examine whether key evidence was neglected before.”
mechanismExposure to how others build a concept, not just what they conclude, is what changes the way of thinking rather than the answer— “For example, it provided opportunities for students to be exposed to others’ conceptualization processes of scientific concepts, prompting students to self-reflect and identify flaws in their ways of thinking.”
mechanismDisputative dialogue works against learning by converting argument into a contest, which narrows openness to alternatives— “Perceptions of interpersonal competition might not only raise concerns about interpersonal relationships or senses of group belonging (Asterhan & Schwarz, 2016 ), but may also reduce cognitive flexibility and a person’s openness to alternative viewpoints (Carnevale & Probst, 1998 ).”
mechanismMisconceptions are robust partly because teaching presents macro, sub-micro and symbolic representations side by side instead of teaching students to move between them— “In current science education, educators and teachers commonly present scientific concepts to students with the three levels of representations, rather than supporting students to develop multileveled ways of thinking (Johnstone, 2000 ; Taber, 2013 ).”
mechanismConsensual co-construction alone consolidates a one-sided argument, which is why the productive pattern needs it together with deliberation rather than instead of it— “This dialogue between discussants showed mere development and consolidation of one-sided arguments, rather than challenging or juxtaposing different alternatives.”
