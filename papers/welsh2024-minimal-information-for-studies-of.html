mechanismthe two classical subtypes are defined by ROUTE: an exosome leaves via the multivesicular body, an ectosome buds from the cell surface. That is a biogenesis distinction, and it is the one the separation methods do not make— “The term ‘exosome’ refers to EVs from internal compartments of the cell that are released via the multivesicular body (MVB), while the term ‘ectosome’ (a.k.a., microvesicle, microparticle) refers to EVs from the cell surface.”
mechanismEV and NVEP are separated in the definition by a single structural fact - whether lipids form a delimiting bilayer - and in practice the two co-isolate, with NVEPs possibly OUTNUMBERING EVs in the sample— “(NVEPs) are all non‐EV particles made from cell‐derived components of one or more molecular classes (e.g., proteins, nucleic acids); lipids, if present, do not form a delimiting bilayer membrane. NVEPs and EVs may have overlapping physicochemical properties, and NVEPs may greatly outnumber EVs in biological matrices.”
mechanismA STANDARDS BODY FORMALLY DEPRECATES THE FIELD'S TWO MOST-USED TERMS: `ISEV RECOMMENDS USE OF THE GENERIC TERM ‘EV’ and operational extensions of this term INSTEAD OF INCONSISTENTLY DEFINED AND SOMETIMES MISLEADING TERMS SUCH AS ‘EXOSOMES’ AND ‘ECTOSOMES’`. In the nomenclature table both are marked `Discouraged unless subcellular origin can be demonstrated` - a CONDITIONAL deprecation, not a ban.— “In general, ISEV recommends use of the generic term ‘EV’ and operational extensions of this term instead of inconsistently defined and sometimes misleading terms such as ‘exosomes’ and ‘ectosomes’ that are associated with biogenesis pathways that are difficult to establish”
mechanismAND THIS CLOSES A COMPLAINT MADE BY THE SAME BODY NINE YEARS EARLIER. `lotvall2014`, the founding document of this series, said `The term ‘exosomes’ is the most commonly used word to designate ANY TYPE OF EV ... The ACTUAL MEANING of this word, however, is NOT UNIVERSALLY ACCEPTED`. It diagnosed the problem and did nothing about the word. MISEV2023 DISCOURAGES IT. A complaint in 2014 became a deprecation in 2023, by the same society, across three staged documents.— “In general, ISEV recommends use of the generic term ‘EV’ and operational extensions of this term instead of inconsistently defined and sometimes misleading terms such as ‘exosomes’ and ‘ectosomes’ that are associated with biogenesis pathways that are difficult to establish”

The 2014 document does not contain the string `MISEV` anywhere - both the expansion and the number were assigned to it retrospectively by its successors.— “the International Society for Extracellular Vesicles (ISEV) updates its ‘Minimal Information for Studies of Extracellular Vesicles’, which was first published in 2014 and then in 2018 as MISEV2014 and MISEV2018, respectively”

conceptextracellular vesicles — the generic term the society recommends, defined by release, a lipid bilayer and inability to replicate— “Definition: The term ‘extracellular vesicles’ (EVs) refers to particles that are released from cells, are delimited by a lipid bilayer, and cannot replicate on their own (i.e., do not contain a functional nucleus).”
conceptoperational extensions — prefixes on `EV` that name the measured characteristic instead of the presumed origin - the document's proposed replacement for biogenesis names— “In general, ISEV recommends use of the generic term ‘EV’ and operational extensions of this term instead of inconsistently defined and sometimes misleading terms such as ‘exosomes’ and ‘ectosomes’ that are associated with biogenesis pathways that are difficult to establish.”
conceptbiogenesis pathways — the route by which a vesicle was produced - the property the discouraged terms name and the one that is `difficult to establish`— “In general, ISEV recommends use of the generic term ‘EV’ and operational extensions of this term instead of inconsistently defined and sometimes misleading terms such as ‘exosomes’ and ‘ectosomes’ that are associated with biogenesis pathways that are difficult to establish.”
conceptmultivesicular body — the internal compartment whose release route defines an exosome— “The term ‘exosome’ refers to EVs from internal compartments of the cell that are released via the multivesicular body (MVB), while the term ‘ectosome’ (a.k.a., microvesicle, microparticle) refers to EVs from the cell surface.”
conceptexosome — EVs released from internal compartments via the multivesicular body. DISCOURAGED by ISEV unless the population was separated and characterised, because the defining route is not what any standard method resolves— “The term ‘exosome’ refers to EVs from internal compartments of the cell that are released via the multivesicular body (MVB), while the term ‘ectosome’ (a.k.a., microvesicle, microparticle) refers to EVs from the cell surface.”
conceptectosome — EVs budding from the cell surface, a.k.a. microvesicle or microparticle. Discouraged on the same ground as `exosome`, and additionally confused by decades of use to mean large EVs of any origin— “The term ‘exosome’ refers to EVs from internal compartments of the cell that are released via the multivesicular body (MVB), while the term ‘ectosome’ (a.k.a., microvesicle, microparticle) refers to EVs from the cell surface.”
conceptExtracellular particles — the overarching term for everything cell-derived in the size range, vesicular or not - the one category in this document defined by what it contains— “Extracellular particles (EPs) is the preferred overarching term for cell‐derived multimolecular assemblies in the nanometre to micron size range, including both EVs and non‐vesicular entities”
conceptnaturally — AN ADVERB DELETED FROM A DEFINITION - it was in the EV definition and was removed because it excluded engineered EVs, which the field wanted inside the category— “‘naturally’ (as in ‘naturally released’) has been removed to avoid unintended exclusion of engineered EVs or EVs produced under various cell culture conditions”
conceptnomenclature — named as one of the four standing obstacles in this field, alongside separation, characterisation and functional studies - a naming problem listed beside three technical ones— “the International Society for Extracellular Vesicles (ISEV) updates its ‘Minimal Information for Studies of Extracellular Vesicles’, which was first published in 2014 and then in 2018 as MISEV2014 and MISEV2018, respectively”
