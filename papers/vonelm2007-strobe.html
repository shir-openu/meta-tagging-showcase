phenomenonreporting quality of observational epidemiological studies
claim-typemethod
transferfrom epidemiology / statistics (observational study design): the epidemiological and statistical methodology of observational study designs (cohort, case-control, cross-sectional) and confounding control, standardized for transparent medical reporting — “We defined the scope of the recommendations to cover three main study designs: cohort, case-control, and cross-sectional studies”
mechanisma reader can only assess a study from what is written down, so completeness of the report is the precondition for any critical assessment at all — “The credibility of research depends on a critical assessment by others of the strengths and weaknesses in study design, conduct, and analysis.”
mechanismthe precedent is that a checklist actually works: CONSORT measurably improved the reporting of randomised trials — “Many medical journals supported this initiative [ 13 ], which has helped to improve the quality of reports of randomised trials [ 14 , 15 ].”
mechanismadoption runs through journals rather than authors - editorial staff of seven journals sat in the room where the list was drafted — “23 individuals attended this meeting, including editorial staff from Annals of Internal Medicine , BMJ , Bulletin of the World Health Organization , International Journal of Epidemiology , JAMA , Preventive Medicine , and The Lancet, as well as epidemiologists, methodologists, statisticians, and practitioners from Europe and North America.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]

limitationin published observational research important information is often missing or unclear (the gap STROBE addresses) — “in published observational research important information is often missing or unclear”
limitationSTROBE covers only three observational designs and does not yet extend to others such as case-crossover or ecological studies. — “We acknowledge that STROBE is currently limited to three main observational study designs.”
limitationNo comprehensive systematic review was done for the individual checklist items and no new research was conducted to fill evidence gaps. — “We did not undertake a comprehensive systematic review for each of the checklist items and sub-items, or do our own research to fill gaps in the evidence base.”
limitationThe group of contributors was shaped by existing networks and was not geographically or disciplinarily representative. — “Further, although no one was excluded from the process, the composition of the group of contributors was influenced by existing networks and was not representative in terms of geography (it was dominated by contributors from Europe and North America) and probably was not representative in terms of research interests and disciplines.”
limitationThe checklist is explicitly not a quality-assessment instrument for observational research. — “We emphasise that the STROBE Statement was not developed as a tool for assessing the quality of published observational research.”
limitationCovers only three observational designs; case-crossover and ecological studies are outside it. — “We acknowledge that STROBE is currently limited to three main observational study designs.”
limitationThe authors explicitly disclaim rigidity and standardisation - the items must be addressed somewhere, in any order or format. — “The STROBE Statement should not be interpreted as an attempt to prescribe the reporting of observational research in a rigid format.”
limitationThe checklist is not a quality-assessment instrument, and the paper says so to forestall exactly that use. — “Also, while clarity of reporting is a prerequisite to evaluation, the checklist is not an instrument to evaluate the quality of observational research.”

findingMuch biomedical research is observational and often inadequately reported, hampering assessment of strengths, weaknesses, and generalisability; STROBE offers a 22-item checklist for accurate, complete reporting. — “The reporting of such research is often inadequate, which hampers the assessment of its strengths and weaknesses and of a study's generalisability”
findingResearch should be reported transparently so readers can follow what was planned, done, found, and concluded. — “Research should be reported transparently so that readers can follow what was planned, what was done, what was found, and what conclusions were drawn”
findingThe checklist is 22 items: 18 common to all three designs, four design-specific. — “18 items are common to all three study designs and four are specific for cohort, case-control, or cross-sectional studies.”
findingThe problem is documented, not assumed: 35% of longitudinal stroke studies did not state their eligibility criteria. — “In a survey of longitudinal studies in stroke research, 17 of 49 articles (35%) did not specify the eligibility criteria [ 8 ].”
findingChoice of confounders often goes unreported, so a reader cannot tell what was adjusted for or why. — “An analysis of epidemiological studies published in general medical and specialist journals found that the rationale behind the choice of potential confounding variables was often not reported [ 6 ].”
findingCase-control studies in psychiatry frequently omit how cases and controls were identified - the step the whole design rests on. — “Only few reports of case-control studies in psychiatry explained the methods used to identify cases and controls [ 7 ].”
