findingIndividual neurons' firing rates, their rate modulation, and the order in which pyramidal cells and interneurons fired were all correlated across three different network states: theta oscillations, spontaneous ripples, and optogenetically induced ripples. — “Firing rates and rate modulations of individual neurons, and multineuronal sequences of pyramidal cell and interneuron spiking, were correlated during theta oscillations, spontaneous ripples, and synthetic ripples.”
findingBecause sequences recur during locally induced ripples that receive no CA3 or entorhinal patterning, the sequential order of CA1 spiking cannot be purely inherited from upstream regions and must partly arise from CA1's own circuitry and single-cell biophysics. — “Thus, multineuronal spike sequences are not exclusively inherited from upstream sources but result from local-circuit dynamics and biophysical properties of the individual neurons.”
findingHalf of the optogenetically induced spike sequences matched the spontaneous ripple sequence recorded at the same site, with a median rank correlation of 0.13. — “Sequences during spontaneous and induced ripples were not independent: 13 of 26 induced sequences (50%, P < 0.001) were consistent with their same-site spontaneous counterparts, with a median rank correlation of 0.13 ( P < 0.001; 0.21 for the consistent sequences; Fig. 4 D ).”
findingInterneurons made up only 12% of the participating units and were only indirectly driven by the light, yet dropping them from the analysis destroyed the cross-state consistency of the sequences, so they are essential to sequence structure. — “Thus, although interneurons comprised only a small subpopulation of the participating units (12%) and were indirectly activated during optical ripple induction, they made an important contribution to the consistency of multineuronal sequences during both spontaneous and induced ripples.”
findingWith pyramidal cells alone the correlation between theta-cycle and ripple sequence order dropped to chance, so the shared ordering across states depends on including interneurons. — “However, when only PYR (median, 22; range, 6–51) were considered, the median cross-class consistency fell to chance level ( P = 0.83; Fig. 1 G ).”
findingThe preferred firing phase of each unit within the ripple cycle was the same for spontaneous and light-induced ripples, so cycle-level spike timing is conserved between the two. — “Therefore, the precise temporal relations between single unit spiking and ripple cycles are preserved during spontaneous and induced ripples.”
findingSequence consistency was real but weak: rank correlations were broadly spread and seldom above 0.5, and the same conclusions followed whether a template or a pairwise comparison method was used. — “Thus, although rank correlations were widely distributed and rarely exceeded values of 0.5 (during theta, ripples, and induced ripples) indicating relatively loose self- and cross-class consistency of multineuronal sequences, the observations are robust to the choice of analysis method.”
findingUnder urethane anaesthesia, sequences were self-consistent within each event class but spontaneous and induced sequences did not match each other, unlike in freely moving mice. — “Spontaneous and induced (cross-class) sequences are not consistent.”
findingfiring rates during theta and during ripples were correlated neuron by neuron — “The firing rates of CA1 pyramidal cells (PYR) and putative interneurons (INT) during theta oscillations and ripple events were correlated (rank correlations: PYR: 0.59, P < 0.001, permutation test; INT: 0.89, P < 0.001; n = 268 PYR and 37 INT recorded from four freely moving mice”
findingsequences recorded during ripples matched those during theta cycles in 10 of 11 sessions — “To compare the relationship between sequential ordering of neurons during individual ripple events and theta cycles (“cross-class consistency”), we used the spike sequences during ripple events as a template against which spiking during each theta cycle was compared. In 10 of 11 cases (91%, chance level, 5%, P < 0.001, exact Binomial test), the sequences during ripples and theta were similar”
findingspike phase within the cycle was preserved between spontaneous and induced ripples — “On a neuron-by-neuron basis, the spike phases during spontaneous and induced ripples were correlated (circular–circular correlation coefficient: PYR: 0.7, P < 0.001, χ 2 test; INT: 0.42, P = 0.04; n = 97 PYR and 26 INT phase-locked during both spontaneous and induced ripples; Fig. 3 ). Therefore, the precise temporal relations between single unit spiking and ripple cycles are preserved during spontaneous and induced ripples.”
findingpyramidal cells fired about 1.8 ms before interneurons on each ripple cycle — “Most units (224 of 268 PYR, 84%; 34 of 37 INT, 95%) exhibited consistent locking to ripple phase ( P < 0.05, Rayleigh test), and phase-locked PYR spiked consistently earlier than phase-locked INT (mean± SEM phases: PYR: 156 ± 1.7°, INT: 254 ± 6.7°; corresponding to time lag of 1.8 ± 0.2 ms”

Mann-Whitney U test — “The marked increase of PYR relative to INT spiking ( P = 0.01, Mann–Whitney U test) is consistent with direct activation of PYR (and indirect activation of INT) in these animals during induced ripples.”
