Priming magnitude is comparable across a word task and an arithmetic task; changing the stimulus COLOUR does nothing; changing the RESPONSE HAND does nothing. Each absence is used as evidence rather than reported as a failure: together they say the effect is indifferent to the surface and to the domain— “The results show that the magnitude of RP was comparable between the two tasks and that changing the color or the response hand had a negligible effect on priming in either task”
sensethe two accounts of repetition priming, set out as alternatives: FACILITATION OF COMPONENT PROCESSES (repetition tunes the pathway that produces the response) against DIRECT STIMULUS-RESPONSE BINDING retrieved from episodic memory (instance theory, event file theory, action-trigger theory). senseTHE RESPONSE-HAND NULL AS A THEORY TEST, and this is why it is in the title. A binding account has the RESPONSE bound to the stimulus at encoding, so changing the hand that responds should disturb the retrieved match. It did not - the effect was `negligible`. The paper does not declare the binding account dead, and neither does this record; what is recorded is that the manipulation its logic predicts should matter, did not— “The results show that the magnitude of RP was comparable between the two tasks and that changing the color or the response hand had a negligible effect on priming in either task”
sensePRIMING IS INDEXED TO THE LEVEL THE TASK IS DIRECTED AT - the principle offered to explain why surface changes do nothing: `when a task at test required only conceptual processing, priming occurred at a SEMANTIC LEVEL rather than at the level of perceptual features ... priming operates at the level of processing at which the task is directed` (Schacter, 1990). The effect does not live in the stimulus; it lives at whatever depth the task made the participant work— “when a task at test required only conceptual processing, priming occurred at a semantic level rather than at the level of perceptual features. This indicates that priming operates at the level of processing at which the task is directed”

conceptfacilitation of component processes — the first of the two accounts: repetition makes the processing that produces the response more efficient. Nothing is bound to anything - the pathway simply runs better
conceptepisodic memory — the second account's engine: the repeated stimulus acts as a RETRIEVAL CUE, and priming is the match between that cue and the context in which the stimulus was met before. Priming as remembering rather than as tuning
conceptinstance theory — the named member of the binding family (with event file theory and action-trigger theory) - which matters here because a binding account is what the response-hand manipulation was built to test
conceptresponse hand — the manipulation that should have mattered and did not: if a response is bound to a stimulus at encoding, changing which hand makes it ought to break the match
conceptstimulus domain — the other thing that turned out not to matter - words against arithmetic equations, compared within subjects for the first time
conceptrepetition priming — the effect, and this paper's contribution is about its SCOPE rather than its size: it is the same magnitude across two very different tasks and survives changes to colour and to the responding hand
