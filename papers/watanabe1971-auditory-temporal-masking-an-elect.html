phenomenonauditory temporal masking
claim-typemechanism
mechanismA faint probe tone drives an abnormally long response latency in collicular neurons, so the probe's discharge arrives at or after the discharge evoked by the following noise burst and is absorbed into it, which produces backward masking — “In the backward masking situation with a masking interval of 0 msec, the latency in response to noise bursts calculated from the beginning of the probe tone becomes 20msec. Accordingly, the unit does not elicit backward masking in the case of a latency of less than 20msec in response to the probe tone.”
relation — “For monotic conditions of forward and backward masking, the parameters of the stimulating sounds used were the same as those employed by ELLIOT (1962a).”
relation — “As shown in Fig. 8, the relationship between the amount of masking and the masking interval between the probe tone and the noise burst shows tendencies similar to those in the psychological data of ELLIOTT (1962a, b) and PICKETT (1959), although the curves indicate some differences from neuron to neuron.”
replicationuntested
limitationOnly a small number of collicular units showed backward masking, so the effect rests on few cells. — “The number of units that showed backward masking in the inferior colliculus was relatively small.”
limitationRecordings were made under anesthesia and restraint; the authors say further work in unanesthetized, unrestrained animals is needed for the higher auditory centres. — “Further study under unanesthetized and unrestrained conditions will make it possible to explore the mechanism of masking at these particular regions.”
limitationA possible fourth mechanism (feedback inhibition from higher levels) could not be demonstrated with the present methods. — “In addition tothese modes, inhibition due to a feedback loop from upper levels may take part in backward masking as a fourth mode,”

findingForward masking weakened as the interval between the noise burst and the probe tone grew at both recording sites, but the decline was more abrupt in the inferior colliculus than in the cochlear nucleus. — “The amount of masking (lower figure) decreased with longer masking intervals as in the cochlear nucleus, although in general the decrease was more abrupt in the inferior colliculus (see also Fig. 8).”
findingBackward masking in the inferior colliculus took three forms: "on" neurons with a long latency to the probe tone (6 units), neurons inhibited by the noise burst (3 units), and "off" neurons (2 units). — “(1)" on" type neurons with long latency to the probe tone (6 units); (2) inhibition by noise bursts (3 units); and (3)" off" type neurons (2 units).”
findingA unit produced backward masking only when its latency to the probe tone exceeded the 20 msec at which the noise burst's own response arrived, so units responding within 20 msec showed none. — “In the backward masking situation with a masking interval of 0 msec, the latency in response to noise bursts calculated from the beginning of the probe tone becomes 20msec. Accordingly, the unit does not elicit backward masking in the case of a latency of less than 20msec in response to the probe tone.”
findingThe single-neuron curves relating amount of masking to masking interval followed the same trends as the published psychophysical masking data of Elliott and Pickett, although individual neurons differed from one another. — “As shown in Fig. 8, the relationship between the amount of masking and the masking interval between the probe tone and the noise burst shows tendencies similar to those in the psychological data of ELLIOTT (1962a, b) and PICKETT (1959), although the curves indicate some differences from neuron to neuron.”
findingOnly a small proportion of inferior collicular units showed backward masking at all. — “The number of units that showed backward masking in the inferior colliculus was relatively small.”
findingFast-adapting neurons form the overwhelming majority of neurons in the inferior colliculus. — “supposition is strongly supported by the recognition that fast-adapting types of neurons have an overwhelming majority in the inferior colliculus”
quantity36 — number of inferior collicular neurons tested for backward masking, of which 11 showed it in three modes — “Backward masking was examined on 36 inferior collicular neurons. Among them, 11 units manifested effects of backward masking in three major modes:”
quantity6 units; 3 units; 2 units — how the 11 backward-masking units divided across the long-latency "on", noise-inhibited, and "off" modes — “(1)" on" type neurons with long latency to the probe tone (6 units); (2) inhibition by noise bursts (3 units); and (3)" off" type neurons (2 units).”
