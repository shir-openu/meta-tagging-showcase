transferfrom politics / electoral competition: The structure of a democratic election — primaries, a winning party that stays in power and shapes later events, and opinion polls — is imported as an explicit working model of how transient neuronal coalitions compete and how one comes to dominate and constitute the conscious percept.— “The winning coalition associated with an object or event would correspond to the winning party, which would remain in power for some time and would attempt to influence and control future events.”
mechanismAttention makes an object conscious by biasing the competition between rival neuronal coalitions while those coalitions are still forming, rather than by acting on an already-formed percept.— “Attention probably acts by biasing the competition among rival coalitions, especially during their formation24.”
mechanismPositive feedback loops iteratively re-excite a neuron, driving its activity past the consciousness threshold and then holding it there for the duration of a snapshot.— “Positive feedback loops could, by iteratively exciting the neuron, push its activity increasingly upward so that the activity not only reaches above some critical threshold, but is maintained there for some time.”
mechanismFeature detectors are built by neurons detecting recurring correlations in their inputs and modifying their synapses so those inputs drive them more easily — the brain is thereby detecting apparent causation.— “A broad answer is that neurons do this by detecting common and significant correlations in their inputs and by altering their synapses (and perhaps other properties) so that they can more easily respond to such inputs.”
mechanismBecause motion is coded as a constant firing rate held fixed within one snapshot, a change of motion has no explicit representation, which is why acceleration is hard to perceive.— “It is not surprising, then, that if the eyes do not move in smooth pursuit, the brain is very poor at recognizing acceleration even though it is good at distinguishing movements36.”
builds-on Hebb's cell-assembly idea— “‘Coalitions’ implies ‘assemblies’—an idea which goes back at least to Hebb22—plus competition among them (see also ref. 23).”
extends Crick & Koch's own 1990 paper, 1995 proposal and 1998 review— “Many of our basic ideas on consciousness, such as the importance of attention and correlated firing, were outlined in our 1990 paper11, and in 1995, we suggested a plausible function for consciousness12 and later updated our ideas in a 1998 review3.”
contradicts the authors' own earlier claim (1990 paper, ref. 11) that synchronized 40 Hz firing is sufficient for the NCC— “We no longer think11 that synchronized firing, such as the so-called 40 Hz oscillations, is a sufficient condition for the NCC.”
