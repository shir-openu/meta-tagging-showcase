phenomenonneural correlates of visual consciousness
claim-typemechanism
transferfrom politics / electoral competition: The structure of a democratic election — primaries, a winning party that stays in power and shapes later events, and opinion polls — is imported as an explicit working model of how transient neuronal coalitions compete and how one comes to dominate and constitute the conscious percept. — “The winning coalition associated with an object or event would correspond to the winning party, which would remain in power for some time and would attempt to influence and control future events.”
mechanismAttention makes an object conscious by biasing the competition between rival neuronal coalitions while those coalitions are still forming, rather than by acting on an already-formed percept. — “Attention probably acts by biasing the competition among rival coalitions, especially during their formation24.”
mechanismPositive feedback loops iteratively re-excite a neuron, driving its activity past the consciousness threshold and then holding it there for the duration of a snapshot. — “Positive feedback loops could, by iteratively exciting the neuron, push its activity increasingly upward so that the activity not only reaches above some critical threshold, but is maintained there for some time.”
mechanismFeature detectors are built by neurons detecting recurring correlations in their inputs and modifying their synapses so those inputs drive them more easily — the brain is thereby detecting apparent causation. — “A broad answer is that neurons do this by detecting common and significant correlations in their inputs and by altering their synapses (and perhaps other properties) so that they can more easily respond to such inputs.”
mechanismBecause motion is coded as a constant firing rate held fixed within one snapshot, a change of motion has no explicit representation, which is why acceleration is hard to perceive. — “It is not surprising, then, that if the eyes do not move in smooth pursuit, the brain is very poor at recognizing acceleration even though it is good at distinguishing movements36.”
relation — “‘Coalitions’ implies ‘assemblies’—an idea which goes back at least to Hebb22—plus competition among them (see also ref. 23).”
relation — “Many of our basic ideas on consciousness, such as the importance of attention and correlated firing, were outlined in our 1990 paper11, and in 1995, we suggested a plausible function for consciousness12 and later updated our ideas in a 1998 review3.”
relation — “We no longer think11 that synchronized firing, such as the so-called 40 Hz oscillations, is a sufficient condition for the NCC.”
replicationuntested
limitationThe framework is deliberately restricted to primate vision and sets aside emotion and self-consciousness. — “As a matter of tactics, we have concentrated on the visual system of primates, leaving on one side some of the more difficult aspects of consciousness, such as emotion and self-consciousness.”
limitationThe authors acknowledge that relevant parts of the brain are omitted from the framework. — “We also outline some general experimental approaches to the problem and, finally, acknowledge some relevant aspects of the brain that have been left out of the proposed framework.”
limitationThe individual ideas are not new; only their combination is claimed as original. — “Most of the ideas we favor have been suggested before, but their combination is original.”
limitationThe hard problem is declared unapproachable head-on and replaced with a different, tractable target. — “No one has produced any plausible explanation as to how the experience of the redness of red could arise from the actions of the brain. It appears fruitless to approach this problem head-on. Instead, we are attempting to find the neural correlate(s) of consciousness (NCC)”
limitationBiological frameworks are said to differ in kind from those in physics, because biology has few rules without exceptions. — “Biological frameworks differ from frameworks in physics and chemistry because of the nature of evolution. Biological systems do not have rigid laws, as physics has. Evolution produces mechanisms, and often sub-mechanisms, so that there are few ‘rules’ in biology which do not have occasional exceptions.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingThe framework's central claim is that the coalition of mutually supporting neurons that wins the ongoing competition at a given moment is what the subject is conscious of. — “In general, at any moment the winning coalition is somewhat sustained, and embodies what we are conscious of.”
findingThe authors retract their earlier position that synchronized firing, in particular 40 Hz oscillations, is sufficient for the NCC. — “We no longer think11 that synchronized firing, such as the so-called 40 Hz oscillations, is a sufficient condition for the NCC.”
findingAn explicit representation of a feature is necessary for that feature to become conscious but is not on its own sufficient to produce the NCC. — “Note that an explicit representation is a necessary but not sufficient condition for the NCC to occur.”
findingConsciousness of a visual scene is claimed to begin at the top of the hierarchy with the gist of the scene, with the details filled in afterwards as activity descends to lower levels. — “Signals then start to move backward down the hierarchy so that the first stages to reach consciousness are at the higher levels (showing the gist of the scene30,31; see also ref. 32), which send these ‘conscious’ signals again to prefrontal cortex, followed by corresponding activity at successive lower levels (to provide the visual details).”
findingThe binding of the several features of one object requires no special binding mechanism: it is simply their joint membership of one coalition, spread over back and front of the brain. — “On this view, the ‘binding’ of the features of a single object/event is simply the membership in a particular coalition.”
findingThe penumbra — the neurons influenced by, but not part of, the NCC — is proposed as the substrate of unconscious priming. — “The penumbra neurons may be the site of unconscious priming48.”
findingPurely parietal damage has never been reported to abolish conscious vision, which the authors take as evidence that the conscious visual mode depends on the ventral stream rather than the parietal dorsal stream. — “There are no recorded cases of purely parietal damage which led to a complete loss of conscious vision.”
findingThe sensory cortex's job is described as building and using highly specific but multidimensional feature detectors, for properties such as orientation, motion and faces. — “The main function of the sensory cortex is to construct and use highly specific feature detectors, such as those for orientation, motion or faces.”
quantity40 Hz — the synchronized-firing frequency the authors previously proposed, and now reject, as sufficient for the NCC — “We no longer think11 that synchronized firing, such as the so-called 40 Hz oscillations, is a sufficient condition for the NCC.”
quantityten — number of headings under which the framework's ingredients are set out — “We have listed the main ingredients of our framework under ten headings.”
definitionneural correlate of consciousness (NCC) — “In round terms, the NCC is the minimal set of neuronal events that gives rise to a specific aspect of a conscious percept.”
definitionframework — “A framework is not a detailed hypothesis or set of hypotheses; rather, it is a suggested point of view for an attack on a scientific problem, often suggesting testable hypotheses.”
definitionprivate (of consciousness) — “By ‘private’, we mean that it is accessible exclusively to the owner of the brain; it is impossible for me to convey to you the exact nature of my conscious percept of the color red, though I can convey information about it, such as whether two shades of red appear to me to be the same or different.”
definitionprojective field — “This term describes the perceptual and behavioral consequences of stimulating such a neuron in an appropriate manner (for further discussion of motor and premotor cortex, see ref. 7).”
definitionexplicit representation — “An explicit representation of a particular aspect of the visual scene implies that a small set of neurons exists that responds as a detector for that feature, without further complex neural processing.”
definitionessential node — “Each node is needed to express one aspect of one percept or another.”
definitionzombie modes — “More recently, we have supported the suggestion14 that in addition to a slower, allpurpose conscious mode, the brain has many ‘zombie modes’15, which are characterized by rapid and somewhat stereotyped responses.”
definitionnet-wave — “A net-wave is a propagating wave of neural activity, but it is not the same as a wave in a continuous medium.”
definitioncoalitions of neurons — “It has many types of excitatory and inhibitory interneurons and acts by forming transient coalitions of neurons, the members of which support one another.”
definitionsnapshots (of conscious awareness) — “We propose that conscious awareness (for vision) is a series of static snapshots, with motion ‘painted’ on them34,35 (Fig. 1).”
definitionbinding — “This is the term used for the process that brings together rather different aspects of an object/event, such as its shape, color, movement and so on.”
definitionpenumbra — “The penumbra, by definition, is not itself conscious, although part of it may become part of the NCC as the NCC shifts.”
