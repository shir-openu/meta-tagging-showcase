phenomenonracial priming and grassroots political mobilization
claim-typeexistence
relation — “We placed the racial resentment questions at the end of the survey because we did not want them to contaminate our treatments rendering “the entire sample one big treatment group, washing out any effect of the racial messages” (Mendelberg 2008 , p. 137).”
replicationuntested
limitationThe treatments used explicit rather than implicit racial cues, unlike much real grassroots mobilisation and unlike prior work that found effects — “Our treatment manipulations, however, were not as extensive in their use of implicit associations as previous work that has found effects (Mendelberg 2001 ).”
limitationThe outcome measure assumes rather than observes that respondents actually completed and sent the letter — “While many organizations regularly use click-through rates as a means of analyzing the effectiveness of grassroots appeals (Congressional Management Foundation 2008 ), because we did not have the ability to assess whether individuals actually filled out the letter and submitted it to be sent to their member of Congress we were forced to rely on an assumption that individuals did fill out the form and then returned to the survey.”
limitationA parallel version of the experiment using racial images produced no effects and is not reported — “Although we do not report our findings here, we also ran the same experiment alternatively using images of white or black workers and found no effects.”
limitationThe racial resentment scale used as the key moderator is itself contested — “We recognize the critique that some scholars have of the racial resentment scale.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingWhite respondents with higher levels of racial resentment were less willing to take political action in support of a policy once it was framed as benefiting racial and ethnic minorities. — “We show that whites with higher levels of racial resentment are less likely to act politically in support of a policy perceived as benefiting ethnic and racial minorities.”
findingExposure to the racial prime did not itself raise measured racial resentment, so the prime did not contaminate the resentment measure. — “Individuals who read the text emphasizing the benefits for minorities did not differ in their levels of racial resentment from other groups.”
findingA parallel version of the experiment that used photographs of white or black workers instead of racial text produced no effects at all. — “Although we do not report our findings here, we also ran the same experiment alternatively using images of white or black workers and found no effects.”
findingThe drop in willingness to contact Congress was not produced by conservatives disliking businesses they read as progressive, because ideology interacted with the racial treatment did not show the negative effect that explanation predicts. — “We do not find that to be the case, suggesting that the decreased willingness of individuals to write their member of Congress after reading the text containing the racial and ethnic cues was not the result of conservatives being disinclined to support businesses they perceived to be progressive.”
findingAmong respondents with low racial resentment the racial cue produced no significant change in the rate of contacting Congress, only a slight decline. — “There is also no significant difference between those individuals with low levels of racial resentment, although, in this case, the percentage of respondents who contacted their member of Congress declined slightly.”
findingDropping respondents who failed the manipulation check leaves the results substantively identical and every response-rate difference significant at the same level. — “However, if we follow the more common, but erroneous, practice of removing individuals who failed the manipulation check from the sample, the results are substantively and significantly the same and all differences in response rates shown in the figures continue to be significant at the same levels.”
findingAdding socioeconomic, demographic and prior-participation controls to the models leaves the estimated treatment effects unchanged. — “The addition of these controls does not change the effects.”
findingRacial resentment and conservatism are related but distinct in this sample, since the two measures correlate only moderately. — “This shows us that, while there is some relationship between conservatism and racial resentment, the racial resentment measure is still capturing attitudes for which political conservatism does not account.”
quantity135 (64 %) — Of the 243 respondents who said they were willing to write their member of Congress, the number and share who spent at least 20 seconds on the letter form, the paper's conversion measure — “Of the 243 individuals who indicated that they were willing to write a letter to their member of Congress, 135 (64 %) of them spent 20 s or more.”
quantity48 % — The completion rate when the threshold for counting the letter form as completed is raised from 20 to 30 seconds, a change that does not alter the results — “Raising the minimum amount of time necessary to be considered as having completed the form to 30 s (lowering the completion rate to 48 %) has no significant effect on the outcomes.”
quantity.902 — Cronbach's alpha for the six-item racial resentment scale under the chosen cut point — “This divided the sample roughly in half and displays a high level of consistency (Cronbach’s alpha = .902).”
quantity.48 — Correlation between the racial resentment measure and the conservatism measure within the sample — “Within our sample, our measure of racial resentment correlates with our measure of conservatism at .48.”
quantity75 cents — Payment per respondent for the Mechanical Turk survey, which took about eight minutes — “Respondents were paid 75 cents for their responses to the survey which took respondents, on average, 8 min to complete, a payment level that is higher than that of other similar experimental survey HITs (Berinsky et al. 2012 ; Krupnikov and Levine 2014 ).”
variableracial and ethnic cues attached to the political cause (racial priming treatment) (independent) — “To address these problems, we examine the willingness of individuals to write their member of Congress in support of a non-racial political cause, which we experimentally treat with racial cues.”
variablenon-racial 'specialized' group comparison condition (independent) — “We also experimentally present a comparison with a non-racial but similar ‘specialized’ group, which allows us to distinguish concerns about race from concerns about specialized benefits objectionable to conservatives.”
