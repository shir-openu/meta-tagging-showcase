findingReviewing the neuroscientific literature, the authors find tentative support for unconscious processing of object categories and words - possibly reaching semantic content - and of emotional expressions, but describe the overall evidence as mixed. — “Although the results are mixed, we find initial support for processing object categories and words, possibly to the semantic level, as well as emotional expressions.”
findingTwo specific high-level capacities lack robust neural support without awareness: telling one individual face from another, and integrating a sentence or a scene into a whole. — “Robust neural evidence for face individuation and integration of sentences or scenes is lacking.”
findingUnconsciously perceived stimuli interact with attention and memory: attention modulates their processing, they can capture attention exogenously but not endogenously, and they can be held in memory over time. — “Attention affects the processing of stimuli that are not consciously perceived, and such stimuli may exogenously but not endogenously capture attention when relevant, and be maintained in memory over time.”
findingThe authors attribute the field's contradictory results to two concrete sources - inconsistent control of awareness across studies and individual differences between participants - and call for stricter awareness measures and within-subject testing of several processes. — “Sources of inconsistency in the literature include variability in control for awareness as well as individual differences, calling for future studies that adopt stricter measures of awareness and probe multiple processes within subjects.”
