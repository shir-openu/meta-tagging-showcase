Broadband achromaticterm·key optical metasurfaceterm·key devices.

Abstract Among various flat optical devices, metasurfacesterm·key have presented their great ability in efficient manipulation of light fields and have been proposed for variety of devices with specific functionalities. However, due to the high phase dispersion of their building blocks, metasurfacesterm·key significantly suffer from large chromatic aberrationterm·dom. Here we propose a design principle to realize achromaticterm·key metasurfaceterm·key devices which successfully eliminate the chromatic aberrationterm·dom over a continuous wavelength region from 1200 to 1680date nm for circularly-polarized incidences in a reflection scheme. For this proof-of-concept, we demonstrate broadband achromaticterm·key metalensesterm·dom (with the efficiency on the order of ∼12%) which are capable of focusing light with arbitrary wavelength at the same focal plane. A broadband achromaticterm·key gradient metasurfaceterm·key is also implemented, which is able to deflect wide-band light by the same angle. Through this approach, various flat achromaticterm·key devices that were previously impossible can be realized, which will allow innovation in full-color detection and imaging.

Introduction Wavelength dispersion is a key characteristic of optical materials, which always plays an important role in designing optical components and systems. In most dielectrics like glasses, the index of refraction decreases with the longer wavelength, which is called normal chromatic dispersionterm·dom 1 . Utilizing such materials, refractive lenses will perform larger focal lengthsterm·key and prisms will deflect at a smaller angle for longer wavelength. Such chromatic aberrationterm·dom significantly degrades the performance of full-color optical applications, such as in communication, detection, imaging, displaying etc. Taking the refractive lenses as an example, complete elimination of chromatic aberrationterm·dom at three and four wavelengths is accomplished by integrating several materials arranged into a single component to achieve the same focal lengthterm·key, which are termed as apochromatic and superachromatic lens 2 , respectively. Although successful, this strategy adds weight, complexity and cost to optical systems, which greatly limits their usage.

In contrast to the traditional bulk optical elements, metasurfacesterm·key 3 – 6 provide a new perspective on flexibly shaping the electromagnetic field 7 by manipulating its phase, amplitude as well as polarization at will via a compact and easy-of-fabrication system. Such promising approaches prove their great features and functionality in a variety of applications, such as nonlinear dynamics 8 , light beam shaping 9 – 11 , highly dimensional holographic images 12 – 15 , polarization control and analysis 16 – 18 , invisible cloak carpet 19 etc. In addition to the compact dimension, the main distinction between metalensesterm·dom (planar lenses based on metasurfacesterm·key) and traditional optical lensesterm·dom is the integrated functionality that allows the achievement of specific characteristics such as multispectral 20 , dual-functional 21 and off-axis focusing 22 . Recent works reported the dielectric metasurfacesterm·key for metalensterm·key with high numerical aperture (NA) and optical planar camera with monochromatic aberrations that are comparable with the commercial products, presenting their potential prospect in real applications 23 , 24 . However, these proposed metasurfaceterm·key devices still exhibit large chromatic aberrationterm·dom, which is mainly attributed to following fundamental properties: the resonant phase dispersion of the building blocks used in metasurfacesterm·key, the intrinsic dispersion in used materials, as well as the different phase accumulation through light propagation in free space with various wavelengths. Many works have shown that optical metasurfacesterm·key working in a relatively broad wavelength bandwidth can be achieved 25 – 30 . However, in these reports the optical performance of resonant elements in the metasurfacesterm·key is unchanged in a broad bandwidth and this is insufficient for the suppression of chromatic aberrationterm·dom.

Up to now, several pioneering works have realized multi-wavelength achromaticterm·key metalensesterm·dom that are capable of eliminating the chromatic aberrationterm·dom at several discrete wavelengths 31 – 37 . Very recently, there has been a successful demonstration of an achromaticterm·key metalensterm·key which is able to suppress the chromatic effect over a 60-nm bandwidth in the visible 38 . However, although continuous, the working bandwidth (about 11.4% of the central wavelength) is still not broad enough for practical purposes and there is still lack of an effective design rule for unit elements in achromaticterm·key metasurfaceterm·key devices. Therefore, much work remains to be done in order to accomplish a broadband achromaticterm·key flat optical component working over a continuous bandwidth because of the abrupt phase change from the resonant unit elements. In this work, we employ the integrated-resonantterm·key unit element in metasurfacesterm·key with smooth and linear phase dispersion combining with geometric phaseterm·dom to design broadband achromaticterm·key flat optical components. For the proof of concept, achromaticterm·key converging metalensesterm·dom and beam deflector with gradient metasurfacesterm·key are demonstrated that are valid at will within a broad infrared (IR) bandwidth from wavelength of 1200 to 1680date nm. The broadband achromaticterm·key metasurfaceterm·key devices based on our approach greatly innovate a complete class of designs in full-color light manipulation and imaging and push them forward to the practical applications.

The geometric phaseterm·dom, also called Berry phaseterm·dom, is produced from the rotation of the resonant elements in metasurfaceterm·key with a circularly-polarized incidence 39 . The produced phase modulation only depends on the orientation of scattered elements, which can therefore work as the solution for the wavelength-independent phase modulation. Since the mechanism of this resonant phase response is completely different from the geometric phaseterm·dom, these two parts of phase won’t disturb with each other, but can be simply merged together.

To maintain the linear relation with 1/ λ , φ shift ( λ ) has to match the form as φ shift ( λ ) = α / λ + β , with α = χ λ max λ min λ max - λ min and β = - χ λ min λ max - λ min , where χ denotes the largest additional phase shift between λ min and λ max at the central position of the BAMLterm·dom. It is the key parameter in the design of BAMLterm·dom, especially for the determination of the diameter of BAMLterm·dom. How to design special unit elements to compensate the phase difference at corresponding position is subsequently the crucial issue. Here, for a proof-of-concept demonstration, the working wavelength is chosen in the commonly used telecom region, i.e., { λ min , λ max }→{1200, 1680date nm}.

Design of integrated-resonantterm·key unit elements In our design, the basic building blocks of the BAMLterm·dom are metallic nano-rods (MNRs) and their assemblies, coupled MNRs. The metal-dielectric-metal structural configuration (insets in Fig. 2 ) is implemented to access a cavity-like resonance so as to enhance the operating efficiency of metasurfacesterm·key 40 , 41 . Generally, a single MNR is able to support multiple plasmonic resonances, such as the fundamental dipolar mode and various high order modes. An abrupt phase shift usually occurs when these plasmonic resonances are excited, which does not fit the requirement of smooth and linear phase difference Δ φ ′( R , λ ) (Supplementary Fig. 1 ). In contrast, the phase distribution between these resonances exhibits a smooth trend with a nearly linear profile against 1/ λ . Since such state is fall in between the two strong resonant modes, it could be termed as an integrated-resonant stateterm·dom. In addition, such integrated-resonantterm·key features provide a promising method to slickly compensate the phase difference for BAMLterm·dom. Moreover, since the produced phase compensationterm·key owns a linear relation with of 1/ λ , the BAMLterm·dom will naturally be achieved at any wavelength within the working bandwidth as long as the largest phase difference Δ φ ′( R , λ min ) is exactly compensated by the designed unit elements. This indicates that the slope of the phase profile can be tuned via tuning the wavelength distance between two resonances. Therefore, one can effectively steer the phase compensationterm·key δ ( λ ) provided by the integrated-resonantterm·key unit elements composed of MNRs or coupled MNRs, which obeys δ λ = S 1 λ - 1 λ max = C 1 λ - 1 λ max ∕ 1 λ R 1 - 1 λ R 2 . The largest phase compensationterm·key produced by these unit elements between λ min and λ max therefore becomes δ = C 1 λ min - 1 λ max ∕ 1 λ R 1 - 1 λ R 2 , which can be directly utilized to cover the largest phase difference Δ φ ′( R , λ min ) required at any position in BAMLterm·dom.

For the case of slight slope S corresponding to the small phase compensationterm·key, a single MNR with large aspect ratio is utilized, in which the fundamental dipole mode and high order mode are excited. When the wavelengths of these two plasmonic modes are tuned far away from each other, the small phase compensationterm·key is realized. The detail information of the single MNR structure with large aspect ratio can be found in Supplementary Fig. 2 . To improve the operating efficiency, multiple MNRs are integrated within an integrated-resonantterm·key unit element. Following this approach, the smallest phase compensationterm·key, also a key parameter for determining the size of BAMLterm·dom, can be achieved at around 30˚, as shown in Fig. 2a . The electric field distributions at the wavelengths corresponding to the dipole mode and second order plasmonic mode are respectively plotted in Fig. 2b .

The larger phase compensationterm·key can be acquired by decreasing the difference between two resonant wavelengths of the MNR. With the aspect ratio of the MNR decreasing, the dipole mode along its short axis can be excited instead of the second order plasmonic modes, being the nearest resonant mode from the dipole mode along the long axis. With the wavelength distance between these two modes shrinking, larger phase compensationterm·key is obtained. As shown in Fig. 2c , two dipole modes can be evidently observed in the operation efficiency spectrum, in which 120˚ phase compensationterm·key is realized. The electric field distributions at these two resonant wavelengths are shown in Fig. 2d , which obviously indicates their identities.

It will turn into another issue when the phase compensationterm·key is larger than 150˚ in such integrated-resonantterm·key MNRs, in that case steering the relation between two resonances is insufficient, and one more resonance is necessary to be introduced into the working wavelength region. However, it is quite challenging to exhibit three resonances within the concerning wavelength band using single MNR. Therefore, a compound structure composed of two perpendicularly aligned MNRs with different lengths and widths is utilized to introduce the third plasmonic resonant mode. Assuming that the second resonant wavelength λ R 2 located near the middle of resonant wavelength region { λ R 1 , λ R 3 }, the slopes of phase spectrum of two wavelength regions { λ R 1 , λ R 2 } and { λ R 2 , λ R 3 } can be close to S 21 = C 21 ∕ 1 λ R 1 - 1 λ R 2 ~ S 32 = C 32 ∕ 1 λ R 2 - 1 λ R 3 , assuming C 21 ∼ C 32 = C . In fact, the phase compensationterm·key can be further increased by introducing more resonances to the integrated-resonantterm·key unit element, which will enable a BAMLterm·dom with larger size (Supplementary Fig. 10 ).

Design and characterizations of the broadband achromaticterm·key gradient metasurfaceterm·key Generally, our design principle can be used to realize various flat optical components without chromatic effect like the broadband achromaticterm·key gradient metasurfaceterm·key (BAGMS). The design of a BAGMS to deflect a normally incident light by the same angle in a broad wavelength range is demonstrated (Fig. 5a ). The building blocks are the same as those used in the above achromaticterm·key metalensterm·key, and the orientation of each unit element is designed to obey the required phase distribution: 5 φ x , λ = - 2 π λ x sin θ d where x is the spatial coordinate and θ d is the reflected angle of anomalous beam, namely the LCP light beam reflection with RCP incidence. To realize the phase requirement for deflecting light with the same θ d at different incident wavelengths, the specially designed unit elements with proper orientation are implemented to compensate the phase difference Δ φ ( x , λ ), as shown in Fig. 5b . The detailed information of design is shown in Supplementary Note 5 . As predicted, all the LCP light reflected angles are kept almost unchanged when the incident wavelength is changed from 1200 to 1650 nm, showing a broadband achromaticterm·key performance in anomalous beam deflection (see Fig. 5c ). Methods Sample Fabrication A 150-nm-thick Au mirror and a 3-nm-thick Cr were first deposited on a Si substrate by e-gun evaporator. A 60-nm-thick SiO 2 dielectric spacer is then deposited by using plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition (PECVD). A 100-nm-thick ZEP520A layer is consequently spin coated at 5000 rpm on the prepared substrate and then bake on a hot plate for 2 min at 180 °C. Subsequently, an Espacer layer is spin coated at 1500 rpm on the ZEP520A layer. Espacer is an organic polymer with high conductivity to reduce the positional error during the e-beam exposure process. An e-beam writing system at the acceleration voltage of 100 keV with 100 pico-ampere of current is used. The structural profile of metasurfacesterm·key is defined by the e-beam exposure and development process. The final sample will be obtained after the 3-nm-thick Cr and 30-nm-thick Au deposition and lift-off process.
