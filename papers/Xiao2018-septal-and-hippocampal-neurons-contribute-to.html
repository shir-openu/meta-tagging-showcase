research question — evidence — “The transmission of auditory information to the hippocampus has been thought to come through two major pathways. One is the lemniscal pathway, which is the ascending auditory pathway from the cochlear nucleus to the auditory cortex (AC) via the lateral lemniscus ( Syka and Masterton, 1988 ; Webster et al., 1992 ; Winer and Schreiner, 2011 ); beyond the AC, auditory signals go to the association cortex, entorhinal cortex (EC) and hippocampus ( Vinogradova, 1975 ; Steward, 1976 ; Germroth et al., 1989 ). The other is the non-lemniscal pathway, which consists of the brainstem auditory nuclei, brainstem reticular nucleus, medial septum (MS) and hippocampus ( Vinogradova, 1975 ; Baisden et al., 1984 ; Moxon et al., 1999 ). However, these pathways have not yet been functionally confirmed.”
contribution — evidence — “The hippocampus has been thought to process auditory information. However, the properties, pathway, and role of hippocampal auditory responses are unclear. With loose-patch recordings, we found that hippocampal neurons are mainly responsive to noise and are not tonotopically organized. Their latencies are shorter than those of primary auditory cortical (A1) neurons but longer than those of medial septal (MS) neurons, suggesting that hippocampal auditory information comes from MS neurons rather than from A1 neurons.”

As the predictive relationship between the CS and US is learned, subsequent presentations of the CS alone elicit a conditional response, such as freezing. Discussion

In the current studies, we explored the auditory responseterm properties of hippocampalterm neurons, the auditory pathwayterm to the hippocampus, and the role of hippocampalterm auditory responses in auditory fear conditioningterm·mth using electrophysiological and behavioral assays combined with pharmacological manipulations. We made four major findings. (1) Neurons in the hippocampus (EC, CA3term, and CA1term regions) responded only to noiseterm burst stimuli in awaketerm mice. (2) Hippocampalterm auditory inputs came from the MSterm and not from A1term. (3) MSterm inactivationterm·mth impaired the acquisitionterm of auditory fear conditioningterm·mth. (4) Auditory fear conditioningterm·mth was not associated with a specific frequencyterm of sound. These findings provided the following implications.

The hippocampus is a well-known structure of the limbicterm system. In our current study, approximately 15% of the neurons in the hippocampus responded to noiseterm burst stimuli ( Figures 1B – D , 2 , 4 , 5 , 6B,C ). This result was consistent with those of the previous studies demonstrating that neurons in the limbicterm system, such as in the amygdalaterm, striatumterm, cingulate cortex and septum, can be driven more effectively by noiseterm bursts and clicks ( Vinogradova, 1975 ; Bordi and LeDoux, 1992 ; Chen et al., 2014 ). A few neurons in the hippocampus responded to intense toneterm bursts ( Figures 2B,C ), as reported previously ( Kaifosh et al., 2013 ; Chen et al., 2014 ). However, no clear TRF or frequencyterm preferences were observed ( Figure 2B ), unlike the neurons in the amygdalaterm and the striatumterm, which are tonotopically organized ( Bordi and LeDoux, 1992 ; Chen et al., 2012 ). Therefore, the auditory pathwayterm to the hippocampus is different from that to the amygdalaterm and the striatumterm.

The auditory system includes the lemniscalterm and non-lemniscalterm ascending pathways ( Hu et al., 1994 ; Jones, 2003 ). The non-lemniscalterm pathwayterm, or the limbicterm system, is sensitive to temperature and drugs, especially to anesthetics ( Vinogradova, 1975 ; Dickinson et al., 2003 ; Kaifosh et al., 2013 ). This is why the noise-evoked responses were blocked by sodium pentobarbital ( Figure 1 ). Therefore, auditory sub-threshold responses have been recorded only in awaketerm subjects ( Abe et al., 2014 ). However, several studies have recorded auditory evoked potentials and single units in anesthetizedterm animals ( Miller and Freedman, 1995 ; Krause et al., 2003 ; Dissanayake et al., 2008 ). This may have been due to the differences in extracellularterm·mth recordingterm·mth and/or anesthetics.

Although the non-lemniscalterm pathwayterm from the brainstem reticular formation relayed by the MSterm projects to the hippocampus ( Vinogradova, 1975 ; Baisden et al., 1984 ; Moxon et al., 1999 ; Kaifosh et al., 2013 ), auditory information from the lemniscalterm pathwayterm is thought to be processed by the association cortex prior to entering the hippocampus via the EC ( Vinogradova, 1975 ; Steward, 1976 ; Germroth et al., 1989 ; Moxon et al., 1999 ). However, the responses of hippocampalterm neurons were different from those of lemniscalterm pathwayterm neurons. Additionally, the shorter latencyterm than the A1term neurons ( Figures 4A,B ) and previous reports ( Bickford-Wimer et al., 1990 ; Moxon et al., 1999 ) did not support this processing. Nevertheless, the onset latencyterm of the MSterm neurons was shorter than those of the EC, CA3term, and CA1term neurons ( Figures 4A,B ). It seems that the auditory inputs to the hippocampus come from the MSterm rather than from A1term. The direct evidence is that the sound-evoked discharges in the EC and CA3term neurons were blocked by silencing the MSterm ( Figure 6 ) rather than by silencing A1term ( Figure 5 ). In addition, this auditory information processing pathwayterm from the MSterm to the hippocampus has been doubly confirmed by behavioral measurements of auditory fear conditioningterm·mth of silenced MSterm animal controls to silenced A1term; inactivationterm·mth of MSterm but not of A1term blocked the CS-induced fear behaviorterm ( Figure 8 ). This septo-hippocampal pathwayterm also processes other sensory information, e.g., visual, olfactory and somatosensory information ( Vinogradova, 1975 ; O’Keefe and Nadel, 1978 ; Kaifosh et al., 2013 ).

Auditory fear conditioningterm·mth includes traceterm fear conditioningterm·mth and delayterm fear conditioningterm·mth. Traceterm fear conditioningterm·mth has a temporal gap between the toneterm (CS) and aversive electrical footshock (US), whereas delayterm fear conditioningterm·mth does not. It is well known that the hippocampus is required for traceterm fear conditioningterm·mth but is not required for delayterm fear conditioningterm·mth ( Raybuck and Lattal, 2011 ). Pentobarbital anesthesia can inactivate the neurons in the hippocampus ( Figure 1 ) and the MSterm ( Figure 3B ).
