phenomenonanyonic excitations in two-dimensional lattice spin models— “Excitations in this model are anyons, meaning that the global wavefunction acquires some phase factor when one excitation moves around the other.”
claim-typemethod— “These anyons can be used to perform universal quantum computation.”
method — evidence— “From now on, we are constructing and studying nonabelian anyons which will allow universal quantum computation.”
transferfrom algebraic topology (homology and cohomology): 1-homologies and 1-cohomologies over Z2 used to describe the protected subspace of the lattice model, so that the stored information is a topological invariant rather than a local one— “In a more abstract language, the algebra (with coefficients from Z2), 1-homologies and 1-cohomologies.”
mechanismA PHYSICAL SYSTEM IS RE-DESCRIBED AS A COMPUTER, AND THE RE-DESCRIPTION IS MARKED AS ONE: a two-dimensional quantum system with anyonic excitations CAN BE CONSIDERED AS a quantum computer, with unitary transformations performed BY MOVING THE EXCITATIONS AROUND EACH OTHER and measurements BY JOINING EXCITATIONS IN PAIRS. `Can be considered as` is a way of regarding, not an identity, and the whole paper rests on it— “A two-dimensional quantum system with anyonic excitations can be considered as a quantum computer. Unitary transformations can be performed by moving the excitations around each other. Measurements can be performed by joining excitations in pairs and observing the result of fusion.”
mechanismA PROPERTY IS LOCATED IN THE PHYSICS RATHER THAN IN THE PROTOCOL: such computation is said to be FAULT-TOLERANT BY ITS PHYSICAL NATURE - so what is elsewhere achieved by a procedure is here claimed as a feature of the material. The same word, fault-tolerance, is being attached to a different kind of thing— “Such computation is fault-tolerant by its physical nature. A quantum computer can provide fast solution for certain computational problems (e.g. factoring and discrete logarithm [1]) which require exponential time on an ordinary computer.”
mechanismA PHYSICAL PROCESS IS RE-DESCRIBED AS AN ALGORITHM AND THE HEDGE IS LEFT IN: the spins' mutual interaction forcing a flipped spin back is said to be QUITE SIMILAR TO THE STANDARD ERROR CORRECTION PROCEDURE FOR THE REPETITION CODE, after which - WE MAY SAY THAT ERRORS ARE BEING CORRECTED AT THE PHYSICAL LEVEL. The identification is offered as a manner of speaking— “If some spin flips to the opposite direction, the interaction forces it to flip back to the direction of other spins. This process is quite similar to the standard error correction procedure for the repetition code. We may say that errors are being corrected at the physical level.”
mechanismTHE ARGUMENT PROCEEDS BY ASKING WHY AN EXISTING PRACTICE IS UNNECESSARY: classical computation CAN ALSO be performed fault-tolerantly, HOWEVER IT IS RARELY DONE IN PRACTICE BECAUSE CLASSICAL GATES ARE RELIABLE ENOUGH - and the next sentence is WHY IS IT POSSIBLE. The paper's question is generated by noticing that a technique is available and unused— “Needless to say, classical computation can be also performed fault-tolerantly. However, it is rarely done in practice because classical gates are reliable enough. Why is it possible?”
mechanismTHE EXPLANATION IS SOUGHT IN THE EASIEST CASE FIRST AND THE CHOICE IS ANNOUNCED: LET US TRY TO UNDERSTAND THE EASIEST THING - why classical information can be stored reliably on magnetic media - before the quantum case is attempted. The order of explanation is stated rather than left implicit, and the chosen starting point is a fact about ordinary technology— “Let us try to understand the easiest thing — why classical information can be stored reliably on a magnetic media. Magnetism arise from spins of individual atoms. Each spin is quite sensitive to thermal fluctuations.”
mechanismA THRESHOLD IS REPORTED AS THE OBSTACLE, AND ITS SIZE IS WHAT MATTERS RATHER THAN ITS EXISTENCE: an arbitrary circuit CAN be simulated with imperfect gates PROVIDED they are close to ideal up to a constant precision - and UNFORTUNATELY THE THRESHOLD VALUE IS RATHER SMALL, IT IS VERY DIFFICULT TO ACHIEVE THIS PRECISION. The theoretical solution is granted and then declared impractical by a number— “An arbitrary quantum circuit can be simulated using imperfect gates, provided these gates are close to the ideal ones up to a constant precision δ. Unfortunately, the threshold value of δ is rather small1; it is very difficult to achieve this precision.”
mechanismA PROBLEM IS DECLARED SOLVED FROM ONE STANDPOINT AND OPEN FROM ANOTHER, WITH BOTH NAMED: physical realization is A BIG CHALLENGE and decoherence is one important problem - but FROM THE PURELY THEORETICAL POINT OF VIEW, THIS PROBLEM HAS BEEN SOLVED. Solved and unsolved at once, distinguished by which standpoint the word is being used from— “Physical realization of a quantum computer is a big challenge for scientists. One important problem is decoherence and systematic errors in unitary transformations which occur in real quantum systems. From the purely theoretical point of view, this problem has been solved due to Shor’s discovery of fault-tolerant quantum computation [2], with…
mechanismAN EXPLICIT CAUTION AGAINST CONFLATING TWO THINGS THE SAME MATHEMATICS DESCRIBES: the local operators SHOULD NOT BE INTERPRETED AS symmetry transformations - the true symmetry transformations are a different set, and MATHEMATICALLY THEY ARE DESCRIBED BY THE SAME ALGEBRA, BUT THEIR ACTION ON PHYSICAL STATES IS DIFFERENT. One formalism, two referents, and the paper stops to say so— “Caution. The local operators should not be interpreted as symmetry transformations. The true symmetry transformations, so-called topological operators, will be defined in Sec. 6. Mathematically, they are described by the same algebra , but their action on physical states is different.”
mechanismA TERM OF ART IS CONCEDED AND THEN SET ASIDE AS NOT THE RELEVANT PROPERTY: ONE MAY SAY that these codes have QUITE POOR PARAMETERS, and WELL, THEY ARE NOT GOOD CODES IN THE SENSE OF a cited definition - HOWEVER the code corrects almost any multiple error of a stated size, SO THESE CODES WORK. The established criterion is granted, and then shown not to be the criterion that matters here— “One may say that the toric codes have quite poor parameters. Well, they are not “good” codes in the sense of [17]. However, the code TOR(k) corrects almost any multiple error of size O(k2). (The constant factor in O(. . .) is related to the percolation problem). So, these codes work if the error rate is constant”
