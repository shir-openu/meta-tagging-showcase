findingMore than half of both inhibitory and excitatory inferior colliculus neurons in the mouse locked their firing to the binaural beat carried in the sound envelope, i.e. they were envelope-ITD sensitive. — “More than half of both GABAergic and glutamatergic neurons showed synchronization to the binaural beat frequency in envelopes”
findingThis is the first demonstration of envelope-ITD sensitivity anywhere in the mouse auditory pathway. — “To the best of our knowledge, the neural sensitivity to the ITD in the envelope has not been shown in the auditory pathway of the mouse previously.”
findingThe proportion of ITD-sensitive neurons did not differ between glutamatergic and GABAergic cells. — “The percentages of the neurons synchronized to the beat frequency were not significantly different between glutamatergic and GABAergic neurons (p = 0.0625, paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test).”
findingCharacteristic delay coincided with the trough rather than the peak of the ITD curve in most cells, identifying them as trough-type. — “These results showed that CD was close to trough in the most IC neurons.”
findingWithin the mouse's physiological ITD range the ITD curve slopes positively toward the contralateral side for both cell types, so firing is higher when the contralateral envelope leads. — “In most ITD curves, the slope of the ITD curve within the physiological ITD range (gray filled square) was positive toward contralateral sides, regardless of the cell type and stimulation type”
findingAbove 256 Hz modulation, phase-locking to the binaural beat exceeded phase-locking to the monaural contralateral stimulus, so the high-frequency ITD sensitivity cannot be built by coincidence detection inside the IC. — “However, in most responses, the VS contra was smaller than the VS beat when the modulation frequency was higher than 256 Hz.”
findingRate modulation transfer functions of most IC neurons of both types fell into the low-rate, low-pass or band-reject classes for sinusoidal modulation. — “Most neurons were classified as low rate, low pass or band reject in the response to SAM, and this was true for both GABAergic and glutamatergic neurons”
findingGlutamatergic neurons that were synchronized to the beat fired faster than unsynchronized neurons of either type at 64 and 128 Hz modulation. — “the firing rates of synchronized glutamatergic neurons were significantly higher than the unsynchronized responses of both GABAergic and glutamatergic neurons at the modulation frequencies of 64 (P < 0.05, Tukey HSD test)”
findingThe slope of the ITD curve near zero did not differ between GABAergic and glutamatergic neurons. — “The slope was not significantly different between the GABAergic and glutamatergic neurons, either (P = 0.519, Two-way ANOVA test).”
findingmore than half of both GABAergic and glutamatergic neurons synchronised to the binaural beat frequency in the envelopes — “More than half of both GABAergic and glutamatergic neurons showed synchronization to the binaural beat frequency in envelopes”
finding47 neurons were recorded, 18 GABAergic and 29 glutamatergic — “We recorded the responses to binaural beat AM sound from 47 neurons, of which 18 were GABAergic and 29 were glutamatergic.”
findingcharacteristic phase was close to 0.5 in both cell types, which is the trough-type pattern — “In the both GABAergic and glutamatergic neurons, CPs were close to 0.5 ( Fig. 5E - G ), both to SAM (GABA, 0.46 ± 0.02, n =5, GLU, 0.50 ± 0.01, n = 9) and raised sine stimuli (GABA, 0.41, n = 1, GLU, 0.46 ± 0.03). This observation is consistent with the trough-type response of most neurons in the mouse IC.”
findingthe squared deviation from the unity line was significantly smaller for mean trough time than mean peak time, 0.87 against 6.62 ms squared — “To compare the proximity to CD, we calculated the squared deviation of each point from the unity line ( Fig. 5H , dotted line) in the scattered plot of mean trough time and peak time. The squared deviation was significantly smaller in mean trough time than in mean peak time (trough, 0.87 ± 0.38 ms 2 , peak, 6.62 ± 1.31 ms 2 , n= 21, P = 0.00061, Wilcoxon signed rank test).”
findingwithin the physiological ITD range the slope was positive toward the contralateral side, whatever the cell type or stimulus — “In most ITD curves, the slope of the ITD curve within the physiological ITD range (gray filled square) was positive toward contralateral sides, regardless of the cell type and stimulation type”
findingthe same directional pattern holds for level differences in these neurons, so the two cues would add rather than oppose — “Interestingly, this firing pattern shaped by envelope ITD matches that of the ILD, because in the IC, the ILD also evokes a higher firing rate when the contralateral sound level is more intense than the ipsilateral sound level”
findingVS contra was larger than VS beat when the modulation frequency was lower than 128 Hz, and smaller than VS beat when it was higher than 256 Hz — “As was shown in the SOC, the VS contra was larger than the VS beat when the modulation frequency was lower than 128 Hz ( Fig. 2C , D ). However, in most responses, the VS contra was smaller than the VS beat when the modulation frequency was higher than 256 Hz.”
findingTHE LOCAL MECHANISM EXCLUDED BY THE PAPER'S OWN DATA: sensitivity at high modulation frequency is not likely to be shaped by coincidence detection in the IC — “These results suggested that the ITD sensitivity to the binaural beat with high modulation frequency was not likely to be shaped by the coincidence detection in the IC neurons.”
findingTHE INGREDIENT IS WORSE THAN THE PRODUCT: a local mechanism needs highly phase-locked monaural inputs, and monaural phase-locking degrades where the sensitivity persists — “However, in order to obtain ITD sensitivity initially, it is necessary for the monaural contralateral and ipsilateral synaptic inputs to be highly phase-locked to the envelope. Our results suggest that the phase-locking of IC neurons to monaural sounds may be insufficient to explain the ITD sensitivity. The phase-locking to the monaural contralateral sound degraded in the IC neurons at higher modulation frequencies ( Fig.2C , D ) despite the fact that some IC neurons had ITD-sensitivity to high-frequency modulation. Thus, it is unlikely that the ITD sensitivity to the high-frequency modulation is shaped within the IC itself.”
findingso the sensitivity is more likely inherited from brainstem neurons than built in the midbrain — “It is more likely that ITD-sensitivity in the IC is inherited from the response properties of the neurons in the brainstem.”
findingARTEFACT BOUNDED RATHER THAN ASSUMED AWAY: the raised sine broadens the spectrum by less than 10 percent even at 512 Hz, so its effect is judged limited — “Since the spectrum width of the carrier noise was shown to affect the sensitivity of the envelope ITD in LSO neurons, the broadening of the spectrum in the raised sine stimuli might affect the ITD sensitivity in the IC neurons. To validate the effect, we measured the spectrum of modeled waveforms and found that the raised sine stimuli had a broader spectrum band than one-octave noise as the modulation frequency increased. However, the broadening of the spectrum band was less than 10% even at a modulation frequency of 512 Hz when the roll-off factor was 16 (data not shown). So, the effect of the spectrum change is likely to be limited.”

findingNULL AGAIN: fewer GABAergic than glutamatergic neurons synchronised to the raised sine, and that difference was not significant either — “Fewer GABAergic neurons were synchronized than glutamatergic neurons but this was not a significance difference (p = 0.125, paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test).”
findingthe ITD slope was neither significantly different between envelope shapes nor between cell types — “The slope was slightly higher, but not significantly so, in the responses to the raised sine (GABA, 15.2 ± 11.3 Hz/ms, n = 12, GLU, 17.7 ± 7.6 Hz/ms, n = 32) as opposed to the SAM (GABA, 4.6 ± 2.6 Hz/ms, n = 27, 9.2 ± 3.5 Hz/ms, n = 47) (P = 0.117, Two-way ANOVA test).”
findingTHE AUTHORS DEFLATE THEIR OWN RESULT: the firing-rate change within the physiological range was very small, so the cue may have minimal effect on these neurons — “The present data shows that the most IC neurons increased their firing when the contralateral sound preceded the ipsilateral sound. However, the change in firing rate within physiological ITD range was very small in most neurons ( Fig. 6 ). So, the ITD may have minimal effects on the firing rate of the IC neurons.”
findingAND OFFER A VESTIGIAL READING: the sensitivity may belong to a circuit mice no longer use for binaural hearing — “It is possible that ITD sensitivity to the neural envelope in IC is part of a neuronal circuit that is no longer used by mice for binaural hearing, similar to that discussed for the Mexican free-tailed bat”
findingthe trough of the ITD curve covers most of the mouse's whole physiological range — “The small head size of a mouse may limit the “physiological” range of ITD to ±50 μsec, and the trough of the ITD curve covers most of that range.”
findingthe escape route offered is echoes: real environments would deliver longer delays than the head alone allows — “Nevertheless, mice might experience longer ITDs in a natural environment with sound reflections and echoes, and these could drive the LSO neurons.”
findinghumans detect these envelope differences well despite limited high-frequency hearing, which is the opposite asymmetry — “In contrast, it is well established that human subjects can detect the ITD in high-frequency amplitude modulated tones ( Bernstein et al., 1994 ; Bernstein et al., 2002 ; Bernstein et al., 2008 ; Bernstein et al., 2012 ; Klein-Hennig et al., 2011 ; McFadden et al., 1976 ; Nuetzel et al., 1976 ). This is despite the relatively limited high-frequency hearing in humans.”
findingthe finding is stated as new for the mouse auditory pathway — “The present results demonstrated that over half of the neurons in the mouse IC respond an ITD in the envelope of a high-frequency sound. In the IC, the neurons are either GABAergic or glutamatergic, and both are sensitive to the envelope ITD. To the best of our knowledge, the neural sensitivity to the ITD in the envelope has not been shown in the auditory pathway of the mouse previously.”
