mechanismCategory-specific gamma indexes local sensory processing that is a necessary precondition for conscious perception rather than its direct correlate— “Thus, an alternative explanation for the findings of Fisch et al. (2009) is that category-specific GBRs in sensory cortices reflect local processing, which is a necessary prerequisite for but not the direct correlate of conscious perception.”
mechanismStronger local preprocessing raises the probability that a stimulus reaches awareness, which manufactures a correlation between gamma and 'seen' reports without gamma causing awareness— “In other words, in a paradigm such as used by Fisch et al. (2009) , it cannot be ruled out that the enhanced GBR just reflected more effective preprocessing of stimuli that enhanced the chances of these stimuli to get perceived consciously.”
mechanismDifferences in unconscious local processing propagate into different conscious outcomes, so local responses can correlate with awareness while being prerequisites of it— “In typical experimental paradigms, differences in local unconscious processing might lead to different outcomes regarding conscious perception, and therefore, specific local responses can correlate with conscious perception without necessarily being direct correlates of conscious experience.”
mechanismthe criterion is invariance, not correlation: a correlate of consciousness must move whenever consciousness moves, whatever moved it— “A clear prediction is that neural processes directly reflecting conscious perception should be present regardless of how it comes about.”
mechanismthe disconfirmation rests on that invariance failing, which is why a single dissociation is enough to reject the proposal— “It is this lack of invariance of the local GBR that makes it unlikely that GBR reflect a mechanism directly responsible for conscious perception.”

mechanismthe equivalence of the two routes is established behaviourally first, across three separate samples, because the whole argument collapses if one manipulation is simply stronger
