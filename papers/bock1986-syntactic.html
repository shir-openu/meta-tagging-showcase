phenomenonsyntactic persistence in language production— “This involves the repetition of syntactic forms across successive utterances.”
claim-typeexistence— “Speakers tend to repeat the syntactic forms of sentences in subsequent utterances that are minimally related in lexical, conceptual, or discourse content.”
method — evidence— “All of the experiments employed a syntactic priming paradigm that created a controlled analog of the situations that have revealed syntactic repetition in natural speech.”
mechanismsyntactic (structural) priming: producing or hearing a sentence structure makes speakers more likely to reuse that same structure in a later, content-unrelated utterance - so syntax is represented and primed independently of meaning— “Speakers tend to repeat the syntactic forms of sentences in subsequent utterances that are minimally related in lexical, conceptual, or discourse content”
extends activation-based theories of language production— “The advantage of the syntactic activation hypothesis is that it provides a unitary explanation for phenomena that otherwise seem quite disparate.”
builds-on the competition model of Bates and MacWhinney (1982; MacWhinney, Bates, & Kliegl, 1984)— “This view of linguistic rules as graded rather than fully determinate is comparable to that incorporated into the competition model of Bates and MacWhinney (1982; MacWhinney, Bates, & Kliegl, 1984).”
extends Levelt and Kelter (1982) and Weiner and Labov (1983) on repetition of syntactic form in natural speech— “A parsimonious explanation of these results, and those of previous investigations (Levelt & Kelter, 1982; Weiner & Labov, 1983), is that the production of sentences results in the activation or strengthening of the syntactic rules or procedures involved in their creation.”
replicates Experiment 2 of this paper— “The results thus replicated those of Experiment 2, but with a different cover task that encouraged fuller processing of the primes.”
sense**THE EFFECT IS ON WHAT SPEAKERS CHOOSE, AND IT IS SYMMETRIC:** `Active and passive utterances EACH increased in frequency by 8% following corresponding primes.` Not a latency, not an accuracy - a shift in which of two grammatical options a speaker takes, in both directions equally. **The symmetry matters**: a bias toward one form would be explicable by that form being easier, and a bias toward whichever form was just heard is not— “Active and passive utterances each increased in frequency by 8% following corresponding primes.”
sense**AND THE DEFLATIONARY READING IS CLOSED IN ONE SENTENCE:** `However, LEXICAL REPETITION DOES NOT SEEM TO BE A NECESSARY COMPONENT of the priming effect.` Prime and target share no words, and the effect survives. **This is the sentence that makes it SYNTACTIC priming rather than a long-range word priming** - and every other priming record in this corpus (meyer1971, neely1977, hutchison2003) is about exactly the relation Bock is ruling out— “However, lexical repetition does not seem to be a necessary component of the priming effect.”
sense**IT MEETS lashley1951 ACROSS THIRTY-FIVE YEARS AND NEITHER CITES THE OTHER.** Lashley: `syntax is NOT INHERENT in the words employed or in the idea to be expressed` - argued from the impossibility of associative chains, with no experiment attached. Bock: syntactic form persists across utterances sharing neither words nor message. **The argument and the demonstration**, both merged into this corpus tonight, thirty-five years and one discipline apart— “The repetition effect was found for two different types of sentences, with use of the alternative forms of each type (active versus passive transitives, and prepositional versus double-object datives) varying as a function of the form of a previous sentence.”
sense**AND THE STRATEGIC EXPLANATIONS ARE NAMED AND REMOVED:** `These variations occurred under conditions in which there was LITTLE OR NO IMPETUS for the active maintenance of the priming sentences in memory, or for the use of syntactic devices to create discourse coherence.` Not rehearsal, not cohesion. **The same move as marcel1983's second mask and erb2019's overlap control** - naming the alternatives and closing them rather than reporting the effect and leaving them standing— “These variations occurred under conditions in which there was little or no impetus for the active maintenance of the priming sentences in memory, or for the use of syntactic devices to create discourse coherence.” [checked against the archived source, not this rendering]
controlthe filler items were tested on a schedule that reached 33, 78, 85 and 100 per cent by the end of each successive quarter of the list — held — rules out that recognition performance depends on how recently an item appeared rather than on the priming manipulation— “In every list, 33, 78, 85, and 100% of all previous filler items were tested by the end of each of the four respective quarters of the list.”

conceptsyntactic persistence — **PRIMING OF FORM RATHER THAN CONTENT.** Hearing a passive makes you produce a passive - about a different picture, with different words. `The repetition effect was found for two different types of sentences ... varying AS A FUNCTION OF THE FORM of a previous sentence`. Every other priming record in this corpus primes an item with a RELATED item; this one primes a structure with no shared content— “The repetition effect was found for two different types of sentences, with use of the alternative forms of each type (active versus passive transitives, and prepositional versus double-object datives) varying as a function of the form of a previous sentence.”
conceptpriming effect — here measured as a change in what speakers CHOOSE to say, not how fast they say it. **`Active and passive utterances each increased in frequency by 8%`** - a production measure, so the effect is on selection among grammatical alternatives rather than on recognition latency— “Active and passive utterances each increased in frequency by 8% following corresponding primes.”
conceptlexical repetition — whether the prime and the target share words. **The obvious deflationary explanation, and the paper closes it:** `lexical repetition does not seem to be a NECESSARY COMPONENT of the priming effect.` Without that sentence the result reads as word priming with extra steps— “However, lexical repetition does not seem to be a necessary component of the priming effect.”
conceptpassive — one of the two transitive forms the effect runs between, and **the alternation is what makes the measure possible** - a speaker describing a picture can say it either way, so a shift in the proportion is a shift in nothing but form— “Active and passive utterances each increased in frequency by 8% following corresponding primes.”
