phenomenonsyntactic persistence in language production
claim-typeexistence
mechanismsyntactic (structural) priming: producing or hearing a sentence structure makes speakers more likely to reuse that same structure in a later, content-unrelated utterance - so syntax is represented and primed independently of meaning — “Speakers tend to repeat the syntactic forms of sentences in subsequent utterances that are minimally related in lexical, conceptual, or discourse content”
relation — “The advantage of the syntactic activation hypothesis is that it provides a unitary explanation for phenomena that otherwise seem quite disparate.”
relation — “This view of linguistic rules as graded rather than fully determinate is comparable to that incorporated into the competition model of Bates and MacWhinney (1982; MacWhinney, Bates, & Kliegl, 1984).”
relation — “A parsimonious explanation of these results, and those of previous investigations (Levelt & Kelter, 1982; Weiner & Labov, 1983), is that the production of sentences results in the activation or strengthening of the syntactic rules or procedures involved in their creation.”
relation — “The results thus replicated those of Experiment 2, but with a different cover task that encouraged fuller processing of the primes.”
replicationuntested
limitationit is unclear whether syntactic intrusions/perseverations can be accounted for in the same way — “In the case of syntactic intrusions or perseverations, however, it is not clear that the effects can be accounted for in a comparable way.”
