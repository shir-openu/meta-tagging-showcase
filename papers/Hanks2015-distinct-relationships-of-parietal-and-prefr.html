findingPosterior parietal cortex encodes the running value of the accumulated evidence as a graded quantity, through a map that does not change over the course of the trial. — “This analysis revealed that PPC encoded the accumulator’s evolving value a(t) using a graded map that is stable across time ( Fig. 3a ).”
findingThe frontal orienting fields instead encode mainly the sign of the accumulator, splitting activity into two clusters corresponding to the two available choices. — “Neurons in the FOF exhibited a more categorical mapping between firing rate and the sign of the accumulator value that tended to cluster into an a>0 and an a<0 group, while the mapping was again stable across time ( Fig. 3b ).”
findingThe FOF tuning curve was significantly steeper at the zero-crossing than the PPC tuning curve, quantifying the graded-versus-categorical distinction. — “A direct comparison of the time-averaged tuning curves showed a significantly larger slope at the zero-crossing for FOF compared to PPC ( Fig. 3c ; PPC: 0.058 ± 0.003, FOF: 0.158 ± 0.015, mean ± 95% CI), which indicates a sharper transition of firing rate between negative and positive accumulator values for FOF.”
findingClicks influenced FOF firing about 100 ms earlier than PPC firing, suggesting sensory input reaches FOF by a route that does not pass through PPC. — “The longer mean latency of the click-triggered average in PPC (~200 ms) compared to FOF (~100 ms) suggests that sensory information in this task may reach the FOF through circuits that bypass the PPC.”
findingSilencing FOF across the whole trial produced a significant bias toward choices on the same side as the inactivation. — “Full-trial inactivation (2 s period from 500 ms before auditory stimulus onset until 500 ms after stimulus end, Fig. 4a ) resulted in a significant ipsilateral choice bias (10.3 ± 3.0%, p<0.01, mean ± SEM across rats).”
findingAmong four 500-ms inactivation windows, only silencing at the very end of the stimulus changed behavior; silencing during early accumulation did not. — “Only peri-choice inactivation led to a significant ipsilateral bias ( Fig. 4b , 10.6 ± 1.0%, p<0.01).”
findingThe same end-of-stimulus specificity held with 250-ms windows: shifting the perturbation just 250 ms earlier abolished the effect. — “Again, only the peri-choice perturbation had an effect on behavior ( Fig. 4c , 5.4 ± 0.8%, p<0.01), while the effect of perturbation just 250 ms earlier was smaller than the peri-choice effect (p<0.01) and not statistically significant (p=0.45).”
findingModel comparison favored a bias applied after categorization over biases acting on the accumulation process itself. — “Consistent with the idea that the FOF’s dominant role is to control the categorical choice, a model-based analysis indicated that a post-categorization bias explained these optogenetic inactivation data significantly better than alternative forms of bias that directly affected the accumulation process 18 (p < 0.05, see Methods; Extended Data Fig. 8 ).”
findingThe inactivation-induced bias was uncorrelated with how many clicks were played or how long the stimulus lasted, as expected if FOF is not part of the accumulator. — “Finally, and again consistent with the FOF playing a role that is separate from the click accumulation process, we found no correlation between choice biases induced by unilateral perturbation and click counts or stimulus duration ( Extended Data Fig. 9 ).”
findingThe authors conclude that evidence accumulation happens upstream of the FOF, challenging the view that prefrontal cortex implements accumulation. — “These results suggest that accumulation occurs upstream to FOF and call into question the prevailing view that prefrontal cortex is part of the neural circuit for accumulation of evidence 3 , 5 , 7 , 8 .”
findingTHE CLASSICAL ANALYSIS MAKES THE TWO AREAS LOOK ALIKE and the tuning curve assay separates them — “Classical analyses uncovered correlates of accumulating evidence, similar to previous observations in primates and also similar across the two regions. However, tuning curve assays revealed that while the PPC encodes a graded value of the accumulating evidence, the FOF has a more categorical encoding that indicates, throughout the trial, the decision provisionally favored by the evidence accumulated so far.”
findingside-selective neurons in both PPC and FOF ramped upwards for preferred-direction stimuli with a slope proportional to the stimulus strength, and downwards for anti-preferred ones — “Side-selective neurons in both PPC and FOF exhibited average firing rates that were initially constant in time and then ramped upwards for stimuli in the preferred direction with a slope proportional to the stimulus strength.
