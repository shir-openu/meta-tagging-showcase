method — evidence— “York Five reaction time experiments explored word-frequency effects in wordnonword decision tasks and in pronunciation and memory tasks”
mechanismrepetition priming: a prior encounter with a word leaves a lasting memory trace that speeds its later recognition, reducing the usual word-frequency effect in lexical decision— “prior presentations of words produced substantial and apparently long-lasting reductions in the basic frequency effect” [UNVERIFIED AGAINST THE STAGED TEXT]
extends theories of the word-frequency effect— “The modification of frequency effects by repetition suggests that natural language frequency effects may be attributed partly to the recency with which words have occurred” [UNVERIFIED AGAINST THE STAGED TEXT]
contradicts Forbach, Stanners and Hochaus (1974) and Kirsner and Smith (1974), whose lexical-decision repetition results diverge from those reported here— “However, two recent reports with a similar paradigm appear to differ with several conclusions reported here”
contradicts Landauer and Streeter (1973) on orthographic neighbourhood differences between common and rare words— “For highfrequency words, each word averaged 6.2 neighbors as compared with an average of 7.9 neighbors for low-frequency words, which is a nonsignificant difference, £(66) = 0.4, in the opposite direction to that reported by Landauer and Streeter”
builds-on Forbach, Stanners and Hochaus's (1974) repetition experiment in the lexical decision task— “Experiments 1 and 2 are similar to an experiment reported by Forbach, Stanners, and Hochaus (1974”
sense**THE DISSOCIATION IS IN THE DECAY, NOT THE SIZE, AND THAT IS WHY IT IS EVIDENCE.** For WORDS: `The mean repetition effect for lags of 0 and 1 intervening trials was 87 msec; for lags of 7 and 15, it was 77 msec.` For NONWORDS the same measure falls from 75 msec to 18. **Nearly identical benefit at zero lag; utterly different survival.** Same task, same lags, same subjects - the only difference is lexicality, and a short-term store cannot produce that pattern because it would decay for both— “The mean repetition effect for lags of 0 and 1 intervening trials was 87 msec; for lags of 7 and 15, it was 77 msec.”
sense**AND THE HEADLINE IS THAT REPETITION EATS THE FREQUENCY EFFECT:** `in the word-nonword lexical decision task, prior presentations of words produced substantial and apparently LONG-LASTING REDUCTIONS in the basic frequency effect.` Showing a low-frequency word once narrows the gap that word frequency opens - **so the field's most reliable variable is partly a fact about recent exposure**, which is the claim that makes this paper matter to a corpus about what terms mean— “Further, in the word-nonword lexical decision task, prior presentations of words produced substantial and apparently long-lasting reductions in the basic frequency effect.”
sense**THE REASONING IS ADDITIVE-FACTORS AND THE SCHEMA CANNOT HOLD IT:** `If word frequency and word repetition each affect DIFFERENT stages of processing in the word-nonword task, their effects should be ADDITIVE` - and they interact, so `the repetition effect and word frequency both affect A COMMON STAGE of processing.` Two variables located in the same mechanism by the shape of their interaction. **There is no edge type for `these share a mechanism`** - not causes, not part-of, not same-as - so an inference the whole paper turns on lives only in this prose. Filed with Q-826's list. **The audit caught this sense quoting two sentences and grounded on one** - the second is now inside the span, which is the same fault I found on Jones2002 three hours ago and the same fix— “If word frequency and word repetition each affect different stages of processing in the word-nonword task, their effects should be additive. That these variables appear to interact rather than add supports the argument that the repetition effect and word frequency both affect a common stage of processing.”
sense**AND THE LIMIT IS STATED AGAINST THEIR OWN HEADLINE:** `This persistent frequency effect suggests that a complete explanation of word frequency will NOT be found in recency and repetition.` Having shown that repetition durably reduces the frequency effect, they say in the next breath that it does not dissolve it. **Compare meyer1971's `we do not have ways to test all the possible explanations` and gotts2012's `similar studies ... have been lacking`** - three papers in this corpus writing the limit flat, forty-five years apart— “This persistent frequency effect suggests that a complete explanation of word frequency will not be found in recency and repetition.”
controlstimuli were counterbalanced across subjects for lag and for case — held — rules out that a repetition effect is carried by the particular items at a particular lag, or by a change of letter case— “Across subjects within each response group, stimuli were counterbalanced in terms of lag and case.”
controlthe design was intended to separate repetition from practice, and the paper reports that it could not do so completely — failed — rules out that the repetition effect is general practice at the task rather than memory for the particular item— “On the other hand, only a few stimuli were involved, and the design was inadequate to rule out completely explanations in terms of practice effects.”
