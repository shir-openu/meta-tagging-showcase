findingSilencing PV interneurons in the prefrontal cortex produced deficits only in the cognitive domain, whereas non-selective PFC lesions disrupted behaviours mapping onto all three schizophrenia symptom categories.
findingBoth PV-silenced and lesioned mice reinvestigated previously explored holes more often than their controls, despite equal overall nose-poke numbers.
findingSpontaneous alternation in the Y-maze was reduced in both PV-silenced and lesioned mice relative to controls.
findingIn the 60-second delayed match-to-place task control mice improved significantly on match trials while PV-silenced mice did not, so temporally extended working memory also requires PV interneurons.
findingInitial water-maze acquisition was normal in both PV-silenced and lesioned mice, indicating spatial reference memory itself does not depend on the PFC.
findingReversal learning was impaired in both manipulations, but PV-silenced mice eventually reached criterion while over 30% of lesioned mice never did, so the PV deficit is transient and the lesion deficit persistent.
findingLesioned mice failed to discriminate a familiar from an unfamiliar conspecific, whereas PV-silenced mice showed normal sociability and social recognition.
findingAmphetamine produced hypersensitivity — higher peak locomotion and slower decay — in lesioned mice only.
