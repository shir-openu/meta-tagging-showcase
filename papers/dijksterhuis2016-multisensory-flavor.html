content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingFlavor perception in food contexts is driven jointly by bottom-up sensory input and top-down expectations rather than by sensory input alone. — “In food-related applications of flavor, both bottom-up (sensory) as well as top-down (expectations) processes are at play.”
findingThe interactions that produce a flavor percept mostly operate without the perceiver's awareness. — “Most of the complex interactions that this leads to take place outside the awareness of the perceiving subject.”
findingThe chapter proposes a model in which five classes of past and present aspects (sensory, surroundings, social, somatic, sentimental) jointly determine flavor perception, liking and choice. — “A model is presented where many, past and current, aspects (sensory, surroundings, social, somatic, sentimental) of a (flavor) perception, together result in the perception of a flavor, its liking. or its choice.”
definitionflavor (as a multisensory, cognitively constructed percept) — “Flavor is multisensory; several interacting sensory systems-taste, smell, and mouthfeel-together comprise "flavor," making it a cognitively constructed percept rather than a bottom-up sensory one.”
definitiontaxonomy of priming situations — “In this chapter, some of the complications this entails for flavor priming are introduced, along with a taxonomy of different priming situations.”
