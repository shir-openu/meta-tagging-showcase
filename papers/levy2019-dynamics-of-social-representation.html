phenomenonsocial representation in medial prefrontal cortex
claim-typeexistence
mechanismElevated baseline cortical noise degrades the mPFC's ability to form category-specific social representations and to drive the synaptic plasticity that refines them — “The strong negative correlation we observe between baseline noise and social category representations suggests that noise plays an important role in the failure of mPFC activity in Cntnap2-/- mice to appropriately represent social stimuli and drive synaptic plasticity processes.”
mechanismLoss of CNTNAP2 impairs targeting of AMPA and NMDA receptors to post-synaptic membranes, removing the plasticity that would otherwise refine odour-category representations with experience — “The loss of experience-based refinement of odor category representation in Cntnap2-/- mice might be linked with the role of CNTNAP2 protein in targeting AMPA and NMDA receptors to post-synaptic membranes78,79.”
relation — “These findings expand upon recent work describing experience-dependent divergence of conspecific sex representations in the hypothalamus76 and medial amygdala77.”
relation — “Furthermore, recent work by Lazaro et al. (“Reduced prefrontal synaptic connectivity and disturbed oscillatory population dynamics in the CNTNAP2 model of autism”, bioRxiv 2018) demonstrates reduced dendritic spine and synapse densities in the mPFC of Cntnap2-/- mice, providing a potential mechanism for the functional impairments we describe.”
replicationuntested
limitationThe social/nonsocial distinction is a distinction between two sets of odours. — “The average unit tuning, calculated as the normalized odor-evoked response magnitude in all units that showed stimulus-associated responses, was higher for both male and female cues than for all nonsocial odors, regardless of odor identity”
limitationTwo of the three behavioural measures did not differ between social and nonsocial stimuli. — “The probability of odor-directed orientation responses was higher during delivery of social cues (Fig. 1c). However, the response latency (Fig. 1d) and the stimulus-evoked increase in locomotion (Fig. 1e) did not significantly differ between social and nonsocial stimuli”
limitationThe paper's central quantity is a log ratio of distances between activity patterns. — “To quantify the separation between representation of social (S) and nonsocial (NS) stimuli for each mouse, we calculated a social distinction index”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingIn the mouse medial prefrontal cortex, nearly a quarter of recorded units were selective for social odours, twice as many as were selective for nonsocial odours or showed mixed responses. — “Almost a quarter of recorded units displayed selectivity to social signals - twice the number of units with nonsocial odor selectivity or mixed social/nonsocial responses (Fig. 2c left and Supplementary Fig. 2c-e).”
findingThe mPFC population code is organised by social versus nonsocial category rather than by odour identity or valence: responses to the two social cues were more similar to each other than to any nonsocial odour. — “The block-diagonal structure of the dissimilarity matrix reflects a category-based organization of the population codebook in the mPFC: encoding distributions of the social cues were significantly more similar to each other than to any of the nonsocial odors, regardless of odor identity or valence (Fig. 3d).”
findingAverage unit tuning was stronger for both male and female social cues than for any of the three nonsocial odours, independent of odour identity. — “The average unit tuning, calculated as the normalized odor-evoked response magnitude in all units that showed stimulus-associated responses, was higher for both male and female cues than for all nonsocial odors, regardless of odor identity (Fig. 2e).”
findingIn wild-type mice the social distinction index rose significantly from the first to the second recording day, showing experience-dependent refinement of the social representation. — “The SDI values consistently and significantly increased between the first and second recording days for all wt mice.”
findingCntnap2 knockout mice showed no experience-dependent change: their social distinction index was unchanged between the two recording sessions. — “In contrast, no consistent change occurred in the Cntnap2-/- group, and mean SDI values in these mice remained unchanged between sessions (Fig. 4e).”
findingThe autism-model deficit is specific to categorisation rather than to stimulus encoding: single-odour decoding was equally accurate in both genotypes, but social/nonsocial category decoding was significantly worse in Cntnap2 knockouts. — “Remarkably, decoding performance for individual odors was similar in wt and Cntnap2-/- mice, whereas performance for odor category (S or NS) was significantly inferior in Cntnap2-/- mice (Fig. 5d).”
findingBaseline (ongoing) population activity was noisier in Cntnap2 knockout mice than in wild-type littermates, with the two genotypes almost completely non-overlapping on this measure. — “We found that this measure of noise was significantly higher in Cntnap2-/- mice compared to their wt littermates (Fig. 6b-c; note that with the exception of one mouse, the noise measure of the two genotypes were completely non-overlapping).”
findingMean baseline firing rates of mPFC units were higher in Cntnap2 knockout mice than in wild-type littermates. — “Consistently, we found that the mean baseline firing rates of mPFC units recorded in Cntnap2-/- mice were significantly higher than those recorded in their wt littermates (Fig. 6a).”
findingAcross both genotypes, the level of baseline neuronal noise was strongly negatively correlated with the social distinction index. — “Remarkably, we found that across genotypes, baseline neuronal noise showed a strong negative correlation with SDI values (Fig. 6d).”
findingBaseline cortical noise predicted how much a mouse's social representation refined with experience: higher noise went with a smaller day-to-day gain in the social distinction index. — “Strikingly, ΔSDI values in individual mice were strongly correlated with baseline cortical noise, such that elevated noise levels were predictive of decreased experience-dependent refinement of mPFC social representation (Fig. 6e).”
findingThe neural preference for social cues was not explained by behaviour: latency to orient and stimulus-evoked locomotion did not differ between social and nonsocial odours. — “However, the response latency (Fig. 1d) and the stimulus-evoked increase in locomotion (Fig. 1e) did not significantly differ between social and nonsocial stimuli.”
