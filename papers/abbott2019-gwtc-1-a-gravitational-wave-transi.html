mechanismtemplate density why noise triggers pile up at low chirp mass - the template bank is much more densely packed there, so noise has more chances to match something— “The concentration of our marginal triggers at low chirp masses is consistent with expectations for noise triggers, because search template waveforms are much more densely packed at low masses.”
mechanismarrival-time difference sky position is reconstructed from the difference in arrival time across separated detector sites, which is why adding Virgo to the two LIGO sites improves localisation so sharply— “As the sky location is primarily determined by the differences in the times of arrival of the GW signal at the different detector sites, LIGO-Virgo coincident events have a vastly improved sky localization, which is crucial for electromagnetic follow-up campaigns”
mechanismpipeline-specific noise a noise trigger is a function of the pipeline that found it, so the same noise is not expected to appear in both searches - which is why agreement between pipelines carries information and disagreement does not immediately count against a candidate— “As noise triggers are generically a function of the details of the pipeline that identifies a trigger, we do not typically expect to see the same noise triggers in each pipeline.”
extends the individual LIGO-Virgo detection papers for GW150914, GW151226, GW170104, GW170608, GW170814 and GW170817, whose significances and parameter estimates this catalog replaces— “The updated results we report here supersede those previously published.”
confirms an independent catalog by another group which reanalysed the O1 data with the PyCBC pipeline and found the same three events, with identical FAR bounds for two of them— “That catalog also presents search results from the PyCBC pipeline for O1 and also finds GW150914, GW151012, and GW151226 as the only confident gravitational-wave events in O1, with identical bounds on FAR to the PyCBC results in Table I for GW150914 and GW151226.”
qualifies the collaboration's own earlier designation of LVT151012 as a trigger rather than an event, which the improved pipelines overturn— “this event meets the criteria of Sec. IVA for a gravitational-wave event, and we henceforth relabel this event as GW151012.”
senseweakly modeled - THE PAPER COINS THIS ABOUT ITSELF AND SAYS WHY. cWB is introduced as an unmodeled search, but the analysis reported here restricts it to signals that chirp in the time-frequency plane, so it is no longer model-free. The authors change the word rather than keep the stronger one— “In contrast, the cWB search relies on no specific physical models of the source waveform, though in results presented here it does impose a restriction that signals are “chirping” in the time-frequency plane. We therefore refer to it as weakly modeled.”
sensemarginal - NEITHER ESTABLISHED NOR EXCLUDED, and the paper holds that line to the end. A marginal trigger passed the false-alarm threshold but was given less than a 50% probability of astrophysical origin by both matched-filter searches. It is not a non-detection and not a weak detection: it is a candidate the collaboration declines to resolve, and it says so in the conclusions as well as the results— “In addition, we list 14 GW candidate events that have an estimated false-alarm rate (FAR) less than 1 per 30 days in either of the two matched-filter analyses but whose astrophysical origin cannot be established nor excluded unambiguously (Sec. VII).”
sensefalse-alarm rate - AN EMPIRICAL RATE MEASURED AGAINST TIME-SHIFTED DATA, NOT A PROBABILITY THAT THE EVENT IS NOISE. Where a candidate outranks every background trigger the search can only report a BOUND, and the bound depends on how much background time was available - which is why some FARs here are larger than the ones previously published for the same events— “This restriction means, however, the minimum bound on the false-alarm rate of candidates that have a higher ranking statistic than any trigger in the background sample is larger than it would be if longer periods of data are used for the time-shift analysis.”
senseindependent, of the three searches - INDEPENDENCE OF METHOD, NOT OF EVIDENCE. The conclusions say `three independent analyses` flatly; what is independent is the pipeline, the ranking statistic and the noise model, while the strain data are the same in all three. The body is more careful than the conclusion, which is worth knowing before the agreement between them is read as three confirmations— “The signals were discovered using three independent analyses: two matched-filter searches [8,9] and one weakly modeled burst search [11].”
senseGW versus marginal - A THRESHOLD CONVENTION, NOT A PROPERTY OF THE UNIVERSE. The boundary is a false-alarm rate ceiling of 1 per 30 days followed by a 50% astrophysical probability, both chosen by the collaboration. An event just under the line and one just over it differ by the convention, and the paper presents the choice as a choice— “we assign the “GW” designation to any event for which the probability of astrophysical origin from either matched-filter search is greater than 50%”
sensedetection, in the phrase `no new gravitational-wave events were discovered` - THE O1 REANALYSIS FOUND NOTHING NEW BUT DID CHANGE AN OLD ANSWER. The same data through better pipelines moved LVT151012 across the threshold and it is now GW151012. So the null result is about new events only; the catalog's contents still changed— “These improved FAR measurements for GW151012 are the most salient result of the reanalysis of O1 with the O2 pipelines; no new gravitational-wave events were discovered.”
