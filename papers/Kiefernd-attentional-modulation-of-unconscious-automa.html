phenomenonmasked semantic priming (N400)— “In two experiments, it was investigated whether masked semantic priming effects can be modulated by temporal attention to the prime using a cueing procedure.”
claim-typemechanism— “The present results show that temporal attention to the prime is a prerequisite for obtaining masked N400 priming effects.”
method — evidence— “ERPs were recorded while subjects performed a primed lexical decision task.”
mechanismTemporal attention amplifies the activation of the masked prime's stimulus representation, and this amplification is what allows an unconsciously perceived prime to trigger the automatic semantic process indexed by the N400— “Attention is thought to amplify the activation of the stimulus representation.”
mechanismAttentional amplification acts on conscious and unconscious stimuli alike, so attention and awareness are separable— “We therefore propose that attention is able to enhance the processing of both consciously and unconsciously perceived stimuli.”
mechanismA temporal window of attention opened around the predicted target boosts processing of primes falling close to it in time— “Temporal attention is assumed to amplify the processing of the masked primes that were presented temporally close to the targets.”
extends Naccache, Blandin & Dehaene (2002)— “The Naccache et al. (2002) study provides supportive evidence for an attentional modulation of unconscious, automatic processes, but also has some limitations.”
contradicts Schneider & Shiffrin (1977); Posner & Snyder (1975)— “These results therefore challenge the classical view of automatic processes to occur autonomously and independent of attentional influences (Schneider & Shiffrin, 1977; Posner & Snyder, 1975).”

sense**THE DEFINITIONAL CLAIM IS QUOTED HERE VERBATIM AND THEN CONTRADICTED BY THE SAME PAPER.** `In classical theories of attentional control, INDEPENDENCE OF CAPACITY-LIMITED ATTENTION is a DEFINING FEATURE of automatic processes (Posner & Snyder, 1975).` If independence from attention is what makes a process automatic, and the process turns out not to be independent of attention, then either it is not automatic or the definition is wrong. **The paper takes the second option and keeps the word** - which is precisely the move this corpus exists to make visible, and it is made in an introduction without comment— “In classical theories of attentional control, independence of capacity-limited attention is a defining feature of automatic processes”
sense**THE RESULT, AND `PREREQUISITE` IS A STRONGER WORD THAN `MODULATES`:** `The present results show that TEMPORAL ATTENTION TO THE PRIME IS A PREREQUISITE for obtaining masked N400 priming effects.` Not amplified, not enhanced - required. Without attention in the prime's time window there is no masked N400 priming at all. **neely1977 built a design to separate the automatic component from the attentional one; this says the separation does not hold at the boundary**— “The present results show that temporal attention to the prime is a prerequisite for obtaining masked N400 priming effects.”
sense**AND THE CONTROL THAT MAKES IT INTERESTING IS ONE CLAUSE IN THE HYPOTHESIS:** masked priming is amplified when attention is directed to the stimulus stream in the prime's time window, `EVEN IN THE ABSENCE OF ANY PRIME AWARENESS.` Attention moves; awareness stays at zero. **Without that clause the result reads as `attending made the prime visible`** - a different and far less interesting paper. Same manoeuvre as marcel1983's second mask and eckstein2007's awareness measures. **Sixth record running where the instrument turns out to be part of the phenomenon**, and the first where the thing being modulated is the definition itself— “We hypothesized that masked priming is amplified when attention is directed to the stimulus stream in the time window of masked prime presentation, even in the absence of any prime awareness.”
controla CONTROL EXPERIMENT identical to Experiment 1 except that the cue stimulus was not task-relevant and did not direct attention — held — rules out that the modulation follows from the cue's physical presence rather than from the attention it directs— “We therefore conducted a control experiment in which the experimental design was the same as in Experiment 1 except that the cue stimulus was not task-relevant and did not prompt subjects to attend to the stimulation stream.”
controlfiller trials at an intermediate cue-prime interval were included — held — rules out that subjects learn the trial-length structure and prepare differently for short and long intervals— “Filler trials with an intermediate CPI were also included in order to have smoother transitions between trial lengths.”
notetemporal attention —causes→ N400 — **`PREREQUISITE` IS WHY THIS IS 0.95 AND WHY `increases` UNDERSTATES IT.** The enforced axis vocabulary offers increases / reduces / eliminates / no-change, and the claim here is stronger than any of them: without temporal attention the effect does not occur. The nearest available value is `increases` and it is recorded with that gap stated rather than papered over. **A fifth value - `is-required-for` - would be the honest one**, and that goes to Shir with the other schema items rather than being invented here. limitationThe cueing manipulation may have been too weak to fully disengage temporal attention from the prime, which could explain why behavioural priming was unaffected.— “Alternatively, although a significant RT effect of the cue–prime intervals suggests an effective manipulation of attention, our cueing procedure could have been less powerful to disengage temporal attention from the masked prime compared to the unattended condition in the Naccache et al. study.”

findingThe masked N400 semantic priming effect was largest when the prime fell inside the attended time window and the prime-target SOA was short.— “Most importantly, this masked N400 priming effect was strongest when the cue interval and the stimulus onset asynchronies were short.”
findingThe authors conclude that temporal attention to an unconsciously perceived prime is a necessary condition for masked N400 priming, so unconscious automatic processes are open to attentional modulation.— “The present results show that temporal attention to the prime is a prerequisite for obtaining masked N400 priming effects.”
findingIn the control experiment where the cue was task-irrelevant, N400 priming shrank and no longer varied with the cue-prime interval, showing the Experiment 1 effect was due to attention rather than trial length.— “Thus, compared to Experiment 1, N400 priming effects were considerably reduced and were not modulated by the CPI.”
findingBehavioural semantic priming was reliable but insensitive to the attentional manipulation, dissociating reaction-time priming from the N400 measure.— “Masked behavioral priming was significant, but did not differ as a function of the CPI.”
findingPrime visibility was at chance for the analysed participants, supporting the claim that the primes were unconscious.— “For the remaining 16 participants, identification performance was distributed around the chance level of 50%, which is expected by mere guessing.”
findingRegression of priming effects on d-prime identification scores found no relationship, ruling out backward priming from partial prime visibility.— “However, for both behavioral and N400 priming effects, regression analysis did not yield any significant correlations, neither at the short CPIs (all ps > .15) nor at the long CPIs (all ps > .09).”
