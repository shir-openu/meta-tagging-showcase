mechanismONE ROUTE CARRIES ALMOST EVERYTHING: 95% of Ukrainian grain leaves by sea from Odessa, Mariupol and Kherson, and all Black Sea ports were blocked - so a single chokepoint converts a regional war into a global supply event— “Indeed, 95% of Ukrainian grain exports are sent by sea via the ports of Odessa, Mariupol, and Kherson, which have suffered significant damage. In addition, all Black Sea ports have been blocked, shutting off most Ukrainian exports.”
mechanismTHE ALTERNATIVE ROUTES ARE BLOCKED BY INFRASTRUCTURE, NOT BY POLITICS: the Ukrainian rail gauge differs from most EU nations', Constanta lacks the capacity, and building storage takes time - so rerouting is a multi-year problem presented as a short-term option— “Further, the Romanian port of Constanta lacks the capacity to manage the influx of Ukrainian crops”
mechanismAN UNKNOWN END DATE DEFEATS THE INVESTMENT THAT WOULD FIX IT: because the embargo's duration is unknown, private capital will not fund the port and rail capacity the alternative routes need. The uncertainty is itself the obstacle— “In addition, because the embargo duration is unknown, attracting private investment for the infrastructure required for such alternatives is challenging”
mechanismPRICE SIGNALS RUN BOTH WAYS AND THE PAPER SAYS SO: high fertilizer prices suppress farmers' use and simultaneously motivate producers to expand supply; high oilseed prices pull more planting into the US and EU. The net effect is not computed— “However, despite the negative adverse consequences of the war, some opportunities may arise and lessen these consequences.”
mechanismTHE SHOCK LANDED ON A SYSTEM ALREADY STRESSED: prices were high before the invasion because of COVID-19 supply disruption, strong demand and poor harvests, so the war is an increment on an existing crisis rather than the origin of one— “Simultaneously, the war came at a bad time for global food markets because food prices were already high due to disruptions in the supply chain caused by the COVID-19 pandemic, strong global demand, and poor harvests in some countries.”
mechanismIMPORT CONCENTRATION IS THE TRANSMISSION MECHANISM: over 30 countries take at least 30% of their wheat from the two belligerents and at least 20 take over 50%, which is what makes distant countries price-takers on this war— “Indeed, over 30 countries rely on Russia and Ukraine for at least 30% of their wheat imports, while at least 20 countries rely on those two countries for more than 50% of their wheat imports, making them very sensitive to price shocks and/or supply shortages”

qualifies accounts that treat the war as the cause of the food crisis; the paper holds that pre-existing rigidities and inefficiencies in global food systems are what turn the war into a crisis— “However, the consequences of the war on food security are being exacerbated by a variety of underlying rigidities, vulnerabilities, and inefficiencies in global food systems.”
sensethe war's `impact` on food security - THE PAPER SEPARATES TWO KINDS AND THE DISTINCTION IS ITS STRUCTURE. IMMEDIATE impacts are physical and observed: exports stopped, ports blocked, farmers conscripted. INDIRECT ones are market-mediated and projected: fertilizer prices, export restrictions, panic buying. A reader who collapses them reads observations and forecasts as one kind of claim— “The paper highlights that the war resulted in immediate and far-reaching cascading consequences on global food security: Ukrainian exports have stopped, conscription and population displacement have caused labor shortages, access to fertilizers is restricted, and future harvests are uncertain.”
sensethe war as CAUSE - THE PAPER'S OWN THESIS IS THAT IT IS NOT THE CAUSE, IT IS THE TRIGGER. The consequences are said to be `exacerbated by a variety of underlying rigidities, vulnerabilities, and inefficiencies in global food systems`, which makes the war a stressor on a system already fragile - and it is why the conclusion is about food-system reform rather than about the war— “However, the consequences of the war on food security are being exacerbated by a variety of underlying rigidities, vulnerabilities, and inefficiencies in global food systems.”
sensevulnerability - DEFINED HERE BY IMPORT CONCENTRATION, not by poverty or by proximity to the war: over 30 countries take at least 30% of their wheat from Russia and Ukraine and at least 20 take more than 50%. That is what makes the effects land `far from the initial occurrence`— “Indeed, over 30 countries rely on Russia and Ukraine for at least 30% of their wheat imports, while at least 20 countries rely on those two countries for more than 50% of their wheat imports, making them very sensitive to price shocks and/or supply shortages”
controlthe paper reports the counter-movement as well as the harm - higher oilseed and wheat prices pulling more planting into the US, EU and Canada, and higher fertilizer prices motivating producers to expand output — partial— “Indeed, early indications show that increased soybean and other oilseed prices will encourage farmers to boost oilseed plantings in the United States and the European Union.” — rules out a one-sided reading in which the war produces only losses - the paper reports the counter-movements (more oilseed and wheat planting, expanded fertilizer production), though it never nets them against the harm— “However, despite the negative adverse consequences of the war, some opportunities may arise and lessen these consequences.”
