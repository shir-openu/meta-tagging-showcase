phenomenoncortical visual pathways for perception and action— “Accumulating neuropsychological, electrophysiological and behavioural evidence suggests that the neural substrates of visual perception may be quite distinct from those underlying the visual control of actions.”
claim-typemechanism— “We propose that the ventral stream of projections from the striate cortex to the inferotemporal cortex plays the major role in the perceptual identification of objects, while the dorsal stream projecting from the striate cortex to the posterior parietal region mediates the required sensorimotor for visually guided transformations actions…
method — evidence— “Despite the interactions between the dorsal and ventral systems, the converging lines of evidence reviewed above indicate that each stream uses visual information about objects and events in the world in different ways.”
mechanismDifferent output requirements (perceptual identification vs online sensorimotor transformation) demand different visual computations, and this drives the anatomical segregation of the two streams.— “These differences are largely a reflection of the specific transformations of input required by perception and action.”
mechanismDorsal-stream processing proceeds without reaching consciousness, which keeps moment-to-moment viewer-centred information from disrupting the perceptual constancies computed in the ventral stream.— “It may be that information can be processed in the dorsal system without reaching consciousness, and that this prevents interference with the perceptual constancies intrinsic to many operations within the ventral system that do result in awareness.”
mechanismSeparate processing modules evolved because vision is put to functionally different uses.— “It seems plausible from a functional standpoint that separate processing modules would have evolved to mediate the different uses to which vision can be put.”
extends Ungerleider and Mishkin's (1982) two-visual-systems model ('what' versus 'where')— “In this article, it is proposed that this distinction ('what' versus 'how') - rather than the distinction between object vision and spatial vision ('what' versus 'where') - captures more appropriately the functional dichotomy between the ventral and dorsal projections.”
