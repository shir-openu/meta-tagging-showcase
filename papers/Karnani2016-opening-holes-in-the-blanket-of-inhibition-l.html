phenomenonlocalized lateral disinhibition by VIP interneurons
claim-typemechanism
transferfrom attention research: the spotlight metaphor, imported as the functional interpretation of a local disinhibitory hole — “VIP interneurons, themselves regulated by neuromodulators, may therefore enable selective patterns of activity to propagate through the cortex, by generating a “spotlight of attention”
transferfrom dynamical systems: attractors, offered as what selectively sculpted circuits might implement — “This type of local control could be use to dynamically sculpt selective cortical circuits out of a neural network type of connectivity matrix, and use them to implement specific perceptions, behaviors, memory states or, more generally, dynamical attractors.”
mechanismA VIP cell inhibits nearby SOM cells, which removes the SOM-mediated blanket inhibition on local pyramidal cells and thereby lets excitation spread through that patch. — “The data in Figure 1 indicate that VIPs specialize in lateral disinhibition and can block FDDI by inhibiting SOMs.”
mechanismThe horizontally narrow VIP axon confines the disinhibitory action to a small cylindrical volume of cortex, setting the size of the hole. — “A horizontally narrow axon ( Fig. 2 A–D ) may restrict the area of effect of a single VIP to a small cylindrical volume of cortex.”
mechanismDuring locomotion many VIPs are recruited at once, so the disinhibition from any single stimulated VIP is swamped and loses its spatial structure. — “This is likely because of the increased neuronal activity and recruitment of many more VIPs than the stimulated one during locomotion.”
mechanism — “Members of the SOM, PV, and chandelier cell populations mediate a broad blanket-like inhibition of most if not all PCs within their axonal arbors ( Fino and Yuste, 2011 ; Packer and Yuste, 2011 ; Karnani et al., 2014 ; Inan et al., 2013 ). This widespread inhibition, in principle, should suppress the propagation of excitatory activity in neocortex.”
mechanism — “In fact, because PV and SOM are activated specifically with fast and slow inputs, and relay fast and slow inhibition, it seems as if the interneuron population was selectively designed to block both fast and slow excitatory activity.”
mechanism — “We find that VIP interneurons have narrow axons and inhibit nearby somatostatin interneurons, which themselves inhibit pyramidal cells. Moreover, via this lateral disinhibition, VIP cells in vivo make local and transient “holes” in the inhibitory blanket extended by SOM cells.”
mechanism — “Targeting the stimulation to other nearby VIPs did not excite the recorded VIP”
mechanism — “As the presence of sensory stimulation ( Fig. 6 ) was required for seeing the disinhibitory effect of single VIP activation (data not shown) the spatiotemporal coincidence of excitation and a disinhibitory hole may be necessary to use the hole in blanket inhibition.”
mechanism — “VIPs may be active individually, e.g., during low-activity states or a specific input from prefrontal cortex ( Harris and Thiele, 2011 ; Zhang et al., 2014 ). This mode of activity may thus form highly localized disinhibited patches of cortex. Alternatively, a few closely spaced VIPs may be preferentially activated together, thus forming larger patches of disinhibited cortex.”

findingOptogenetically silencing SOM interneurons abolished the disynaptic inhibition between paired pyramidal cells, giving direct loss-of-function evidence that SOMs mediate lateral inhibition. — “However, when we exposed the slice to green light during this time to inhibit SOMs, the disynaptic inhibition was blocked, confirming that SOMs mediate FDDI ( Fig. 1 D–G ).”
findingActivating VIP interneurons during the presynaptic spike train also blocked the disynaptic inhibition, showing VIPs can veto lateral inhibition. — “When we activated VIPs during the train of action potentials in one PC, the FDDI in another nearby PC was blocked ( Fig. 1 K , M ).”
findingReconstructed VIP axons were narrower than SOM or PV axons and descended away from the pia. — “VIP axons tended to descend away from the pia and were narrower than SOM or PV axons”
findingSOMs postsynaptic to a VIP were significantly closer to it than unconnected SOMs, so VIP inhibitory output is spatially restricted. — “SOMs that were inhibited by VIPs were closer to the presynaptic VIP (112 ± 25 μm, n = 15) than SOMs that were not connected (163 ± 14 μm, n = 46, p = 0.01, rank sum test).”
findingTwo-photon activation of a single VIP in vivo disinhibited visual responses selectively in cells near the stimulated VIP. — “The disinhibited cells were located significantly closer to the activated VIP than all imaged cells ( Fig. 6 E ; disinhibited cells 66 ± 9 μm and all cells 101 ± 2 μm, p = 0.002 by rank sum test) indicating that VIPs have a local effect on neural activity in vivo .”
findingThe disinhibitory reach of one VIP cell measured about 120 μm in radius, i.e. a hole roughly 240 μm across. — “Plotting the change in visual responses upon VIP activation in all cells as a function of distance ( Fig. 6 F ) suggests that VIPs have an effective disinhibitory radius of ∼120 μm (120–180 μm being the first bin not significantly above zero, p = 0.06 by Z test against zero).”
findingSpontaneous VIP activity in awake mice was tied to activity of cells within about 50 μm, whereas pyramidal-cell activity showed almost no such distance dependence. — “This analysis showed that spontaneous activity in VIPs is closely tied to activity in the other cells within a 50 μm radius, whereas activity in PCs showed a weak distance dependence with nearby cells ( Fig. 3 E ).”
