claim-typemechanism— “The heterosis is produced, in these cases, by interaction of polygene complexes, which have become mutually adjusted, or coadapted, by natural selection in the course of the evolutionary process.”
method — evidence— “Population cages of the type described by WRIGHT and DOBZHANSKY (1946), but somewhat improved in construction, were used.”
mechanismNatural selection coadapts polygene complexes only within a population, so chromosomes brought together from populations that never interbreed have not been mutually adjusted and their heterozygotes lack heterosis.— “Natural selection may have produced different polygene complexes in different populations, coadapted within but not be tween the populations. If this hypothesis is corr~ct, inversion heterozygotes which carry two chromosomes derived from different populations may not show as much heterosis as is observed within these populations…
mechanismHeterosis arises from interaction between polygene complexes carried by differently arranged chromosomes, which the inversions protect from being broken up by crossing over in the heterozygote.— “Heterosis is here the outcome of interaction of polygene complexes borne in the chromosomes with different gene arrangements and protected by the inversions from disintegration by crossing over in the heterozygotes.”
mechanismA second, distinct kind of heterosis arises when deleterious recessive mutants accumulated in the population are sheltered by their normal dominant alleles in the heterozygote.— “The first kind arises from the presence in populations of deleterious recessive mutant genes shel tered by their normal dominant alleles.”
mechanismViability and adaptive value diverge because larval survival is only one of several components (adult longevity, fecundity, sexual activity) that jointly determine net fitness.— “The answer is that the relative viability under competition is only one of the variables which determine the adaptive value of a genotype.”
builds-on Wright and Dobzhansky (1946) and Dobzhansky (1947a, b) population-cage results on within-population heterosis— “Indeed, experimental populations kept in so-called "population cages" have shown that the inversion heterozygotes which carry two third chromosomes with different gene arrangements derived from the same population are superior in fitness to the corresponding homozygotes (WRIGHT and DOBZHANSKY 1946; DOBZHANSKY 1947 a, b).”
senseheterosis - KIND ONE, AN ACCIDENT OF MUTATION: superiority arising because deleterious recessive mutants are hidden by their dominant alleles, which is a by-product of the mutation process— “The first kind arises from the presence in populations of deleterious recessive mutant genes shel tered by their normal dominant alleles.”
senseheterosis - KIND TWO, A PRODUCT OF SELECTION: specific interaction effects from complexes of linked polygenes, called overdominance, engendered by natural selection as adaptation— “The second kind of heterosis is due to complexes of linked polygenes which give specific "heterotic" interaction effects in heterozygotes”
