phenomenonheterosis in chromosomal inversion heterozygotes
claim-typemechanism
mechanismNatural selection coadapts polygene complexes only within a population, so chromosomes brought together from populations that never interbreed have not been mutually adjusted and their heterozygotes lack heterosis. — “Natural selection may have produced different polygene complexes in different populations, coadapted within but not be tween the populations. If this hypothesis is corr~ct, inversion heterozygotes which carry two chromosomes derived from different populations may not show as much heterosis as is observed within these populations themselves.”
mechanismHeterosis arises from interaction between polygene complexes carried by differently arranged chromosomes, which the inversions protect from being broken up by crossing over in the heterozygote. — “Heterosis is here the outcome of interaction of polygene complexes borne in the chromosomes with different gene arrangements and protected by the inversions from disintegration by crossing over in the heterozygotes.”
mechanismA second, distinct kind of heterosis arises when deleterious recessive mutants accumulated in the population are sheltered by their normal dominant alleles in the heterozygote. — “The first kind arises from the presence in populations of deleterious recessive mutant genes shel tered by their normal dominant alleles.”
mechanismViability and adaptive value diverge because larval survival is only one of several components (adult longevity, fecundity, sexual activity) that jointly determine net fitness. — “The answer is that the relative viability under competition is only one of the variables which determine the adaptive value of a genotype.”
relation — “Indeed, experimental populations kept in so-called "population cages" have shown that the inversion heterozygotes which carry two third chromosomes with different gene arrangements derived from the same population are superior in fitness to the corresponding homozygotes (WRIGHT and DOBZHANSKY 1946; DOBZHANSKY 1947 a, b).”
replicationuntested
limitationThe cages were not run long enough for the inferior chromosome type to be eliminated, so the predicted outcome was not observed to completion. — “At the end of the experiments, the CH chromosomes were rare in cages Nos. 48 and 49, but much longer observation would be needed to see them eliminated completely.”
limitationIn the California-Mexico cage No. 55 the viability differences among genotypes did not reach conventional significance. — “The differences are, however, mostly below the conventional level of statistical significance.”
limitationThe paper opens its discussion by saying the term names several scarcely related phenomena that the field treats as one. — “are common names for a group of scarcely related phenomena”
limitationFitness measured at one developmental stage can point the opposite way from fitness overall, and the paper gives its own case. — “The adaptive value (net fitness) of a type is not necessarily proportional to its survival value at all developmental stages. In our experiments, some types which showed relatively higher mortalities than other types between the egg and the adult stage proved nevertheless to be adaptively superior to the latter”
limitationThe effect is a property of chromosomes that evolved together, so the same gene-arrangement label does not carry the same behaviour between localities. — “Polygene complexes which occur in geographically distant localities have not undergone the process of coadaptation”

findingInversion heterozygotes built from two third chromosomes of the same population show heterosis, but heterozygotes built from chromosomes of geographically remote populations generally do not. — “In D. pseudoobscura, inversion heterozygotes which carry two chromosomes derived from the same population usually show heterosis, while heterozygotes which carry chromosomes from geographically remote populations usually do not.”
findingIn every geographically mixed cage one homozygote was sharply less viable than the heterozygote (quotients 0.17 to 0.76) while another homozygote was more viable than it, so the heterozygote was intermediate rather than superior. — “In every experiment, one of the homozygotes is sharply inferior in viability to the heterozygote (quotients 0.17 to 0.76).”
findingWhen the design was inverted so that the homozygotes carried chromosomes of mixed geographic origin and the heterozygotes carried same-population chromosomes, heterosis reappeared, showing it depends on the origin of the third chromosomes rather than on the genetic background. — “Heterozy gosis for ST and AR third chromosomes from the same population results in an increase in viability, even in the presence of a geographically mixed ge netic background of chromosomes other than the third.”
findingEgg-to-adult viability and adaptive value can point in opposite directions: in cages 46 and 47 the adaptive values were ST/AR > ST/ST > AR/AR, the reverse of the measured viability order. — “In both cages the adaptive values are ST / AR > ST /ST > AR/ AR, in other words, the reverse of the viability relations in cage No. 46 (see above).”
findingIn the hybrid cages one chromosome type steadily displaced the other instead of settling at an equilibrium; Arrowhead of Mather origin rose from 50 to 91 percent while Chiricahua of Pinon origin fell to 9 percent over about 13 months. — “By March 1948, AR rose to 91 percent and CH declined to 9 percent respectively.”
findingWith chromosomes from California and Mexico combined, the Pinon Arrowhead homozygote was significantly more viable than the heterozygote and the Mexican Chiricahua homozygote was at least equal to it, so the heterozygote was inferior to both homozygotes. — “But in cage No. 56, a very inter esting result is obtained, i.e., one of the homozygotes (AR P / AR P) is quite sig nificantly more viable, while the other homozygote (CHM/CHM) has an equal or a slightly greater viability than the heterozygote.”
findingA genotype's net fitness is not proportional to its survival at every developmental stage; types with higher egg-to-adult mortality nevertheless proved adaptively superior in the cages. — “The adaptive value (net fitness) of a type is not necessarily proportional to its survival value at all developmental stages.”
