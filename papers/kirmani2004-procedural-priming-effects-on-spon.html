phenomenonprocedural priming of spontaneous inference formation— “Procedural priming refers to how the frequent or recent use of certain cognitive procedures on one task can lead to a greater propensity to use the same procedures on a subsequent task.”
claim-typemethod— “The main objective of the paper was to demonstrate the usefulness of procedural priming as a way of assessing an inference's spontaneity when the inference involves a relationship or rule.”
method — evidence— “Subjects were 84 undergraduates from a large North American university who participated in a study for course credit in a Marketing class.”
transferfrom psychology (social and cognitive priming research): The procedural priming paradigm, developed and used in psychology, is imported into consumer/marketing research as a tool for inducing and detecting spontaneous product inferences.— “Although procedural priming has been used in psychology, it has not been applied in a marketing context, despite its potential as a managerial tool for inducing favorable product inferences.”
mechanismThe facilitative prime makes the effort-belief production accessible, so the quality inference is drawn at the moment the ad cost information is encoded; at judgment the subject only retrieves the stored evaluation instead of computing one.— “Specifically, the facilitative prime is expected to lead to greater spontaneous inferencing at the time of encoding, which, in turn, allows for a simple retrieval of that inference at time of quality judgment.”
mechanismRetrieval consumes less cognitive capacity than computation, so a load imposed at judgment slows computed judgments much more than retrieved ones - which is what makes response time a diagnostic of spontaneity.— “Since retrieval operations are believed to be less cognitively demanding than computational operations, response times will be quicker if a spontaneous inference had been made (Kardes, 1988).”
mechanismThe suppressive prime installs a rival attribution (effort signals personal gain), which competes with and blocks later use of the effort investment rule.— “The suppressive prime, in contrast, makes a competing inference rule – high investment of effort implies potential for personal gain – more accessible, thereby decreasing the likelihood that the effort investment rule will be used on a subsequent task” [UNVERIFIED AGAINST THE STAGED TEXT]
builds-on Kirmani and Wright (1989)— “Kirmani and Wright (1989) found that subjects in a no prime condition were just as likely to infer high quality from high advertising expense, as subjects in a prime condition that affirmed the tenability of the ad cost–quality relationship.”
extends Broniarczyk and Alba (1994b)— “Moreover, this line of reasoning potentially explains the findings of Broniarczyk and Alba (1994b), who reported a low incidence of use of the ad cost–quality inference by subjects despite being presented with data that showed a high correlation between advertising expense and quality.”
sensecognitive procedure - defined by the paper as the `how` of a task rather than its content, which is the move that lets a prime about charitable giving reach a judgment about advertising— “A cognitive procedure may be thought of as a set of cognitive operations that characterize the performance of a particular task. In other words, it represents the `how' of task performance in cognitive terms.”
sensespontaneous, of an inference - occurring without explicit prompting and often without conscious intention; a property of HOW the inference arose, not of what it says— “An inference is thought to be spontaneous when it occurs without explicit prompting and often without conscious intention (Newman & Uleman, 1989; Winter & Uleman, 1984).”
sensethe effort investment rule - the advertising claim with the advertising removed: a rule about persons and undertakings, which is what makes it primeable in a charity scenario and testable in an advertising one— “Abstracted from the context, the inference rule may be described as one that says, if a person is observed to have put in a substantial amount of effort into a particular undertaking, then it must be that he/she believes the undertaking to be a worthwhile one. We label this the effort investment rule.”
senseindirect measurement of spontaneity - chosen because THE MEASUREMENT WOULD CREATE THE THING IT TESTS: asking directly whether the inference was made can produce an inference that would otherwise not have been made— “The reason for relying on an indirect measure is that a direct measure might prompt an inference that would otherwise not have been made.”
senseprime, facilitative versus suppressive - a distinction drawn by the prime's relation to a target rule rather than by its subject matter. Both scenarios describe the same donation; only the reason offered for the effort differs— “The facilitative prime condition primed the effort investment rule, while the suppressive prime scenario made salient an alternative motive (personal gain) as the reason for the expended effort.”
controla CONFOUND CHECK: perceived advertising cost was measured on three seven-point scales and compared across conditions — held — rules out that the procedural prime also changed how expensive the campaign LOOKED, so that any effect is a cost inference rather than a procedural one— “Confound check To ensure that perceptions of campaign cost were equivalent across conditions, perceived advertising cost was measured as the average of three seven-point scales (very low/high; too little/much; below/above average; Cronbach's alpha=0.78).”
controla separate undergraduate pretest of the cost cue before the main study — held — rules out that the cost cue does not do what the design assumes it does— “A final pretest was conducted on a separate undergraduate sample to ensure that the cost cue to be used in the description of the target product would have the intended effects on quality perceptions.”
