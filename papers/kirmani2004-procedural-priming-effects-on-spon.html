phenomenonprocedural priming of spontaneous inference formation
claim-typemethod
transferfrom psychology (social and cognitive priming research): The procedural priming paradigm, developed and used in psychology, is imported into consumer/marketing research as a tool for inducing and detecting spontaneous product inferences. — “Although procedural priming has been used in psychology, it has not been applied in a marketing context, despite its potential as a managerial tool for inducing favorable product inferences.”
mechanismThe facilitative prime makes the effort-belief production accessible, so the quality inference is drawn at the moment the ad cost information is encoded; at judgment the subject only retrieves the stored evaluation instead of computing one. — “Specifically, the facilitative prime is expected to lead to greater spontaneous inferencing at the time of encoding, which, in turn, allows for a simple retrieval of that inference at time of quality judgment.”
mechanismRetrieval consumes less cognitive capacity than computation, so a load imposed at judgment slows computed judgments much more than retrieved ones - which is what makes response time a diagnostic of spontaneity. — “Since retrieval operations are believed to be less cognitively demanding than computational operations, response times will be quicker if a spontaneous inference had been made (Kardes, 1988).”
mechanismThe suppressive prime installs a rival attribution (effort signals personal gain), which competes with and blocks later use of the effort investment rule. — “The suppressive prime, in contrast, makes a competing inference rule – high investment of effort implies potential for personal gain – more accessible, thereby decreasing the likelihood that the effort investment rule will be used on a subsequent task” [UNVERIFIED AGAINST THE STAGED TEXT]
relation — “Kirmani and Wright (1989) found that subjects in a no prime condition were just as likely to infer high quality from high advertising expense, as subjects in a prime condition that affirmed the tenability of the ad cost–quality relationship.”
relation — “Moreover, this line of reasoning potentially explains the findings of Broniarczyk and Alba (1994b), who reported a low incidence of use of the ad cost–quality inference by subjects despite being presented with data that showed a high correlation between advertising expense and quality.”

content-observations: finding · quantity · variable · definition · key-term — grounded in the full text
findingJudged product quality was lower after the suppressive prime than after either the facilitative prime or no prime, which did not differ from each other. — “As expected, quality perceptions were more favorable in the facilitative prime and no prime conditions than in the suppressive prime condition”
findingOnly in the facilitative prime condition was the speed of the quality judgment unaffected by a concurrent memory load, indicating the quality inference had already been made spontaneously at encoding. — “In the facilitative prime condition, however, response times were equivalent across cognitive capacity conditions, suggesting that low capacity subjects had spontaneously generated a quality inference”
findingIn the suppressive prime and no prime conditions, constraining cognitive capacity slowed quality judgments significantly, as expected if the judgment had to be computed on the spot. — “Planned comparisons revealed that response times in the suppressive prime and no prime conditions were significantly slower when cognitive capacity was low than high”
findingThe advertising cost-quality inference is not normally spontaneous, but becomes spontaneous once the cognitive procedures that underlie it are primed. — “Overall, the pattern of response times is consistent with the notion that the ad cost–quality inference is not ordinarily a spontaneous one. It can, however, be spontaneous when the cognitive procedures mediating the inference are primed”
findingPriming the effort investment rule in an interpersonal charity scenario made subjects form product quality evaluations in a later advertising task without being asked to. — “Specifically, we primed the cognitive procedures expected to mediate the ad cost–quality inference (i.e., the effort investment rule) and found evidence that subjects had made quality evaluations without being prompted to do so”
findingWhen cognitive capacity was unconstrained, prime type produced no difference in response times - a null result at odds with the authors' predictions. — “When cognitive capacity was high, there were no significant differences in response times across the prime conditions (all Fs <1).”
findingThe authors argue the advertising cost-quality rule is not learned from observed correlation between ad spending and quality but is an instance of a general attribution that effort signals belief. — “Our findings suggest that the ad cost–quality inference may not originate from a perceived correlation between the two variables in the real world, but from a more general principle that says effort reflects one's belief”
quantity84 — number of undergraduate subjects in the main experiment — “Subjects were 84 undergraduates from a large North American university who participated in a study for course credit in a Marketing class.”
quantity76 — number of subjects in the pretest that checked the primes produced the intended effort attributions — “A pretest on 76 undergraduates at a large North American university drawn from the same population as the main study demonstrated that the primes were successful in leading subjects to the intended attributions of effort.”

definitionprocedural priming — “Procedural priming refers to how the frequent or recent use of certain cognitive procedures on one task can lead to a greater propensity to use the same procedures on a subsequent task.”
definitioncognitive procedure — “A cognitive procedure may be thought of as a set of cognitive operations that characterize the performance of a particular task.”
definitioneffort investment rule — “Abstracted from the context, the inference rule may be described as one that says, if a person is observed to have put in a substantial amount of effort into a particular undertaking, then it must be that he/she believes the undertaking to be a worthwhile one. We label this the effort investment rule.”
definitionfacilitative prime — “A facilitative prime is one that makes accessible cognitive procedures that are presumed to underlie the desired inference rule.”
definitionspontaneous inference — “An inference is thought to be spontaneous when it occurs without explicit prompting and often without conscious intention (Newman & Uleman, 1989; Winter & Uleman, 1984).”
