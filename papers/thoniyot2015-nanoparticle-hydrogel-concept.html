Types of NP‐Hydrogelterm·key Composites and Their Applications The innovative combination of nanoparticlesterm·key and hydrogelsterm·key create synergistic, unique and potentially useful properties that are not found in the individual components. Properties imparted to the composites depend on the type of nanoparticlesterm·key incorporated, which in turn is determined by the proposed application of the designed composite. Different types of nanoparticleterm·key‐hydrogelterm·key composites and their associated properties and applications are described below.

Metal NP‐Hydrogelterm·key Composites Silver NP‐Hydrogelterm·key Composites Silver (Ag) NPs are known for their antimicrobial properties and have been widely used in dental fillings and, more recently in wound and burn dressings to prevent infections. 30 Ag‐NPsterm·dom bind non‐specifically to bacterial membranes and other components, inducing structural changes that increase membrane permeabilityterm and mitochondrial dysfunction. 31 Controlled release of Ag‐NPsterm·dom is necessary to sustain antimicrobial efficacy. Ag‐NPsterm·dom have been incorporated into PAAm, 33 polyacrylic acid (PAA), 34 NIPAAm, 35 methyl methacrylate 36 and polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) based hydrogelsterm·key. 37 These Ag NP‐hydrogelterm·key composites show promise as functional anti‐microbial coatings. Semi‐interpenetrating network (IPN) hydrogelsterm·key offer an excellent alternative for applications requiring higher mechanical toughness. Conclusions and Outlook We have reviewed nanoparticleterm·key‐hydrogelterm·key composites as a state‐of‐the‐art, versatile class of materials suitable for a wide range of applications. Synthetic methods and strategies and the unique synergistic properties of the composite that is absent in individual components, together with their applications, have been summarized. Nanoparticleterm·key‐hydrogelterm·key composites exhibit multi‐functional and stimuli responsiveterm·key properties, making them ideal for “smart” materials, including i) antimicrobial gels/barriers/matrices; ii) photo thermally active hydrogelsterm·key; iii) soft material catalysts; iv) environmental absorbents; v) drug deliveryterm·key vehicles; vi) optical detection sensors and vii) soft actuators. Potential applications include i) safe, clinically implantable nanoparticleterm·key‐hydrogelterm·key composite systems for bio‐sensing and therapy; ii) environmental remediation systems for catalytic oxidation of toxins/removal of pollutants; iii) recyclable catalytic nanoparticleterm·key hydrogelterm·key composites for chemical synthesis and iv) composite hydrogelterm·key patches for cosmetic applications.

We expect that the classification of synthetic approaches and applications of nanoparticleterm·key‐hydrogelterm·key composites presented in this reviewterm·mth provide a better understanding of the system and enable the reader to design innovative combinations for new applications. Control of the covalent and supra molecular interactions by synthetic design and prediction of the resultant properties in the nanoparticleterm·key‐hydrogelterm·key composite are major areas to be developed in this emerging field. Such predictions upon experimental validation will form the foundation of design for the next generation nanoparticleterm·key‐hydrogelterm·key composites with optimal properties for a desired application. In the coming years, such design of nanoparticleterm·key‐hydrogelterm·key composites will not only result in advanced applications, but will also steer the fundamental understanding of material interactions, aiding computational prediction of properties of new composites from existing components.
