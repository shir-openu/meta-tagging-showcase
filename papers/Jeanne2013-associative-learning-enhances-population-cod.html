transferfrom psychology (animal learning theory): the concept of 'occasion setting' is imported to reinterpret the CLM motif representation as a predictive mapping onto the learned response rather than a sensory trace — “In this context, which psychologists refer to as occasion setting ( Schmajuk and Holland, 1998 ), we suggest that the neural representation of motifs in CLM is less a sensory trace than a predictive mapping of the learned behavioral response.”
transferfrom animal learning psychology: occasion setting - the idea that a stimulus signals which response is appropriate rather than predicting reward itself - used to reinterpret what the cortical representation is for — “In this context, which psychologists refer to as occasion setting ( Schmajuk and Holland, 1998 ), we suggest that the neural representation of motifs in CLM is less a sensory trace than a predictive mapping of the learned behavioral response.”
transferfrom statistics: multinomial logistic regression, chosen because stimulus identity is categorical and cannot be parameterised — “Because motif identity is best regarded as a nominal, or categorical, variable (i.e. motifs cannot be easily described with a small number of parameters), we use multinomial logistic regression (MNLR) to find the optimal classifier that maximizes the predictability of motif identity from the firing rates of multiple neurons (Experimental Procedures). This technique is particularly well-suited to our data because it can be applied to any number of neurons and any number of nominal categories (i.e. motifs).”
mechanismwhen noise correlation runs opposite to tuning similarity, a downstream reader can subtract the responses of dissimilarly tuned neurons, cancelling the shared noise while adding the signal, so the same spikes carry more information — “In contrast, negative relationships can improve population coding because common noise among dissimilarly tuned neurons can be subtracted, which strengthens the signal while dissipating the noise.”
mechanismlearning-driven changes to feed-forward synaptic weights (with excitatory-inhibitory noise cancellation) decorrelate similarly tuned neurons and preserve or increase correlation among dissimilarly tuned ones, producing the inverted correlation structure — “The plausibility of such circuitry is supported by a recent network model showing that learning-driven modulations to feed-forward synaptic weights yields decreased noise correlations for similarly tuned cortical neurons, and increased noise correlations for dissimilarly tuned neurons”
mechanismthe trigger for the correlation change is the learned stimulus-to-action mapping (the motif signalling which port to peck), not reward pairing, familiarity or exposure, which were equated across motif classes — “Thus, learning-dependent changes in the inter-neuronal correlation patterns depend on associations formed between stimuli and behavior, rather than experience, familiarity, or reward contingency.”
mechanism — “Compared to independent noise, positively correlated noise between two similarly tuned neurons impairs encoding because no form of response pooling can attenuate the shared noise without simultaneously attenuating the signal”
mechanism — “In contrast, positively correlated noise between two oppositely tuned neurons can improve encoding because subtracting one response from the other can both attenuate the shared noise and strengthen the signal”
mechanism — “To distinguish these two sources of increased performance, we compared the classification performance without correlations (i.e. with trials shuffled, which does not alter individual neuron responses) to that with correlations intact.”
mechanism — “All the motifs used during training were heard equally often in the context of the task and paired equally with reinforcement, but only the task-relevant motifs signaled the correct behavioral response on each trial. Thus, any effect of learning mediated directly by reinforcement should apply to all of the training motifs.”
mechanism — “As expected, each of the single task-relevant motifs evoked responses primarily to a single port, following the learned responses from training ( Figure 1E ). In contrast, responses to the task-irrelevant motifs were evenly distributed between both response ports ( Figure 1E ). Importantly, overall response rates for all the motifs were similarly high”

findingFor motifs that had guided the birds' choices, the usual positive coupling between signal and noise correlation was inverted to a negative one. — “For task-relevant motifs, this relationship was negative (Spearman correlation coefficient: r = −0.15, p = 0.051, Fig. 4A ): larger signal correlations were accompanied by smaller noise correlations.”
findingEqually familiar but behaviourally uninformative motifs, and never-heard novel motifs, retained the canonical positive coupling, so the inversion was specific to task relevance rather than to exposure or reward. — “For task-irrelevant and novel motifs, in contrast, the relationship was positive (task-irrelevant: r = 0.19, p = 0.012; novel: r = 0.23, p = 0.0022; Figure 4B,C ): larger signal correlations were accompanied by larger noise correlations.”
findingThe mean level of noise correlation itself did not differ between motif classes, so the learning effect lies in the correlation structure rather than in correlation magnitude. — “Surprisingly, there were no differences in the mean noise correlation between motif classes (repeated measures ANOVA, p = 0.21; Figure 3A,C ).”
findingThe magnitude of signal correlation was likewise unaffected by task relevance. — “However, we found no evidence that task-relevance influenced the magnitude of signal correlations ( Figure 3B ; Friedman test, p = 0.18).”
findingCorrelations improved decoding of task-relevant motifs more than of the other classes, confirming that the restructured correlations translate into better discrimination. — “We indeed found that task relevant motifs exhibit a higher classification ratio than the task-irrelevant or novel motifs (Friedman test, p = 0.018; Figure 6B ), consistent with our observations of the correlation structure.”
findingThe coding benefit conferred by correlations was flat with population size for irrelevant and novel motifs but grew steadily with population size for task-relevant motifs. — “We find that the effect of correlations on coding of task-irrelevant and novel motifs is small and does not depend on population size; in contrast, for task-relevant motifs, a modest effect in pairs of neurons grows considerably with population size”
