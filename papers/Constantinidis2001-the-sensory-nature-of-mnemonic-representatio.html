phenomenondelay-period activity in primate prefrontal cortex during visual working memory— “The primate prefrontal cortex (PFC) is a critical node of the network that mediates working memory, one component of which is the ability to retain and mentally manipulate a stimulus over the time scale of seconds1,2.”
claim-typeexistence— “Our results indicated that during the delay period when sensory information was no longer available, neuronal discharge was modulated by the luminance of the stimulus appearing in the receptive field, and was directly correlated with psychophysical performance in the task.”
method — evidence— “To explore the relationship between neuronal activity and behavioral performance, we recorded from the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex of monkeys trained to perform a delayed discrimination task.”
mechanismDorsolateral PFC runs two superimposed processes - a slowly decaying sensory representation plus a response-preparation signal that ramps toward the saccade - whose sum produces the observed delay activity— “Based on these considerations, we propose that dorsolateral PFC encompasses neurons engaged in at least two separate processes: a sensory representation that remains fairly constant or slowly decays during the delay period, and a response preparation that increases toward the onset of the saccade (Fig. 8).”
mechanismThe motor plan emerges from a network of neurons that keep holding sensory information, rather than from a discrete sensory-to-motor transformation stage in single neurons— “Our present findings favor the view that a motor plan is an emergent property of a network composed in part by neurons sustaining sensory information, rather than involving a discrete decision stage in which sensory parameters are transformed into motor variables by single neurons”
contradicts previous studies of delay-period activity in a discrimination task concluding that responses reflect the outcome of a perceptual decision (the paper's refs 18,19)— “In contrast to these results, previous studies analyzing activity in the delay period of a discrimination task concluded that neuronal responses best correlate with the outcome of a perceptual decision18,19.”
builds-on previous dorsolateral prefrontal experiments using the anti-saccade paradigm, spatial match-to-sample and conditional response tasks (the paper's refs 10,28,29)— “The present findings are in agreement with previous experiments in the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex suggesting that neuronal activity in a mnemonic task represents the remembered visual stimulus, as demonstrated by the anti-saccade paradigm, spatial match-to-sample and conditional response tasks10,28,29.”
sensedelay period activity - MODULATED OR NOT DEPENDING ON WHERE THE STIMULI WERE: not significantly modulated by a distractor outside the receptive field while the target was inside, and significantly dependent on contrast ratio when the distractor was inside it; the same measure yields a null and a positive result, and only the spatial condition separates them— “Delay period activity was not significantly modulated by the presence of a distractor outside the receptive field, when the target appeared inside (Fig. 4a; regression analysis, p > 0.3). However, delay period activity was significantly dependent on contrast ratio when the distractor appeared in the receptive field”
sensedelay activity as memory - AN INFERENCE THE PAPER REPORTS AS DISPUTED: neurons discharge while a stimulus is held in memory, and it has been argued that temporal correlation does not necessarily imply involvement of that activity in perceptual memory, so the field's central inference is named as contested inside a paper that relies on it— “Although neurons in the primate prefrontal cortex discharge while a stimulus is maintained in memory, it has been argued that temporal correlation does not necessarily imply involvement of this activity in perceptual memory”
sensethe analysed sample - THE WHOLE RECORDING, BY DECISION: rather than dropping neurons whose responses were mainly to the cue or the saccade, the authors state they used the entire sample so as not to ignore those neurons' contribution to delay period activity— “We based the analysis of our results on the entire sample, rather than ignore the contribution of primarily cue and saccade neurons to the delay period activity”
