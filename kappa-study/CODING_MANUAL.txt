CODING MANUAL - statistical-tag assignments
==========================================================
You are the SECOND, INDEPENDENT coder. Do not consult the project's existing
decisions; they are deliberately withheld from this sheet.

UNIT OF ANALYSIS
  One row = one (paper, statistical term) assignment. You judge only whether the
  term is used, in that paper, in a genuine STATISTICAL / PROBABILISTIC sense.

DECIDE  accept  or  reject   (write exactly one word in the `decision` column)

ACCEPT when the evidence shows the term denoting the statistical concept, e.g.
  - a reported test, estimate or distributional quantity ("p < .05", "SD = 3.2")
  - an explicit methodological statement ("we ran an ANOVA", "a Bayesian prior")
  - a substantive discussion of the concept itself, even without numbers

REJECT when the match is lexical rather than statistical, e.g.
  - "significant" used to mean "important", with no inferential claim
  - a symbol collision: H0 as a Hubble constant / Hamiltonian; p as a probability
    label or a physical parameter rather than a p-value
  - "mean" as "average person/thing" in ordinary prose, "variance" as everyday
    variation, "regression" in a non-statistical sense
  - the occurrence sits only in a title/reference/bibliography, not the paper's own text
  - the evidence is a generated summary or tag box, not the article's prose

IF UNSURE
  Judge on the evidence text alone. If the evidence is insufficient to establish a
  statistical sense, REJECT (the burden is on the assignment). Add a short note in
  the `note` column - notes are analysed, not ignored.

DO NOT
  - do not look the paper up; judge the quoted evidence
  - do not try to guess what the first coder decided
  - do not leave a row blank
