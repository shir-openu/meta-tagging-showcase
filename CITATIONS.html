What the three numbers mean. “Quoted from the citing paper” is the relations whose evidence sentence was found in that paper’s own text (its opening, up to 120 characters, ignoring letter case). “Target identified” is the relations whose link was checked and recorded as right. What was checked: the citation, as our record of the citing paper gives it, against the linked record’s authors, year and title — its first name is the linked record’s first author, every other name it gives is an author of that record, and no other record here fits. Where the citing paper’s own reference list was read as well, the verdict on that link says so; for most links it was not. “Target unresolved” is the candidates that are not linked — a name and a year could not decide them, the check judged the match wrong, or the match has not been checked. They are listed with the reason, and they are not links.

These are not suggestions. Elsewhere this site proposes links the literature has not yet drawn, and those need adjudicating. Every relation below is one a paper states about another paper in its own words. The relation is the paper’s, not ours; what we checked, link by link, is which paper it names.

How to read a doubtful one

A citation is usually a name and a year — “Devine (1989, Experiment 2)” — rather than a title. Every relation below was matched on names and a year, never on a title, which is why a shared surname and year is sometimes not enough to tell two papers apart — and why one name and one year can fit exactly one paper here and still mean another. So a link is made only after the match was checked — the citation as our record gives it, against the linked record’s authors, year and title — and recorded as right. Anything else is NOT a link: it is listed at the foot of this page as an unresolved candidate, with the reason, and it is not counted as a relation. Relations to work outside this corpus are not shown at all: this page is only the part where both ends are here.
