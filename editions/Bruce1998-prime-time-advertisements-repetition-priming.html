senses**THE PRIME WAS NOT ADMINISTERED - IT WAS A POSTER IN A CORRIDOR.** Faces on subject recruitment advertisements primed laboratory face recognition, `even when the advertisements make no specific mention of experiments involving face recognition` and even when subjects `were not obliged to look at them`. **This is the only record in the corpus where the exposure happened outside the experiment**: dijksterhuis1998 doses by duration, Schmidt2009 by the prime-target gap, bargh1996 by a scrambled-sentence task, and every one of them is delivered by an experimenter to someone who has already consented. Here it happened to people who did not yet know they were in a study
“Repetition priming was found for both poster and lab faces, although the priming effect was smaller from the poster faces”

senses**AND THE DISSOCIATION IS IN THE DERIVATIVE, NOT THE LEVEL, WHICH IS A SHARPER CLAIM:** `there is LITTLE EVIDENCE that repeated or extended exposure to items influences REPETITION PRIMING AT ALL ..., WHEREAS it is clear that increase in exposure duration and/or frequency DOES AFFECT EXPLICIT MEMORY for the items.` **More exposure buys explicit memory and buys priming nothing.** Two systems that differ in whether they respond to MORE, rather than in whether they respond - which cannot be explained by one measure being less sensitive, the objection that sinks a difference in means
