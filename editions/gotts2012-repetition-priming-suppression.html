concepts[4]0.95the paper's words
senses**THE PUZZLE, STATED AS A PUZZLE, WHICH IS WHY THIS RECORD IS WORTH HAVING:** `How is it that REDUCTIONS IN NEURAL ACTIVITY CAN MEDIATE BETTER BEHAVIORAL PERFORMANCE?` **The corpus now holds four positions on that question, all merged today and none of them citing another in this set:** Desimone1996 calls suppression `an intrinsic property of visual cortical areas`; Carlesimo2004 reads an rCBF decrease as `the reduced neural activity needed to reprocess recently seen words`; mcmahon-olson2007 concludes suppression `ACCOMPANIES repetition priming but is UNLIKELY TO BE ITS CAUSE`; and this paper says the puzzle is real and rate is the wrong variable
“However, the relationship between repetition priming and repetition suppression also presents a major puzzle: How is it that reductions in neural activity can mediate better behavioral performance”
senses[0]0.95the tagger's words
senses**AND THE ANSWER IS A REPLY TO McMAHON AND OLSON RATHER THAN A CONTRADICTION OF THEM.** `as cells are firing at LOWER OVERALL RATES, they are firing MORE SYNCHRONOUSLY with one another, leading to more efficient neural processing` - and in simulation, `as the model's firing rates DECREASED with repetition, the synchronization of the spike times SIMULTANEOUSLY INCREASED.` **Both papers agree the rate drop is not the mechanism.** They differ on whether anything else in the same signal is: McMahon and Olson tested rate and selectivity and found neither sufficient; Gotts proposes a variable neither of them measured
“Importantly, as the model’s firing rates decreased with repetition, the synchronization of the spike times simultaneously increased.”

senses**IT NAMES THE GAP IN ITS OWN EVIDENCE, WHICH A PROPOSAL PAPER USUALLY DOES NOT:** `despite exciting recent work on the role of neural synchronization in cognitive processes such as attention and perception, similar studies in the domain of learning and memory - and priming, in particular - have been LACKING.` **The mechanism is offered together with the statement that the direct evidence for it has not been collected in this domain.** Compare meyer1971's `At present we do not have ways to test all the possible explanations of these effects` - forty-one years apart, the same willingness to write the limitation flat
“We argue that despite exciting recent work on the role of neural synchronization in cognitive processes such as attention and perception, similar studies in the domain of learning and memory ––and priming, in particular––have been lacking.”
