sensesAMBIGUITY OF THE OUTCOME IS OFFERED AS THE REASON COGNITIVE PRIMING EFFECTS ARE SMALLER THAN SOCIAL ONES, WHICH INVERTS THE USUAL READING. `The more specific and LESS AMBIGUOUS the outcome (such as when responding to the word doctor after seeing the word nurse), THE SMALLER the expected priming effect because there is less wiggle room for the prime to alter the response.` The usual account of that same effect-size gap is that social priming effects are inflated. This says they are large because the outcome is loose - a property of the MEASURE, not of the phenomenon - and it makes the gap a prediction rather than an embarrassment. **Both readings fit the same numbers, and this corpus holds papers from both sides of the gap**
“The more specific and less ambiguous the outcome (such as when responding to the word doctor after seeing the word nurse), the smaller the expected priming effect because there is less wiggle room for the prime to alter the response”

sensesAND IT IS FALSIFIABLE, WITH THE TEST SUPPLIED: `A situation that does not leave much room for interpretation IS UNLIKELY TO SHOW A CONSISTENT OR LARGE PRIMING EFFECT.` The same outcome task was given two NAMES - Kay et al.
