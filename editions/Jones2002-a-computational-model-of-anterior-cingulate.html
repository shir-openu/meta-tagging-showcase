response conflict anterior cingulate cortex cognitive control conflict model Repeated high-frequency stimuli build residual activation (an expectancy) in one stimulus-response pathway, so a low-frequency stimulus must overcome that preactivation, and the resulting competition is what registers as conflict in ACC. Conflict at the decision layer produces ACC-indexed signal that lowers strategic priming, which raises control (slower, more accurate responding) on subsequent trials. Because the incorrect nontarget decision unit in one-response tasks is not connected to a response execution unit, transient wrong-channel activation never becomes an overt error, so the task difference arises downstream of conflict detection rather than at it. response conflict (as simultaneous coactivation of incompatible responses) response conflict (origin in constraints on response generation) repetition priming and alternation priming conflict (computational measure based on the Hopfield energy) stimulus frequency (low / equal / high) task type (one-response go/no-go and oddball vs two-response 2AFC) local sequential history (repetition/alternation category over the previous five trials) reaction time and error rate ACC activity (peak event-related amplitude in the ACC region of interest) simulated conflict index lesioned model (strategic priming fixed at its mean, conflict-control loop removed) ANOVA correlation partial correlation confidence interval standard score (z score) z scores t-test p-value mean mean RT and percentage of error simulation

The endpoint rule refused it. **Fifth edge tonight lost or moved on wording**, and this one is the most ordinary of them: a paper defines an abbreviation once and then uses it, which every paper does. So the relation comes from the sentence that spells the region out. The DIRECTION is the careful part either way: the ACC does not CAUSE conflict - conflict is a property of the response system and the ACC is where it is read off, which is why ACC activity serves as a conflict measure throughout the imaging literature.
