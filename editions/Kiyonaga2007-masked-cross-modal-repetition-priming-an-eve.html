sensesTHEY CONSIDER Holcomb2005'S DISTINCTION FOR THIS CASE AND DECLINE IT - AND Holcomb AND Grainger ARE AUTHORS ON BOTH PAPERS. The obvious extension of `pre-activation versus establishing connections` is that cross-modal priming, being slower and later, is the connection-building kind. `HOWEVER, WE WOULD ARGUE that while semantic priming may indeed require the conscious linking of related representations, SUCH A MECHANISM WOULD BE SUPERFLUOUS FOR CROSS-MODAL REPETITION PRIMING since the visual prime and auditory target presumably have THE SAME SEMANTIC REPRESENTATION.` The argument is that the distinction does not APPLY here, not that it is wrong - a author group declining to extend its own account into the case it most obviously fits
“However, we would argue that while semantic priming may indeed require the conscious linking of related representations, such a mechanism would be superfluous for cross-modal repetition priming since the visual prime and auditory target presumably have the same semantic representation”

sensesA METHODOLOGICAL CRITERION FOR WHETHER AN ERP COMPONENT EXISTS AT ALL, cited to a standard and RANKED BY STRENGTH: `THIRD, THE MOST COMPELLING EVIDENCE for component independence is when components show DIFFERENTIAL SENSITIVITY TO EXPERIMENTAL VARIABLES (Rugg & Coles, 1995).` The first two pieces of evidence are morphological - two peaks with a positivity between them, different scalp distributions - and the authors say plainly that the functional one beats them. **A component is real when something moves it that does not move its neighbour**, and that is a rule about how to individuate a measure, not a finding about words
