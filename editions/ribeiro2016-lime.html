presence or absence of a word, or of a contiguous patch of similar pixels - while the model may work over embeddings or a three-channel tensor INTERPRETABILITY IS DEFINED AS AUDIENCE-RELATIVE, not as a property of the model class: the authors state that a linear model, a gradient vector or an additive model MAY OR MAY NOT be interpretable, because the criterion has to take the user's limitations into account and the audience-relativity is made concrete rather than left as a caveat: practitioners may be able to read a small Bayesian network where laymen are better served by a few weighted features THE IMPOSSIBILITY IS STATED BEFORE THE CRITERION IS WEAKENED: an explanation cannot be completely faithful unless it IS the complete description of the model, so the requirement imposed instead is that it be faithful in the vicinity of the instance being explained AND THE IMPLICATION IS STATED TO RUN ONE WAY ONLY: local fidelity does not imply global fidelity - a feature that matters globally may not matter locally, and the reverse - while global fidelity would imply local, and globally faithful interpretable explanations are said to remain a challenge the explainer is required to treat the model as a BLACK BOX, and the reason given is not only that current classifiers are uninterpretable but that model-agnosticism buys the ability to explain classifiers that do not exist yet THE TRADE-OFF IS PUT IN THE OBJECTIVE RATHER THAN LEFT TO JUDGEMENT: unfaithfulness in the neighbourhood is minimised while a separate measure of the explanation's own complexity is held low enough for a human to read, and the authors note the same formulation admits different explanation families, fidelity functions and complexity measures the neighbourhood is explored BY SAMPLING rather than by assumption, precisely to keep the method model-agnostic: no assumption is made about the model, and the local loss is approximated by drawing samples weighted by proximity to the instance samples are drawn both near the instance and FAR from it, weighted by proximity -
