sensesPRIMING DEFINED AS AN ORDINARY FEATURE OF BEING SOMEWHERE: `Priming refers to the INCIDENTAL ACTIVATION of knowledge structures, such as trait concepts and stereotypes, BY THE CURRENT SITUATIONAL CONTEXT.` **Not a laboratory operation - a description of what situations do to people continuously**, of which the experiment is a controlled instance. That is the same move srull-wyer1979 makes when it says accessibility rises with the number of activations `IN THE PAST`, and it is what licenses generalising from a word-scramble task to walking down a corridor
“Priming refers to the incidental activation of knowledge structures, such as trait concepts and stereotypes, by the current situational context”

sensesTHE DESIGN LOGIC IN ONE SENTENCE: `The same priming techniques that have been shown in prior research to influence IMPRESSION FORMATION produce similar effects when THE DEPENDENT MEASURE IS SWITCHED to social behavior.` **Only the dependent measure changed.** The manipulation is held constant against the 1977-79 accessibility work and the outcome is swapped from a judgement to an action - which makes this a claim about the SCOPE of an established effect rather than a new effect. **And it means the replication debate about this paper is unavoidably also about higgins1977 and srull-wyer1979**, both of which are in this corpus

concept_edgesstereotype —causes→ behavior
“Across three experiments, the activation of a trait construct or a stereotype in one context resulted in behavior consistent with it in a subsequent unrelated context”
direction on the measure: increases · strength of the relation: 0.9 — **`increases` MEANS MORE STEREOTYPE-CONSISTENT BEHAVIOUR, NOT MORE OF ANY MEASURED QUANTITY, AND THE DISTINCTION MATTERS HERE.** In Experiment 2 the measurement WENT DOWN - participants primed with `elderly` WALKED MORE SLOWLY. A reader taking `increases` as `the number went up` would have that experiment backwards. The axis is recording consistency with the primed content, which is what the quote says, and the three experiments move three different dependent variables in three directions.
