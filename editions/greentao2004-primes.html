differs in: WHAT THE DENSITY IS MEASURED AGAINST. Szemerédi's theorem is about 'any subset of THE INTEGERS of positive density'; the primes are not such a subset. The transference principle is about 'any subset of a sufficiently PSEUDORANDOM SET (or measure) of POSITIVE RELATIVE DENSITY'. Same word, two ambient sets, and the whole proof lives in the gap between them · strength of the relation: 0.9 — CHANGING THE DENOMINATOR IS THE PROOF TECHNIQUE, NOT AN ARTEFACT OF ONE. The primes are too sparse for Szemerédi; so 'one may PLACE (a large fraction of) THE PRIMES INSIDE A PSEUDORANDOM SET of "almost primes" ... with positive relative density', and the transference principle carries the theorem across. The object is not changed and the theorem is not reproved - THE AMBIENT SET AGAINST WHICH DENSITY IS COUNTED IS CHANGED.
