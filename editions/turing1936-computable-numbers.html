Turing machine universal machine computability halting problem Entscheidungsproblem circle-free circular computable number effective calculability universal computing machine THE TERM IS INTRODUCED IN SCARE QUOTES AND GIVEN A BRIEF DESCRIPTION RATHER THAN A DEFINITION: the COMPUTABLE numbers MAY BE DESCRIBED BRIEFLY as the real numbers whose expressions as a decimal are CALCULABLE BY FINITE MEANS. The quotation marks and the word BRIEFLY both mark that the real definitional work is still to come THE OBJECT OF STUDY IS CHOSEN FOR TECHNICAL CONVENIENCE AND THE AUTHOR SAYS SO: the subject is OSTENSIBLY the computable numbers, several other objects would be ALMOST EQUALLY EASY to treat, THE FUNDAMENTAL PROBLEMS INVOLVED ARE THE SAME IN EACH CASE - and the numbers were chosen FOR EXPLICIT TREATMENT AS INVOLVING THE LEAST CUMBROUS TECHNIQUE. What the paper is about is a presentational decision THE DEFINITION IS OFFERED IN THE FIRST PERSON POSSESSIVE: ACCORDING TO MY DEFINITION, a number is computable IF ITS DECIMAL CAN BE WRITTEN DOWN BY A MACHINE. The criterion is a capability of a device, and the sentence marks that this is one definition among possible others THE ADEQUACY OF THE DEFINITION TO THE INFORMAL NOTION IS ANNOUNCED AS SOMETHING TO BE ARGUED IN ITS OWN SECTIONS: two sections are given over to ARGUMENTS WITH THE INTENTION OF SHOWING that the computable numbers INCLUDE ALL NUMBERS WHICH COULD NATURALLY BE REGARDED AS COMPUTABLE - so the match between the formal class and the natural one is itself a claim requiring support TWO NEIGHBOURING TERMS ARE SEPARATED BY A COUNTEREXAMPLE RATHER THAN BY ARGUMENT: the computable numbers DO NOT include all DEFINABLE numbers, AND AN EXAMPLE IS GIVEN of a definable number which is not computable. The boundary between the two words is established by exhibiting one object that falls on the far side of it ANOTHER AUTHOR'S TERM IS RECORDED AS EQUIVALENT AND VERY DIFFERENTLY DEFINED: Church has introduced an idea of EFFECTIVE CALCULABILITY, WHICH IS EQUIVALENT TO MY COMPUTABILITY, BUT IS VERY DIFFERENTLY DEFINED - and the PROOF of the equivalence is deferred to an appendix. Two vocabularies, one extension, and the identity is a theorem rather than a convention THE CENTRAL ADMISSION, AND IT IS THIS PROJECT'S PROBLEM STATED IN 1936: NO ATTEMPT HAS YET BEEN MADE to show that the COMPUTABLE numbers include all numbers WHICH WOULD NATURALLY BE REGARDED AS COMPUTABLE, and ALL ARGUMENTS WHICH CAN BE GIVEN ARE BOUND TO BE, FUNDAMENTALLY, APPEALS TO INTUITION, AND FOR THIS REASON RATHER UNSATISFACTORY MATHEMATICALLY. Whether a formalism captures the notion it was built for cannot be proved, only argued - said by the person who built the formalism THE QUESTION BEHIND THE DEFINITION IS NAMED, AND IT IS NOT A MATHEMATICAL QUESTION: THE REAL QUESTION AT ISSUE IS WHAT ARE THE POSSIBLE PROCESSES WHICH CAN BE CARRIED OUT IN COMPUTING A NUMBER. The formal apparatus is answerable to a claim about what can be done, not about what follows from axioms A TAXONOMY OF WAYS TO ARGUE THAT A DEFINITION CAPTURES ITS CONCEPT, WITH A CONDITION ON THE SECOND: (a) A DIRECT APPEAL TO INTUITION; (b) A PROOF OF THE EQUIVALENCE OF TWO DEFINITIONS - IN CASE THE NEW DEFINITION HAS A GREATER INTUITIVE APPEAL; (c) GIVING EXAMPLES OF LARGE CLASSES of numbers which are computable. **An equivalence proof is evidence for adequacy only if one of the two definitions is the more intuitive** - otherwise it moves the problem rather than solving it A FEATURE OF THE APPARATUS IS DECLARED INESSENTIAL BY AN APPEAL TO THE READER'S ASSENT: the two-dimensional character of the paper IS SOMETIMES USED in elementary arithmetic, but SUCH A USE IS ALWAYS AVOIDABLE, and - I THINK THAT IT WILL BE AGREED - it is NO ESSENTIAL of computation. The idealisation is argued for rather than assumed, and the argument offered is that the reader will agree A RESTRICTION IS JUSTIFIED BY THE INDISCRIMINABILITY THAT WOULD FOLLOW WITHOUT IT, AND THEN MINIMISED: if an infinity of symbols were allowed there would be SYMBOLS DIFFERING TO AN ARBITRARILY SMALL EXTENT - and the effect of the restriction IS NOT VERY SERIOUS, because SEQUENCES of symbols can always be used in place of single ones. The bound is defended by what could not be told apart THE SAME ARGUMENT IS APPLIED TO THE MIND, AND ITS FORM IS EXPLICITLY REUSED: the number of STATES OF MIND which need be taken into account is finite, THE REASONS FOR THIS ARE OF THE SAME CHARACTER as those restricting the symbols, because an infinity of states of mind would include some ARBITRARILY CLOSE that WILL BE CONFUSED - and again the restriction DOES NOT SERIOUSLY AFFECT COMPUTATION, since complicated states can be traded for more symbols on the tape THE ATOM OF THE ANALYSIS IS DEFINED BY THE LIMITS OF IMAGINATION: the operations are split into SIMPLE OPERATIONS WHICH ARE SO ELEMENTARY THAT IT IS NOT EASY TO IMAGINE THEM FURTHER DIVIDED. What counts as indivisible is fixed by what cannot readily be pictured as divided, not by a property of the operations AN EMPIRICAL CLAIM ABOUT READING IS ADMITTED INTO A MATHEMATICAL ARGUMENT AND MARKED AS SUCH: compound symbols, if too lengthy, CANNOT BE OBSERVED AT ONE GLANCE - THIS IS IN ACCORDANCE WITH EXPERIENCE - and the demonstration offered is that WE CANNOT TELL AT A GLANCE whether two long strings of nines are the same. A fact about human perception is doing load-bearing work in a proof about computability computable machine numbers tape symbols configuration finite universal decimal m-configuration mathematical-proof
