mechanismsResidual activation (the one-locus account): using a construction leaves its combinatorial node and the lemma linked to it partly activated, so the same structure is cheaper to produce for a while. The lexical boost falls out of the same machinery - a repeated verb re-activates both the lemma AND the strengthened link to the node - which is what makes it ONE locus.
“On this account, priming is due to residual activation of combinatorial and lemma nodes, as well as the links between the two (as illustrated in Panel C of Figure 2 ). More specifically, lexically independent priming occurs as a result of the residual activation”

mechanismsImplicit learning: producing or understanding a structure requires having learned how meaning relationships map onto functional elements and onto constituent order, and every sentence experienced re-weights those mappings. On this account priming is not a trace that decays but the visible edge of the same learning that acquired the grammar.

sensesstructural priming versus syntactic priming - A NAME CHOSEN AGAINST A THEORY, and the clearest instance of it in this corpus. The review drops `syntactic priming` (Pickering's own term from 1999) for `structural priming` on the explicit ground that the old name PRESUPPOSES THE EXISTENCE OF SPECIFICALLY SYNTACTIC REPRESENTATIONS - the very thing the method is used to test. A term retired because it smuggles in the conclusion, with the reason stated in the sentence that retires it
“Some researchers use the term syntactic priming (e.g., Pickering & Branigan, 1999 ), but we shall use structural priming because it encompasses abstract linguistic priming that need not be syntactic (e.g., Bock, Loebell, & Morey, 1992 ), and indeed does not presuppose the existence of specifically syntactic representations”
senses[1]0.95the tagger's words
sensespriming as a DIAGNOSTIC INSTRUMENT - the reason the field runs these experiments at all, and the review states the logic in general form before applying it: because being affected by repetition depends on how knowledge is represented, the pattern of what does and does not prime is used to READ OFF the representations. The effect is evidence about something else
“the tendency to be affected by the repetition of aspects of knowledge can be used to diagnose the nature of that knowledge. So in this case, the tendency to repeat aspects of sentence structure helps researchers identify some of the representations that people construct”
