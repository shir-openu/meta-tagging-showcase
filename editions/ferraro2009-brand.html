sensesTHE MECHANISM IS PROVED BY MISATTRIBUTION, WHICH IS THE STRONGEST FORM AVAILABLE FOR A FEELING: `Study 2 shows that, IF THE EXPERIENCED FLUENCY generated by repeated exposure CAN BE ATTRIBUTED TO ANOTHER SOURCE, then the effect WILL BE ATTENUATED.` Give the ease somewhere else to have come from and the preference goes with it. **That rules out the alternative that repetition simply builds a memory or a habit** - a memory would not care where you thought the ease came from
“Study 2 shows that, if the experienced fluency generated by repeated exposure can be attributed to another source, then the effect will be attenuated”
senses[0]0.95the tagger's words
sensesAND THE DOSE-RESPONSE IS NON-MONOTONIC: `Study 3 suggests that TOO MUCH FLUENCY CAN BACKFIRE, which is consistent with the SATURATION MODEL.` More exposure is not more effect. **This is the one finding here that a replication is most likely to invert by accident**: increasing the number of exposures to be safe would move the design past the peak and find less. A corpus that records `repeated exposure increases choice` without the saturation clause would license exactly that mistake
“Study 3 suggests that too much fluency can backfire, which is consistent with the saturation model proposed by Fazendeiro et al”

sensesUNAWARENESS USED AS A CONTROL RATHER THAN REPORTED AS A FINDING: `the participants in study 4 WERE NOT AWARE of the brand and hence COULD NOT BE AWARE of its association with the in-group or out-group, SO CONSCIOUS CORRECTION WAS NOT POSSIBLE.` **This is higgins1977's discounting cue turned into a design feature.** Higgins found that noticing your own bias ATTENUATES it; here, showing that participants could not notice is what licenses reading the result as uncorrected. Same mechanism, opposite use - one paper measures it, the other rules it out by construction
