sensesTHE DEFINITION AND THEN THE REFUSAL OF ITS CONNOTATION, IN CONSECUTIVE SENTENCES. Self-sufficiency is `an INSULATED STATE wherein people put forth effort to attain personal goals and prefer to be separate from others` - and then: `The term as we define it DOES NOT IMPLY A VALUE JUDGMENT and encompasses a mixture of desirable and undesirable qualities.` **That second sentence is the paper's whole argumentative move.** `Self-sufficiency` in ordinary English is a compliment; stripped of its valence it can carry `worked harder` and `helped less` at once, which is exactly what the nine experiments show
“The term as we define it does not imply a value judgment and encompasses a mixture of desirable and undesirable qualities, which may help explain the positive and negative consequences of money”

sensesTHE OBJECT IS AN IDEA AND THE PAPER SAYS SO BEFORE ANY EXPERIMENT: `In this Report, `money` refers to a distinct entity, a particular economic concept ... we use the term money to represent THE IDEA OF MONEY, NOT PROPERTY OR POSSESSIONS.` Nobody is given, shown or promised wealth. **A stipulative definition placed before the method**, which is rare in this corpus and is what makes the result a claim about concepts rather than about incentives - and it is precisely the move a critic must accept before the findings can even be stated
