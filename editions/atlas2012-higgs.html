a signal strength that scales the predicted signal - and it is defined so that zero is the background-only hypothesis and one is the Standard Model Higgs signal, so the hypothesis under test is a value of that one factor hypothesised values of that strength are tested with a profile likelihood ratio, which extracts the signal strength from a full likelihood fit whose function INCLUDES all the parameters describing the systematic uncertainties and their correlations exclusion is decided by a stated numerical rule rather than by judgement: a signal strength is excluded at 95% confidence when its CLs value falls below 5%, and a Standard Model Higgs of a given mass counts as excluded when the value one is excluded at that mass the size of an excess is first expressed as the probability that background alone could produce a fluctuation at least that large, and only then converted into the equivalent number of standard deviations - the local significance that local number is then corrected for having searched a whole mass range rather than one point: the ratio of global to local probability is the trials factor, and the paper states what it depends on - the range of hypotheses considered, the mass resolutions of the channels combined, and the size of the excess itself the tests are run in steps across the hypothesised mass rather than at one chosen point, and the asymptotic approximation the results rest on is not assumed to hold - it is validated by a separately described method the combination across channels is built on the same single signal strength together with an explicit identification of which nuisance parameters correspond to the CORRELATED sources of systematic uncertainty the jet energy scale and resolution are handled by a deliberately simplified scheme -
