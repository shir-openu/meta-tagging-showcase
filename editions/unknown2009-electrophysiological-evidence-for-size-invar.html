sensesPRIMING IS THE INSTRUMENT AND INVARIANCE IS THE SUBJECT, stated in the abstract's first sentence: `This experiment examined INVARIANCE IN OBJECT REPRESENTATIONS through measuring event-related potentials (ERPs) to pictures in a masked repetition priming paradigm.` Whether priming SURVIVES a transformation is the measurement of whether the representation is invariant to it - so the experiment is about object representation, and priming is the apparatus. The pickering2008 distinction (Q-744) in a third literature, after Argiris2021
“This experiment examined invariance in object representations through measuring event-related potentials (ERPs) to pictures in a masked repetition priming paradigm”

sensesNECESSARY, ALTHOUGH NOT ALWAYS SUFFICIENT - an explicit logical qualifier, which is rare enough in this corpus to be worth a row of its own. `Overlapping perceptual information between the prime and target is NECESSARY, ALTHOUGH NOT ALWAYS SUFFICIENT, to activate a higher level semantic representation.` Necessity and the denial of sufficiency in one clause, and the design earns both halves separately: the mirror condition gives early overlap WITHOUT the later effect (not sufficient), and the size-mirror condition removes the early effect and the later one with it (necessary). The record's mechanisms[0] paraphrases only the necessity half

sensesTHE LATE STAGE IS MORE INVARIANT THAN THE EARLY ONE, which is the opposite of the obvious ordering. `The N400 unlike the N190/P190 appears to be FULLY invariant to size, since the repetition effect for the small prime condition DID NOT DIFFER from the repetition effect for the identity priming condition` - a null stated with its statistical basis attached. The early perceptual effect survives the same manipulation but pays for it: it `compensates for differences in size, BUT NOT WITHOUT A SMALL PROCESSING COST`. So invariance is not a property a representation has or lacks; it is graded, and it is cheaper further up
