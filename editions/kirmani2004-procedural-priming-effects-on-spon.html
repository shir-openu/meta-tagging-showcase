procedural priming cognitive procedures effort investment rule spontaneous inference facilitative prime suppressive prime no prime cognitive capacity retrieval computation demand effects spontaneous inferencing quality perceptions response times The facilitative prime makes the effort-belief production accessible, so the quality inference is drawn at the moment the ad cost information is encoded; at judgment the subject only retrieves the stored evaluation instead of computing one. Retrieval consumes less cognitive capacity than computation, so a load imposed at judgment slows computed judgments much more than retrieved ones - which is what makes response time a diagnostic of spontaneity.
