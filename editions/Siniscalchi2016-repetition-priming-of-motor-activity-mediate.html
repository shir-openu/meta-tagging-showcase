“repetition priming is characterized by increased performance as a behavior is repeated. Although repetition priming is observed in many species and contexts (see, e.g., Epuru et al. 1995 ; Schacter and Buckner 1998 ; Sharma et al. 2003 ; Sinnamon et al. 2000 ), there have been relatively few studies that have characterized underlying mechanisms”
THE SAME WORD AT BOTH ENDS OF ITS POSSIBLE EXTENSION, IN ONE CORPUS. One paper applies `priming` to a preparation with no subject at all - a buccal ganglion on a dish, where the criterion is that a motor pattern becomes better articulated on repetition. Another makes the ABSENCE OF A SUBJECT'S AWARENESS the defining criterion, and proposes an operational test built entirely on asking the person what they noticed. **A definition requiring unawareness and a definition with nobody to be unaware cannot be the same definition**, yet neither paper marks the word as belonging to another field. Both are ordinary uses in their own literatures. This is not a disagreement and not an error by either: it is the extension of a term stretched past the point where one definition can cover it, which is the object this project was assembled to document.
