senses**THE PRIME WORKS LEAST ON THE PEOPLE IT IS ABOUT, AND THIS IS PREDICTED IN ADVANCE RATHER THAN EXPLAINED AFTERWARDS:** `the prime is expected to activate an otherwise DORMANT aspect of the self-concept for people LOW in moral identity centrality but HAVE LITTLE INFLUENCE on people with highly central moral identities BECAUSE THEIR MORAL SELF-SCHEMAS ARE ALREADY ACCESSIBLE.` higgins1977 raises exactly this variable in ONE CONJECTURAL SENTENCE - `There may even be individual differences in the relative accessibility of different categories` - and does not test it. **This paper builds four studies on it and finds it REVERSES the priming effect**, so the same trait that makes someone morally consistent is the one that makes them unprimeable. A 1977 aside and its test 32 years later, both in this corpus. (The term `chronic accessibility` is Higgins, King & Mavin 1982 and appears nowhere in higgins1977.) A study sampling only committed people would find nothing and conclude the effect is not real
“In other words, the prime is expected to activate an otherwise dormant aspect of the self-concept for people low in moral identity centrality but have little influence on people with highly central moral identities because their moral self-schemas are already accessible”

senses**PRIMING PLACED AS ONE ROUTE AMONG THREE TO THE SAME STATE:** `The activation potential of any particular knowledge structure can be the result of several factors ranging from FREQUENT PRIMING, to INHERITED PERSONALITY ORIENTATIONS, to REPEATED ACTS OF self-definition (Higgins, 1989).` Three roads to accessibility, of which the experimental one is only the fastest. **That sentence does more for a corpus about priming than most results do, because it says what priming is an INSTANCE OF** - and it makes the individual-difference moderator in this paper a consequence of the theory rather than a nuisance variable. senses**AND STUDY 4 REVERSES THE MODERATION OVER TIME, WHICH A ONE-SHOT STUDY WOULD HAVE SCORED AS A NULL:** initially the prime does little for high-centrality people, exactly as the ceiling account predicts. But under repeated feedback that others are behaving selfishly, `WITHOUT A MORAL PRIME, participants for whom moral identity was highly central DID NOT SUSTAIN an increased level of cooperative behavior in the face of selfish behavior by others.` **The prime's value is not the initial push but the resistance to decay.** A corpus that records only peak effect sizes cannot represent this finding at all

senses[2]0.95the tagger's words
senses**THE MODERATION IS STATED TWICE AND THE SECOND TIME IT IS A RESULT:** `However, the influence of moral priming IS NOT UNIFORM ACROSS PEOPLE. Rather, moral priming appears to have STRONGER effects on people for whom moral identity has relatively LOW centrality because such priming has GREATER POTENTIAL to increase the accessibility of moral identity within the working self-concept.` The mechanism is a headroom argument, and it is testable: it predicts that any manipulation raising a construct's accessibility will show the same inverse relation to that construct's chronic strength. **This record and higgins1977 are the two ends of that claim in this corpus, and the two ends are not equal** - higgins1977 says only `THERE MAY EVEN BE individual differences in the relative accessibility of different categories`, a speculation with no test attached, while this paper makes that variable its moderator and predicts where a prime will fail
“However, the influence of moral priming is not uniform across people.
