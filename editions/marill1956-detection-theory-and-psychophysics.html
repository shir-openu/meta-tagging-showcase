d' detectability detection situation forced-choice method detection theory traditional psychophysical models A bandpass filter in the auditory system whose passband is about 13.5 db wider than the ideal filter admits proportionally more noise power, so the detector behaves exactly like the ideal detector but requires a correspondingly higher signal-to-noise ratio. When two signal components fall in non-overlapping auditory filters, the two filter outputs are statistically independent, so the two detectors' decisions are independent as well. A decision based on the signs of two independent detector outputs can never beat the better single detector, which is why the 500+1100 cps signal is only as detectable as its more detectable component. ideal detector symmetrical payoff matrix two-category forced-choice method uniform gaussian noise equivalent rectangular bandwidth signal level in decibels frequency separation of the two signal components detectability (probability of a correct response) masking noise level and power spectrum background noise level across runs t-test the t-test technique least squares a least-squares method for fitting the curves proportion of variance explained behavioural-experiment
