sensesKNOWING A STEREOTYPE IS NOT BELIEVING IT, AND THE KNOWING IS EQUAL IN EVERYONE. The model separates `knowledge of the racial stereotype, which Study 1 suggested both high and low-prejudice persons possess, and PERSONAL BELIEFS about the stereotyped group`, and Study 2 found automatic activation `EQUALLY STRONG AND EQUALLY INESCAPABLE` across both groups. **So low prejudice is not the ABSENCE of the automatic response - it is the PRESENCE of a controlled one.** Everything the model claims about change depends on that being two systems rather than one
“Study 3, however, provided evidence that controlled processes can inhibit the effects of automatic processing when the implications of such processing compete with goals to establish or maintain a nonprejudiced identity”
senses[0]0.95the tagger's words
sensesA DISAGREEMENT ARGUED ON WHAT THE RIVAL POSITION WOULD IMPLY, NOT ON THE EVIDENCE. Crosby et al. held that nonconsciously monitored responses are MORE TRUSTWORTHY than consciously mediated ones - that the automatic response reveals what someone really thinks. Devine: `I DISAGREE FUNDAMENTALLY WITH THIS PREMISE. Such an argument DENIES THE POSSIBILITY FOR CHANGE in one's attitudes and beliefs, and I view this as a SEVERE LIMITATION of the Crosby et al. (1980) analysis.` **First person singular, and the objection is to a CONSEQUENCE.** The rival is not shown to be wrong about the data; it is shown to entail something the author will not accept, and that is stated openly rather than dressed as an empirical point
“In the context of the present model in which automatic processes and controlled processes can be dissociated, I disagree fundamentally with this premise. Such an argument denies the possibility for change in one's attitudes and beliefs, and I view this as a severe limitation of the Crosby et al”
