repetition priming repetition suppression Sharpening neural synchronization processing efficiency Repetition makes task-engaged cells fire more synchronously even as their average rates fall, so processing becomes more efficient and behaviour improves despite reduced activity. Coincident input spikes sum on the receiving cell's membrane before the depolarization decays, so synchronous inputs cross spike threshold more reliably than the same number of asynchronous inputs. In the Bayesian alternative, learned top-down predictions inhibit the bottom-up sensory evidence, so the residual prediction error - and hence the measured activity - shrinks with repetition.
