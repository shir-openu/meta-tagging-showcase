special relativity Lorentz transformation simultaneity speed of light Principle of Relativity postulate luminiferous ether absolutely stationary space synchronous stationary clocks electromotive force auxiliary concept mass of the moving electron definition of force A CONJECTURE IS RAISED TO THE STATUS OF A POSTULATE BY DECLARATION, AND NAMED IN THE SAME SENTENCE: the epistemic status of the claim is changed by an act of writing rather than by evidence, and the name it will carry hereafter is fixed in the same breath. A second postulate is introduced alongside it and described as ONLY APPARENTLY irreconcilable with the first AN ENTITY IS DECLARED SUPERFLUOUS RATHER THAN REFUTED: the luminiferous ether is not argued to be absent but shown to be unnecessary, because the view to be developed will not require an absolutely stationary space provided with special properties, nor assign a velocity-vector to a point of empty space A TERM IS COINED EXPRESSLY TO MAKE A VERBAL DISTINCTION, AND THE STATED PURPOSE IS PRESENTATIONAL: the coordinate system is called the stationary system in order to render the presentation more precise and to distinguish it VERBALLY from others that will be introduced later A COMMON TERM CANNOT BE DEFINED AT ALL WITHOUT A STIPULATION, AND THE PAPER NAMES WHICH PART IS STIPULATED: an A-time and a B-time have been defined, but a common time for A and B CANNOT BE DEFINED AT ALL unless it is established BY DEFINITION that light takes the same time from A to B as from B to A. The equality is established, not measured THE PROPERTIES THE DEFINITION NEEDS ARE ASSUMED, NOT DERIVED: that the definition of synchronism is free from contradictions and possible for any number of points is ASSUMED, and so are the two relations quoted - the symmetry of synchrony and its transitivity - which are stated as universally valid rather than shown A DEFINITION IS DECLARED SATISFACTORY AT ONE SCOPE AND UNSATISFACTORY AT ANOTHER, WITH BOTH SCOPES NAMED: substituting the position of a watch's hand for time works when defining a time exclusively for the place where the watch is, and stops working as soon as events at different places must be put in one series. The word does not fail - it fails OUTSIDE A STATED RANGE ONE WORD IS GIVEN TO TWO DIFFERENT OPERATIONS AND THE PAPER'S RESULT IS THAT THEY DISAGREE: two measuring procedures are set out, the outcome of each is called THE LENGTH OF THE ROD, and they are told apart only by a trailing qualifier - in the moving system, in the stationary system - after which the text states plainly that the second differs from the first THE PREVIOUS THEORY'S ASSUMPTION IS MADE VISIBLE BY BEING NAMED AS TACIT: current kinematics is said to assume TACITLY that the two operations give precisely equal lengths, and the assumption is then restated in other words - that a moving rigid body may be perfectly represented by the same body at rest TWO SETS OF OBSERVERS APPLY THE SAME STATED CRITERION AND REACH OPPOSITE VERDICTS: one finds the clocks not synchronous while the other DECLARES them synchronous, from which the paper concludes that no ABSOLUTE SIGNIFICATION can be attached to the concept of simultaneity. The concept survives and loses its claim to hold independently of a frame THE SAME EQUATIONS ARE CLOTHED IN WORDS TWICE AND THE TWO READINGS ARE LABELLED BY THEIR ERA: identical mathematics is given an OLD MANNER OF EXPRESSION, in which a moving charge feels an extra electromotive force, and a NEW MANNER OF EXPRESSION, in which it feels the electric force found by transforming to its own rest frame. THE CONTENT IS ONE AND THE VOCABULARY IS TWO, and the paper marks the difference as historical rather than factual A CONCEPT IS DEMOTED TO AN AUXILIARY THAT OWES ITS EXISTENCE TO THE DESCRIPTION FRAME: electromotive force is said to play MERELY the part of an auxiliary concept, which OWES ITS INTRODUCTION to the circumstance that electric and magnetic forces do not exist independently of the state of motion of the coordinate system. The term is explained by the bookkeeping, not by the world A CLASS OF QUESTIONS IS DISSOLVED RATHER THAN ANSWERED: the asymmetry the paper opened with is said to DISAPPEAR, and questions as to the SEAT of electrodynamic electromotive forces in unipolar machines are said to NOW HAVE NO POINT - retired without being settled either way A DEFINITION IS CRITICISED AS NOT ADVANTAGEOUS RATHER THAN WRONG, AND THE CRITERION IS SIMPLICITY OF THE RESULTING LAWS: a footnote records that the definition of force given here is not advantageous, as Planck first showed, and that it is more to the point to define force so that the laws of momentum and energy take their simplest form. THE GROUND FOR PREFERRING ONE DEFINITION IS WHAT IT DOES TO THE REST OF THE THEORY AN INEXACTITUDE IN THE CENTRAL CONCEPT IS ACKNOWLEDGED AND EXPRESSLY NOT DISCUSSED: a footnote says an inexactitude LURKS in the concept of simultaneity of two events at approximately the same place and can only be removed by an abstraction - and then declines to take it up. The residue is named, located, and left NOT EINSTEIN'S VOICE - THE 1999 EDITION'S OWN NOTE. THE MOST FAMOUS SYMBOL IN THE THEORY IS NOT THE AUTHOR'S: the note records that the 1923 English translation modified Einstein's notation to conform to that in use by the 1920s, and that `c` for the speed of light replaced the `V` he actually wrote in 1905 NOT EINSTEIN'S VOICE - THE 1999 EDITION'S OWN NOTE. A SYMBOL COLLISION WAS INTRODUCED BY TRANSLATION: the moving system's coordinates were introduced in the original paper WITHOUT BEING EXPLICITLY DEFINED, the 1923 translation used (X, Y, Z) for them, and the note says this CREATED AN AMBIGUITY between the X co-ordinates of the fixed system and the parallel axis of the moving one theory
