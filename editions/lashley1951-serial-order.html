A symposium address, not an empirical report: no new data are collected, so 'method' is recorded as review of existing evidence and argument. The paper is one of the earliest sources for the idea of preliminary partial activation ('priming') of expressive units, though the word is used descriptively rather than as a measured effect. senses**WHAT THIS RECORD DOES AND DOES NOT SAY ABOUT THE REST OF THE CORPUS.** Lashley's target is the associative chain - `Watson (26) sought to identify thought with inaudible movements of the vocal organs, LINKED TOGETHER IN ASSOCIATIVE CHAINS` - and this corpus is largely built on measuring association between elements: meyer1971's free-association norms, collins1975's spreading activation, hutchison2003's association strength. **They are not the same claim.** Lashley is about the ORDER of action and speech; spreading activation is about retrieval, and retrieval has no ordering problem to solve. The honest statement is narrower and still worth holding: the corpus contains, from 1951, a sustained argument that association cannot by itself produce order - and from 1971 onward, a literature that measures association and rarely asks what orders anything
