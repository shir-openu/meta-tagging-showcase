A word is not a concept. attention in a machine-learning paper is a lookup over key–value pairs, and its author says so explicitly — not anything psychological. In a neuroscience review the same word carries James’s classic definition, which that review says conflates two processes now known to be separable. Neither paper is using the word loosely. They are different concepts, and only reading both senses side by side shows it.

Why the list is not longer

A sense label here is an explanation, not a name — one paper writes “time – the reading of one clock, defined only for the place that clock occupies” and, two rows later, a second sense of the same word. Compared as whole labels, 2,501 senses share nothing at all, which is correct: no two papers explain a word identically. What joins is the term before the dash. Everything below is a term two fields have both bothered to define.
